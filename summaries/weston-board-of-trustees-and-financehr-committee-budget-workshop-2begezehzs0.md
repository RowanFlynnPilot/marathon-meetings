## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees and Finance/HR Committee held a joint budget workshop covering the 2027 preliminary budget, including wage and benefit analysis, general fund breakdown, special revenue funds, and a discussion of potential modified summer hours. No final budget decisions were made, as the board has three more budget workshops remaining before an end-of-October deadline; the primary formal action taken was reclassifying an engineering technician from FLSA-exempt to non-exempt status, with retroactive effect to his July 6 start date.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comments
Three residents spoke. A woman identified as Grace (603 Jessica, Weston) raised concerns about the proposed Friday afternoon closures and asked that overtime calculations for reclassified hourly employees be based on a 40-hour workweek, not eight hours per day; staff confirmed Weston uses the over-40-hours standard. Jim Pel criticized the lack of a clear, readable answer to a previously requested 1%-salary-impact figure, argued against closing Village Hall on Fridays as inequitable to public-facing departments, and suggested extended evening hours instead; he also complained the PowerPoint was not in the packet until the day of the meeting. A third resident, John of 1506 Daily Avenue, questioned why a Weston ordinance required a licensed plumber to replace an anti-siphon faucet rather than allowing a homeowner to do it themselves.

### Approval of August 24, 2026 Joint Budget Workshop Minutes
The Finance/HR Committee approved the minutes on a motion by Daniels, seconded by [inaudible]. The Board of Trustees approved the same minutes on a motion by Beck, seconded by Ermeling. Both votes carried without dissent.

### 2027 Budget Review: Wage and Benefit Analysis
Finance staff presented slides showing that a 1% wage increase costs approximately $17,583 in the general fund, with the caveat that the calculation excludes elected officials and election workers who receive no scheduled increases, causing confusion when trustees tried to verify the math. Trustee Christina [inaudible] noted the numbers did not 'math out' and requested that future spreadsheets include contextual notes or assumptions. Staff explained that the benefit figures also incorporate a preliminary 10% health insurance increase and WRS cost increases, making a straight 1% calculation misleading. Village Administrator [inaudible] and Finance Director Jessica defended releasing complex data at the meeting rather than in advance to prevent misinterpretation, while trustees pushed back, saying advance access is necessary for informed deliberation. Both sides acknowledged the tension and agreed future budget documents would include explanatory notes.

### General Fund Breakdown and Transportation Aids
Staff presented the $9.6 million 2026 general fund, noting that roughly 55% is locked in public safety contracts with Mountain Bay and South Area Fire (SAFER), leaving the board meaningful discretion over only about $3.3 million. Administrator Michael [inaudible] explained that transportation aid revenue rose significantly after years of steep annual declines from roughly 2012–2021, driven by reduced infrastructure spending in TIF districts, but warned the aid formula is expected to plateau or decline again around 2030–2031. Trustees were cautioned not to commit transportation aid dollars to non-transportation purposes. Staff also used the Wisconsin Policy Forum's municipal comparison tool to show Weston has among the lowest general government expenditures and property tax levy per capita compared to neighboring communities, and is second-lowest in shared revenue per capita in the comparison group. The village's Moody's bond rating of Aa3 (issued August 2026) was noted as solid, constrained partly by Marathon County's Aa2 ceiling.

### Special Revenue Funds
Staff reviewed six special revenue funds. The refuse/recycling fund is projected to draw down its $156,000 fund balance by approximately $62,000 in 2026 due to unexpected diesel fuel surcharges under the Harder's contract; staff indicated the $25.50 resident fee may need to increase to $27.10 for 2027, pending August data. The aquatic center fund is projected to roughly break even or gain $8,000 in 2026 after fee increases ($5 to $7 daily, higher season passes boosted revenues despite lower attendance); a $100,000 capital borrowing placeholder was included for a pool leak repair whose actual cost is still unknown. TIF District No. 1 is closing, with a roughly $400,000 one-time levy adjustment expected; approximately $1.95 million is being held to retire CDA lease revenue bond debt. TIF District No. 2 (Schofield Avenue) carries a roughly $470,000 deficit projected through end of 2027 after the state slashed assessed value in early 2025. Room tax: the village's 30% share produced about $197,000 to the Weston account in 2025 out of $461,000 paid to the CVB; the fund balance is growing to approximately $230,000 through Q2 and staff flagged it must be spent on tourism purposes before the next two-year CVB agreement renewal in 2027. The Civic, Social and Park Trust fund will resume $500 scholarships through DC Everest for the first time in years, funded by improved CD interest rates (approximately 4.01%).

### FLSA Reclassification of Engineering Technician
Public Works Director Michael [inaudible] explained that a newly hired engineering technician serving as on-site inspector for the Concord A project had been incorrectly classified as FLSA-exempt (salaried) since his July 6 hire date. The Finance/HR Committee and board both voted to reclassify the position to non-exempt (hourly) retroactive to July 6, 2026. Staff estimated the retroactive overtime owed is less than $1,000 in wages, and noted the position generates significant savings versus contracting out inspection at roughly $120/hour compared to approximately $30–35/hour in-house. The Finance/HR Committee voted first, then the Board of Trustees adopted a corresponding motion.

### Potential Modified Summer Hours for 2027
Village President Maloney introduced a discussion on potentially adopting a compressed summer schedule (e.g., Monday–Thursday extended hours with a half-day Friday) similar to neighboring municipalities: Rothschild, Kronenwetter, and Schofield operate 7 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Monday–Thursday and 7–11 a.m. Friday; Rib Mountain does 7:30–5 and 8:30–1; Wausau uses Monday/Wednesday/Thursday 7:30–4:30 with a late Tuesday and half-day Friday. Staff clarified that public works already runs four 10-hour days (Memorial Day–Labor Day) and would be unaffected. Utilities and parks staff would also be excluded. Staff noted a prior attempt at modified hours generated employee and public complaints about early start times and reduced Friday access. Two public commenters had already expressed opposition (Pel) and partial concern (Grace). No decision was made; the topic was deferred to future budget workshops for further discussion.
