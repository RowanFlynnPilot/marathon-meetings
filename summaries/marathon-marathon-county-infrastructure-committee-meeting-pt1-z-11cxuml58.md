## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Infrastructure Committee met to discuss the Bug Tussle broadband project, which faces a significant scope modification because insufficient bond funds remain to complete the originally planned fiber loop. Bug Tussle representatives presented updates on current infrastructure, BEAD program awards, and marketing efforts before the committee voted to enter closed session to deliberate on the proposed scope change, potential litigation, and a new $45-50 million bonding request. The meeting is significant to residents because the southern fiber loop — a key component of the original broadband expansion agreement — is unlikely to be completed as planned.

## Key Discussions

He described the road as 'alligatored' and noted the worst damage occurs where trucks turn toward the loading ramp, likely due to salt spillage. ### Bug Tussle Broadband Project — Infrastructure Status Presentation
Bug Tussle representatives — including George [Quins], Avery Shelbine, and Chris Hverson appearing virtually — presented current project status: 206 live fiber miles in Marathon County, 16 fixed wireless sites deployed, 19 multi-purpose towers, and approximately 5,800 locations passable by fixed wireless and 2,800 by fiber. They noted the original plan called for 288 miles of fiber; the current plan will deliver approximately 281 miles by year-end after completing 74 additional miles, with the southern loop (~45 miles) being dropped as financially infeasible due to insufficient homes along the route. Outstanding construction segments include the Huitt Tower (2.7 miles), Stratford Water Tower (7.19 miles), Hatley/East Side Sheriff's Tower (4.5 miles), and Brokaw East (3.86 miles), all contracted to United Electric for fall 2024 construction.

### Subscription/Take Rate Decline
Supervisor Aaron Griner questioned why subscription rates had declined from the June presentation — fixed fiber service from 4.15% to 2.99% and fixed wireless from 5.19% to 1.37%. Bug Tussle's Avery Shelbine attributed the decline partly to customer churn (subscribers moving to faster Bug Tussle fiber plans or SpaceX) and partly to a recalculation of the total addressable market using updated engineering assessments, which significantly increased the denominator for the take-rate calculation. George [Quins] acknowledged that Bug Tussle had not focused adequately on marketing during the construction phase and described new sales and digital marketing efforts now underway.

### Scope Modification — Southern Loop Removal and County Board Approval
Supervisor Robinson and County Attorney Jerry Klene led substantive discussion about whether the elimination of the southern fiber loop constitutes a formal scope change requiring county board approval. Klene stated Bug Tussle was told as early as a January meeting in Green Bay that dropping the southern loop would require board approval, and this was reiterated before the June educational meeting. Administrator Lance Leonard confirmed there are insufficient project funds remaining to complete the original scope and directed Bug Tussle to produce a formal addendum comparing the original bond issuance documents to the revised scope, including exact mileage differences, for a future committee review. Supervisor Guy asked whether microwave transmission could serve as an adequate substitute for the southern loop; Klene said it would not be ideal but could be made functional if Stratford Water Tower, Huitt Tower, and the east tower connections are completed to provide redundancy for public safety.

### Bug Tussle Future Bonding Request
George Quins briefly outlined a proposed new bond in the range of $45-50 million, described as primarily supporting the BEAD program match requirements, expanded fiber passings, marketing and sales investment, and refinancing of existing financial commitments. He indicated the bond would be structured as a multi-county instrument shared across Bug Tussle's partner counties in Wisconsin. Detailed financials were reserved for the closed session.

### Motion to Enter Closed Session
Administrator Leonard and County Attorney Brian [last name inaudible] recommended closed session to discuss scope modification details, litigation risk, and Bug Tussle's confidential financial projections. Supervisor Guyer moved to enter closed session under Wisconsin Statutes 19.85(1)(e) and 19.85(1)(g); the motion was seconded and passed on a roll call vote with all members voting aye.
