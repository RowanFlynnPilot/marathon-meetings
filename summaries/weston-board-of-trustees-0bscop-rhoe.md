## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees met and addressed several significant items, including approving a 10-year fixed-rate loan at 4.95% for the new Mountain Bay Metro Police Department headquarters after the original lender submitted terms inconsistent with what the board had approved. The board also narrowly passed (4-3) a limited written public comment policy stating written comments will not be read aloud, after extended debate, and approved an early-start construction permit for footings and foundation at the new police station site. Three public commenters raised concerns about Kennedy Park finances, auditorium acoustics, and due diligence regarding a natural gas pipeline near the police station property.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Three residents spoke. Jim Pence criticized the lack of a financial plan for Kennedy Park (Miller Park North), called for canceling new lights and redirecting the $100,000 raised toward operating costs, and raised concerns about agenda item 42 — arguing the borrowing terms were inconsistent with what the court approved and that a 5-year balloon payment was never mentioned in the original August approval. John Alman, 1506 Bailey Avenue, complained that attendees in the back of the room cannot hear proceedings and suggested a simple microphone solution rather than spending on ball diamonds. Cynthia Russell, 5907 Trisha Avenue, expressed concern about lack of due diligence regarding a 36-inch TC Energy natural gas pipeline on or near the Mountain Bay Metro Police Station property, drew comparisons to the Camp Phillips development failure, and requested an independent survey, formal written engagement with TC Energy, and a written policy for future land purchases near utility easements.

### Approval of September 21st Board of Trustees Meeting Minutes
The minutes were approved with one requested correction: a trustee noted that language describing completed 'contracts for the chief and deputy' was misleading and should be reworded to reflect that union staffing contracts — not personal employment contracts — were completed. The administrator agreed to make the correction. Motion by Olson, second by Clark; approved unanimously.

### Department Head Reports
Parks & Rec Director Sean reported the Kennedy Park community foundation account balance was updated, the Gold Games are scheduled again for 2027, and that the master plan needs revisiting since the farmers market is not relocating, which affects plans for an additional baseball field. Trustees discussed maintenance costs (reduced due to artificial turf), turf lifespan (10–20 years), and future capital costs. Planning & Development Director Jen reported on conference takeaways including data centers, solar, and conditional use permit practices; she also noted work underway to add farm stand regulations to code by spring. Police Chief reported 2,493 attendees at the Monster Bash and 596 traffic stops in the reporting month. Technology Director Nate reported an initial proposal of $69,000 for wireless microphones from one vendor and noted a second proposal was pending; trustees expressed sticker shock and discussed lower-cost alternatives including corded systems.

### Discussion/Action on Draft Policy for Receipt and Handling of Written Comments at Public Hearings
Extended debate occurred over multiple provisions. Trustee Olson objected to a Wednesday noon deadline for written comments to be included in the packet, arguing submissions should be accepted the day of the meeting. Administrator Jamie clarified the policy was not designed to restrict submission but only to set packet inclusion deadlines, with later submissions handled as copies. An amendment by Trustee Clark to require that copies of written comments be made available to the public at the meeting table failed 5-2. The original motion to approve the policy as presented also failed 5-2. Trustee Ermeling then moved a simplified motion that written comments will not be read aloud; that motion passed 4-3 with Maloney, Lopes-Serrao, Ermeling, and Zeyghami voting yes, and Beck, Clark, and Olson voting no.

### Discussion/Action on Proposed Site Plan Modifications and Early Start Permit — Mountain Bay Metro Police Station
Staff explained that a revised site plan was needed after the TC Energy natural gas pipeline easement required the building footprint to shift westward; the new site plan is outside the pipeline easement, with parking lot crossings approved by TC Energy. The board was asked to authorize staff to issue an early start permit limited to footings and foundation, with final amended site plan approval deferred to the November board meeting. Staff noted the revised layout may actually cost less due to reduced asphalt and removal of a door and sidewalk. Motion passed 4-3 with Beck, Olson, and Ermeling voting no.

### Discussion/Action on Engineering Design Contract — Bowman Street Water and Sanitary Sewer Replacement
The board approved awarding the design contract for water and sanitary sewer utility replacements on Bowman Street between Business 51 and McIntyre to Becker Hobby for $22,500. Trustees confirmed this was design only, not the full construction project. Motion by [inaudible], second by Zeyghami; approved unanimously.

### Discussion/Action on Engineering Services Contract Amendment — Business 51 Storm Pond Construction Project
The board approved an engineering contract amendment with Strand Associates for construction oversight related to a clean water fund loan through the DNR for a regional storm pond project serving Weston, Rothschild, and Schofield. Trustee Clark questioned why the state DOT had no financial stake given that state highway runoff flows to the pond; Public Works Director Michael acknowledged ongoing frustration with the DOT's position that they are not subject to the same phosphorus removal requirements. The village's cost share was noted as approximately 80%. Motion by Zeyghami, second by Lopes-Serrao; approved unanimously.

### Discussion/Action on Debt for Village of Weston Portion of New Police Headquarters
Administrator Jamie explained that Community First/Inner City bank had submitted loan documents with a 5-year balloon or rate reset, inconsistent with the board's prior approval of a 10-year fixed rate at 4.69%. After negotiations, the bank offered a fourth option: 10-year fixed at 4.95%. Trustees discussed the risk of the balloon/reset options given uncertain future interest rates and concluded the 4.95% 10-year fixed was the best path forward. Trustee Olson moved to accept the 10-year fixed rate at 4.95%, seconded by Clark; approved unanimously. Finance Director noted $400,000 already levied for debt service would immediately reduce the principal upon issuance.

## Public Comment
