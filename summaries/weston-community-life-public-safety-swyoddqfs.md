## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Community Life & Public Safety Committee met on September 8th to address overnight parking regulations for winter snow removal, animal fancier license standards, and code enforcement updates. The committee approved a recommendation to the village board to modify the winter overnight parking ordinance, adding alternating-day parking restrictions on six high-density streets from October 31st to May 1st. The animal fancier license size standards discussion was tabled pending input from a humane officer at the next meeting.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Jim Pinsenalt of PC Street West addressed the committee with two concerns. First, he expressed alarm that code enforcement cases are rising while the village board was simultaneously considering reducing building hours and granting additional paid holidays to staff, questioning how existing cases could be handled under those conditions. Second, he argued that the animal fancier license ordinance lacks specific standards for dogs and other animals, noting that guidelines exist for chickens but not for dogs or cats, and called for defined minimums for space, food, water, shelter, and outdoor exercise areas to enable fact-based rather than feelings-based decisions.

### Code Enforcement Update
Staff noted that the increase in August code enforcement cases was largely attributable to grass complaints following the end of a drought period. The committee discussed the ongoing situation at 4020 Scoffield Avenue, with staff reporting that Lock Reinvestments had been in contact and was preparing new plans for dumpster enclosures for the entire property; once approved by staff and their hauler Harters, permits would be entered into the system at no charge and work was expected to be completed that fall. Staff also raised the ongoing issue of violation notice letters taking up to 10 days to reach residents, exceeding the required response windows, and noted that staff was working to address this through email outreach and planned ordinance review during the slower winter months.

### Police and Fire Department Monthly Reports
Police Chief Hunt reported that calls for service stood at approximately 2,367, with traffic citations at 311 and traffic stops at 437, describing numbers as consistent and stable. He noted that citations would increase slightly with school back in session, and that traffic crashes were up slightly to 60. The chief also reported that skate park calls for service had been down compared to prior years, attributed informally to the prevalence of e-scooters and e-bikes, and that e-moto enforcement had resulted in at least one tow and citations with cooperative parents. A question was raised about the 'other' category in the fire response report, which could not be answered and was flagged for follow-up at the next SAFER board meeting.

### Overnight Parking Regulations (Section 82.107)
Staff member Michael presented a revised proposal to reduce the number of streets with alternating-day parking restrictions from over a dozen to six high-density locations, primarily near apartment complexes like Foxrun, where snow plows have historically been unable to pass due to cars parked on both sides. After extensive discussion, the committee agreed on a uniform 2:00 a.m. to 7:00 a.m. no-parking restriction on all village streets to align with neighboring municipalities, plus alternating-day restrictions (Monday/Wednesday/Friday/Sunday on one side, Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday on the other) on the six listed streets from 7:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. The police chief expressed a preference for overnight enforcement as officers are less busy then, and noted tickets are approximately $10. A committee member also requested that ordinance language be added to formalize the process by which streets are added to the restricted list, requiring justification and committee/board approval. A motion was made and passed to recommend these changes to the village board, including sign cost estimated at just over $2,000.

### Animal Fancier License Size and Configuration Requirements (Section 10-123)
Trustee Brent raised this item following a recent animal fancier permit denial by the village board, where a lack of specific size standards in the ordinance created difficulty in the decision-making process. Chief Hunt strongly advised against adding detailed size requirements, arguing the ordinance's current subjectivity allows humane officers to use professional judgment, and that setting explicit standards could invite more applications and reduce enforcement flexibility. He noted 18 calls for service at the property in question from May through August. Staff noted that the humane officers receive specialized training through UW-Madison's veterinary school. The committee agreed to table the ordinance amendment discussion and invite one or more humane officers to a future meeting to provide education on what standards they apply, before deciding whether changes are needed.

### Code Enforcement Follow-up: 1706 Newport Avenue
A committee member reported homework assigned at the prior meeting regarding citations issued at 1706 Newport Avenue, a chronic nuisance/hoarding property. Citations totaling $4,145 were issued to one resident (with $1,300 remaining delinquent) and $2,656 to another (with $969 remaining delinquent), across 38 citations dating back to 2022. Staff noted 47 total cases at that address going back to 2008, and that staff invested 15–18 hours on the most recent case alone. The property has been cleaned up three times through non-summary abatement, with costs including dumpster rental billed to the property and subject to tax intercept if unpaid. Staff noted that administrative fees were added to bills this cycle for the first time to begin recouping staff time costs, and discussed the contrast with cities like Oshkosh that charge $50 per enforcement letter sent.

## Public Comment
One speaker offered public comment: Jim Pinsenalt of PC Street West. He raised two issues: (1) concern that rising code enforcement cases were incompatible with a proposal before the board to reduce building hours and add paid holidays for staff, and (2) a call for the committee to establish defined size, sanitation, food, water, and outdoor exercise standards for the animal fancier license ordinance, noting that standards exist for chickens but not for dogs or other animals.
