## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees held its regular meeting, approving the village's Bicycle and Pedestrian Plan (6-1), a Camp Phillips condominium plat (6-1), and parking regulation amendments, while tabling a proposed public comment policy for further legal research. The meeting also included department reports highlighting a utility clerk resignation, a successful K9 fundraiser raising $42,000, and a three-year SAFER contract ratified without attorney fees.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comments
Jim Pensel of 5002 [Street], Weston, raised several concerns: unfinished playground equipment installation more than two years after purchase with no billing to the installer at DOT rates; lack of progress identifying key development sites despite discussion three years ago; poor audio quality in the meeting room since installation; a $54 shredding charge he suggested could be eliminated by using the community shredding event; a $125 phone usage reimbursement to Director Higgins for code enforcement that he asked be clarified; and concerns that the draft public comment policy could limit First Amendment rights and should be reviewed by the village attorney before adoption.

### Committee and Department Reports
Trustees reported that CLIPS reviewed parking regulations and invited the humane officer to their next meeting; the Parks Committee held a well-attended open house for the Mock-Miller park project. A SAFER contract was ratified for three years with no attorney fees. Mountain Bay Metro health insurance will increase 2.5% for 2027. Planning Commission approved a preliminary plat for a commercial condo north of the municipal center and discussed conditional use permit compliance issues, including Tommy's Express failing to submit required wellhead protection reports for five years. Public Works Director Michael reported that overlay prep work done in-house in August saved approximately $120,000 compared to contractor costs, and that utility clerk Stacy resigned to join a local plumbing company, opening a new vacancy. Technology Director Nate reported applying for state cybersecurity grants, with results expected in March. Finance Director Jessica flagged rising fuel costs approaching $13,000 for September.

### Resolution 2026-025: Camp Phillips Condominium Plat
The board moved to approve the Camp Phillips condominium plat. A trustee asked for clarification that this was associated with Lorra, who also owns PME properties with a conditional use permit question. The motion was approved 6-1 with one trustee voting nay, reason cited as related to the conditional use permit concern.

### Resolution 2026-020: Bicycle and Pedestrian Plan Adoption
Consultant Jody Rider of HKGI presented the completed Bicycle and Pedestrian Plan, which was funded 80% by a WisDOT TAP grant and 20% by the village. The plan documents 53.9 miles of existing facilities and proposes a 10-20 year framework for expanding the network, including priority, medium-term, and long-term projects with cost estimates. Trustees discussed e-bike and e-moto enforcement concerns, ADA intersection improvements, and the plan's role as a component of the comprehensive plan. Trustees emphasized the plan is aspirational and subject to available funding and property owner cooperation under current eminent domain law. Trustee Beck asked for medium- and long-term cost totals. Marathon County was noted to have requested a copy. The plan was approved 6-1.

### Consent Agenda - Approval of Vouchers
Trustees questioned several line items including a $177,000 plow truck chassis purchase, a charge from 'Def Grip Motorsports' for vehicle undercoating/rustproofing, cell phone and iPad costs through Cellcom, a $23,000 bond counsel charge from Corlis and Brady related to the safe water drinking loan closing, and a small greeting room supplies charge. Staff clarified all items satisfactorily. The consent agenda was approved unanimously.

### Ordinance 26-015: Parking Regulations Amendment
A trustee identified a date discrepancy in the ordinance — the recommended language cited November 1 to March 31 while the ordinance language cited October 31 to May 1. After discussion the board agreed the correct dates should be November 1 to April 1. The ordinance was approved as corrected.

### Draft Policy for Receipt and Handling of Written Public Comments
Trustees held an extended debate over whether written comments should be read aloud at meetings, what deadlines should apply, and whether profane or derogatory content should be excluded from minutes and the public record. Village President Maloney stated he would not read letters critical of himself or the board, and preferred written comments be placed in the packet rather than read aloud. Trustee Beck argued time deadlines for submission were too restrictive and that comments received before a meeting should be distributed to the board. Trustees raised First Amendment concerns and noted that similar questions arose with the village's social media policy. The original motion by Clark was withdrawn after trustees agreed more legal research was needed on what can lawfully be excluded from the public record and what the village is legally required to do with written comments. The item was then tabled to the following month.

### Legal Representation Agreement with Dempsey Law
The board discussed retaining Dempsey Law to provide legal review of the bank loan agreement for the Mountain Bay Metro building. One trustee questioned whether the attorney, who also represents Mountain Bay Metro Police Department, presented a conflict of interest. Staff clarified that legal review of the borrowing agreement is legally required regardless of which attorney is used, and that Dempsey's familiarity with the loan's purpose would simplify the process. The transcript ends before a final vote is recorded.
