## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees met on Monday, September 21, 2026, approving several resolutions including the Camp Phillips condominium plat (6-1) and the village bicycle and pedestrian plan (6-1), and tabling a draft policy on written public comments for further legal research. The board also addressed a final change order closing out the Alderson Street and Gelmouth Avenue intersection project at $846,959.25, and approved a legal representation agreement with Dempsey Law for the municipal building borrowing.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
One resident, Jim Pensel, addressed the board with several concerns: whether staff billed the playground installer for repairs to missing and damaged equipment still incomplete more than two years after installation; the lack of progress on identifying key development sites despite prior discussion; the poor audio system in the meeting room, which he asked the board to prioritize fixing; a $54 charge for paper shredding that he suggested could wait for the community shredding event; a $125 phone reimbursement to Director Higgins for code enforcement and how it was calculated; and concern that the last section of the new public comment policy may limit First Amendment rights, requesting village attorney review before adoption.

### Acknowledge Reports from Boards, Committees, and Commissions
Trustees reported on various committee activities. A trustee noted that CLIPS reviewed parking regulations and invited the humane officer to its next meeting. Another trustee reported on a well-attended open house for the Mockingbird Miller park project. From SAFER, a trustee highlighted that the police chief and deputy ratified a three-year contract without attorney fees, and that Mountain Bay Metro health insurance would increase 2.5% for the upcoming year. From planning, a preliminary plat for a commercial condo north of the municipal center was approved and a lengthy discussion on conditional use permit compliance issues is expected to come back the following month.

### Department Reports
Administrator Jamie noted the village signed a Public Finance Authority bond agreement for the Wausau Hotel Group, clarifying it carries no village obligation. The clerk reported high absentee ballot activity ahead of an upcoming election. Public Works Director Michael reported that overlay prep work in August saved approximately $120,000 compared to contractor costs, irrigation repairs at Western Field were completed under warranty, and the village utility clerk resigned. Fire Chief reported mold remediation underway at Station 2 caused by a water leak, with flooring replacement in the kitchen area planned for October. Technology staff reported applying for state cybersecurity grants, with results expected in March.

### Resolution 2026-025: Camp Phillips Condominium Plat
The resolution to approve the Camp Phillips condominium plat and related documents was moved by Trustee Lopes-Serrao and seconded by Trustee Clark. Brief discussion confirmed the applicant also owns PME properties and other holdings in the region. The board approved the resolution 6-1.

### Resolution 2026-020: Village of Weston Bicycle and Pedestrian Plan
Jody Rider of HKGI presented the completed bicycle and pedestrian plan, funded 80% by a WisDOT TAP grant and 20% by the village, developed over approximately one year with extensive community engagement including surveys, open houses, and stakeholder meetings. The plan maps a long-term 10-to-20-year network of sidewalks, shared-use paths, and on-street bike facilities, identifies priority, medium-term, and long-term projects, and addresses ADA accessibility, safety intersections, and connections to schools, parks, and regional trails. Trustees discussed e-bike and e-moto enforcement concerns, the non-binding nature of the plan, eminent domain constraints on trail acquisition, and the fact that Marathon County requested a copy. The plan was approved 6-1 after a motion by Trustee Zeyghami and second.

### Ordinance 26-015: Parking Regulations Amendment
The ordinance amending parking regulations was moved by Trustee Clark and seconded by Trustee Zeyghami. During discussion, a trustee identified a discrepancy in the draft dates — the recommended language cited November 1 to March 31 while the ordinance body cited October 31 to May 1 — and the board agreed to standardize the winter parking restriction period as November 1 to April 1. The ordinance passed as amended.

### Draft Policy for Receipt and Handling of Written Comments at Public Meetings
Substantial debate occurred over a proposed policy governing written public comments. Trustee Clark moved approval and Trustee Zeyghami seconded. Key disagreements centered on whether written comments should be read aloud, what deadlines should apply for submission, whether profane or derogatory content should be excluded from the public record, and consistency between treatment of in-person and written comments. Multiple trustees expressed concern about censorship and First Amendment implications, and Trustee Beck raised questions about what other communities do and what is legally required. The motion was ultimately withdrawn by Trustee Clark, and the board then moved and approved tabling the item for one month pending additional legal and policy research.

### Legal Representation Agreement with Dempsey Law
The board considered approving a legal representation agreement with Dempsey Law — specifically attorney Lee Tory — to handle the legal review required for the village's borrowing related to the municipal building, with the attorney billing at $240 per hour. Trustee Clark raised a potential conflict of interest given that the same attorney works with Mountain Bay Metro Police Department, which is also a party to the building transaction. Staff explained that legal review is required for the borrowing and that Tory's familiarity with the Mountain Bay building project would likely reduce costs compared to bond counsel. Trustee Zeyghami moved approval, Trustee Clark seconded, and it passed.

### Sanitary Sewer Easement Vacation on Wausau Tile Property
Trustee Lopes-Serrao moved approval and Trustee Olson seconded. No significant discussion was recorded. The motion passed.

### Alderson Street and Gelmouth Avenue Intersection Project Change Order No. 2
The board considered a final change order to close out the Alderson Street and Gelmouth Avenue intersection project at a total contract price of $846,959.25 with Hos Construction. A trustee confirmed this figure covered the construction contract and noted that one additional MSA engineering invoice may still be forthcoming. The project came in under the anticipated $1 million total. The change order was approved unanimously.

## Public Comment
One resident, Jim Pensel, spoke during public comment. He raised concerns about: the playground installer's failure to complete repairs more than two years after installation and whether the village has been billing the contractor for staff repair time at DOT rates; lack of progress on identifying key development sites despite discussion approximately three years prior; the poor quality of the meeting room audio system, which he requested be made a formal agenda item for action at the next meeting; a $54 paper shredding charge he suggested could be avoided by using the community shredding event held at the same facility; a $125 phone reimbursement to Director Higgins for code enforcement and how the amount was determined; and concern that the final section of the proposed public comment policy could limit First Amendment rights, requesting village attorney review before adoption.
