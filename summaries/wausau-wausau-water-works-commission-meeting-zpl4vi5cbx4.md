## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Water Works Commission met on September 1, 2026, primarily to receive a detailed financial presentation from Brian Ramer of Ehlers on long-range cash flow projections and potential rate adjustments for both the water and wastewater utilities. The commission heard that the water utility is currently about 13% rate-deficient under PSC math, with deficiency growing to 20% by 2027 if no action is taken, and received an update on a PFAS exceedance detected in wastewater effluent. No formal rate decisions were made; staff indicated they would bring the rate discussion back next month for a possible decision.

## Key Discussions
### Minutes of August 12th Meeting
A motion was made by Galire and seconded by Martins to approve the minutes of the August 12th meeting. There was no discussion. The motion passed unanimously.

### Director's Report - Wastewater Project Updates
Staff reported that the Cherry Street project is essentially complete with one punch list item remaining, to be addressed on-site Thursday. The Headworks Screening project remains ongoing with a substantial completion deadline of October 15 and final completion of November 15. The Crocker Street Lift Station project was noted as new, with a pre-construction meeting scheduled for September 2nd. No questions were raised by commission members.

### Ehlers Financial Presentation - Water Utility Rate Analysis
Brian Ramer of Ehlers presented a 10-year financial forecast for the water utility, explaining that under PSC math the utility is currently about 7.3% rate-deficient for 2026, growing to approximately 13% now and 20% by August 2027 if no action is taken. He outlined three options: do nothing (infeasible), use simplified rate adjustments of 3% in 2027 and 2028 followed by a 16% conventional rate case, or file a conventional rate case sooner yielding approximately a 14% adjustment. Commission member Hadley questioned how approximately $5 million in PFAS lawsuit proceeds (PAS funds) and the payment-in-lieu-of-taxes (pilot) would affect rate calculations; Ramer explained the PSC has not consistently reduced rate adjustments based on lawsuit proceeds and that reducing the pilot would lower the rate adjustment but create a general fund revenue gap. Martins asked about the timeline for filing, and Ramer confirmed the commission has until July 31, 2027 before the PSC math resets. Staff director Eric noted the commission had previously expressed interest in monthly billing and tiered rate structures for low-volume users, and recommended a small rate increase for 2027 with planning for a larger rate case in 2028 or 2029. No vote was taken; the director indicated the matter would return next month for a possible decision.

### Ehlers Financial Presentation - Sewer Utility Rate Analysis
Ramer presented the sewer utility financial outlook, noting debt coverage has been creeping back toward minimum thresholds and that the last rate adjustment was April 1, 2023. He recommended small, more frequent inflationary rate adjustments — using 4% as an illustrative example — which would increase an average residential quarterly sewer bill by approximately $4.30. Unlike the water utility, the sewer utility is not regulated by the PSC and the commission has full authority to set rates. No vote was taken.

### PFAS Monitoring Update - Wastewater Effluent
Ben Brooks reported that the first compliance-required effluent sample taken May 7th returned a PFOS level of 9.44 parts per trillion, above the proposed limit of 8 parts per trillion, which he described as unexpected given prior favorable readings. Staff has reviewed sampling protocols and found no evidence of cross-contamination, concluding the PFAS is entering the system from external sources. Brooks indicated plans to test collection system locations to identify the contamination source and noted the commission may need to consider amending the city sewer ordinance to require testing by large industrial users. Biosolids results were noted as favorable; an additional biosolids test is planned to establish compliance under the permit effective May 1st. Commission members raised no formal objections; the item was informational.

### WDNR Response to 2025 Compliance Maintenance Annual Report
Brooks reported that the DNR's response letter to the 2025 annual compliance report was entirely positive, with all grade A ratings. The DNR commended the utility for properly reporting a sanitary sewer overflow (SSO) in September 2024 and noted the utility cleaned 48.9% of the collection system and televised 10%. The DNR letter stated the utility met all requirements and encouraged staff to keep up the good work.
