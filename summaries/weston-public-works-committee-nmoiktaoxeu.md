## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Public Works Committee met on October 12, 2026, covering a range of infrastructure and utility topics including ongoing road construction projects, a proposed pedestrian crossing on Business 51, sewer rate options for 2027, and an infiltration/inflow study. The committee approved several engineering contracts and directed staff to develop additional sewer rate scenarios for a future decision, with a goal of reaching at least a break-even position.

## Key Discussions
### Sidewalk Snow Plowing Policy
Staff noted receipt of an email from a resident whose side yard abuts Fuller Street regarding sidewalk snow plowing. Staff explained the village generally does not plow residential sidewalks except where there is no boulevard and snow is plowed directly onto the sidewalk. A committee member requested it be placed on next month's agenda for discussion, and staff agreed.

### CIP/DIP Project Update
Staff reported Western Avenue is substantially complete with warranty concrete work ongoing; contractor Treeweiler requested that some larger concrete panel replacements be deferred to spring to avoid fresh concrete being damaged by road salt, which staff said would remain under warranty at no extra cost. Committee members debated the cause of widespread concrete spalling in central Wisconsin, noting it is not isolated to Weston. Bladel Avenue and Concord at Bayberry Street were described as nearing asphalt paving. Well Four is complete, Well Three rehabilitation is ongoing with an April go-live target. The Military Road project is finished. Granite Ridge subdivision has four homes under construction.

### Fleet and Street Operations Report
Street operations staff reported stockpiling over 52,000 yards of sand from ongoing projects for future road subgrade improvements in areas like Howland Avenue and Woodland Drive. A committee member raised a question about respiratory PPE for the employee spray-painting crosswalks and requested staff check the SDS for the latex-based paint given it is being spray-applied. Staff agreed to follow up on that safety question.

### Business 51 Sidewalk Crossing at Newport
Staff reported following up with the DOT after last month's meeting. The DOT indicated their preliminary safety evaluation found no documented pedestrian impacts (no one hit), which did not meet their threshold for warranted crossing funds. Staff and committee members argued that pedestrians are observed walking in the median to reach signalized intersections, which is unsafe even if no collision has occurred. The DOT indicated they need the village to formally indicate interest in the crossing before refining cost estimates; current figures of $20,000 for road widening and $10,000–$30,000 for behind-sidewalk grading were described as very preliminary. A committee member moved to proceed with pursuing the crossing including flashing beacons and to also refer the matter to the Public Safety Committee; the motion passed unanimously.

### Proclamation 2026-002: Crash Responder Safety Week
Staff explained the Wisconsin DOT's Traffic Incident Management Enhancement program requested municipalities issue a proclamation for Crash Responder Safety Week (November 2–6, 2026) to raise awareness about slowing down and moving over for crash responders. The committee moved to recommend approval of the proclamation, which passed unanimously.

### 2027 Sanitary Sewer Rate Options
Staff presented six rate scenarios to address a 2025 operating loss of approximately $440,000 and a projected $100,000 increase in Rim Mountain Metro costs for 2027. Options ranged from doing nothing to recovering losses over two or three years or matching neighboring community rates. Committee members discussed the trade-offs between rate shock and gradual increases, whether to establish a tiered industrial rate, and whether INI study results reducing inflow could affect future costs. No rate was set; the committee directed staff to bring back additional scenarios (described informally as options G and H between scenarios E and F), historical Weston cost trend data, and Rim Mountain Metro multi-year billing history for further discussion next month, with a goal of implementing a new rate by January.

### Sanitary Sewer INI Study Results
Staff presented a preliminary excerpt from Inpipe Technologies' infiltration and inflow study covering the sewer system in the Rothschild boundary area (Alderson/Weston Ave to Vulkman, west to Highway 29). Staff explained the study uses flow trackers to distinguish groundwater infiltration from rain-driven inflow and can prioritize problem pipe reaches. Staff identified basins 3.0 and 2.0 near Highway 29 and Alderson as areas of concern not yet addressed. The committee acknowledged the report and directed staff to invite Inpipe Technologies to present the full study results at the next meeting, with 20 minutes allocated.

### Potential Utility Easement: Executive Court to Kramer Lane
Staff described an opportunity to secure a utility easement as a private property owner at 7209 Executive Court purchases a strip of land from a neighbor to the north, which would enable future water main looping out to Kramer Lane. Without the loop, the entire Edgewood Estates subdivision relies on a single connection at North Apache and Edgewood. The committee agreed it was worth pursuing and voted to direct staff to analyze potential easement costs and return with findings at a future meeting.

### Engineering Design Contract: Vulkman Street, Business 51 to McIntyre
Staff presented a revised quote from Becker Hoppy for $22,500 to design water main infrastructure on Vulkman Street from Business 51 to McIntyre, matching Rothschild's shared cost for their half of a water main redundancy project. Staff recommended pausing the McIntyre portion pending clarity on the junior high school's future. The committee approved the $22,500 contract.

### Business 51 Storm Pond Construction Services Contract
Staff presented Strand Associates' proposal of $92,800 for construction oversight and clean water fund loan administration for the Business 51 storm pond project, with Weston's share at $65,424 and the remainder split with Rothschild and Scoffield. Staff noted the DOT has not yet confirmed participation. A committee member suggested making approval contingent on partner community approval; the motion passed as amended with that contingency.

### 2027 Street Maintenance Plan
Staff presented a draft plan totaling approximately 2.36 miles of asphalt overlay and just under 4.5 miles of GSB sealant, plus concrete repairs on Scoffield Avenue (westbound lanes), repaving of East Everest Avenue (rated 2 on PASER scale) near the new Mountain Bay Metro Police Department entrance, and a portion of Woodland Drive. Total budget is $850,000 across operating and CIP funds. Staff noted the plan hits the budget limit and the goal is to get bids out over winter to avoid spring scrambles. The committee approved the plan as presented.
