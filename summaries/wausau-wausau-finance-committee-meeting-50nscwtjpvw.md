## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Finance Committee met on October 13, 2026, taking action on several budget and operational items including pet licensing fee changes, sale of America 250 banners, a lead hazard grant amendment, and a lead service line loan resolution. The meeting featured significant public comment opposing the city's Flock license plate reader camera system, and concluded with the committee scheduling a final 2027 budget workshop for October 20 at 6:30 p.m. to address outstanding amendments including liquor license fees, capital spending, and motor pool financing.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment – Flock Camera System
Three residents from the Highland Park Boulevard area spoke in opposition to the city's Flock automated license plate reader (ALPR) camera contract. Danielle Fischer argued the cameras are not a proven crime deterrent and raised concerns about facial recognition, cell phone geo-fencing, and data storage by Flock. Felix Gallo asked the committee to remove the $50,000 Flock appropriation from the 2027 budget, issue a written termination notice under section 11.16 of the agreement, and pass an ordinance requiring a public council vote before future surveillance technology acquisitions; he also alleged Police Chief Barnes had been misinformed about camera placement. Courtney Olson, identifying herself as a domestic violence agency director, cited documented misuse of Flock data to track victims and noted that the Wisconsin Institute for Law and Liberty is suing Waukesha County over warrantless Flock database searches; she asked for a written legal opinion from the city attorney on whether the December payment could still be stopped.

### Item 3A: Dog and Cat License Fees
Alder Watson proposed adjusting the spayed/neutered dog or cat fee to $25 and the microchip discount to $10 for 2027, with any funding shortfall covered by reserves. Staff noted that puppies and kittens are currently licensed at a half-year rate, which can result in the city paying the county more than it collects, and suggested potentially eliminating the half-year puppy/kitten rate via ordinance amendment in the future. Watson's motion on the two fee changes was seconded by Alder Neil and approved by the committee.

### Item 3B: Sale of America 250 Wausau Banners
Staff reported receiving calls from residents interested in purchasing the decorative banners used during the summer. The proposed prices were $25 for tapered banners and $35 for rectangular banners. Alder Watson moved to approve the sale at the prices recommended in the staff memo; the motion was seconded by Alder Tierney and approved unanimously.

### Item 3C: Second Amendment to Marathon County Health Department Lead Hazard Reduction Grant Agreement
Staff presenter Tammy Strasser explained the amendment addresses audit findings requiring updated monitoring language, authorizes the county health department to purchase an XRF lead-testing machine using grant funds, and broadens the reimbursable positions to a catch-all category. She also noted the health department received a new $4 million state grant to expand lead abatement countywide. The motion was made by Alder Neil, seconded by Alder Hennekey, and approved.

### Item 3D: Resolution to Reimburse Expenditures – Safe Drinking Water Revolving Loan Program (BIL SFY 27-28)
Staff explained this is a standard reimbursement resolution allowing the city to incur lead service line replacement costs before the loan closes, then get reimbursed. The city has requested approximately $18.16 million for 2027, though final allocations from the DNR could come in lower, which would reduce the scope of the program. Alder Henke asked about the risk of lower-than-expected reimbursement and was told the program would simply be scaled back. The motion was made by Alder Watson, seconded by Alder Neil, and approved.

### Item 3E: Sole Source Request – Zoll Monitors and Ventilators for Fire Department
Fire Chief [name not stated] requested the item be postponed two weeks so the EMS Division Chief could present details. Alder Tyrrell raised concerns that the cardiac monitors appeared on the capital deferred projects list at $215,000 while simultaneously being brought forward as a sole source purchase, calling it 'disingenuous.' The chief explained the intent is to use Fire Assistance to Firefighters (FAT) money to save approximately $80,000 by acting before an April deadline, avoiding CIP funds. The committee agreed by consensus to table the item for two weeks.

### Item 3F: Third Amendment – CIP Master Partnership Agreement (2027-28 Lead Service Line Replacement)
Staff explained this is an annual amendment to authorize Community Infrastructure Partners to continue professional services for the lead service line replacement program, now covering both 2027 and 2028 contingent on DNR funding. Alder Henke asked whether costs would scale with any reduction in project scope and was told yes. Alder Watson moved approval, seconded by Alder Henke, and the committee approved.

### Item 4: August 2026 General Fund Financial Report Review
Finance staff reported that motor pool costs allocated to police, fire, and public works are expected to exceed budget and a budget amendment will be brought to the October 27th meeting. The refuse budget is also running over due to a diesel fuel escalator clause in the current contract. Revenue is tracking well, with licenses and permits strong, investment income exceeding budget despite slightly lower rates, and municipal court fee revenues expected to increase following a state adjustment raising court costs from $38 to $48 per citation in Q1 2026. No action was taken; the report was placed on file.

### Item 3G: 2027 Budget – Discussion of Amendments and Fee Schedule
The committee discussed a spreadsheet of implemented and non-implemented 2% budget reduction measures. Members raised concerns that several of the four or five vacant positions counted as savings may not represent genuine permanent reductions — particularly the surveyor position (offset by contracted services), two fire positions (6-month deferrals only), and a third city attorney position. The chair emphasized the need to address long-term debt, noting that capital borrowing will significantly impact the 2028 levy, and called for a closer look at deferrable capital projects. Discussion of liquor and cigarette license fee increases was raised by Alder Tyrrell, with staff noting state statute caps and the need to benchmark against neighboring municipalities. The committee scheduled a final budget workshop for Tuesday, October 20, 2026 at 6:30 p.m.

## Public Comment
Three residents spoke in opposition to the city's Flock ALPR camera contract. Danielle Fischer (1212 Highland Park Blvd) argued the cameras are not a proven crime deterrent and raised privacy concerns about data use and storage. Felix Gallo (1206 Highland Park Blvd) asked the committee to remove the $50,000 Flock appropriation from the 2027 budget, issue a termination notice under contract section 11.16, and pass a surveillance technology ordinance; he alleged cameras are positioned inside city neighborhoods including near residents' homes and disputed Police Chief Barnes's characterization of their placement. Courtney Olson (1206 Highland Park Blvd) cited domestic violence victim tracking as a documented misuse, referenced ongoing litigation in Waukesha County over warrantless Flock database searches, and asked the city attorney for a written opinion on the termination clause and whether the December payment could be stopped by an 8-of-11 council vote.
