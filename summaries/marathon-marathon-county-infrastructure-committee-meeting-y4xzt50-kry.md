## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Infrastructure Committee met on November 5th, 2026 and took several significant actions, most notably approving an ordinance to allow ATV/UTV access on State Highway 34 and separately voting to request the state DOT approve flashing warning signs for that segment. The committee also received updates on the 2027 budget, the routine state highway maintenance agreement, the Wausau MPO long-range transportation plan, and IT department projects, though no votes were taken on those informational items.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of October 1, 2026 Minutes
The minutes were approved without discussion. A motion was made by Watson and seconded by Coyle. The motion carried on a voice vote.

### 2027 Annual Budget Overview
County Administrator Leonard and HR/Finance/Property Committee Chair Robinson presented a brief overview, noting the deadline for proposed budget amendments was 8:00 a.m. the following morning for Finance Committee review before county board adoption. Robinson highlighted that one driver of budget growth is funding for the highway facility. No amendments were proposed and no votes were taken.

### ATV/UTV Access on State Highway 34
Supervisor Gal raised safety concerns about ATV/UTV traffic mixing with high-volume truck traffic on State Highway 34, citing approximately 6,000 vehicles per day compared to the county's 1,500-vehicle threshold for county trunk highways, and noting that ATVs traveling at 35 mph would share the road with trucks doing 60 mph. Jeff Gates, a member of the public, stated that hundreds of riders would use the route and that the current detour adds roughly 25 minutes of travel time. DOT representative Mike Crutchmer confirmed the DOT reviewed the draft ordinance but noted he deferred to access engineer Rich Hendrick on specifics. Vice Chair Gal moved to approve the ordinance with a condition requiring flashing warning lights; Supervisor Dickinson argued the signage question was separate and moved to divide the question into two parts. After procedural discussion involving corporation counsel, the motion was split: the original ordinance was approved unanimously on a voice vote, and a separate motion requesting the state DOT approve flashing warning signage for the ATV route on State Highway 34 passed 5-2.

### Safe Routes to School Program and Cronin Area Request
Commissioner Lang explained that Marathon County's historical practice is to co-sign Safe Routes to School applications rather than serve as the primary sponsor, as the primary benefit is local. He noted the next application window is fall 2027, and that North Central Regional Planning assists communities with applications. No action was taken.

### Routine Maintenance Agreement Update
Commissioner Lang and DOT Operations Manager Mike Crutchmer reviewed the annual routine maintenance agreement under which Marathon County maintains state roads. Discussion focused on cost reimbursement concerns, particularly the dramatic increase in plow blade costs — from roughly $300 to $750 for a 3-foot blade — and the inadequacy of the 5-year rolling average reimbursement rate to keep pace. Supervisors Gal and Sybert asked for data on the county's overall financial partnership with the state. No vote was taken; staff was directed to gather reimbursement and cost-sharing figures.

### MPO Long Range Transportation Plan Update
Staff member Lori presented the update, noting four roadway segments identified as Level of Service D: State Highway 153/Main Street between 4th and Old Highway 51, Bridge Street over the Wisconsin River, West Thomas Street between South 3rd and Cleveland Avenue, and Grand Avenue between Townline Road and Thomas Street. The draft plan will be released for public review later in November through December. No vote was taken.

### CCIT Projects Update
CCIT Director Greg Klein highlighted open enrollment IT support for the city and county, an upgrade to NetMotion VPN software across 128 squad cars to meet new criminal justice encryption requirements, and ongoing transition of network support for the UWSP campus on River Drive ahead of a January 1st handoff. A question arose about the status of the former Marathon County Credit Union building on Thomas Street, with the administrator confirming the occupant has not yet vacated. No vote was taken.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. The chair asked if anyone wished to speak and seeing none, moved on.

## Action Items
- Marathon County ordinance allowing ATV/UTV access on State Highway 34 approved and forwarded to county board for adoption
- Staff directed to consult with Wisconsin DOT regarding approval of flashing warning signage for the ATV/UTV route on State Highway 34 and report back to committee
- Any proposed 2027 budget amendments to be submitted by 8:00 a.m. the following morning to Administrator Leonard, Finance Director Fensky, or corporation counsel
- Commissioner Lang to provide data on county cost-sharing and reimbursement rates with the state under the routine maintenance agreement
- Draft MPO long range transportation plan to be distributed to Infrastructure Committee members for review and comment in late November through December
- Next Infrastructure Committee meeting set for Thursday, December 3, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.
