# Wausau Joint Finance and Human Resources Meeting

**Organization:** City of Wausau

**Summarized:** 2026-11-16 06:30 UTC

## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Joint Finance and Human Resources Committee met on November 10, 2026, addressing a range of items including a 3% salary scale increase for general employees, a contentious sole-source purchase of Zoll cardiac monitors and ventilators for the fire department, lead service line funding amendments, and year-end budget modifications. The most significant debate centered on the fire department equipment purchase totaling $214,521, with disagreement over how future payments would be funded before the item ultimately passed 4-1.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Courtney Olsen of Highland Park Boulevard spoke on item 2G, the 2026 year-end budget modifications. She expressed support for the committee's prior 5-0 vote to remove a $50,000 Flock Safety camera payment from the 2027 budget and move it to motor pool expenses. She urged the committee to request a written opinion from the city attorney confirming that the next Flock invoice belongs to the 2027 budget and that the city can exit the contract under Section 11.16 without penalty, and asked that written notice be sent to Flock by November 15 to terminate the contract before a December invoice is generated.

### Revised Salary Structure for General Employees (Joint HR/Finance)
Human Resources staff presented a proposed 3% cost-of-living increase to the wage scale for non-represented general employees and seasonals, citing a 4.2% CPI-U increase and a 3.6% Social Security COLA. The presenter noted this is consistent with negotiated increases for transit (3.5% in 2026, 3% in 2027) and below the 4% increases for police and fire in 2027. Both the HR and Finance committees voted to approve without notable opposition; Alder Larson moved for HR and Alder Killion seconded; a separate motion carried for Finance.

### Sole Source Request — Zoll Monitors and Ventilators for Fire Department
EMS Division Chief Matt O'Mullen presented a sole-source request totaling $214,521 for new cardiac monitors (reaching end of 8-year service life) and ventilators (over 15 years old with no available maintenance). He argued that purchasing both together before an April 2027 price increase saved approximately $40,000 through grouped pricing, avoided warranty costs, and trade-in value, with the first year's payment (~$40,000) covered by FAP (Fire Ambulance Program) funds. Alder Tierney questioned why the item was not in the 2027 budget and expressed reluctance to support it without a commitment to use FAP dollars in future years. Alder Hanke moved an amendment requiring 15% of subsequent four annual payments to come from FAP, but it was withdrawn after Alder Neal argued the payment structure was separate from the sole-source approval. A further amendment to strike payment language from the motion passed 4-1 (Tierney opposed), and the amended sole-source request then passed 4-1 with Tierney opposed. The committee acknowledged a financing agreement with Zoll would need to return to committee and council.

### 2027 Operating Plan for Business Improvement District No. 1
The committee approved the 2027 operating plan for BID No. 1, the downtown River District, with no notable discussion. Alder Watson moved, Alder Neal seconded, and it passed unanimously.

### 2027 Beat Patrol Grant for Wausau Police Department
The committee approved the recurring Beat Patrol Grant for the police department. Alder Watson asked where the $40,000 city match would come from and was told it is already included in the 2027 budget. Watson then moved to approve; Alder Neal seconded; passed unanimously.

### Budget Amendment for 2027 Lead Service Line Safe Drinking Water Award
Finance Director Mary Ann presented last-minute changes to the lead service line funding: anticipated principal forgiveness dropped from $7.9 million to just under $5 million, and the utility loan rate increased from 0.25% to 2.585% due to program competitiveness statewide, increasing net city borrowing by approximately $1.5 million on the homeowner side and $1.95 million on the utility side. Alder Neal moved to approve and Alder Watson seconded, but Watson withdrew her second after expressing concern that the committee had insufficient time to analyze the changes. Without a second, the motion died. The underlying budget figures already approved remain in place, and the amendment can be reintroduced when the financial assistance agreement from the DNR comes back to committee.

### 2026 Property Tax Equivalent for Wausau Water Works (PILOT)
The committee approved the annual payment in lieu of taxes from Wausau Water Works with no substantive discussion. Alder Watson moved, Alder Neal seconded, passed unanimously.

### Agreement with Wausau Cemetery Association
This item was deferred to a future meeting at the request of a committee member, as the staff presenter (Jamie Polly) had left for the county budget meeting and was expected to return but time ran out before the council session.

### Budget Amendment for 2026 Year-End Budget Modifications
Finance Director Mary Ann explained the amendment covers projected year-end shortfalls, primarily in the motor pool (including $470,000 for the police department) and refuse/recycling fuel surcharges. Offsetting revenues include approximately $527,000 in additional SAFER grant income, crossing guard salary savings, and additional investment income. The net budget adjustment was estimated at approximately $1.3 million. Alder Hanke moved, Alder Watson seconded, and it passed unanimously.

### Comprehensive Purchasing Card Policy
The committee approved an updated city credit card policy with no discussion. Alder Neal moved, Alder Watson seconded, passed unanimously.

### Closed Session and Return to Open Session — Business 51 Parcel Payments
The committee went into closed session under Wis. Stat. §19.85(1)(e) to deliberate on property parcel payments for the Business 51/County Highway N and Business 51/Sturgeon Eddy Road projects. Upon returning to open session, the chair moved to approve an administrative revision and payment of $469.59 for parcel 1 (111 Sturgeon Eddy Road) and $200 for parcel 7. Alder Hanke seconded; passed unanimously.

## Public Comment
One speaker offered public comment. Courtney Olsen, a resident of Highland Park Boulevard, spoke on item 2G (2026 year-end budget modifications). She supported the committee's prior 5-0 vote removing a $50,000 Flock Safety camera payment from the 2027 budget and moving it to motor pool. She made two requests: (1) that the city attorney confirm in writing that the next Flock invoice (~December 15) belongs to the 2027 budget and that Section 11.16 allows exit without penalty; and (2) that the city send Flock written termination notice under Section 11.16 by November 15 so the 30-day notice period expires before the invoice is dated, thereby ending the contract and up to $100,000 in remaining payments.
