## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Human Resources, Finance & Property Committee met on Wednesday, August 12, 2026, addressing a range of budget and operational matters including a modified tattoo and body art licensing fee schedule, 2027 budget development discussions, new position requests, a resolution advancing the new highway facility toward final design and bidding, and a proposed non-lapsing contingency fund for the medical examiner's office. All major items were approved, with the committee emphasizing levy stabilization strategies and efficient use of existing debt for the highway shop project.

## Key Discussions
### 6B6: Modification of health department fee schedule — tattoo and piercing licensure
Three members of the public spoke during public comment about agenda item 6B6 before it was taken up out of order. The health department's Kate Floric and Administrator Leonard explained that the existing fee structure was unclear for large temporary events like a tattoo convention planned in Rothschild, and that the proposed change would restructure fees on a per-practitioner-tier basis (up to six practitioners per license) rather than requiring a separate license for each vendor. Floric noted the change would result in a slight decrease for permanent establishments and would more equitably cover the county's inspection costs. The committee discussed timing concerns raised in public comment and confirmed the new structure would be less costly for the upcoming event under the grouped-license model. The motion carried unanimously and will go to the full county board later in August.

### 6A1: 2027 budget development and policy recommendations
Administrator Leonard and finance staff presented an overview of 2027 budget development, noting net new construction came in at 1.545% and equalized value grew 6.25%, down from 9% the prior year. Staff explained that levy limits cap the county's ability to capture home value growth, with the net new construction figure yielding approximately $800,000 in additional levy capacity. Supervisors debated whether to cut spending, with Vice Chair Pool and others acknowledging the difficulty given fixed costs. Chair Robinson contextualized the county's tax rate decline from $6.87 per thousand in 2006 to approximately $3.45, and Administrator Leonard emphasized maximizing the operating levy is necessary to service existing debt and maintain services. The discussion was informational with no vote taken.

### 6A2: New position requests and reclassifications for 2027 budget
Administrator Leonard presented a comprehensive list of position requests and reclassifications across multiple departments, noting that all items except the facilities/capital management change and an administration reclass (going to executive committee the following day) had already been approved by their respective standing committees. Supervisor Griner expressed concern about the pace and lack of clarity on prior committee approvals, requesting items be considered separately or tabled, while Vice Chair Boots (participating via WebEx) and Chair Gibbs both affirmed the positions had undergone thorough vetting at prior standing committees with unanimous support. Chair Gibbs moved to approve the reclassifications and position changes for use in building the 2027 budget, contingent on executive committee approval of the administration reclass; the motion carried, with Griner noting he would have preferred more information upfront.

### July 2026 claims and question costs
The committee reviewed July claims totaling approximately $13,685,665. Chair Gibbs joked that avoiding health insurance and asphalt costs would help the budget, but the committee moved to approve. The motion carried unanimously.

### Claims disallowance — Schillinger claim
Corporation counsel explained the statutory process by which the county disallows damage or injury claims, noting the insurance carrier recommended disallowance of the Schillinger claim. After brief clarification on voting procedure, Chair Gibbs moved to disallow the claim consistent with the insurance carrier's recommendation, the motion was seconded, and it carried unanimously.

### 6B1: Resolution authorizing phase three construction of proposed new highway facility
Administrator Leonard and facilities director Chris presented an update on the proposed new Wausau-area highway shop, noting design is nearly complete, zoning changes have been approved by the Village of Kronenwetter, and preliminary site clearing has occurred. The resolution authorizes staff to finalize construction documents and release a bid package, with a guaranteed maximum price (GMP) expected in September and a final project authorization anticipated no later than October 15. The committee also discussed financing strategy at length: rather than issuing new debt at current ~4% rates, staff proposed transitioning existing Lake View campus debt currently serviced by North Central Healthcare revenues onto the debt levy, freeing those North Central funds to pay for the highway project over time — estimated to cover a $20–25 million gap by approximately 2030–2033 depending on equalized value growth. A committee member raised questions about solar energy not being pursued in earnest alongside geothermal; staff explained geothermal was prioritized for efficiency, longevity, and IRA tax credits, and noted a coal pile across the street was a factor against rooftop solar. The resolution was moved and carried; it had previously passed at the infrastructure committee.

### Amendment to 2026 budget for Local Road Improvement Program (LRIP) funding
The committee approved a budget amendment to capture LRIP funds, which had also been approved at the infrastructure committee the prior week. Chair Gibbs moved, Vice Chair Pool seconded, and the motion carried unanimously.

### 2027 Capital Improvement Program (CIP) tentative project list
Facilities director Chris presented the preliminary 2027 CIP, noting approximately $4.97 million in available funding against a much larger list of requests. Roughly 80% of available funds are earmarked for CCITC equipment, highway road projects, and fleet/rolling stock. Notable unfunded items include facility parking lot repairs, annual restroom replacement, courthouse HVAC updates, sheriff's detention facility envelope work, and several parks projects. A culvert replacement on County Trunk HH requires an additional $250,000 beyond existing 2026 CIP funds. Staff noted a projected shortfall of approximately $24,162 that could potentially be addressed through sales tax or interest revenue adjustments before budget adoption. Vice Chair Pool moved to approve the tentative list; the motion carried unanimously.

### 6B4: Fund balance policy amendment — non-lapsing contingency account for medical examiner's office
Administrator Leonard recommended creating a $250,000 non-lapsing contingency account for the medical examiner's office, citing the department's revenue volatility, expensive specialized equipment (including a $300,000 full-body X-ray machine), and its quasi-enterprise business model contracting with other counties. Supervisor Griner questioned whether $250,000 was excessive given the department had not recently had such large expenditures. Chair Gibbs supported the account, framing it as funding depreciation through business revenues rather than tax levy and comparing it to an enterprise fund model, while also calling for annual review of the cap amount. Chair Robinson noted at public safety that all contingency fund amounts should be reviewed regularly. Chair Gibbs moved to approve amending the fund balance policy to create the account with an annual review, and the motion carried unanimously.

## Public Comment
Three speakers addressed the committee during public comment, all relating to agenda item 6B6 (tattoo and piercing licensure fees). The first speaker, representing the Village of Rothschild in a professional capacity as tourism marketing director, described the economic impact of an upcoming three-day tattoo convention planned for Rothschild in October, estimating approximately $497,000 in total economic impact for the area and roughly $1,500 in county tax revenue, and urged the committee to consider this context when evaluating licensing costs. The second speaker, who identified himself as the owner of a licensed tattoo establishment in Marathon County, said he was not opposing a fee restructuring in principle but asked the county to consider applying any changes to permanent establishment fees at each business's next renewal rather than mid-year, citing concerns about fairness and consistency. The third speaker said she had been assisting the organizers of the Cedar Creek Ink Fest tattoo convention with licensing and fee questions; she described the existing fee structure as potentially requiring 46 to 60 separate establishment licenses at a cost of $9,568 to $18,480, noted that research shared with the department led to a reconsideration of the approach, and stated that Sauk County had licensed the same organizers' event as a single establishment. She said the organizers' position is that a single event-level license may be legally correct under existing statute, and asked for a clear and confirmed licensing structure in time for the October event.
