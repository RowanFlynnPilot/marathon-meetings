## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Parks and Recreation Committee met on September 30, 2024, covering updates on Kennedy Park renovations, the Muler Park master plan, aquatic center repairs, and a farmers market end-of-season survey. The committee voted to conduct a fall farmers market survey with an added question about Kennedy Park usage, and approved moving forward with an updated aquatic center facility evaluation.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of minutes from August 24th meeting
Minutes were moved by Scott and seconded by Roger. The committee approved them unanimously on a voice vote.

### Public comments
A resident named Grace, of 603 Jessica Street, offered three comments: she thanked committee members for asking probing questions about the ice rink proposal at Kennedy Park; she requested a more formal, structured presentation on Kennedy Park's progress and future plans, rather than informal monthly updates; and she asked whether Prohaska Park has a sign, bathrooms, and regular mowing. Staff confirmed the park has a sign and that only the walking trails are mowed, with no formal amenity area.

### Update on Kennedy Park renovation
Staff reported that contractors are scheduled to arrive the following week to address touch-up paint on poles, crack block and missing mortar, dugout door adjustments, sod restoration from irrigation leak repairs, and irrigation system restoration. Lights are scheduled for delivery the week of the 19th and will be installed immediately upon delivery. A representative from Friends of Kennedy Park said there were no additional updates. No action was taken.

### Update on Muler Park master plan
Staff reported that following the September 3rd public input meeting, the consultant revised the concept design to add tennis and basketball courts and relocated the shelter near the pickleball/basketball/tennis area. Public feedback on the concept was largely positive with few suggested changes. Three emails were received requesting that any future playground addition be fully ADA-accessible. Staff noted playground equipment at Muler was intentionally excluded from the seven-playground replacement project due to its relative newness (installed 2007), but replacement would be planned if the master plan moves forward. A cost estimate is being prepared and will return to the committee for approval or denial. No action was taken.

### Update on aquatic center repairs
Staff reported the contractor moved up their schedule and now expects to begin work at the aquatic center the week of October 5th, earlier than the previously anticipated late October window. Work includes leak detection, a valve and motor replacement, and winterization. Staff is preparing the mechanical room for the contractor's arrival. Electrical and commercial pool permits through the village are being processed. No action was taken.

### Discussion and action on farmers market end-of-season survey
Staff presented revised survey questions for a second end-of-season farmers market survey, noting the first survey was issued too early in the season. The proposed survey has nine questions including an open-ended final question. Committee members discussed whether to include a vendor-specific survey; staff noted the existing survey already asks respondents to identify as vendors. A committee member suggested adding a 10th question asking how respondents otherwise use Kennedy Park, which received broad support. Trustee Beck, participating online, supported conducting the survey now while the season is fresh. The committee voted to proceed with the survey, to be released around October 1st and run approximately 30 days.

### Discussion on collecting feedback regarding ice skating rinks at Kennedy Park
Staff recapped discussions from March and April about the cost and usage of the Kennedy Park outdoor ice rink, noting that since the warming house closed in 2019, attendance tracking has been limited but staff observe regular use. Cost to maintain the rink is primarily utilities—approximately $800 in water usage annually—since labor costs remain fixed regardless. Committee members debated whether a public survey would be useful, with concern that respondents without context might vote against a low-cost community amenity. One member noted the rink is the only full-size outdoor hockey rink (200x85 feet) in the area. The committee reached a consensus not to conduct a standalone ice rink survey, but agreed to add a question about Kennedy Park usage to the existing farmers market survey. No formal vote was taken on the ice rink item.

### Discussion and action on completing an updated aquatic center evaluation
Staff noted the last aquatic center evaluation was completed in 2016 at just under $7,000, and that significant capital expenditures have followed, including over $102,000 in leak repairs this year and another motor and valve replacement project exceeding $100,000. Staff estimated a new evaluation could cost $7,000–$12,000. A committee member raised the question of whether to wait given ongoing discussions about a possible pool partnership with the Everest School District, which recently mailed a facilities survey to residents. After discussion, the committee agreed that an evaluation could actually inform the partnership decision. A motion was made and seconded to proceed with the aquatic center evaluation; the motion was approved unanimously on a voice vote.

Staff answered that it does have a sign, and that only the walking trails are mowed. A person identified as David joined the meeting online but declined to offer public comment when invited.
