# Marathon County Board Meeting

## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Board of Supervisors met on September 17th and took action on multiple agenda items, most notably approving an amendment to Chapter 11.02 of the county's General Code of Ordinances updating the animal waste management ordinance after an extensive 18-month collaborative process with farmers and conservation groups. The board also approved numerous rezoning ordinances and postponed a vote on an infrastructure committee appointment after a supervisor raised concerns that the previous month's 'no' votes were based on 'sour grapes' rather than qualifications.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment: Animal Waste Ordinance
Seven of eight public speakers addressed the proposed animal waste ordinance amendment. Dairy farmers Kevin Schmiky (River Run Dairy, ~90 cows), Andrew Belter (~250 cows), Shannon Niclay (~300 cows, 2,100 acres), and Patrick Suchka (~400 acres) all expressed support, citing the value of nutrient management plans for farm finances and water quality. Michael Toris of the River Alliance of Wisconsin praised Marathon County as a statewide leader in collaborative watershed work. Tom Mueller of Miltrim Farms (~3,000 cows) supported the ordinance while stating it 'does not go far enough.' James Yetus spoke in opposition, calling the ordinance 'government overreach' and arguing that his 135-year farm operation should not be forced to adopt a nutrient management plan, and that the work group was stacked with supporters.

### Public Comment: Town of Green Valley Rezoning
Mitch Maguire, speaking on behalf of the Town of Green Valley town board, argued that the board formally disapproved a rezoning of a parcel from urban agricultural zoning, that the county board must honor the town's statutory authority, and that the half-acre parcel was commercially used by a shoreline protection contractor (Lakeside Rip) and should be zoned commercial, not agricultural. Neighbor Rosley Maguire raised concerns about precedent, saying other residents had already expressed intent to rezone their own half-acre lots for storage if the petitioner's request was approved.

### Infrastructure Committee Appointment (Item D14)
Supervisor Sybert addressed the item, reporting that after the previous month's 19-vote 'no' result, he called 15 of those 19 supervisors and 'not one of those 15 could say he wasn't qualified,' characterizing the opposition as 'sour grapes.' Sybert moved to postpone the vote until next month so supervisors could better evaluate the candidates. Supervisor Straw asked whether the infrastructure committee could function without a chair, and was told the vice chair would serve as acting chair and the committee could appoint an acting chair under Robert's Rules. The motion to postpone passed.

### Town of Green Valley Sawyer Daget Rezoning (Ordinance 33-26, Item E16)
County land resources manager Shad Harvey explained that the 27-acre parcel is split-zoned and adjacent to general agricultural land, and that staff recommended rezoning to general agriculture to allow the property owner to build a large structure to store commercial equipment. Harvey clarified that the parcel is not a half-acre — it is 27 acres — and that the town's formal disapproval resolution did not meet statutory certification requirements. ERC voted unanimously to approve. Supervisor Ritter noted ERC considered sending it back to the town but both the petitioner and town representatives wanted a decision at the committee meeting. Supervisor Straw said he would vote against it out of deference to the town's position. Supervisor Morash noted that delay would leave the owner's equipment exposed outdoors over winter. The board approved the rezoning on a non-unanimous vote.

### Animal Waste Ordinance Amendment (Chapter 11.02, Ordinance 41-26, Item E24)
County conservationist Kirsty Hidenrike and conservation analyst Matt Repang presented the ordinance, which was also reviewed by outside attorney Jane Landredy of Stafford Rosenbaum. Hidenrike described an 18-month process involving six public open houses, 13 ERC meetings, six farmer workgroup sessions, and 2,500 mailed notices. The ordinance targets winter spreading of liquid manure on frozen soils in February and March, requires nutrient management plans for relevant operations, and includes phased implementation timelines and emergency spreading provisions. Supervisor Sundowski asked detailed questions about which farms are covered, what monitoring is planned, and whether storm water from cities also contributes to algae blooms. Supervisor Morash raised concern about approving the ordinance without knowing the fee amount; Hidenrike explained that fees only apply to farms that winter-spread liquid manure and refuse to obtain a nutrient management plan, and that fee amounts would go through ERC and the county board before the 2028 implementation deadline. The amendment was approved.

### DC Everest School District Facility Study (Educational Presentation)
DC Everest Superintendent Dr. Casey Nye and Director of Buildings and Grounds Jason Jablonski presented a two-year facility study driven by declining enrollment and an aging 75-year-old junior high building. The proposal involves decommissioning the junior high, redistributing grades to existing buildings, adding bus drop-off improvements at Mountain Bay Middle School, and adding a wing at the senior high. Enrollment has dropped from approximately 6,000 to 5,700 students with incoming classes of 300-350 versus graduating classes of 450. Dr. Nye said the projects could be completed with no additional tax impact if a pool renovation is excluded. Board Chair Gibbs asked about long-term enrollment trajectory. Nye noted a junior high walkthrough is scheduled for October 6th for interested supervisors.

## Public Comment
Eight speakers signed up for public comment. Seven spoke on the animal waste ordinance: dairy farmers Kevin Schmiky, Andrew Belter, Shannon Niclay, and Patrick Suchka all expressed support, citing the collaborative process and financial and environmental benefits of nutrient management plans. Michael Toris of the River Alliance of Wisconsin praised Marathon County's watershed collaboration. Tom Mueller of Miltrim Farms (3,000 cows) supported the ordinance but said it did not go far enough. James Yetus of the town of Easton spoke in opposition, calling it government overreach and arguing the workgroup was not representative of opponents. Two speakers — Mitch Maguire and Rosley Maguire — spoke on the Town of Green Valley rezoning, urging the board to honor the town's formal disapproval and warning of precedent-setting risk if agricultural zoning is applied to a commercial contractor's parcel.

## Action Items

- Infrastructure committee chair appointment vote postponed to next month's board meeting
- Town of Green Valley Sawyer Daget rezoning (Ordinance 33-26) approved over town's objection; town retains veto authority
- Town of Green Valley rezoning (Ordinance 34-26), Town of Gunther reszones (Ordinances 35-26, 36-26, 37-26), Town of Hall rezoning (Ordinance 38-26), Town of McMillan rezoning (Ordinance 39-26), and Town of Reed rezoning (Ordinance 40-26) all approved
- Consent agenda items C11 through C13 approved unanimously
- Finance department recognized for GFOA Distinguished Budget Presentation Award for 2026 budget
- County administrator to deliver budget on September 29th; special Human Resource Finance and Property Committee meeting at 1 p.m. that day
- Wisconsin Counties Association conference resolution on local control for large-scale solar and wind projects — Supervisor Pool requested Marathon County vote yes
- ERC and conservation staff directed to develop KPIs and regular check-in reporting on water quality improvements following ordinance implementation
- Fee structure for winter spreading license to be developed through ERC and county board before 2028 implementation
