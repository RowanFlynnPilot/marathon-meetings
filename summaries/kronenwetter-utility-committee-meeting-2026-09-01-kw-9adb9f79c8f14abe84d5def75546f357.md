## Meeting Overview
The Kronenwetter Village Utility Committee held its September 1, 2026 meeting covering financial reports for both water and sewer utilities, a staffing update, ongoing lift station construction delays with liquidated damages, and several policy discussions including a water tower cell lease renewal with AT&T, a septic/holding tank request from Northland Lutheran High School, data center ordinance planning, and the proposed county highway garage project. The committee approved the AT&T water tower lease renewal and directed staff to invite Northland Lutheran representatives to a future meeting to advance discussion on a possible ordinance amendment allowing seasonal holding tanks.

## Key Discussions
### Utility Director Report
Utility Director Greg reported that lift station 2 panel installation is scheduled for the following Wednesday after WPS installed the wrong transformer. He also reported that a water department employee gave two weeks notice, requiring the position to be posted, and that the 2027 budget will include a request for an additional utility department employee due to growth and treatment plant demands. Green Bay Python TV is performing televising and cleaning of manholes on Kowalski Road.

### Lift Station 8 Construction Delays and Liquidated Damages
Lift station 8 remains visually incomplete with the original substantial completion date of July 15 having passed. The contractor now estimates completion by end of September, though committee members expressed skepticism. Liquidated damages of $500 per day are being imposed on the contractor covering the entire project scope, including boring under Highway 51/52 and connection to lift station 7. Approximately 35% of the roughly $2.4 million contract remains unpaid, giving the village leverage.

### Financial Report - Water and Sewer Utility Funds
Finance staff reported water utility revenues through seven months at $450,000 versus $449,000 last year, with operational expenses at $282,000 versus $261,000 — essentially flat. The sewer utility showed a $121,000 surplus versus $268,000 last year, with expenses at $559,000 versus $414,000, driven largely by Remote Metro Sewage District charges of $324,000 compared to $216,000 the prior year. Discussion centered on whether the elevated Remote Metro costs reflect a new normal due to infiltration from a wet spring, and whether rate adjustments will be needed in 2027. General obligation debt capacity stands at 17% used with $49 million available; staff discussed scenarios for upcoming sewer utility borrowing using either revenue bonds or general obligation debt pending completion of the 2025 audit.

### Water Tower AT&T Cell Lease Agreement
The committee reviewed a proposed five-year lease agreement with AT&T for antenna space on the village water tower at $27,000 in year one with a 2% annual escalator, down from the most recent payment of $35,123 and previous years' rate of $31,360. One committee member (Alex Fedwick) voted no, expressing concern that insufficient time was spent negotiating a better rate, while others acknowledged the need to move forward. The motion passed 4-1.

### Septic Tank/Holding Tank Ordinance - Northland Lutheran High School
Greg explained that Northland Lutheran High School has sought approval for a holding tank near their athletic fields' concession stand for approximately 18 months, initially with informal encouragement from former development director Peter Wagner who is no longer with the village. The current ordinance prohibits septic tanks within the sewage district. Committee members expressed willingness to work with the school but raised concerns about opening the door to other applicants and noted the need for strict, narrow ordinance language covering seasonal use, non-residential nature, distance requirements, and tank size limits. The committee directed staff to invite Northland Lutheran representatives, including their plumber, to the next utility committee meeting.

### Data Center Ordinance Discussion
The committee discussed the village board's one-year moratorium on data center approvals and the need for utility-side input on what regulations to pursue over the next 12 months. Key concerns included water consumption limits, requirements for closed-loop cooling systems, sewer discharge capacity, and the difficulty of identifying data center customers before projects are finalized. Committee members noted the wide range of data center sizes and that high water and power consumers could mask their identity as industrial customers. No action was taken; the committee agreed to continue researching ordinances from other municipalities.

### County Highway Garage Update
Greg reported that SD Ellenbacker is ready to begin construction on the county highway garage pending state plan approval, which could come any day or take several more weeks. The contractor is seeking preliminary approval just for the foundation to begin work before cold weather. Discussion also addressed the garage's planned use of a private well for brine-making operations, with committee member Craig Mortensen raising detailed concerns about salt runoff contaminating groundwater given historical problems at other county facilities, and recommending the village review the stormwater and drainage plan for the site.
