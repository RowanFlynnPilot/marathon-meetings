## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Board Executive Committee held a wide-ranging meeting covering the Bugtussle bond default crisis, the Hub City Wind Farm joint development agreement, a county road funding policy, and strategic plan updates. The committee voted unanimously to enter closed session to discuss strategy related to Bugtussle's failure to make payments on a $25 million conduit bond, leaving the county on the hook for a $347,409 shortfall, and also agreed to call a special full board meeting for the following Tuesday at 6 p.m. to address both the Alliant/Hub City Wind JDA response and the Bugtussle situation.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Two speakers addressed the committee. Joe Bergen of the Town of Day criticized the Alliant/Hub City Wind Farm proposed Joint Development Agreement (JDA) as vague and one-sided, noting provisions such as 90-day complaint response windows and unspecified haul routes, and urged the county to disregard the JDA and draft its own protective agreement. Ed Ruschinski, town chairman of Bergen, spoke about Moon Road, which accesses Big Oplain Park, arguing that the town maintains the road without property tax revenue from the park and requesting county funding assistance; he also suggested the county consider a user fee for horse trailer use on trails.

### Approval of September 17, 2026 Executive Committee Meeting Minutes
Supervisor Maras moved approval and Supervisor Rosenberg seconded. No discussion was held and the minutes were approved unanimously on a voice vote.

### Strategic Plan Development Update and Next Steps
Vice Chair Boots reported that the 20 draft strategic objectives had been sent to the public and returned, with compilation planned for November and an informational packet expected for the December executive meeting. Staff discussed potentially adding data points for every strategy, not just each objective, which may push final adoption to January or February 2027. Supervisor Deb [last name inaudible] raised a concern that strategic plan language promoting agricultural land preservation conflicted with wind and solar development; Boots and staff noted that even if included, state statutes governing projects over 100 megawatts would still control. Approximately 150 public responses were received.

### Comprehensive Revision to Ordinances to Reference Fee Schedule
Administrator Leonard briefly described the proposal to remove specific fee dollar amounts from ordinances and reference the newly adopted fee schedule instead. Supervisor Robinson raised concerns that some items — including Children with Disabilities Educational Board compensation, Civil Service Commission pay, vehicle registration fees, and statutorily set delinquent property tax percentages — were not appropriate for this process. Administration agreed to provide a redlined version and the committee voted unanimously to postpone action to the December executive committee meeting.

### County Policy Regarding Funding of Town Roadways Providing Access to County Facilities (Moon Road and Duncan Road)
Deputy Administrator Homan and Director Pauley presented updates to a 2015 resolution governing county assistance to towns for roads accessing county parks and forest units, including two newly proposed additions: a section of Moon Road (Town of Bergen to Big Oplain Park) and Leather Camp Drive (Town of Bavent). Extensive debate followed on where to draw policy lines, particularly regarding highway facilities like the Kronenwetter salt shed and whether the resolution should expand beyond parks and forests. Supervisor Greiner supported gathering traffic count data specifically for Moon Road given its unique situation as the only county park with this access issue. Supervisor Boots, Robinson, and Ritter expressed caution about expanding scope without more research. The committee voted unanimously to postpone to no later than the January executive committee meeting, with administration directed to gather maps, traffic count methodology, historical cost data since 2015, information on peer county practices, any legal requirements for such a policy, existing township road agreements, and tonnage impacts from forestry operations.

### Item 6D: Alliant/Hub City Wind JDA and Special Board Meeting
Chair Gibbs explained that Marathon County Resolution 5526 required the Alliant/Hub City Wind proposed JDA to be brought before the full board. He proposed calling a special board meeting the following Tuesday at 6 p.m. to allow all 38 board members to weigh in on a response — whether further negotiation, revision, or termination of discussions. The committee reached consensus on Tuesday evening and Gibbs said he would notify all board members to reserve that date.

### Bugtussle Relationship Update and Discussion of Special Board Meeting
Administrator Leonard recapped the Bugtussle bond default: Bugtussle failed to deposit required funds into the debt service fund by October 15, received default notices on October 26-27, and the trustee made a November 2 payment from reserves. Bugtussle has failed to replenish the fund, leaving a shortfall of $347,409.01 that the county must pay under its guarantee by April 8, 2027. The county's annual obligation on the $25 million conduit issuance runs approximately $1,504,443 per year through 2051. The committee voted unanimously by roll call to enter closed session under Wisconsin Statutes 19.85(1)(e) and 19.85(1)(g) to discuss asset recovery strategy and potential litigation without revealing the county's hand to Bugtussle.

## Public Comment
Two speakers offered public comment. Joe Bergen, Town of Day, criticized the Hub City Wind Farm proposed JDA as vague, one-sided, and inadequate — citing a 90-day complaint response window, unspecified haul routes, no decommissioning bonding, and lump-sum road repair provisions — and urged the county to reject it and draft its own protective agreement. Ed Ruschinski, town chairman of Bergen, requested county funding assistance for Moon Road, which accesses Big Oplain Park, arguing the town receives no property tax from the park's roughly 900 acres in Bergen despite heavy road traffic, and suggested the county consider a horse trailer user fee for park trails.
