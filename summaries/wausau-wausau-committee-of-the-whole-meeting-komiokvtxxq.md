## Meeting Overview
The Wausau City Council held a Committee of the Whole on July 13, 2026, covering three major topics: lead service line replacement funding and a proposed mandatory ordinance, a proposal to pursue the UW-Stevens Point Marathon campus for police relocation and central fire conversion, and a discussion on budget process and revenue diversification. The committee voted 9-1 to direct staff to respond to Marathon County's RFP for the UW campus, and unanimously referred budget process improvements to the Finance Committee.

## Key Discussions
### Lead Service Line Replacement Funding and Proposed Mandatory Ordinance
Sean Aged of Community Infrastructure Partners reported that Wausau has replaced over 2,600 lead service lines to date, received over $20 million in principal forgiveness grants — more than any Wisconsin community outside Milwaukee — and maintains a 96% homeowner consent rate. He noted that having a mandatory ordinance would improve Wausau's ranking on the DNR's project priority list and help access harder-to-reach homes in disadvantaged census tracts that qualify for 100% principal forgiveness. Alderperson Martens expressed concern about mandating work inside residents' homes, while Alderperson Watson and Alderperson Lukens acknowledged the ordinance's value for securing funding but emphasized that any mandate would only apply when grant money is fully available to cover private-side costs. Director Lynman clarified the ordinance would go before the utility commission the following day before returning to council, and that the existing language struck a balance allowing the city flexibility in funding approaches.

### Relocation of Wausau Police Department to UW-Stevens Point Marathon Campus / Central Fire Conversion
Chief Barnes presented an opportunity to respond to Marathon County's RFP for the vacant UW-Stevens Point Marathon campus, envisioning North-South Hall as a new police department, the existing police facility converted to central fire, and potential partnership with the Wausau School District for administrative offices in the southern portion of the building. Director Randy Gro explained the ask was simply to authorize staff to draft an RFP response and approve a budget modification (through Finance and Council) of up to approximately $50,000 for facility assessments, noting no money would be spent unless the city is selected. Alderperson Larson and Alderperson Tierney raised concerns about building age, deferred maintenance, and the long-unaddressed need for a fleet facility, arguing the fleet facility must be a top priority before police and fire projects. Alderpersons Watson, Slonski, Wiskowski, and Neal expressed support for exploring the opportunity given its time-sensitive nature and potential long-term savings, noting a closed session was planned for August 12 to discuss RFP strategy. The motion to direct staff to pursue the RFP response passed 9-1.

### Budget Process and Priorities
Alders Watson and Lukens initiated the discussion, advocating for an earlier and more deliberate budget process — beginning in April or May — to give alders and the public more time to engage with priorities before the fall budget crunch. Finance Director Maryann (presenting remotely) provided a detailed overview showing an estimated $1 million structural shortfall for the 2027 budget even before full motorpool funding, driven by $1.7 million in wage and benefit increases, a $300,000 increase in the hauler contract, rising debt service, and reduced shared revenues. She outlined revenue diversification options including a vehicle registration fee, stormwater utility, and refuse/recycling fee, noting Wausau has not implemented these tools that many peer communities have. Alderperson Trueblood cautioned that all such fees are effectively taxes and expressed concern about the burden on residents already facing rising costs of living. Alderperson Slonski noted the true shortfall could reach $2.5 million if the motorpool were fully funded. The committee voted unanimously to refer the budget process improvement discussion to the Finance Committee to work with the finance director and mayor on a more detailed, collaborative timeline.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered.

## Action Items
- Staff directed to draft a response to Marathon County's RFP for the UW-Stevens Point Marathon campus for police relocation and potential fire department conversion.
- A budget modification of up to approximately $50,000 for facility assessments related to the UW campus proposal to be brought through Finance Committee and full Council at the August meeting.
- A closed session scheduled for August 12 to discuss RFP response strategy for the UW campus proposal.
- Finance Committee directed to work with Finance Director and Mayor to develop a more detailed and earlier budget process timeline, with full implementation targeted for fiscal year 2028.
- Lead service line replacement ordinance to go before the Utility Commission the following day before returning to City Council.
- Staff to explore revenue diversification options including vehicle registration fee, stormwater utility, and refuse/recycling fee.
- Fleet facility solution to be brought to Infrastructure Committee in August.
