## Meeting Overview
The Village of Kronenwetter held its first budget workshop for the 2027 fiscal year on October 19, 2026, with joint participation from the Administrative Policy Committee and Village Board. Village Administrator John and Finance Director Jim presented a comprehensive budget overview proposing a 5.7% tax rate increase (from $4.03 to $4.26 per $1,000 assessed value), a 6.99% levy increase to $2.9 million, and a $30 garbage fee surcharge due to diesel fuel costs, with the combined residential impact estimated at $62–$97 annually. No formal votes were taken; the session was a discussion and review workshop with a second session planned for October 20.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
One member of the public, Bernie Kramer of 2150 East State Highway 153, Mosinee, spoke during public comment. He expressed concern about the village moving away from municipal water service toward private wells and septic systems in new residential development, arguing this would hurt utility revenue (90% of which comes from residential users), increase costs for future homeowners, and undermine the village's own long-range planning within the septic service area utility.

### Tax Levy and Tax Rate
Village Administrator John presented a proposed tax rate increase from $4.03 to $4.26 per $1,000 assessed value, a 5.7% increase, and a levy increase from $2.7 million to $2.9 million (6.99%, approximately $190,000). He noted the village could legally levy as high as $4 million and a rate of $5.87 but was proposing well below that maximum. Trustee Mary Solheim raised concern that a 7% levy increase is unsustainable given only 0.651% net new construction, and staff acknowledged this while pointing to incoming developments (Green Tree, FAA property, TID 1 distribution center) as reasons for optimism.

### Garbage Fee Surcharge
Staff proposed a $30 garbage fee increase per household attributable entirely to a diesel fuel surcharge from Harter's contract, triggered when diesel exceeds $4 per gallon. The village has been absorbing roughly $6,000 per month in extra fuel costs since March 2026 (approximately $60,000 total) from contingency reserves and TID III closure revenues. Staff recommended the surcharge remain until diesel prices drop below $4, at which point it would be removed. The base rate increase of $9.12 previously planned was set aside in favor of this surcharge-only approach.

### Water and Sewer Rates
Staff reported no proposed rate increases for water or sewer in 2027. A 2.5% sewer rate increase had been anticipated for January 2027 tied to a borrowing, but the borrowing was delayed, pushing the first debt service payment to 2028. A new water well and water tower are planned but unlikely to be funded until 2028 or later, pending PSC review. No action was taken.

### Police Department Budget
A second new officer budgeted for Q4 2026 will not be hired this year due to a thin applicant pool; Chief Terry plans to wait for a stronger candidate pool, possibly December academy graduates. Both new officers will be fully funded for 12 months in 2027. Health insurance for the police union came in at 14.2% increase (down from an initial 17% quote). Staff are exploring moving police union employees to the TeamCare plan to reduce per-employee health costs, with the union reportedly not opposed. Crossing guard pay was proposed to increase from $16.48 to $20 per hour due to recruitment difficulties.

### Fire and EMS Budget
EMS ambulance runs are tracking at approximately 492 for 2026 against a budget of 420, and staff are projecting up to 500 runs for 2027, up from 390 actual in 2025. Administrator John said this requires a budget adjustment for 2026 and elevated funding in 2027. The Riverside formula for billing runs does not change for 2027 but includes a 1% increase effective 2028.

### Public Works Budget
The road maintenance budget is proposed to increase from $386,000 to $400,000 (3.63%), roughly in line with CPI at 3.7%. Winter plow blades are up $5,000 and road salt is up approximately $8,000. Marathon County landfill tipping fees are increasing about $7,000. The grader lease payment was already reduced by $4,000 after a prior board-approved trade-in. State highway aid to the village increased $52,000 (the maximum 15% allowed) due to increased infrastructure spending, including the Kronenwetter Drive project.

### Community Development and Planning Tech Position
Staff reported they are close to making an offer to a planning technician candidate to assist Planning Director Nathan. The position was only funded for nine months in 2026; it will be funded for a full 12 months in 2027. Administrator Jim emphasized urgency in hiring given high foot traffic at village hall for zoning questions and incoming development activity.

### TID 1 Expenditure Period - Trustee Challenge
Trustee Dan Lesniak raised a concern that it may not be legally permissible to make new expenditures from TID 1 because its expenditure period has ended, except potentially for administration costs. Administrator John said auditors and financial advisor Ehlers had previously indicated administration costs, assessment costs, and audit costs can flow through, but agreed to confirm the question definitively at the next night's meeting.

### General Fund Revenue Overview
Staff identified several revenue improvements: shared revenue up about $10,000 (2%); a $54,000 annual Magellan termination payment that had never been budgeted previously; highway aid up $52,000; and building permits trending up from $40,000 budget to an estimated $60,000. Cable franchise fee revenue is declining significantly, estimated at $60,500 for 2027 versus a prior budget of $90,000, attributed to residents switching to streaming services. Total general fund revenue is proposed to increase approximately $657,000 or 11%, with fund balance drawdowns for one-time items including $90,000 for year two of the revaluation, $75,000 for municipal building CIP projects, and a $10,000 carryover for potential TID 5 creation work.

## Public Comment
One speaker offered public comment: Bernie Kramer, 2150 East State Highway 153, Mosinee. He criticized the village for allowing new residential development to proceed with private wells and septic systems rather than connecting to municipal water and sewer, arguing this undermines utility revenue, increases long-term costs for homeowners, and contradicts the village's own planning documents. He also criticized what he characterized as illogical decision-making and the reduction in lot count in some developments.
