## Meeting Overview
The Kronenwetter Village Board held its September 14, 2026 meeting, approving three action items: an annual stormwater pollution discharge memorandum of agreement with neighboring municipalities, a zoning change request for a residential parcel on Leah Road, and the appointment of Kim Coyle as interim village clerk with a $500 biweekly pay stipend. Public comment focused on the Kowalski Road interchange and a school bus safety concern on Greenacres Road.

## Key Discussions
### President's Announcements
President Joling noted that a committee assignment issue has been referred to legal counsel and forwarded to APC for a possible ordinance change. He also shared that he sent a letter to an Iowa data center developer who mentioned building in southeastern Wisconsin, seeking information relevant to Kronenwetter's planning. A clerk candidate interview was scheduled for Thursday at 4:30 p.m.

### Public Comment - Kowalski Road Interchange
Guy Friedel of 2240 Ruby Drive urged the board to reassess and advance the Kowalski Road interstate interchange project, citing recent developments: Marathon County's highway department relocating to Kronenwetter and a likely 230,000 square foot distribution center south of Highway XX between Tower and Trailwood. He noted Wisconsin DOT modeling staff and the Wausau MPO have affirmed the interchange would significantly reduce traffic on I-39/Business 51 north, and referenced detailed 2009 engineering plans by Dick Schneider. He requested a special closed-session meeting with the Planning Commission, RDA, and Schneider. President Joling responded that Representative Jacobson sees little funding hope currently but expects opportunities to reset after the new year.

### Public Comment - Greenacres Road School Bus Safety
Mike Skalecki of 1724 Greenacres Road described dangerous school bus turnaround maneuvers at the dead-end road, including a bus backing out blindly onto County Highway X and a FedEx truck passing a stopped bus. He requested the village consider adding a cul-de-sac at the end of Greenacres Road, noting changed demographics with three families now having school-age children. President Joling directed DPW Director Greg to look into the matter, and noted the bus company Lamers refuses to travel the road, leaving children outside the quarter-mile walking policy limit.

### Administrator's Report
Administrator Davell highlighted a promising tour with Advanced Laser Technologies, interested in property north of Kowalski Road near the school for a manufacturing facility that could benefit TID No. 1; some zoning issues remain to be worked through. He reported the Fun Ride fundraiser raised approximately $3,148, putting the total with grants near $20,000 for Bushka Park. He flagged that rising diesel prices (over $6/gallon) will likely require resetting the trash special assessment in the 2027 budget, and that union negotiations with the Teamsters are scheduled for September 18.

### Police Chief's Report
The chief reported a significant increase in calls, with the department on pace to exceed 8,000 calls in 2026 compared to 7,100 in 2025, calling June through August the busiest stretch since 2019. The department remains one officer short and has received zero qualified applications in its latest posting despite a recruiting trip to Mid-State Technical College; the posting was extended five weeks. Trustees discussed a recent high-speed pursuit at 120 mph through Kronenwetter involving unmarked vehicles, and the chief confirmed the department assisted with that pursuit and others.

### Consent Agenda - August 24, 2026 Minutes
Trustee Coyle moved to accept the August 24, 2026 Village Board meeting minutes as presented; Trustee Mortensen seconded. No discussion was recorded. The motion carried on a voice vote.

### Resolution 2026-12 - WPDES Memorandum of Agreement
DPW Director Greg explained this annual MS4 stormwater permit agreement with neighboring municipalities including Ross, Weston, Rib Mountain, and Marathon County, allowing communities to share public education credits toward DNR compliance requirements. Trustee Coyle moved to approve; Trustee Solheim seconded. The motion carried unanimously on a voice vote with no discussion.

### Zoning Change - William Swineinski, 1900 Leah Road
Planner Nathan presented a zoning change request from AR to RR5 for 26.7 acres at 1904 Leah Road, noting the Plan Commission had already conditionally approved a certified survey map pending this zoning change. He characterized the U-shaped parcel as already substantially surrounded by the target zone on three sides, and recommended approval. Trustee Lesniak moved to approve the zoning change and the accompanying resolution (designated 26-XX pending numbering); Trustee Stowell seconded. The motion carried 7-0 on a roll call vote.

### Appointment of Interim Village Clerk
Administrator Davell explained the appointment was a necessity, not a preference, as Kim Coyle is the only certified staff member available. He noted Marathon County's clerk and deputy clerk will come to the village to assist with election compliance. Trustee Kelly Coyle recused herself and left the meeting at 6:35 p.m. Trustee Myszka moved to appoint Kim Coyle as interim clerk with a $500 biweekly stipend; the motion was seconded (seconded by Craig Mortensen per context). The board specified the stipend would continue until a qualified clerk is physically in the office. The motion carried 6-0 on a roll call vote.

## Public Comment
Two residents spoke. Guy Friedel (2240 Ruby Drive) urged the board to hold a special meeting to reassess the Kowalski Road interstate interchange project, citing DOT modeling support, the county highway department relocation to Kronenwetter, and a likely 230,000 sq ft distribution center nearby; he referenced 2009 engineering plans by Dick Schneider and noted prior village spending of approximately $800,000 on related infrastructure. Mike Skalecki (1724 Greenacres Road) requested the village revisit adding a cul-de-sac at the end of Greenacres Road to address dangerous school bus turnaround maneuvers on County Highway X, describing three school-age families now on the road whose children cannot safely access bus service.
