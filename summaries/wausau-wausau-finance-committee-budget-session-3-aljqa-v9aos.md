# Wausau Finance Committee Budget Session #3

## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Finance Committee held its third budget session for the 2027 budget, covering special revenue funds, capital projects, TID districts, debt service, and enterprise funds. A significant portion of the meeting focused on the city's growing debt burden, with Alderperson Hani presenting eight budget proposals including capping borrowing, finding $4.2-5.2 million in deferrable capital projects, cutting the city administrator position, and requesting a 1% general fund reduction. The committee agreed not to meet Thursday as planned, instead giving time for members to submit written amendments for a future meeting.

## Key Discussions
### Special Revenue Funds Overview
Finance Director Maryanne presented the special revenue funds including community development block grant, economic development, environmental cleanup, room tax, recycling, animal control, hazmat, and the 400 Block Riverlife fund. She noted the environmental cleanup fund reserves are declining now that an insurance annuity from the Holtz Kroy settlement has ended, and costs from EPA oversight — including a $121,000 bill last year — will eventually return to the levy. She also suggested consolidating some special revenue funds into the general fund to build up the expenditure restraint base, noting the city receives over $1 million in expenditure restraint grant funding.

### Capital Projects Discussion
Engineering staff walked through 2027 infrastructure projects including Ethel Street reconstruction, 11th Avenue, 17th Avenue STP Urban design, asphalt overlay (proposed increase from $600,000 to $800,000), lead service line replacement, and sidewalk repair. Alderperson Hani raised concern about borrowing for basic infrastructure and asked whether projects like Ethel Street could be deferred to 2028; engineering staff explained deferral simply shifts the entire street reconstruction schedule back a year and increases ongoing maintenance costs. A committee member questioned why the city pays for alley paving excavation costs while residents are assessed only for asphalt, prompting discussion about revisiting that policy.

### TID Districts Review
Economic Development Manager Patrick presented TID 8 (MBX South demolition and environmental cleanup, with a $250,000 WEDC brownfield grant applied for), TID 11 (business campus site prep and marketing for a large development site off Innovation Way), and TID 12 (limited expenditures due to ongoing deficits, projected to remain in deficit for approximately 10 years). Jefferson parking ramp HVAC replacement was identified as a critical project after one of two redundant systems failed. The former Sears ramp east wall — exposed after the Sears building demolition — needs $700,000 in waterproofing and rehabilitation. A Forest Street engineering and planning project of $150,000 was discussed as enabling future LRIP and STP Urban grant applications.

### Debt Service Analysis
Maryanne presented a detailed debt analysis showing the city's outstanding general obligation debt at approximately 33.38% of equalized value (limit is 5%), projected to rise to 33.81% after proposed 2027 borrowing. She compared the 2016 infrastructure borrowing of $2.8 million at 1.54% interest (resulting in a $338,000 annual payment) to the proposed 2027 borrowing of $6.2 million estimated at approximately 4.27% (resulting in roughly $770,000 annually plus $216,000 for the lead service line loan). She proposed using TID 8's approximately $11 million in remaining increment capacity to absorb roughly $2.18 million in infrastructure costs, reducing the levy impact by approximately $275,000 annually. She also suggested a 10-year plan to incrementally add $130,000 per year to levy-funded capital to eventually take asphalt overlay and concrete repairs off debt.

### Enterprise Funds: Metro Ride, Airport, Parking
The committee discussed that Metro Ride and the municipal airport operate at deficits covered by the general fund. Alderperson Hani raised constituent concerns about deficit operations, while Alderperson Tierney noted some municipal services like transit are inherently not self-sustaining but enhance quality of life. It was explained that Metro Ride cannot expand service to surrounding municipalities like Rib Mountain because those communities have not invited the city in and would need to help subsidize service, as Wisconsin does not permit regional transit authorities. New Metro Ride buses are coming through the Volkswagen emissions settlement. Airport revenue discussion included land lease payments, property taxes on privately-owned hangars, and past fuel-flow surcharge revenue that was discontinued when aviation fuel prices rose.

### Storm Water Utility Discussion
Engineering Director Eric provided an overview of a storm water utility concept at committee request, explaining that currently 65% of storm water costs are borne by residential taxpayers rather than major impervious-surface users such as large parking lots and commercial buildings. A storm water utility would distribute costs based on equivalent dwelling units tied to impervious surface area, bringing in non-taxable entities like nonprofits as payers, with a corresponding reduction in the levy. Maryanne noted a consultant study funded by a grant is completed and a presentation is scheduled for the October 27th city council meeting.

### Alderperson Hani's Budget Proposals and Next Steps
Alderperson Hani presented eight budget proposals including: capping 2027 borrowing at $17-18 million or reducing debt by $1 million; identifying $4.2-5.2 million in deferrable capital projects; a 1% general fund spending reduction; removing $150,000 for the city administrator position; a motorpool charge reconciliation; a current accounting of committed versus unassigned general fund balance; asking the mayor to direct Metro Ride, airport, and parking to develop 5-year financial plans; a reconciled capital project schedule; and a 5-year debt forecast separated by general government, water, and wastewater. The committee agreed not to meet Thursday, directing members to submit written amendments to Maryanne, who will distribute them and incorporate approved changes as a budget insert. Alder Watson was noted as also having proposals to submit.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. The chair noted no members of the public were present.
