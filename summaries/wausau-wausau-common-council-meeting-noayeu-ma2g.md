## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Common Council met on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, taking several significant actions including approving a new full-time human resources coordinator position, passing a data center zoning ordinance, and directing the city to cease use of Flock license plate reader cameras and physically cover them within two business weeks. The Flock camera vote was the most debated item of the evening, with extensive discussion about contract obligations, civil liberties, and the police department's operational role, ultimately passing 9-1 as amended.

## Key Discussions
### Stormwater Utility Study and Stormwater Quality Management Plan Presentation
Justin Gataski of Strand Associates presented a two-part report covering a DNR-approved stormwater quality management plan update and a stormwater utility feasibility study, both funded in part by a $170,000 WDNR grant (50% city, 50% grant). He explained that Wausau currently meets total suspended solids removal requirements but falls far short of the 68.6% total phosphorus reduction required under the Wisconsin River TMDL, achieving only 20.3%. The presentation recommended Alternative 2—a mix of constructing wet detention ponds and water quality trading with neighboring communities—as the best path to compliance. Gataski also outlined a potential stormwater utility charging residents roughly $100–$175 per ERU annually as a more equitable, user-based alternative to tax funding; Alderperson Lukens asked about schools and artificial turf fields under such a system, and Alderperson Hoenecke asked about the top public concerns with implementation, which Gataski identified as resident education about equitable cost reallocation.

### Consent Agenda
Two items were pulled from the consent agenda before the vote: File 26-0707 (full-time HR coordinator) and File 26-1013 (Terrace Beautification Special Committee). The remaining consent agenda items were moved by Watson, seconded by Neal, and approved 10-0 without discussion.

### File 26-0707: Creation of Full-Time Human Resources Coordinator Position
Alderperson Watson raised concerns about hiring a permanent position before a planned organizational audit, suggesting contracted services as a temporary alternative. Council President Kilian and Alderperson Martens countered that the HR department is severely understaffed and that the position had been discussed and supported by the HR committee unanimously over many months; Martens also noted that the city's annual financial audit flagged the need to capture institutional knowledge. Police Chief (responding to a question about the $10,000 professional development allocation) explained it was drawn from savings generated by bringing crossing guard services in-house. The motion passed 9-1.

### File 26-1013: Terrace Beautification Special Committee
Alderperson Larson pulled the item, expressing concern about adding more government committees and raising questions about public health, tree maintenance conflicts, and tall grass. Alderperson Trueblood, whose committee originated the resolution, explained that the current boulevard planting ordinance dates to the 1970s and is vague; the special committee would be temporary, include city staff and citizen members, and use Madison's ordinance as a template. Alderperson Lukens confirmed that the sustainability committee had been consulted and that the committee's role was investigative, not action-oriented. The item passed unanimously on a voice vote.

### File 26-0706: Reconsideration and Approval of Data Center Zoning Ordinance
The council first voted 8-2 to reconsider the previously passed ordinance, then debated the main motion. Alderperson Lukens asked whether the ordinance required confirmation of adequate utility capacity; Planning Director Edmonson confirmed the ordinance requires applicants to submit energy service, water consumption, wastewater, and sound reports, allowing the city to deny a conditional use permit if capacity is insufficient. Alderperson Hoenecke raised concerns about industry-funded reports being biased; Edmonson said the city can contract for independent studies at the applicant's expense. Alderperson Martens raised concerns about NDAs, and Edmonson stated she generally opposes NDAs for transparency reasons but deferred to the city attorney on specifics. The ordinance passed 9-1.

### File 26-1014: Safe Drinking Water Revolving Loan Program Resolution for Lead Service Line Replacement
The resolution declaring official intent to reimburse expenditures from the Safe Drinking Water Revolving Loan Program for the fiscal year 2027-28 lead service line replacement program was moved by Watson and seconded by Slonski. No discussion was recorded and it passed 10-0.

### File 23-1012: Resolution to Cease Use of Flock Cameras (via Suspension of Rules)
The council first suspended the rules 8-2 to bring the item to the floor. Extended debate followed on contract obligations, civil liberties, and operational logistics. Police Chief explained that access to the Flock system could be turned off internally but that cameras—owned by Flock—would continue capturing images stored for 30 days unless physically covered, and that Flock's contract prohibits customers from tampering with hardware. City Attorney Ann confirmed the council has authority to direct the mayor to cease use, but noted the specific wording directing the police department to cover cameras was procedurally awkward. Alderperson Watson successfully moved an amendment directing the appropriate city department, by order of the chief executive officer, to physically cover the cameras within two business weeks; the amendment passed 9-1. On the main motion as amended, Alderpersons Lukens, Tierney, and Martens all cited changed circumstances since the original approval—including documented misuse by law enforcement officers in other Wisconsin cities, Flock selling anonymized data to third parties, and AI-enhanced image processing beyond original assurances—as reasons to act now rather than wait for the contract to expire December 31st. Alderperson Trueblood expressed concern about abandoning the contract and setting a precedent of overriding prior council decisions. The main motion as amended passed 9-1.

## Public Comment
Five residents spoke during public comment. A resident who identified herself as executive director of a domestic violence agency and chair of the Wisconsin Domestic Violence Fatality Review Initiative urged the council to vote yes on Item 9 to end Flock camera use, citing documented misuse by officers in Milwaukee and Menasha, noting that 42 Wisconsin agencies have ended or decided to end Flock contracts, and requesting written termination notice to Flock by November 15th under Section 11.16. A resident spoke in favor of allowing boulevard planting, describing residents who do so as investing in their neighborhoods, and identifying themselves as a master gardener. A resident urged the council to cover Flock cameras immediately and remove them from the budget, also commenting favorably on the stormwater presentation. A resident urged the council to discontinue Flock camera use immediately and not fund any similar technology in future budgets, calling it a bipartisan issue. A resident urged the council to cover the Flock cameras and remove them from future budgets, citing many things the city needs to spend money on and noting broad public opposition to Flock.

## Action Items
- Consent agenda items (excluding two pulled items) approved 10-0
- Full-time Human Resources Coordinator position created and approved 9-1
- Terrace Beautification Special Committee of the Infrastructure and Facilities Committee created unanimously
- Data center zoning ordinance (File 26-0706) approved as amended 9-1
- Safe Drinking Water Revolving Loan Program reimbursement resolution for lead service line replacement approved 10-0
- Rules suspended 8-2 to bring Flock camera resolution to the floor
- Resolution directing cessation of Flock data use and physical covering of Flock cameras approved 9-1 as amended; appropriate city department to cover cameras within two business weeks on direction of the chief executive officer
- City to send Flock written notice of termination and non-renewal; city attorney to provide written opinion on litigation risk
- Council President Slonski asked to place 'calling the question' procedure on the rules committee agenda for review
- Sustainability survey deadline reminder: October 31st
