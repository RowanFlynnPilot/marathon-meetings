## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees met on Monday, June 15, 2026, holding a lengthy session covering a range of infrastructure, public safety, and community issues. The board voted to refer the farmers market location debate to the Parks Committee with the survey extended through June 30, conditionally approved two liquor license renewals with conditions attached, and discussed options for staffing the SAFER fire agency following a failed spring referendum. Additional action was taken on road projects, water utility financing, and an ethics commission referral to the HR/Finance Committee.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Three residents spoke. The first raised questions about SAFER's fund balance of approximately $1.2 million, asking why those funds weren't being used for firefighter staffing rather than being drawn down to pursue grants, and requested clarification during the SAFER agenda item. A second resident raised concerns about parking congestion and safety on streets adjacent to Kennedy Park during the farmers market, citing utility lines in the right-of-way, blocked sightlines at her driveway, and increased turnaround traffic. A third speaker criticized the board for conducting a survey after already moving the farmers market, noted that 39% of 275 respondents rated the new location poor or very poor and 57% preferred the former location, and also challenged the board on fire department staffing and borrowing for Kennedy Park baseball fields.

### Committee Chair and Department Reports
Trustee Zeyghami reported that approximately 15 million gallons of clean water are being discharged into the sewer system monthly at a cost of about $20,000, totaling roughly $240,000 annually, and urged enforcement of penalties. The fire chief report noted six part-time positions being offered, hail storm vehicle damage handled through fleet insurance at a $2,500 deductible (one truck had nearly $9,000 in damage alone), and two firefighters injured during a successful water rescue on Lake Wausau. Village President Maloney announced the fire chief had received the state fire chiefs association award for the year. Public Works Director Michael reported a speed study on West Navigation Avenue yielded an 85th percentile speed of 48.8 mph, supporting a return to the 45 mph posted limit between Von Kennel and County J by the following Thursday, consistent with current ordinance; the section from Camp Phillips to Von Kennel remains 35 mph per an April ordinance amendment.

### Farmers Market Feedback Survey and Possible Actions
The board discussed updated survey results showing 322 responses as of that afternoon, with significant dissatisfaction around parking and traffic at the Kennedy Park location. Staff and trustees debated parking remedies including no-parking signs on the east side of Alderson Street, roped-off organized parking areas, and additional police patrols on Saturday mornings when congestion peaks around 9–11 a.m. The board acknowledged the former site is no longer available and that the village hall site lacks sufficient square footage for vendors and parking. After debate, the board approved a motion to refer the matter to the Parks Committee and extend the survey through June 30, with the possibility of reopening it in August or September; a suggestion was also floated to gather metadata on survey respondents' referral source in future iterations.

### SAFER Staffing and Post-Referendum Discussion
Administrator Gabbert outlined that a resolution would need to be adopted by August 25 to place another referendum on the November ballot. Finance Director Jessica explained SAFER's uncommitted fund balance is just under $1.3 million and that the board had discussed incrementally drawing it down to the $500,000–$600,000 range, but that this money cannot be used for ongoing personnel costs. The board discussed that three firefighters hired in the current budget year were funded using roughly $50,000 in village fund balance and $30,000 from SAFER's fund balance along with other adjustments, and that this approach is not repeatable. With the anticipated TIF closure generating an estimated $503,000 in additional levy capacity, Jessica suggested it may be possible to fund three additional firefighters without a referendum, though the figures are estimates pending state data in August. Several trustees expressed skepticism that another $600,000 referendum would pass, with one suggesting a smaller, more realistic dollar amount if a referendum is pursued. No action was taken; the safer board will revisit in July.

### Ethics Commission Discussion
Trustee Olson requested discussion on whether the village should establish an ethics commission, board, or complaint process for elected officials, appointees, committee members, and staff, noting his intent was simply to gauge board interest and find an appropriate venue for further work rather than to address specific past allegations. Village President Maloney and other trustees clarified there have been no secret meetings, and Maloney offered to answer any questions. The board expressed general support for the concept as a transparency measure, and voted to refer the matter to the Finance and HR Committee for further development.

### Liquor License Renewals — Hutch's Kelly Club and RJT and Sons
The board conditionally approved renewal of Hutch's Kelly Club LLC's Class B licenses contingent on payment of $662.76 in past-due utility fees; the clerk confirmed the balance had already been paid before the vote. For RJT and Sons LLC, the board conditionally approved renewal contingent on proof of rehabilitation, payment of the license fee, and payment of a past-due supplier balance of $91,825 disclosed the day of the meeting; one trustee voted nay on both the amendment and the main motion, resulting in a 5-1 vote. The clerk noted the new licensing term begins July 1 and that failure to meet all conditions means no license will be issued.

### Infrastructure and Utility Projects
The board approved Resolution 2026-016 (sewer utility compliance annual report to DNR), Resolution 2026-017 (special assessments for Plato Avenue/Pine Park Street to Alderson Street reconstruction), and Resolution 2026-018 (Alderson Street and Gelnik Avenue intersection project). The board also approved a topsoil screening contract at $5.50 per cubic yard for an estimated $38,500, Shirley Avenue paving quotes, an MSA contract amendment for right-of-way services on the Alderson/Gelnik project, a change order for the same project, and an amendment to the Safe Drinking Water Loan Program agreement increasing principal forgiveness from $1.4 million to $1.79 million for the PFAS treatment well project. The board acknowledged that the Bellingwood Avenue watermain replacement received zero bids due to too-tight a timeline and deferred the project to an undetermined date pending coordination with Rothschild's road repaving schedule.
