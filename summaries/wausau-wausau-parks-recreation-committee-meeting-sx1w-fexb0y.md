## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Parks & Recreation Committee met on October 5, 2026, approving a $5,500 bid for ash tree removal at Pleasant View Park due to emerald ash borer infestation, amending a Junior Achievement agreement to terminate development rights at the Wangard property, and holding a quasi-judicial hearing on a homeowner's appeal of a city forester fee for damaging a city tree during unpermitted excavation. The committee also received updates on park projects including completion of the skate park, and discussed but did not vote on a potential county-owned parcel purchase on 28th Avenue for a future city park.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of August 31, 2026 Meeting Minutes
Alder Swansky moved to approve the minutes of the preceding meeting. Alder True Blood seconded. The minutes were approved unanimously on a voice vote with no corrections or additions.

### 3A: Award of Bid for Pleasant View Park Ash Tree Removal
Parks staff Jamie and city forester John presented the situation: emerald ash borer infestation has affected the majority of ash trees at Pleasant View Park, particularly around the sledding hill. Only one bid was received from Shriber Logging at $5,500, which covers mobilization costs as the timber market is depressed following closure of a local pulp mill. Staff noted the removal will significantly change the park's wooded appearance but that inaction would leave hazardous dead trees threatening the sledding hill amenity. Alder True Blood asked what would happen without action and received confirmation the park's appearance will change regardless. The $5,500 will come from the urban forestry/emerald ash borer dedicated budget. Alder Swansky inquired about the likelihood of additional bids; staff expressed confidence no other bidders would come forward given the small parcel size and city constraints. The committee voted unanimously to award the bid.

### 3B: Amendment of the Junior Achievement Agreement (Wangard Property)
Patrick Gatman from city planning and Vinnie presented an amendment to terminate Junior Achievement's rights to construct a 'champions area' in Area B of the Wangard property agreement. This cleanup was prompted by a certified survey map process for the property. Junior Achievement confirmed they have no intention to build there in the future. The committee approved the amendment unanimously on a voice vote after no substantive debate.

### 4A: Purchase of County-Owned Parkland on 28th Avenue for Future City Park
Jamie presented a county-owned parcel on 28th Avenue, originally purchased in 2006 for a highway corridor that was never built, now offered to the city. The roughly 3-acre parcel was originally labeled 'private park' in a 1974 development. Staff reported after a site visit that the land is very rocky, steeply graded, and largely undevelopable except for a small fill area potentially suitable for a playground. The city's park dedication fund holds approximately $536,064 that could be used for purchase. One committee member stated they would not personally support the purchase given the department's existing workload and the site's limited development potential. The matter was deferred to closed session for further deliberation on potential purchase price and interest.

### 4B: Park Updates – Athletic Park, Oak Island, River Edge Trail, Riverlife
Jamie announced the skate park is now complete and is working with donor families on a plaque unveiling. Alder Swansky asked about the cost of ongoing vandalism to Riverlife Park restrooms; Jamie noted the locking mechanisms on the south-side bathrooms have been difficult to replace due to jamming of the electronic locks and said a cost summary would be provided at the next meeting.

### 5A: Hearing – Appeal of Tree Assessment Fee, 4011 Stone Ridge Drive
The committee conducted a formal quasi-judicial hearing on homeowner Jonathan Mafam's appeal of a city forester fee for damaging a city tree during unpermitted excavation. City plumbing inspector Dennis Keski testified that he found trenching had been done without an excavation or lateral permit and that as of the morning of the meeting no permit had been obtained. City forester John Kahan, a board-certified master arborist, testified the tree was losing leaves early due to root damage caused by the trenching, that the permit process would have identified a safer route or offered tree removal for a $550 fee, and that a pre-existing seam/occlusion in the tree was unrelated to the dieback. Mafam testified he purchased the home in September 2024, suffered a flooded finished basement and $30,000 in losses in the first spring, and was trying to install a French drain system; he stated he did not know he needed a permit, did not know the tree was a city tree, his contractor abandoned the job incomplete, and he was asking for leniency. He disputed John's characterization of his visit to the property. After testimony and committee questions, Alder Slonsky moved to adjourn into closed session for deliberation; the motion passed on a 4-0 roll call vote.
