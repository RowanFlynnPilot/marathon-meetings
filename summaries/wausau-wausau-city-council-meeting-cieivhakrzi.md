# Wausau City Council Meeting

## Meeting Overview
The Wausau City Council met on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, and took action on a range of items. The council defeated a resolution to set the 2026 payment-in-lieu-of-taxes (a tax charged to the city water utility in place of property taxes) at a reduced rate of $1.59 million, meaning the full calculated amount of nearly $2 million will now apply to the utility. The council approved a new city purchasing card policy, though several alderpersons said it needed stronger consequences and more specificity. The council approved a deed restriction on city-owned land at 1300 Cleveland Avenue, with added deadlines requiring the Department of Public Works to stop training there by February 1, 2027, and the Parks Department to find a new location for timber logs and chips by May 1, 2027. The council voted to fill the District 9 vacancy left by Alderperson Vicki Tierney's resignation through a special election in April rather than an interim appointment. The council then moved to closed session to discuss a legal settlement in a lawsuit against Georgia-Pacific.

## Key Discussions
### DECA Month Proclamation
Mayor Diny read a proclamation declaring November 2026 as Wausau DECA Month, honoring the Wausau West DECA chapter. Two student members from the club attended and had given the mayor a deskside briefing the prior week. A student speaker thanked the council and described DECA as more than a club. No vote was required; the proclamation was ceremonial.

### Sustainability Committee Report — Composting and Food Waste
Jesse Karns of the Sustainability, Energy and Environment Committee presented the committee's work on a Climate and Resilience Blueprint, focusing on waste. A representative of Sue's Growing Supply then presented their curbside food waste recycling program, called Hero, which diverts food scraps from landfills to a composting facility on County Road W. The program costs residents $150 to $350 per year depending on service level. The presenter said participation is their biggest challenge and asked for the council's public endorsement to build community trust. Alderperson Wiskowski asked whether per-household costs would drop with more participants; the presenter said a formal cost analysis had not been done. Alderperson Lukens asked about the status of Walmart as a client; the presenter explained the route no longer ends in Wausau so waste goes elsewhere. No vote was taken; the item was an informational update.

### File 23-1121 — 2026 Pilot Payment (Payment in Lieu of Taxes) for Wausau Waterworks
This resolution would have set the annual payment-in-lieu-of-taxes — a tax the city water utility pays to the city's general fund in place of property taxes — at a reduced $1.59 million instead of the full calculated amount of just under $2 million. Alderperson Killian said she opposes the pilot as a regressive fee that charges all water users equally regardless of income. Alderperson Tierney said the pilot is a hidden tax on water bills that kicks money back to the general fund and that she campaigned against it. Finance Director Maryanne Groeschel explained the Public Service Commission sets the formula and the resolution gives the utility a discount; without it, the full amount applies. Alderpersons Martins, Trueblood, and Hoenecke raised questions about transparency and the impact on residents' budgets but no amendment was offered. The motion failed 4-6, meaning the full pilot amount of nearly $2 million will apply to the utility.

### File 98-0707 — Comprehensive Purchasing Card Policy
Alderperson Trueblood, who had asked this item be pulled from the consent agenda, said the new policy does not substantively change oversight and questioned whether it was a knee-jerk response to media reporting on credit card misuse. Mayor Diny and Finance Director Groeschel said the old policy dated to the late 1990s and that the update tightens tax-exempt use, adds prohibited purchase categories, and creates a lost-receipt form. Alderperson Hoenecke said he could not support the policy as written because it lacked explicit language on who approves department-head purchases, did not specify audit frequency, and had consequences he considered insufficiently firm. Alderperson Wiskowski asked about training for staff and was told a meeting with HR was scheduled for the following Monday. Alderpersons Killian and Tierney asked for more specificity and consistent discipline across departments. Alderperson Slonski noted that using credit cards in lieu of purchase orders is standard nonprofit practice and that the city carries no balance — the cards are paid monthly. The motion passed 6-4.

### File 26-1113 — Property Disposition: 108 Myron Street
Alderperson Killian, who supported the item at committee, said she wanted to flag serious safety concerns about the high levels of black mold inside the structure. She said Marathon County Health has no specific mold regulations like those for asbestos, but the contamination level warrants extreme precautions for demolition workers and neighbors. She said the property should be demolished, not sold. No other discussion was recorded. The motion passed 10-0.

### File 26-1119 — Deed Restriction and Non-Conforming Uses at 1300 Cleveland Avenue
Four residents spoke during public comment asking for the deed restriction on 1300 Cleveland to include timelines for the city to stop industrial uses. At council, Alderperson Tierney asked why the resolution still allowed DPW training on a property promised for non-industrial use. Economic Development Director Randy Fifick said the EPA assessment is due in March and that a future RFP process is coming, but the deed restriction is meant to lock in non-industrial use now. Alderperson Killian then moved an amendment adding deadlines: DPW to cease training by February 1, 2027, and the Parks Department to find a new location for timber logs and chips by May 1, 2027. DPW's Dustin confirmed their equipment is already off the property and the February deadline works. Parks Director Jamie said she would comply if directed, though it may cost more. The amendment passed 10-0. The amended resolution passed 9-1. Alderperson Larson expressed concern that the deed restriction was unnecessary given existing residential zoning and said he had hoped for a compromise allowing temporary log storage during emerald ash borer removal work.

### File 25-1109G — 2026 Budget Modification
Finance Director Groeschel briefly described the item as a year-end true-up adjusting the budget based on actual projections. No discussion was recorded. The motion passed 10-0.

### File 02-0430 — District 9 Aldermanic Vacancy
The council first voted 10-0 to suspend its rules so this item — which had not gone through committee — could be heard. Alderperson Tierney is resigning; her term runs through the end of December. Alderperson Neal moved to fill the seat by council appointment until a special election could be held in April. Several alderpersons — including Martins, Wiskowski, Killian, Hoenecke, and Lukens — argued against an appointment, saying it could give one candidate a council-endorsed advantage and that the district could be served by neighboring alderpersons in the interim. Alderperson Martins then moved to amend by striking the appointment and going straight to a special election; that amendment passed 6-4. The amended motion — holding only a special election, with nomination papers circulating December 1 through January 5 and an April election — passed 9-1. The city clerk noted a February primary would cost roughly $10,000 if three or more candidates file, but that cost would be shared if other races also need a February primary. Multiple alderpersons expressed gratitude to Tierney for her service.

### Closed Session — City of Wausau v. Georgia-Pacific LLC
Alderperson Lukens read the motion to adjourn to closed session under Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(g), which allows closed meetings to confer with legal counsel about litigation strategy. The purpose was to consider a settlement offer in the federal case City of Wausau v. Georgia-Pacific LLC et al., Case No. 3:25-CV-0000004, in the US District Court for the Western District of Wisconsin. The roll call vote passed unanimously.

## Public Comment
Four residents spoke about the city-owned property at 1300 Cleveland Avenue. The first speaker said the property is zoned residential, has a deed restriction already, and asked the council to fix the resolution because it is too open-ended and sets no timeline for city departments to vacate. A second speaker thanked the council for moving toward non-industrial land use and asked that the deed restriction include language barring any future industrial use and set realistic timelines — 6 or 12 months — for DPW and parks to leave the site. The third speaker, a nearby resident, said she supports the deed restriction as written but would like timelines added; she noted the DNR confirmed contamination in July 2025 and the remediation case remains open. A fourth speaker said his children live near the property and asked the council to set a timeline so cleanup is done before his children turn 18.

## Action Items
- Pilot payment (payment in lieu of taxes) for Wausau Waterworks failed — full PSC-calculated amount of just under $2 million now applies to the utility for 2026
- Purchasing card policy approved; Finance Director to meet with HR on December 1 to plan staff training rollout; finance staff will begin auditing card transactions periodically
- 108 Myron Street approved for acquisition, demolition, and placement in the property disposition program with safety precautions for mold
- Deed restriction approved for 1300 Cleveland Avenue barring industrial use; DPW must cease training on the property by February 1, 2027; Parks Department must find a new location for timber logs and chips by May 1, 2027
- 2026 budget modification adopted based on year-end projections
- Special election ordered for District 9 to fill Alderperson Tierney's vacancy; nomination papers circulate December 1 through January 5; April election with potential February primary if three or more candidates file; no interim appointment
- Council moved to closed session to consider a settlement offer in City of Wausau v. Georgia-Pacific LLC, US District Court Western District of Wisconsin
