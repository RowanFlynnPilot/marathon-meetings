## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Finance Committee met on September 22, 2026, and took action on several significant items including approving a budget modification for the 700 Grand Avenue apartment development (2-1), recommending the city fully fund lead service line replacements for the remainder of the program, and advancing a feasibility study for converting the W. Stevens Point campus into a public safety building. The committee also tabled a proposed increase to dog and cat licensing fees pending more information from Marathon County, and voted to enter closed session to discuss a potential purchase of county highway department property.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment — Pet Licensing Fees
Jamie Hoover, 4023 Brierwood, Wausau, addressed the committee opposing the proposed increase to dog and cat license fees. Hoover argued the 66.67% proposed increase for spayed/neutered animals was excessive, compared Wausau's proposed $25 fee unfavorably to larger cities like Appleton ($6), Milwaukee ($12), and Oshkosh ($5), and noted Marathon County does not charge for cat licenses while Wausau does. Hoover also raised broader concerns about cumulative fee increases on residents including the wheel tax, water costs, and storm water fees, and warned that higher fees would discourage licensing compliance.

### 700 Grand Avenue Development Agreement and Tax Increment Grant — Multi-Year Budget Modification
Patrick presented the budget modification for the apartment project at Grand Avenue and Thomas Street, noting the project was nearing completion ahead of the anticipated December 31, 2026 deadline, with certificate of occupancy expected the same day or next. Alder Tierney objected to approving the budget modification before final paperwork and invoices were in hand, comparing it to closing on a house without documents, and suggested waiting two weeks. Staff clarified the action was only a budget modification — not authorizing a payment — and that the city would not cut a check until all compliance documentation was verified. Tierney maintained her objection; the motion passed 2-1 with Tierney opposed.

### Budget Amendment for W. Stevens Point Campus — Public Safety Building Feasibility Study
Chief Barnes presented a $50,000 budget amendment for feasibility studies to evaluate converting the W. Stevens Point campus into a combined police and fire public safety facility. He explained the item had previously failed at Common Council 6-1 with one abstention and three absences, in part because a member's abstention functionally counted as a no with only eight members present. Barnes noted the city's proposal to Marathon County for the RFP process was generating interest, with meetings held with County Administrator Lance Leonard, and that the $50,000 would cover architectural and engineering studies, including quotes of $25,000 from FJMA Architects and $15,000 from Five Bugles. Alder Watson expressed support for proceeding with due diligence, and the motion passed unanimously.

### Lead Service Line Replacement — Options for Private-Side Costs
Community Infrastructure Partners (CIP) presented a detailed financial overview showing Wausau has replaced over 3,500 lead service lines and is the top per-capita replacer in the nation. Presenters warned that construction costs have risen 56% over two years and recommended completing the remaining 2,800–2,900 lines by 2028 to lock in quarter-percent interest loans and principal forgiveness grants, potentially saving $17–$37 million versus waiting until the 2037 EPA deadline. Three options were presented: Option A (city covers to 75% of cost, homeowners pay gap), Option B (flat $1,000 homeowner charge), and Option C (city covers all). Alder Watson moved for Option C — city covers full cost for the remainder of the program — citing equity concerns and the low interest rate available. Alder Tierney agreed, noting it was unfair to charge residents based on neighborhood. The motion passed unanimously.

### Procurement Policy Amendment — Suspension and Debarment Verification
Finance Director Maryanne explained that the city received audit findings in 2023 and 2024 for failing to properly document suspension and debarment checks required for federal grants. The issue stemmed from inconsistent processes and staff turnover, particularly at Metro Ride transit. No vendors were actually found to be debarred — the finding was procedural. The proposed policy amendment adds mandatory documentation steps, with additional technology fixes planned including a Workday workflow prompt and a SAM.gov API integration to auto-check vendors before payment. The committee approved the amendment unanimously with no significant debate.

### Resolution of Public Depositories
This was a housekeeping item to update the resolution by removing former City Clerk Caitlyn and adding new City Clerk Rachel. The motion was approved unanimously without discussion.

### Dog and Cat License Fee Increases
Staff proposed restoring the original 2026 fee increase proposal — raising the spayed/neutered dog/cat fee from $23 to $30 and reducing the microchip discount from $8 to $5, for a net fee of $25 — after the council had amended it last year. Chief Barnes explained the animal control fund has never been self-sustaining, with the cat contract with the Marathon County Humane Society costing $37,000 in 2025. Alder Tierney said she could not support such a large jump and suggested a $2–$3 increase instead. Alder Watson questioned whether Marathon County had yet set its own fees for the coming year, noting the city's action could be premature; staff confirmed they had not yet heard from the county. The committee agreed by consensus to postpone the item to the next meeting pending confirmation of Marathon County's fee schedule.

### Offer to Purchase County Highway Department Property — 1430, 1308, and 1212 West Street
The committee briefly acknowledged this item in open session before moving to close session, as the real estate negotiation required confidential deliberation under Wisconsin State Statute 19.85(1)(e). A roll call vote was taken to enter closed session; all three members voted in favor. The chair indicated it was unlikely the committee would reconvene in open session to take action that evening.
