## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Finance and HR Committee met on November 16 and took action on accounts receivable write-offs and 2026 budget amendments, both of which were approved unanimously for forwarding to the village board. The committee deadlocked 3-3 on a motion to recommend Option G for sanitary sewer rate increases, with the item deferred to the December 21 meeting for updated financial figures.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
One resident, identified as Jim, spoke about the budget amendments on page 86, questioning $20,000 and $6,000 line items for computer equipment and asking what specific equipment was being purchased and whether purchases had already been made. He also questioned the $20,000 allocated to fix the boardroom audio system, noting the audio has not worked properly since the data system was installed over five years ago and arguing it was improper to budget funds for a project without a contract or firm price tag.

### Approval of Minutes
Minutes from the previous meeting were approved without discussion. A motion was made and seconded; the vote was unanimous in favor.

### Work Product Transmittals: October Budget Status Reports, TIF 1 and TIF 2, Legal Details
Committee members reviewed the reports with limited questions. One member asked whether refuse and recycling invoices were paid to date and was told there is a one-month billing delay. Another member asked about a $240 charge for a pet open records request on top of a prior $1,560 charge, and staff confirmed it was a timing error and should be the final billing. A question was raised about when salaries would move from TIF funds back to the general fund; staff said the TIF closure audit is still in progress with no definitive date. The transmittals were acknowledged and approved unanimously.

### Accounts Receivable Write-Off Transfers to Tax Roll
Staff presented a list of unpaid accounts receivable to be written off and placed on the property tax roll for collection. Committee members raised questions about the village's billing policy, noting that an older 2018 policy called for three reminder statements and 1.5% monthly interest accrual, while the current 2020 policy calls for only one additional statement. Staff acknowledged the interest accrual provision has not been followed and recommended updating the policy to reflect actual practice. One committee member noted that a recently listed debtor had personally called the individual, who paid that morning after being informed of the bill. The committee approved the write-offs as presented and directed staff to bring the accounts receivable policy back for review at a future meeting.

### 2026 Budget Amendments
Staff presented budget amendments, explaining that many line items in the 40-series funds reflect projects already approved through debt borrowing and are being formally coded into accounts. Questions were raised about carryover items from 2025 including server equipment, Kennedy Park work, and road projects not completed before winter. A committee member asked about the $20,000 boardroom audio improvement; staff acknowledged estimates coming in are higher than $20,000 and that the figure was a placeholder to signal commitment to the project. Staff also explained that pickle ball court funds were reallocated after a planned partnership with a pickle ball organization fell through, and that a Mach Miller master plan including pickle ball courts would be presented to the board that same evening. The amendments were approved unanimously with a recommendation to the village board.

### Sanitary Sewer Rates
Staff and committee engaged in extended discussion of eight rate increase options (A through H) for 2027 sewer rates, driven by rising costs from the Rib Mountain Metropolitan Sewage District, which accounts for roughly 50% of the sewer budget and is mid-construction on a capital project financed by a 20-year clean water fund loan. The Public Works and Utility Committee had recommended Option F, which would generate an estimated net income of $318,000 in 2027. Several committee members expressed preference for Option G as a middle ground, while others questioned whether the financial projections were reliable given discrepancies between staff narrative and the income statement figures. A motion was made to recommend Option G; the motion failed on a 3-3 tie vote. The committee reached consensus to defer the item to the December 21 meeting so staff could return with reconciled and updated financial figures.
