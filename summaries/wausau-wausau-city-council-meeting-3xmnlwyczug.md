## Meeting Overview
The Wausau City Council held its July 14, 2026 regular meeting, featuring proclamations for the Daughters of the American Revolution centennial, the Air Venture Cup race weekend, and a certificate of achievement for a young aviator. Major decisions included approval of a data center zoning ordinance with an added 200-foot residential setback amendment, approval of a planning option for a North Second Street development with Eminent Development Corporation, and approval of the Riverlife South term sheet with Wangard Partners for a 143-unit market-rate residential development on the former Hammerblow site — the latter passing 8-3 after significant debate over TIF incentives and public opposition.

## Key Discussions
### Proclamations and Certificate of Achievement
Mayor Diny presented a proclamation declaring July 19, 2026 as Wausau Chapter Daughters of the American Revolution 100th Anniversary Celebration Day, accepted by chapter representatives. A second proclamation declared July 17-19, 2026 as Air Venture Cup Race Weekend; a representative noted over 120 aircraft expected from 29 states plus international participants in a roughly 430-mile race. Mayor Diny also presented a Certificate of Achievement to Hazel Schaefer, a 10-year-old EAA Chapter 640 member selected as the 2.5 millionth Young Eagle flight applicant, who has logged 42 hours in 35 aircraft types.

### Ordinance 26-0706: Data Center Zoning Regulations
Pulled from the consent agenda at public request, this ordinance creating a new zoning section regulating data centers was debated extensively. Assistant City Planner Carrie Edmonds explained the ordinance requires conditional use permits and disclosure of water use, energy demand, wastewater, noise, and sustainability impacts, guided by five findings-of-fact criteria under Act 67. Alderperson Watson moved to amend the ordinance to add a 200-foot minimum setback from residential areas, which passed 8-3 after debate; Alderpersons Trueblood and Wiskowski expressed preference for passing the base ordinance now and revisiting it in a few months rather than amending on the fly, while Martens and Lukens expressed support for the amendment. The ordinance as amended then passed 11-0. Trueblood later moved to schedule a formal revisit of the data center ordinance at the second council meeting in September, which was accepted without objection.

### Ordinance 26-0705: Rezoning 1010 South 16th Avenue to PUD for Brass Properties LLC
Alderperson Kilian stated he would not support the rezoning because all residents who spoke at the June 16 Plan Commission public hearing, plus a written submission, opposed it. Alderperson Martens said he supported it and praised the downward-facing lighting plan for dark sky compatibility. The rezoning passed 9-2.

### Resolution 26-0710: Planning Option with Eminent Development Corporation
Economic Development Director Patrick Gatorman explained the planning option allows Eminent Development Corporation to conduct due diligence on city-owned parcels at North Second Street and East Bridge Street for a proposed 30-50 unit mixed-use development; no land price or subsidy is negotiated at this stage. Alderperson Kilian opposed even the planning option, stating she believed the developer intended to seek land valued at $540,000 for one dollar. Alderpersons Trueblood and Martens raised concerns about density given nearby apartment projects and suggested townhouses or duplexes might better fit the neighborhood. Alderperson Wiskowski asked about prior community engagement and requested that community feedback be included in future packets. Alderperson Hoenecke, as ED committee chair, supported the option citing the city's difficulty attracting development interest to long-vacant properties. The motion passed 7-4.

### Suspension of Rules (Rule 12A)
A motion to suspend rules to allow items to come directly to the council was made by Neal and seconded by Slonski. Alderperson Trueblood questioned whether all items had gone through standard process, but the mayor noted staff had already narrowed six potential suspension requests down to those truly needed. City Attorney Ann Jacobson clarified suspension of rules is generally not debatable. The suspension passed 8-3.

### Resolution: Bargaining Agreement with Amalgamated Transit Union Local 1168
A joint resolution from the Human Resources and Finance committees approving a bargaining agreement with ATU Local 1168 for July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2028 was brought under suspension of rules after passing unanimously in both committees. No council discussion was recorded. The resolution passed 11-0.

### Resolution 26-0713: Firefighter Paramedic Apprenticeship Program
The fire chief explained the 18-month apprenticeship program is funded entirely by a $3.2 million federal grant through a partnership with Northcentral Technical College; apprentices are limited-term city employees and the city bears minimal financial risk. Alderperson Wiskowski provided context that the HR committee had not approved it at the prior meeting due to unresolved questions about benefits, which were since addressed in the MOU. Alderpersons Watson and Trueblood praised the program and the community partnerships involved. Trueblood asked why items reach a 'must-decide-now' status; the mayor noted the submission deadline had already passed. The program was approved 11-0.

### Resolution 26-0708: Riverlife South Term Sheet with Wangard Partners
Dave Ryel of Wangard Partners presented a proposal for 143 market-rate residential units on the former Hammerblow site at 920 North First Street and 15 Fulton, requiring $2 million in environmental remediation funded by the developer. Financial consultant Keith from Ellers explained the city negotiated the assistance down from an original ask of $9.4 million over 20 years at 99% increment return to 80% of TIF increment over 17 years, with the land sold for $100,000. Alderpersons Kilian and Tierney opposed the deal, citing widespread public opposition and concerns about selling assessed $1.4 million land for $100,000 and granting $4.84 million in additional incentives. Alderpersons Neal, Lukens, Slonski, and Trueblood ultimately supported it, noting the land produces zero tax revenue, soil remediation costs make it unattractive to other developers, and the assistance is performance-based. Watson moved to call the question, which passed 10-0 with one abstention. The term sheet was approved 8-3.

## Public Comment
Five speakers addressed the council. A property management company president spoke in favor of the Wangard Riverlife South development, saying it checks all the boxes as a proven developer and wise low-risk project despite creating rental competition for her company. A resident spoke on the data center ordinance, supporting the creation of regulations but asking whether the ordinance provides clear standards for evaluating applications, what protections exist for taxpayers on infrastructure costs, and requesting the item be pulled from the consent agenda for full discussion. A written comment from a resident (read into the record) criticized the council's continued approval of apartment developments while ignoring low-income housing needs, cited Marathon County as among the worst in the state for low-income housing, and asked Mayor Diny not to rubber stamp spending proposals. A resident spoke on data centers, stating he would support an outright ban if legally possible, expressed concern that AI poses an existential threat, and asked the council to consider a 200-foot setback from residential areas given limited local discretion under Act 67. A resident spoke on the data center ordinance, calling it ludicrous that such an important item was placed on the consent agenda, arguing the regulations do not go far enough, and warning that data center developments benefit companies at residents' expense. During post-business public comment, the resident who had spoken on data centers returned to critique TIF policy, arguing Wausau's use of TIF since the 1980s has failed to deliver promised financial benefits and contributed to fiscal problems. A second resident spoke during post-business comment urging the council to consider green space and community gardens as alternatives for vacant city parcels. The fire chief also spoke during post-business comment to clarify a media report about police overtime and officer training time banks.

- Bargaining agreement with ATU Local 1168 (July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2028) approved
