## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Infrastructure and Facilities Committee met on Thursday, October 8, 2026, and took action on several significant items, most notably voting to amend the city's Department of Public Works winter maintenance policy to permit snowplow counterflow to traffic on center median boulevard streets, a change sought by residents of Highland Park and the southeast side for three years. The committee also set 2027 construction assessment rates, approved two alley/street vacations, approved a painted curb extension project downtown, and received updates on 2026 construction projects and the 2027-2028 capital infrastructure plan.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment – Counterflow Snowplowing on Boulevard Streets
Ten speakers addressed the committee urging a return to plowing snow toward center medians on Highland Park Boulevard and Oaklair Boulevard on the southeast side, a practice changed without public notice approximately three winters ago. Speakers cited safety concerns for elderly, disabled, and other vulnerable residents, challenged DPW's cost and legal justifications, and praised a city attorney memo confirming that Wisconsin Statute 346.05(2) permits snowplow counterflow to traffic during active maintenance. Alderperson Carol Lukens (District 1) spoke formally in support of the residents, citing the legal memo and asking the committee to recommend forwarding the matter to the full council by October 13. Alderperson Tom Neal (District 4) also spoke during public comment, stating he would support the measure when it reaches the full council.

### Consent Agenda – Authorizing Downtown Snow/Ice Removal
The consent agenda item authorizing downtown snow and ice removal was moved by Alderperson Slonski and seconded by Alderperson Wiskowski. No discussion was held and it was approved unanimously by voice vote.

### Approval of Minutes
Alderperson Wiskowski moved to approve the minutes of the preceding meeting, noting they were 'very thorough.' The motion was seconded and approved unanimously by voice vote.

### Vacating and Discontinuing Alley Bounded by Lake View Drive, Bertha Street, Elmwood Boulevard, and Midway Boulevard
The committee discussed vacating this alley, with city engineering staff noting there is a sanitary sewer in the alley requiring the city to retain an easement before the vacation is finalized. A friendly amendment was added to the motion to include language retaining the easement for sewer purposes, accepted by the mover and seconder. The amended motion passed unanimously by voice vote.

### Vacating and Discontinuing 134-Foot Southerly Extension of Liberty Ridge Way
A representative speaking on behalf of the property owners supported the vacation, stating the owners wish to treat the dead-end extension as a private drive with security and signage. City engineering staff noted sewer and water infrastructure in the extension requiring easement retention for both utilities. Alderperson Hoenecke moved approval contingent on the city retaining necessary easements; Alderperson Slonski seconded after confirming engineering had no opposition. A committee member asked clarifying questions confirming the rest of Liberty Ridge Way would remain a public road; only the short southerly dog-leg segment is being vacated. The motion passed unanimously.

### Assessment Rate for 2027 Construction Projects
City engineering staff presented the current 2026 rates ($55/accessible foot for roadway, 100% of actual cost for driveway approaches, 50% for new sidewalk, no assessment for replacement sidewalk, $500 per sewer lateral) and suggested raising the sewer lateral assessment to $1,000, noting the actual cost to the city runs $1,500–$2,500 per lateral. Alderperson Wiskowski asked about cost trends, learning construction costs have nearly tripled over 20 years. Alderperson Hoenecke questioned the logic of mixing dollar amounts and percentages; staff explained fixed per-foot rates allow homeowners to know their assessment before bids. Alderperson Slonski advocated for more frequent, smaller incremental increases rather than large jumps. Alderperson Martens moved to approve the 2027 rates with street reconstruction raised to $60 per accessible foot (from $55), along with the proposed sewer lateral increase to $1,000; the motion passed unanimously.

### Winter Street Maintenance Policy – Counterflow Plowing
Alderperson Slonski moved to amend the DPW winter maintenance policy to permit snowplow counterflow to traffic whenever otherwise safe to do so; the motion was seconded. City attorney Ann Jacobson and DPW Director Dustin clarified the legal framework: Wisconsin Statute 346.05(2) allows counterflow plowing on two-way streets (divided or undivided by a median) during active maintenance, but not on one-way streets. DPW staff explained the policy change three years ago stemmed from a misinterpretation of state patrol guidance that was intended for one-way streets. Alderperson Wiskowski expressed frustration that cost and time comparison data requested at the prior meeting was not provided; DPW staff explained the difficulty of producing estimates without clear directional guidance. Staff confirmed plow drivers would be protected by city liability policy as long as they operate within the amended policy, and that internal guidance to drivers would be drafted. The motion passed unanimously. The committee also discussed recommending the item be placed on the October 13 city council agenda.

### West Hill First Edition Preliminary Plat
Staff presented the preliminary plat for a large development on West Hill by developer Sid Sorenson, noting it had been reviewed and approved at the staff level by police, fire, the city planner, public works director, and water/sewer. Alderperson Hoenecke moved to approve; Alderperson Wiskowski seconded. The motion passed unanimously with no significant discussion.

### Approving Painted Curb Extension Project – Wausau River District
Staff presented the River District's proposal for painted curb extensions using plastic bollards and road paint at the intersection of Fourth and Scott Streets downtown to improve pedestrian visibility and safety. The project was previously approved by the Bike and Pedestrian Committee. Alderperson Slonski moved to approve; Alderperson Martens seconded. Alderperson Martens noted the bollards are removable in winter and praised the traffic-calming benefits. Alderperson Wiskowski suggested the committee explore painted street treatments at additional downtown intersections, referring that idea to the Bike and Ped Committee. Alderperson Hoenecke asked about the River District's six-year commitment to cover bollard replacement costs and confirmed verbal assurances from the Center for Visual Arts regarding low- or no-cost repainting by local artists. The motion passed unanimously.

### 2027-2028 Capital Infrastructure Projects Discussion
Staff noted alternate funding sources for some projects, including $800,000 in ELRI funds for a 17th Avenue project. Alderperson Hoenecke raised concerns about the city's borrowing trajectory and proposed exploring reductions of $4–5 million in capital borrowing to avoid adding to debt; he stated he would work with Director Lynman to identify potentially deferrable projects. Alderperson Martens flagged that the drinking water treatment facility solar array project ($1.98 million) had effectively been removed after the city failed to receive a U.S. EPA grant, and noted that a downtown street traffic flow design ($150,000 from TID 12) was still under discussion. He cautioned against deferring road reconstructions, citing constituent expectations and the ripple effect on the five-year plan.

### 2026 Construction Projects Update
Staff reported that the downtown utility project (Second Street and Division Street reconstruction), Grant Street utility upgrades, and Stark Street reconstruction (5th to 12th) are all 100% complete. Eighth Avenue reconstruction is approximately 90% complete with final paving expected the week of October 19. Asphalt overlay streets, the West Wausau DOT/STB project, and four sidewalk projects are complete. Concrete rehabilitation work is ongoing downtown around the mall and First Street. The riverfront parking lot by Athletic Park is nearly complete, with paving expected the following week. Staff noted this is one of the earliest completion years on record. Discussion also touched on lead service line replacement coordination, website communication improvements, and the need for a simple public-facing guide to road repair processes; Alderperson Slonski provided the DPW pothole hotline number on the record as 715-261-6960.

## Public Comment
Ten speakers addressed the committee. A resident of Everest Boulevard in Highland Park argued for a reasonable compromise on counterflow snowplowing, citing Wisconsin statutes, challenging DPW's cost and safety justifications, and noting no accident data tied to the prior plowing method had been presented. Two residents identifying themselves as Scott Johnson and Michael Johnson, of Everest Boulevard, spoke as advocates for elderly, disabled, and otherwise vulnerable neighbors who struggle with large snow windrows left in driveways under the current plowing approach. Alderperson Tom Neal (District 4), giving his address as 916 Hamilton Street, stated he would support the petition when it reaches the full council. A resident of the first 100 block of Oaklair Boulevard described specific access and safety concerns at her property due to a shared driveway, a split boulevard, and a neighboring gas station; she asked the committee to send the matter to the full council for a vote. Alderperson Lou Larson (District 10), giving his address as 904 South 21st Place, challenged the consistency of DPW's information over prior meetings, disputed claims about CDL liability and equipment costs, and questioned why the policy changed without council or mayoral notice. A resident of Oaklair Boulevard thanked Alderperson Wiskowski for his responsiveness and asked the committee to pass a motion directing DPW to plow to the center median and send the item to full council. A resident of Oaklair Boulevard, identifying herself as Karen Briggs, described 51 years living on the street with a shared driveway, urged the committee to pass the proposal and invited members to visit the southeast side in winter to experience the conditions firsthand. A resident of Elmwood Boulevard spoke in support of both the counterflow plowing petition and the alley vacation bounded by Lake View Drive, Bertha Street, Elmwood Boulevard, and Midway Boulevard. A resident of Everest Boulevard in Highland Park criticized the absence of verified data from DPW and called for real data to inform the committee's decision. A resident of Highland Park Boulevard presented a data-driven summary arguing that counterflow plowing is safer, has worked for 50 years without recorded incident, is confirmed legal by the city attorney, and that the worst-case DPW cost estimate of $6,000 per year is far less than the $17,000–$31,000 estimated cost of maintaining median sidewalks. Alderperson Carol Lukens (District 1), giving her address as 725 Ethel Street, spoke formally in support of the residents, cited the city attorney memo confirming the legality of counterflow plowing under Wisconsin Statute 346.05(2), and asked the committee to amend the winter maintenance policy and forward the recommendation to the October 13 council meeting.

## Action Items
- Winter maintenance policy amended to permit snowplow counterflow to traffic on center median boulevard streets whenever otherwise safe; committee discussed recommending placement on the October 13 city council agenda
- 2027 construction assessment rates set: street reconstruction at $60 per accessible foot, driveway approaches at 100% of actual cost, new sidewalk at 50% of actual cost, replacement sidewalk at no assessment, sewer lateral replacement at $1,000
- Alley bounded by Lake View Drive, Bertha Street, Elmwood Boulevard, and Midway Boulevard approved for vacation with city retaining sanitary sewer easement; city attorney's office to prepare easement document
- 134-foot southerly extension of Liberty Ridge Way approved for vacation with city retaining sewer and water easements
- Painted curb extension project at Fourth and Scott Streets approved, with River District responsible for bollard replacement costs through 2032
- West Hill First Edition Preliminary Plat approved
- Staff directed to include DPW pothole hotline (715-261-6960) and road construction information in next city newsletter
- List of vacant, disused, or unplanned city properties to be placed on the next committee agenda

- Standing committee descriptions to be brought back at next meeting

- Alderperson Wiskowski's suggestion to explore additional painted street treatments downtown referred to the Bike and Pedestrian Committee
