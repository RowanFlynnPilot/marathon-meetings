# Wausau Finance Committee Budget Session #2

**Organization:** City of Wausau  
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-npwmRTKxi8  
**Summarized:** 2026-10-08 15:36 UTC

## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Finance Committee held its second budget session for the 2027 budget, hearing presentations from the police, fire, transit, and general government departments. The session was dominated by a lengthy debate over Flock automated license plate recognition cameras, with several alderpersons expressing concern about civil liberties and data privacy while Police Chief defended the technology and its contractual obligations through 2028. No formal votes were taken during this session; budget amendments were held for later consolidation.

## Key Discussions
### Police Department 2027 Budget
Police Chief presented a largely cost-to-continue budget with targeted savings totaling approximately $150,000, including: eliminating the contract with All City Management Services for crossing guards (saving ~$40,000–$45,000), cutting the CSO budget by roughly half (~$45,000), reducing the non-dog animal control (cat contract) budget from $37,000 to $18,500, and pursuing a renegotiated school resource officer contract with the school district at 100% district-funded cost for the school year (projected new revenue of $56,000). The department will also reduce sworn positions from 81 to 80 following expiration of Jed Alexander Foundation funding for downtown beat officers, while repurposing a patrol officer to continue the two-officer downtown program.

### Flock ALPR Camera Debate
Alder Tom Neil called for removing the $50,000 Flock camera line item from the budget, citing concerns about corporate data practices and residents' distrust of the company. Alder Hani echoed concerns about Fourth Amendment implications and the potential for abuse of dragnet surveillance, though both alderpersons stated they trusted the current department leadership. The Police Chief defended Flock vigorously, stating the city's contract runs through December 31, 2028 with no exit-for-convenience clause, that data is city-owned and cannot be sold to third parties, that images are deleted after 30 days, and that exiting the contract could expose the city to financial liability. Mayor Doug Diny supported keeping the cameras, noting Wausau's policy is among the best in the state. Alder Tierney requested the department's written Flock policy be shared with all council members. The Police Chief noted a second public presentation to the Public Health and Safety Committee is scheduled for October 19th.

### Animal Control Fee Amendment
Alder Watson proposed a budget amendment to reduce the 2027 pet license fee for spayed/neutered dogs or cats to $25 (from $30), with a $10 microchip discount, and fund any resulting revenue shortfall from the animal control fund balance of approximately $146,000. Finance Director Maryanne clarified that the fund balance is separate from approximately $41,954 held in a liability account owed to the county. Watson expressed intent to use the coming year to gather data on the program and have a deeper policy discussion about whether animal control should be partially levy-supported rather than fully user-fee funded. The amendment was tabled for later consolidation with other budget amendments.

### Fire Department 2027 Budget
Fire Deputy Chief presented a budget relying on modest cuts across multiple line items — including eliminating the FDIC national conference travel, cutting psychological services, reducing software subscriptions, and proposing to purchase a robotic mower (~$10,000 savings) to eliminate the mowing contract at Station 2. The operations division chief position will remain vacant through July 1, 2027, and a retiring 40-hour employee's position will also be left open for six months to meet budget targets. The Chief noted the department is currently down six members from full staff and that overtime is a growing concern. A presentation on making the community care paramedic position permanent is forthcoming to the HR and Finance committees, with Aspirus verbally committing to continue funding support after the three-year trial ends in 2027.

### Transit (Metro Ride) 2027 Budget
Transit Director Ron (approximately six months in the role) presented a budget reduced by $81,635 through historical spending analysis and adjustments. Key drivers of savings include 10 new buses replacing 2009-era vehicles, which will dramatically reduce fuel and maintenance costs as most of the fleet will be under warranty. The CAD AVL system cost increase will be fully covered by FTA ARPA funds through 2028, but will create a significant budget increase after 2029. A forthcoming UMO Wisco fare payment system, not yet before the council, would add operating costs but is projected to increase ridership. The FTA's indirect cost reimbursement rate increased from 10% to 15%, generating roughly $500,000 in general fund revenue that offsets the transit levy.

### General Government Departments 2027 Budget
The city attorney's office and other general government departments presented small budgets with limited flexibility, primarily consisting of personnel, dues, licensing fees, and court-related costs. One assistant position in the city attorney's office remains frozen and unfilled. The office noted it recently left Westlaw, its online legal subscription service, as a cost-saving measure. Specific dollar amounts for general government savings were not detailed in the portion of the transcript captured.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. The chair noted no members of the public were present.
