## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Police & Fire Commission met on September 28, 2026, conducting organizational business including electing a new vice president and secretary after Commissioner Tom Garver's departure from the commission. The meeting featured substantive updates from Fire Deputy Chief Justin and Police Chief Barnes covering apprenticeship hiring, budget constraints, the proposed UW campus acquisition, and the department's Flock license plate recognition camera system.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment – Tom Garver Farewell
Outgoing Commissioner Tom Garver, of 4620 Huntington Court, addressed the commission to mark his departure, citing 'circumstances beyond our control' requiring him to leave. He praised the commission's culture of hiring for character, commended department leadership, and expressed confidence in newly appointed commissioners. The chair noted Garver was recently inducted into the Army Sports Hall of Fame in September for his hockey career at West Point, where he holds the program's all-time wins record with 75 victories.

### Election of Commission Vice President
With Garver's departure creating a vacancy in the vice president role, Commissioner Lisa nominated Veta Perkins (referred to as 'V') for vice president. Another commissioner moved to close nominations, and the motion to elect Perkins as VP passed unanimously. The chair also appointed Lisa as commission secretary without a formal vote, stating it was an appointment rather than an election.

### Approval of August 24 Meeting Minutes
The chair asked for corrections to the August 24 minutes. None were raised. A motion to approve was made and seconded, passing unanimously with no discussion.

### Fire Department Monthly Update
Fire Deputy Chief Justin reported nine new apprentices starting that day from a pool of 45 applicants, calling the field strong. He noted six current vacancies in the department; seven candidates were interviewed for firefighter-paramedic positions but only two will advance to the PFC for approval, leaving four vacancies remaining. He also highlighted new scheduling software intended to reduce administrative burden on battalion chiefs and reduce mandatory overtime, new training software in development, a successful DEA audit of the department's controlled substances practices led by Division Chief Matt Tormolan, and the widely publicized rescue of two occupants of a small aircraft that made an emergency water landing in Lake Wausau after dark, one of whom was later identified as a gubernatorial candidate.

### Police Department Monthly Update
Chief Barnes reported that a recent police hiring process drew 40 applicants after targeted social media advertising in Wisconsin, and described the preliminary interview pool as the best the department has seen. He said the department anticipates hiring eight or nine officers in the next 12 months due to three current openings and five or six expected retirements. On budget, Barnes said the department is leaving two positions unfilled through August to meet required savings, and that the overall authorized FTE count is dropping from 81 to 80 in 2027 as foundation funding for one of two downtown homeless outreach officer positions expires — the city will fund one position and the other will simply not be backfilled. Barnes also updated the commission on the city's proposal to acquire the UW-Wausau campus, noting the city is seeking six months for due diligence and has requested a $50,000 budget modification for expert consultants; a first council vote failed due to lack of quorum and one abstention, but the finance committee has since approved the modification and it will return to the full council. Barnes addressed the Flock license plate recognition camera system, stating the department owns 20 cameras at $50,000 per year on a five-year contract, that Flock does not sell city data, and that data is deleted after 30 days; a formal presentation to the PFC is planned for October or November.

### Deputy Chief Promotional Process
Chief Barnes outlined the timeline for selecting a new deputy chief: letters of interest and resumes were due October 2nd, officer listening sessions with the commission (without Barnes or Fire Chief Todd present) are planned for mid-October, candidate written assignments and interviews with Barnes are set for November 11th, and PFC interviews and a vote are planned for mid-to-late November with a January 17th promotional date. Commissioners discussed whether the PFC interviews could be held during the regular November 23rd meeting; Barnes said it was feasible but noted interviews could take several hours and might require a reduced agenda.

## Public Comment
One speaker offered public comment: outgoing Commissioner Tom Garver, 4620 Huntington Court, who used the public comment period to formally address the commission upon his departure. He praised department leadership, emphasized the importance of hiring for character, and thanked his fellow commissioners.
