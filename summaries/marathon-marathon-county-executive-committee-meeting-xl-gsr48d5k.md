## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Executive Committee met on August 13, 2026, and took two notable votes: forwarding to the full county board a discussion on whether to pursue a Joint Development Agreement (JDA) with Alliant Energy regarding the Hub City Wind project, and approving a reclassification of a senior administrative assistant position in the county administrator's office. The meeting featured extensive public comment and expert testimony on the Hub City Wind project, an update from ice arena user groups on fundraising efforts, and a presentation from Advanced Wisconsin Employment on a new workforce program called Life Works.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of July 15, 2026 executive committee meeting minutes
Chair Gibbs called for a motion on the minutes. Supervisor Robinson moved and Supervisor Morash seconded. There was no discussion and the motion carried unanimously.

### Hub City Wind project — PSC and JDA research presentation by CPZ Director Lori Kimmons
CPZ Director Lori Kimmons presented two staff memos summarizing conversations with PSC staff about the intervention process and outreach to other Wisconsin counties with wind and solar projects. She reported that PSC staff could not recall an instance where intervention resulted in a complete denial of an application, and that counties contacted — including Columbia, Iowa, Kenosha, St. Croix, and the Town of Saratoga in Wood County — broadly favored JDAs over intervention due to cost and time. Staff's overall recommendation was to engage early with the developer and begin JDA discussions.

### Hub City Wind project — Panel discussion with Town of Saratoga Chair Laura Fure, attorney Andy Phillips, and UW-Extension's Bill Omin
Laura Fure, town chair and zoning administrator for the Town of Saratoga, described her community's experience with a solar JDA, noting they wished they had known more upfront about battery storage systems, transmission line easements, and associated dangers. Attorney Andy Phillips explained that under Wisconsin statute 196.491, counties have no authority to override a PSC-issued CPCN for projects 100 megawatts or more, making JDAs the most effective tool for securing local protections. UW-Extension professor Bill Omin agreed with Phillips's legal analysis and noted that the Wisconsin legislature intentionally preempted local authority to promote renewable energy, and that the only effective remaining tools for counties are updating their comprehensive plan and negotiating a JDA. Both experts said moratoriums are legally defensible only if short-term and demonstrably aimed at developing regulations, not blocking a project outright.

### Hub City Wind project — Committee discussion and vote to forward to full county board
Committee members raised a range of questions, including whether Alliant Energy is legally required to enter a JDA (the answer was no), the timeline for JDA negotiations (three to six months if parties are cooperative, but potentially longer), protections against developer insolvency, and whether a moratorium was viable given the county's existing wind ordinance. Supervisor Dickinson expressed skepticism about developer motivations for a JDA, suggesting it was primarily a public relations tool. Supervisor Griner noted the meeting had focused almost entirely on the JDA option without fully exploring alternatives such as moratoriums or comprehensive plan updates. Vice Chair Boots moved, seconded by Supervisor Morash, to forward to the full county board a recommendation to discuss whether to pursue a JDA. The motion passed but was not unanimous.

### Ice arena user groups update — facility planning and fundraising
County Administrator Brian Leonard briefed the committee on staff's engagement with ice arena user groups following December direction, including a meeting with user group representatives and outreach for a FEMA Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities (BRIC) grant application. Chris Toner of the Wausau Community Ice Center described early-stage private fundraising efforts and asked the committee to keep the rinks open while funding is developed, to continue joint grant discussions with staff, and to allow time for the process. Superintendent Bushman, who had to leave, submitted a written statement supporting continuation of the county's ice services relationship with the school district. Central Wisconsin Ice representative Colin Helky and Storm girls hockey representative Jana Whitmer were also present. Supervisor Robinson suggested setting a two-month timeline for a follow-up meeting with milestones, and the committee agreed to that direction without a formal vote.

### Advanced Wisconsin Employment — Life Works program presentation
Workforce Services Director Libby Hidman and colleagues presented the Life Works program, a three-week job readiness training targeting justice-involved individuals, people with disabilities, and other underserved populations, with a first cohort of six participants launching the following Monday. The program includes soft skills, NTC manufacturing training, employer tours, and connections to community resources. Hidman noted that federal Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act funding has declined roughly 40% since 2021, driving the need for alternative funding. The committee directed the organization to follow the standard committee referral process: first to the Extension Education Economic Development Committee, then potentially to HR Finance and Property, and then to the full board.

### New position request — reclassification in County Administrator's office
County Administrator Leonard requested approval of a reclassification of a senior administrative assistant to office manager, citing significant changes to the position resulting from a 2024 ordinance amendment that transferred foreclosure process oversight to his office. He stated the cost increase could be absorbed within his existing budget without new levy. HR Finance and Property had approved the reclassification the prior day. Supervisor Robinson moved and Supervisor Arson seconded; the motion carried unanimously.

## Public Comment
Ten residents signed up and spoke during public comment, each given three minutes. The first speaker, Joe Bergen from the Town of Day, urged the committee to design any JDA carefully with safeguards for the county and raised concerns about tourism impacts from turbines near Rib Mountain. The second speaker, Dan Bar from the Town of Nolton, expressed strong support for wind and solar energy as a response to climate change and endorsed a well-crafted JDA as the best protective tool. The third speaker, a resident, expressed support for reducing greenhouse gas emissions while acknowledging concerns from those living near the project, and called for sharing information and making honest policy tradeoffs. The fourth speaker, a Wausau resident speaking also on behalf of Wisconsin Farmers Union's Marathon County chapter, warned against entering a JDA without sufficient safeguards, characterizing it as giving away county power, and cited the Farmers Union's educational work on rural residents' rights in the energy sector. The fifth speaker, a resident from the Township of Opalme, opposed the project, contending that turbines are industrial rather than agricultural, warning of harm to livestock and farms, and criticizing the county's farmland preservation plan for not addressing turbines or solar. The sixth speaker, Mark Radkey from the Town of Opalme, noted that the power generated by the turbines would not go to central Wisconsin, questioned why turbines could not be sited elsewhere, and stated that the alternative energy advisory committee survey and public forums showed overwhelming opposition to a JDA. The seventh speaker, James Yeetus from the Town of Easton, argued forcefully against the project and urged the county to fight on behalf of its citizens rather than accommodate the developer, citing health, property value, farmland, and landscape concerns. The eighth speaker, Dean Beck from the Town of Easton, raised concerns about turbine efficiency degradation from insect debris, advocated that any utility payments be held in escrow for affected landowners, and urged the committee to use existing treble-damages road statutes rather than relying solely on a JDA. The ninth speaker, Wendy Rogolski from the Town of Green Valley, participated online and asked the committee to ensure that the fundamental policy decision of whether to pursue a JDA be made openly and voted upon publicly rather than decided in closed session, and urged that any closed session be limited strictly to negotiating strategy. The tenth speaker from the Town of Brighton argued against the JDA, cited counties in other states where wind projects were stopped before construction, called for a moratorium, recommended the county apply for intervenor status and hire independent third-party experts, and presented an evidence matrix of 30 potential challenges tied to PSC CPCN requirements.

## Action Items
- Forward to the full county board a recommendation to discuss whether to pursue a Joint Development Agreement with Alliant Energy regarding the Hub City Wind project
- County staff to continue operating ice arenas while fundraising and grant processes proceed
- Staff directed to develop a timeline with milestones for ice arena funding and planning, with a follow-up meeting within two months
- Advanced Wisconsin Employment directed to present Life Works funding request through the Extension Education Economic Development Committee, then HR Finance and Property, then potentially the full board
- Reclassification of senior administrative assistant to office manager in the County Administrator's office approved and to be built into the 2027 budget
- Next Executive Committee meeting scheduled for September 17 at 3 p.m.
