## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Public Works Committee held its regular meeting on November 10, 2026, covering a detailed infiltration and inflow (I&I) study presentation for the Rothschild sewer basin, a sewer rate increase recommendation, and approvals of several intergovernmental agreements and design contracts. Key decisions included recommending Option F for 2027 sewer rates, approving a pedestrian crossing design contract at County Trunk X and Sternberg Avenue, approving a storm pond construction agreement, and approving an intergovernmental agreement with the Town of Weston for Ross Avenue reconstruction.

## Key Discussions
### I&I Study Presentation
Sean Dudy of Inpipe Technology presented results of a 44-day infiltration and inflow study covering approximately 26,000 feet of sewer pipe in the Rothschild area south of Highway 29. The study identified two 'red' priority areas — basin 2 and basin 3 — totaling about 3,300 feet, where groundwater infiltration and rain-driven inflow were highest, with one area showing 500-600% flow increases during rain events. Staff noted they had already identified a low manhole in one hot spot and discussed potential slip lining as a remediation tool. Committee members asked about flow volumes (approximately 1.9 to 5 gallons per minute in problem areas) and whether CCTV inspection had been done; staff confirmed historical televising exists but has not yet targeted these specific areas. Staff framed the study as providing data-driven direction to focus limited maintenance dollars rather than treating the entire neighborhood.

### VIP (Village Improvement Projects) Update
Public Works Director Michael provided updates on numerous active projects: Weston A special assessments are expected for the board next Monday; Scullfield A has concrete work remaining for spring 2027 with Treweiler as subcontractor; Bladl Avenue final paving is complete; Concord and Bayberry paving began the morning of the meeting; Ross Avenue Metro to Alderson project is targeted for February bid; Fuller Street punch list work was completed. The Ross Avenue and County Road X design contract (item 14) was previewed, with committee members raising concerns about insurance and liability limitation language in the consultant contract. Staff confirmed they would align the contract with the village's standard attorney-reviewed template before board execution.

### Street Operations Update
Staff reported completion of storm sewer work at wells 7 and 8 near the golf course, reconstruction of the outfall structure at Kathy Street including a new trash guard to prevent children from entering the pipe, overlay of Heron Street's northbound lane using a combination of contracted RC Pavers equipment and village-hauled asphalt, and replacement of a culvert on Harney Avenue. Fall curbside yard waste and leaf pickup operations were described as requiring at least four DPW staff members daily, with vac trucks averaging 10 loads each at 20 cubic yards per load. A committee member asked staff to quantify the cost savings from using in-house labor alongside contracted paving services; staff agreed to compile those figures for the December meeting, noting two competing bids differed by about $15,000.

### 2027 Sanitary Sewer Rate Policy Discussion and Recommendation
Staff presented updated rate options following the previous month's discussion, noting that simply breaking even (Option A) was not considered viable by committee members. Discussion centered on Options G (projected net income ~$160,000) and F (projected net revenue ~$113,000). One member noted that at average usage of 12,000 gallons per year, the difference between G and F is roughly $5 per quarter or $20 per year per household. Committee members cited the need for funds to support slip lining projects, rising Mountain Metro treatment costs (up nearly $800,000 since 2019), and aging infrastructure including 10 miles of asbestos water main. The committee voted to recommend Option F, with staff noting rates could be lowered in future years if significant surplus developed.

### Sidewalk Snow Removal Policy
Discussion was prompted by a resident letter from someone abutting Fuller Street who is handicapped and cannot clear their sidewalk. Committee members unanimously agreed that creating an individual exception would be unworkable and set a problematic precedent, with one member stating 'if you don't follow it, you just don't even have one really.' The committee voted to make no substantive change to the snow removal policy but directed staff to update the written policy to reflect current actual practices, including newly added paths not previously listed. One member suggested the village newsletter could solicit volunteer 'sidewalk angels' to assist residents in need; staff agreed to include that in the newsletter.

### County Trunk X and Sternberg Avenue Pedestrian Design Contract
Staff explained the village did not receive the TAP (Transportation Alternatives Program) grant it had applied for, meaning pedestrian crossing improvements at County Trunk X and Sternberg Avenue — identified in the West Elementary School Safe Routes to School plan — would now be fully at village expense, estimated at $150,000-$200,000 construction cost with a design fee around $45,000. The improvements include a refuge island and pedestrian-activated warning lights. The committee voted to recommend awarding the design contract, contingent on staff reviewing and aligning the consultant's liability limitation language with the village's standard contract template before execution.

### Business 51 Storm Pond Construction Intergovernmental Agreement
Staff presented a construction-phase intergovernmental agreement for the Highway 29/Business 51 storm water pond shared with the Village of Rothschild and City of Schofield. Staff noted updated hydraulic modeling now shows the pond will remove 225 pounds of phosphorus annually — nearly double the originally estimated 122 pounds — which will delay the need for additional phosphorus removal projects. The village's cost share percentage increases slightly as a result, but staff framed this as favorable overall. The committee voted to recommend the village board approve the agreement.

### Ross Avenue/Town of Weston Intergovernmental Agreement
Staff presented a draft intergovernmental agreement with the Town of Weston for reconstruction of Ross Avenue from Riverbend Road to Paula's Avenue, programmed for 2028 construction with a surface transportation grant covering approximately 75% of costs (over $2.2 million). The cost split is approximately 51% village and 49% town based on right-of-way analysis. The agreement includes annexation provisions so that if town land annexes to the village post-construction, the village would reimburse the town for its proportionate remaining debt payments. A committee member raised a question about whether driveways should be explicitly included in the annexation reimbursement language, and staff agreed to refine that language. The committee voted to recommend approval with possible minor clarifications on pre- and post-annexation cost provisions.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. A representative from Inpipe Technology (Sean Dudy) was present to deliver the I&I study presentation as a scheduled agenda item, not as a public commenter.
