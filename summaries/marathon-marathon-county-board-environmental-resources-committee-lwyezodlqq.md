## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Environmental Resources Committee held its November 3, 2026 meeting, conducting four rezoning public hearings and several town zoning reviews. The most significant action was the committee's decision to postpone a contested farmland preservation-to-low-density-residential rezoning request (MK7 Enterprises LLC in the Town of Marathon) to allow the town additional time to respond, following substantial opposition from neighbors, an attorney representing adjacent landowners, and a local farmer who owns surrounding acreage. Three other rezoning petitions were approved without significant opposition.

## Key Discussions
### 5A1: Weissaki Rezoning - General Agriculture to Rural Estate, Town of Green Valley
Staff presenter Garrett Pogle reported that both the Town of Green Valley and county staff recommended approval. Adjacent property owner William Kaiser raised concerns about an old dump on the property, including potential groundwater contamination and who would bear responsibility. Pogle explained that any new property owner inherits responsibility for existing violations, but that the dump predates the 10-year enforcement window, limiting the county's authority to act. The committee approved the rezoning to forward to the county board.

### 5A2: Schultz Rezoning - Rural Residential to Rural Estate, Town of McMillan
Pogle reported that both the Town of McMillan and county staff recommended approval of the rezoning. No public comment was offered. The committee approved the motion without notable debate, with Supervisor Drebeck moving and Supervisor Shamborn seconding.

### 5A3: MK7 Enterprises LLC Rezoning - Farmland Preservation to Low Density Residential, Town of Marathon
This was the most contested item of the meeting. Attorney Anders Helquist of Weld Riley Law Firm argued on behalf of adjacent property owners that the application failed all four statutory requirements to rezone out of farmland preservation, noting that the applicant's own application admitted two of the four standards were not met, that future land use maps show the parcel as agricultural/woodland, and that there were conflicts with surrounding agricultural uses. Local farmer Tyler Sehafer, who owns surrounding acreage, warned of road infrastructure inadequacy, grain bin noise conflicts, and noted that 100% of approximately 30 residents at a prior town meeting opposed the project. Land surveyor Dan Higinbotham, associated with the project, testified that wetland delineation was underway and soil evaluations were scheduled for the following Tuesday. Additional opposition came from Town of Marathon residents Randall Walcott and Karen Milbeck, who cited misleading communications about the project's intent and concerns about road damage from log trucks. Committee members debated extensively, with Chair Ritter noting a potential inconsistency with how the committee had handled a prior Town of Green Valley case based on future land use maps. Corporation Counsel advised that if a denial motion was made, it needed substantive reasons on the record. A motion to approve by Supervisor Sefelt failed for lack of a second. A denial motion by Supervisor Bro was ruled out of order by the chair after Corporation Counsel indicated it relied on factors not properly before the board (septic suitability). The committee ultimately voted to postpone the matter to no later than the January ERC meeting to give the Town of Marathon an opportunity to respond.

### 5A4: Spencer Joint Revocable Trust Rezoning - Farmland Preservation to General Agriculture, Town of McMillan
Staff reported that both the Town of McMillan and county staff recommended approval. No public comment was offered. Supervisor Shamborn moved to approve based on findings of fact, conclusions of law, and the town's recommendation; the motion carried without notable debate.

### 5B1 & 5B2: Town Zoning Changes - Town of Castle and Town of Weston
Both town-initiated zoning changes were presented by staff as having met all application requirements. Town of Castle sought a partial rezone from farmland to M1 Light Industrial; Town of Weston sought a rezone of Bowman Dairy Farm LLC property from Rural Residential 5-acre to Rural Residential 2-acre. Both were approved by the committee without public comment or significant debate.

### 6A1: Data Center Ordinance Development Timeline
Director Lori Mski presented a timeline for developing a data center ordinance following a moratorium put in place in August. Staff described a research and survey phase currently underway, with outreach to surrounding counties and municipalities. The timeline targets a public hearing in June, well ahead of the one-year moratorium deadline, with a committee framework check-in planned at the six-month mark. No action was taken; the update was informational only.

### Item 7: 2027 Budget Overview and PFAS Grant Opportunities
Supervisor Robinson noted the prior day's budget presentation and reminded committee members that amendment requests were due by noon Friday. Robinson also highlighted two new DNR PFAS grant opportunities: a community-wide sampling grant for private wells and an individual private well sampling program, noting a 20% match requirement that could potentially be offset by in-kind contributions or staff time. Robinson thanked the committee for ongoing work on fee structures given levy restraints.

## Public Comment
Public comment was incorporated into the public hearings. For item 5A1, adjacent property owner William Kaiser raised concerns about an old dump on the Weissaki property and potential groundwater contamination. For item 5A3, attorney Anders Helquist (Weld Riley Law Firm) testified on behalf of the Marcott family and other adjacent property owners arguing denial on all four statutory grounds; local farmer Tyler Sehafer testified against the rezoning citing road infrastructure, agricultural conflicts, and near-unanimous community opposition at a prior town meeting; land surveyor Dan Higinbotham testified in a neutral/informational capacity about the project's due diligence process; Randall Walcott (Town of Marathon) opposed the rezone citing a flawed public process and asked the committee to send it back to the town; and Karen Milbeck (Town of Marathon) raised concerns about road damage from log trucks and potential tax increases.
