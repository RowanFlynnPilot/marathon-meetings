## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees held its regular meeting on Monday, July 20, 2026, taking several significant actions including unanimously approving a $6.7 million contract with 8 Pine Inc. to construct a new Mountain Bay Metropolitan Police Department headquarters. The board also received a detailed facilities planning presentation from DC Everest Area School District leadership regarding a potential April referendum to fund an $81–94 million project decommissioning the junior high and redistributing grades. Additional actions included approving the Concord Avenue reconstruction contract, an aquatic center leak detection contract, and several personnel and policy items.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
One member of the public, Jim Pinsel, addressed the board on three topics: opposition to any removal of legal detail review from the Finance and HR Committee's monthly oversight; a request that EMPD dissolution funds ($362,354.32 per the administrator's report) be specifically earmarked to reduce borrowing for the new police headquarters building rather than used for other purposes; and a call for the board to set budget goals for the coming year including fully funding the fire department and minimizing property taxes.

### DC Everest Area School District Long-Range Facility Planning Presentation
Superintendent Casey Nye and Assistant Superintendent of Operations Kelly Strike presented the district's long-range facility plan, which involves potentially decommissioning the 75-year-old junior high, moving eighth graders to the middle school (creating a 6–8 building) and ninth graders to the senior high (creating a 9–12 building), with an estimated referendum cost of $81–94 million targeted for April. Dr. Strike noted the junior high's aging infrastructure including failed clay and galvanized pipes discovered shortly after her arrival, and that a 'fix only' approach would cost at least $40 million with no visible improvement. Board members asked questions about operational costs of the retained junior high shell, enrollment trends (currently 5,730 students, down from roughly 5,900 five years ago), staffing reduction strategy (attrition-based), the aging senior high, and a possible new or repaired community pool adjacent to GTCC. This was a discussion-only item with no vote taken.

### Mountain Bay Metro Police Department Building Contract Award (Item 54)
Hooshang Zeyghami summarized the bid results: five alternatives were received, three were eliminated, and the base bid plus alternatives one and two from 8 Pine Inc. came in at $6,390,100, with the total project including fiber optic line, lockers, security, and furniture estimated at $6.7 million against a $7 million budget. Weston's share is approximately $4,355,000 (65%) with Rothschild covering 35%. Village Finance Director Ryan noted an intergovernmental agreement between Weston and Rothschild would be finalized in August before contracts are signed, with groundbreaking targeted for end of September. Trustee Beck expressed disappointment in the process that led to the need for a new building but acknowledged the project came in well under comparable statewide projects and supported approval. The board approved the contract unanimously 7-0 on a roll call vote.

### SAFER Staffing Following April Referendum (Item 37)
The board discussed the SAFER board's approval of adding three firefighter/paramedic positions for 2027. The fire chief noted that adding three positions does not operationally improve ISO ratings or put additional apparatus on the road because fire/EMS staffing works in groups, not individually. Mark Maloney and others expressed support for the three positions as a start while calling for a longer-term joint planning conversation with Rib Mountain. The board acknowledged the decision by motion without further action on specific staffing targets.

### 2027 Budget Priorities and Budget Adoption Schedule (Item 46)
The board discussed setting budget priorities and approved a budget meeting calendar with joint Finance/HR and Board sessions running from August through a November 16 public hearing and adoption. Discussion touched on zero-based budgeting (deemed very labor-intensive), the pool's ongoing costs, utility operator turnover, public works staffing needs, and the need to bring budget priorities back as a formal agenda item in August. The board moved the September 7 meeting to September 8 to avoid Labor Day and set meeting times for the joint sessions.

### Aquatic Center Leak Detection Contract (Item 53)
Before voting, the board had a lengthy discussion about whether to continue investing in the aging pool, which was noted to be built in 1999, cost over $100,000 in repairs this year, and has never broken even financially. Luis Lopes-Serrao raised the possibility of a partnership with Rothschild and Scoffield if those communities close their pool, which had been explored previously but paused. The board ultimately approved a contract with Newman Pools for leak detection and repair, with the understanding that broader pool strategy decisions remain unresolved.

### Concord Avenue Reconstruction Contract Award (Item 52)
The board approved awarding the Concord Avenue reconstruction contract to H Suns Inc. for a total of $2,228,977.70, which was noted to be higher than the original estimate. Staff explained that the project has not yet been borrowed for and the amount will be incorporated into 2026/2027 capital borrowing. The vote was unanimous.

### Metropolitan Police Department Use of Outbuildings at Public Safety Building Site (Item 45)
The board discussed and approved allowing Mountain Bay Metro PD to continue storing evidence in the two outbuildings at the village's public safety building site at no cost, granting the request of the Mountain Bay Metro Oversight Board. The police chief explained the department had vacated the interior of the building, turned in all keys, and was seeking only temporary use of the outbuildings until the new police headquarters is complete. Staff noted the village had been receiving approximately $900/month from MBMPD for 30% of utility costs in the main building, which would no longer apply.

### Legal Detail Review by Finance and HR Committee (Item 47)
Following public commenter Jim Pinsel's concerns and a question raised at the Finance/HR committee, the board debated whether the committee should continue reviewing monthly legal billing detail. Members agreed the purpose is to flag unusual expenditures and provide oversight. The board voted unanimously to continue the practice of the Finance and Human Resources Committee reviewing legal details.

## Public Comment
One member of the public, Jim Pinsel, spoke. He expressed strong opposition to any proposal to remove legal detail from the Finance and HR Committee's monthly review, citing it as essential oversight and referencing a past instance where it revealed significant legal fees related to a public records dispute. He also urged the board to earmark the approximately $362,354 in EMPD dissolution funds specifically for reducing borrowing on the new police headquarters building rather than allowing those funds to be diverted. Finally, he called on the board to set explicit budget goals for the upcoming year, specifically fully funding the fire department and prioritizing minimizing property taxes.
