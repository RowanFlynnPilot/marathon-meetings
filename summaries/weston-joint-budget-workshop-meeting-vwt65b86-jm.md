# Joint Budget Workshop Meeting

**Organization:** Village of Weston  
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VwT65b86_JM  
**Summarized:** 2026-11-21 03:44 UTC

---

## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston held a Joint Budget Workshop Meeting on Monday, October 19, 2026, reviewing the proposed 2027 budget across all funds and taking action on the refuse/recycling fee and the public hearing notice. The board approved a $30 increase in the annual garbage fee to $235.50 for 2027, and approved the 2027 budget public hearing notice (5-2) with a projected tax rate decrease of approximately 8 cents per thousand dollars of assessed value, setting a public hearing for November 16.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
One member of the public spoke, raising several concerns: the refuse fund reserve being depleted by fuel surcharges rather than direct billing; an estimated $100,000 in fuel surcharges being absorbed by the village without billing the Town of Weston for its approximately 5% share; a request to explain a tax rate chart on page 54 that he alleged was misleading because it reflected a property revaluation rather than actual spending reductions; and a question about why the Mountain Bay Metro Police Department budget showed a 4.63% increase when less than 3% was approved by the oversight board.

### Approval of October 5, 2026 Joint Budget Workshop Meeting Minutes
Minutes were approved by both the Finance & Human Resources Committee and the Board of Trustees. Trustee Beck noted a correction: the minutes recorded her vote on a 6-to-1 motion as an aye but it should have been a nay. Staff confirmed the correction would be made.

### Acknowledge Work Product Transmittals (September Budget Status Report, TIF Reports, Legal Details)
Each transmittal was acknowledged separately by the Finance & Human Resources Committee. During discussion of the September budget status report, Trustee Ermeling asked about the Board of Trustees line showing only about $38,000 spent against a $63,000 budget; staff explained the majority is wages with remaining accounts held for training and lodging that are not always used. Trustee Clark asked about interest income, and staff explained it derives from general fund bank accounts and investments. The legal details prompted a question about a $1,500 attorney cost related to an open records request; staff explained it was resolved with no further appeal pending.

### Review and Discussion of 2027 Budget — General Fund
Staff reported the projected tax rate decrease had narrowed from 10 cents to approximately 8 cents per thousand after updated assessment ratio data arrived on Friday, with the assessment ratio dropping to 0.86, triggering a future reassessment obligation. Trustee Ermeling raised numerous line-item questions including DPW director salary moving back from TIF to the general fund, a street operations bonus incentive of $66,000 tied to transportation aid increases, the Mountain Bay PD budget showing a 4.63% increase due to a prior-year accounting restructuring rather than a true contract increase, and $8,000 in credit card fees for the aquatic center explained as fully offset by user fees. Trustee Beck asked about pool revenue being budgeted lower than current-year actuals; staff explained they average multiple years to avoid overestimating. Discussion arose about moving the $15,000 contingency salary line to the appropriate department budgets; staff explained it is kept in contingency because wage approvals are not finalized at budget time.

### Review and Discussion of 2027 Budget — Special Revenue Funds, Debt, Capital, and Utilities
Staff noted TIF 1 is at zero and approximately $1.5 million will be allocated to taxing districts after the final audit. The farmers market budget increase from $18,000 to $25,000 was discussed and attributed to budgeting closer to actual historical expenditures. Extensive debt discussion ensued: Trustee Beck questioned whether the $4 million bank note for the Mountain Bay police building was included in the $49.38 million combined principal figure presented by the village's financial advisor; staff and Trustee Clark confirmed it was included after locating the orange-highlighted footnote in the advisor's Table 1. Trustees expressed concern about total village outstanding debt of approximately $55 million and the long-term sustainability of continued borrowing. Staff recommended bringing the village's debt policy (last reviewed in May 2008) back for board review early in the new year, and the board supported that direction. On utilities, staff flagged the need for a sanitary sewer rate increase in 2027, noting Remote Metro costs had grown by nearly $1 million over the prior decade in ways that were difficult to predict.

### Refuse/Recycling Fund and Potential Increase in Garbage Fees
Staff presented options for increasing the annual garbage fee from $205.50, showing projected fund balances at $225, $230, and $235.50. The estimated fund balance at year-end 2026 is $62,400 and covers both village and town residents. Staff clarified the Town of Weston pays the same per-unit rate as village residents and is billed accordingly, disputing a public commenter's assertion that the town was not being charged its share of fuel surcharges. Trustee Rickert moved to set the fee at $235.50 for 2027 with a re-evaluation the following year; the Finance & Human Resources Committee approved unanimously. The Board of Trustees then approved the same recommendation; Trustee Olson voted nay.

### 2027 Budget Public Hearing Notice
Staff presented the proposed public hearing notice and noted the estimated assessed tax rate would be updated to approximately $5.52 per thousand. A motion to approve the notice with the updated rate was made and passed by the Finance & Human Resources Committee. The Board of Trustees then voted 5-2 to approve, with Trustees Clark and Beck voting nay. The public hearing was set for the November 16 board meeting.

## Public Comment
One member of the public spoke. He raised four concerns: (1) the refuse fund reserve is being depleted by fuel surcharges rather than by directly billing for the service, and the fund will be insolvent within months; (2) the Town of Weston is on the village's trash contract and subject to the same fuel surcharges but the village is not billing the town for its estimated $100,000 share, and he called on the board to include billing the town for its approximately 5% portion of 2026 surcharges and to bill monthly going forward; (3) a tax rate chart on page 54 appeared misleading because the dip shown for 2025 resulted from a property revaluation, not reduced spending, and he challenged staff to show a single-family home tax bill that actually decreased; (4) the Mountain Bay Metro Police Department budget showed a 4.63% increase while the oversight board approved less than 3%, and he asked where the additional money goes.
