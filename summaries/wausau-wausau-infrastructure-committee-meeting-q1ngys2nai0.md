# Wausau Infrastructure Committee Meeting

## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Infrastructure and Facilities Committee held a lengthy meeting dominated by extensive public comment on boulevard snow plowing practices, with approximately 15 residents and two alderpersons testifying in favor of returning to the historical practice of plowing snow toward center boulevards rather than to the curb. The committee discussed the issue but took no formal action on snow plowing, instead directing city staff and the city attorney to research legal options — including a Wisconsin state statute potentially allowing plows to operate against traffic with proper lighting — and return with costed options at a future meeting. The committee also discussed boulevard landscaping policy, the 2027-2030 capital infrastructure plan, and committee jurisdictional definitions.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment — Boulevard Snow Plowing
Approximately 15 speakers, including residents of Elwood Boulevard, Highland Park Boulevard, Oaklair Boulevard, Everest Boulevard, and Midway Boulevard, testified against the city's current practice of plowing snow to the curb rather than to the center boulevard. Speakers cited safety hazards including blocked driveways, obscured sightlines, inaccessible fire hydrants and sand barrels, and inability of emergency vehicles to pass. Alderperson Carol Lukans (District 1) testified that the boulevard plowing change was never presented as a distinct policy item when the winter maintenance policy was approved unanimously in January 2024, and argued it required separate council approval. Resident Felix Gallow presented a data analysis he said showed returning to center-boulevard plowing would save tens of thousands of dollars annually. Alderperson Lou Larson (District 10) noted a Wisconsin state statute (346.05 sub-2) permits plows to operate against traffic for highway maintenance with proper lighting, and argued no new $40,000 equipment was needed.

### Item 4: Boulevard Planting Subcommittee
Alder Wiskowski moved to establish a special exploratory subcommittee to develop a boulevard planting scheme, addressing questions about what plantings — including native plants, pollinator gardens, and possibly vegetable gardens — would be permissible in the right-of-way between street and sidewalk. Alder Lukans raised that the sustainability committee was also working on related issues, prompting a friendly amendment that the subcommittee work in cooperation with the sustainability commission. Engineering staff noted concerns about utility excavation permits disturbing plantings and sightline issues near intersections. The motion as amended carried.

### Item 5A: Snowplowing Policy Discussion
Committee members discussed the boulevard plowing issue following public comment. Alder Hanniki and Alder Slonsky both praised the level of citizen engagement and expressed openness to revisiting the policy. Public Works Director Lynman clarified that the department's prior practice of not plowing against traffic came from legal guidance received years ago that a road would need to be closed first, and said he did not intentionally mislead the public. The city attorney confirmed that Wisconsin statute 346.05(2) does appear to allow plowing against traffic with proper lighting, and that the city's insurer would cover such operations, but said a deeper review of administrative codes and DOT requirements was needed. The committee directed staff and the city attorney to return at a future meeting with a formal legal opinion, equipment cost analysis, and multiple costed options for how to address boulevard plowing, but took no formal action at this meeting.

### Item 5B: 2027-2030 Infrastructure and Facility Capital Projects
Engineering staff presented the five-year capital infrastructure plan. Alder Hanniki asked for clarification on grant funding assumptions, learning that $600,000 of the $900,000 in 2027 grant funds was tied to the solar array project (likely to be removed after finance committee rejection) and $300,000 was a state reimbursement for East Wausau Avenue right-of-way acquisition rather than a true grant. The committee requested staff add project justification narratives to future capital plan presentations and provide a breakdown identifying legally obligated projects versus discretionary ones.

### Item 6: Committee Jurisdiction and Definition of Duties
The committee reviewed a memo from Assistant City Attorney Bonino outlining the Infrastructure and Facilities Committee's statutory duties, including recommending purchase and sale of city real estate for municipal purposes and disposition of underutilized city-owned land. Alder Hanniki and others noted apparent overlap with the Economic Development Committee's jurisdiction over development parcels, and Alder Martin provided historical context that the two committees have informally divided responsibilities. Alder Hanniki proposed a joint meeting between the two committees to clarify boundaries. Alder Wiskowski requested that review of vacant and underutilized city-owned real estate be added to a future committee agenda.

## Action Items
- City attorney to provide a formal, researched legal opinion on Wisconsin statute 346.05(2) and any applicable DOT or administrative code requirements for plowing against traffic on boulevard streets
- Public Works Director Lynman to research history of when and why the policy of closing roads before plowing against traffic was implemented and who directed it
- Staff to return at a future meeting with multiple costed options for boulevard plowing, including equipment costs, labor costs, and legal requirements for each option
- Staff to investigate repositioning of sand barrels in boulevard neighborhoods to more accessible locations
- Staff to research any individual liability implications for CDL drivers plowing against traffic
- A special exploratory subcommittee to be established to develop boulevard planting guidelines, working in cooperation with the sustainability commission
- Engineering staff to add project justification narratives to capital plan presentations for constituent clarity
- Engineering staff to provide a breakdown of legally obligated versus discretionary capital projects in the five-year plan
- Review of vacant and underutilized city-owned real estate to be added to a future Infrastructure and Facilities Committee agenda
- Chair to explore scheduling a joint meeting between the Infrastructure and Facilities Committee and the Economic Development Committee to clarify overlapping jurisdictions
