## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Health and Human Services Committee met and took action on a grant application for the PATH (Projects for Assistance in Transition from Homelessness) program, approving the county to move forward with applying for up to $150,000 in state funding to address homelessness among individuals with severe mental illness. The committee also received updates on a budget-neutral arrangement to provide temporary child welfare supervision support to Lincoln County, mid-year budget reports showing no concerns, and strategic plan community survey progress. The meeting was notable for its focus on a 67% increase in homelessness in Marathon County between 2022 and 2025.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of September 2, 2026 Meeting Minutes
A motion to approve the minutes was made by Supervisor Crumbs and seconded. The motion carried without opposition.

### 5A: Authorization for Application for Funding from Wisconsin DHS PATH Program
Health department staff member Megan presented the PATH grant opportunity, explaining that Marathon County has seen a 67% increase in homelessness from 2022 to 2025 and that the grant would fund a case manager and one-time rental assistance targeting individuals experiencing homelessness with severe mental illness. She said the county plans to apply for approximately $150,000, with a 1-to-3 match requirement that Administrator Leonard clarified could be fulfilled through in-kind contributions like existing staff time rather than new county dollars. One supervisor asked about worst-case scenarios if only $45,000 were awarded, and Megan confirmed a reduced scope would still be viable. Supervisor Rosenberg asked about HUD's shift away from Housing First toward transitional housing, and Megan confirmed the grant aligns well with that changing landscape. Supervisor Cavelli noted that the homelessness task force had identified mental health as a key gap. The motion to authorize the application passed unanimously.

### 6A: Strategic Plan Development Update Including Public Survey
Chair Boots noted the community survey would go live in October as part of strategic plan development. Supervisor Cavelli asked about expected response rates, referencing past survey efforts. Administrator Leonard and health department staff member Amanda noted that when the life report survey transitioned from paper to online, responses jumped from just under 400 to 1,792, suggesting confidence in the online format.

### 6B: Social Service Department Support to Lincoln County for Child Welfare
Administrator Leonard explained that Marathon County is providing temporary child welfare supervision support to Lincoln County, which has experienced turnover in supervisory positions. Social Services Director Christa confirmed that Lincoln County has an offer out for their child welfare manager position and that the arrangement is expected to conclude by end of 2026. All Marathon County staff time, including after-hours on-call support, will be tracked through a time study and fully reimbursed by Lincoln County at no net cost to Marathon County.

### 6C: Year-to-Date Budget Reports
Finance staff member Sam presented mid-year budget reports through June 2026 for the health department, veterans services, and social improvement fund, noting no concerns in any department. The health department payroll benchmark was at 46.47%, veterans services was at 92.91% of revenue budget largely due to tax levy timing, and the social improvement fund had $11.27 million in the grants fund. Sam noted a budget amendment will be needed at year-end for the four-county consortium.

### 7A: Future Discussion Items and Announcements
A supervisor requested a future presentation or report on suicide awareness and prevention data, and health department staff agreed to track that through the PULSE framework. Administrator Leonard announced the 2027 budget had been delivered to HR, Finance and Property Committee and encouraged supervisors wishing to propose amendments to contact finance director Fensky, Leonard, or corporation counsel to ensure any amendment maintains the required balanced budget. He also reminded the committee of a special meeting the following day at 3 p.m. and asked members unable to attend in person to contact Chair Gibbs to arrange remote participation.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered.
