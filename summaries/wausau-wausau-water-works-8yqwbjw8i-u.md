## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Water Works Commission held its regular October meeting, receiving informational updates on corrosion control treatment (ortho phosphate) implementation requirements from the DNR, ongoing construction projects, and options for spending PFAS settlement funds on capital projects. The commission approved one action item — sole source procurement of magnesium hydroxide for the wastewater division — and tabled final decisions on PFAS settlement fund allocation until November when more accurate financial figures will be presented.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of September 1, 2026 meeting minutes
Martins moved and Gellhar seconded approval of the minutes. No discussion was noted. The motion passed on a voice vote with no opposition.

### WDNR Final Decision on Corrosion Control Treatment (Ortho Phosphate)
Staff (identified as Eric and Scott) briefed the commission that the DNR issued a final decision in July requiring ortho phosphate to be operational no later than July 9, 2028. Staff noted a plan to install a temporary feed system before the permanent system is bid out in early 2027 to allow pipes to season ahead of required sampling. A commission member (Martins) asked whether progress on lead service line replacement could reduce or delay the ortho phosphate requirement; staff clarified it also benefits copper and in-home fixtures, so the requirement stands regardless. Staff noted a conflicting regulatory timeline — installation required by July 9, 2028 but sampling not due until June 9, 2029, leaving only one month rather than a full year. No action was requested; this was informational only.

### Project Update: Headworks Screening and Crocker Street Lift Station
Ben reported that the wastewater headworks project is nearing substantial completion with an October 15 deadline expected to be met; a programming issue by Hydrodyne was resolved. The Crocker Street lift station project commenced September 22, with underground piping installed; Alliant Energy (WPS) is expected to complete new service this week and the lift station itself will be installed by the contractor (HOS) in spring with substantial completion by April 9, 2027. There was brief confusion among commissioners and staff about the exact street intersection (Crocker and [inaudible cross street]) before staff clarified it is on the northwest side.

### PFAS Settlement Fund Expenditure Options for Capital Projects
Staff presented a spreadsheet of proposed capital projects highlighted for potential PFAS settlement funding use over the next five years, including the ortho phosphate installation, noting $1.67 million in EPA funding already secured with a remaining shortfall proposed to be covered by settlement funds rather than borrowing. Staff acknowledged the spreadsheet totals are not accurate and promised a corrected version for November. A commission member raised the DNR's 'clawback' position — requiring at least 50% of settlement funds be used to pay down the low-interest emerging contaminant loan used for the GAC system — and staff noted this is legally disputed by multiple municipalities. Commission member Hadley asked about the lead replacement contract; staff explained the DNR only permitted a three-year lock-in on construction pricing despite staff seeking five years, which commissioners criticized as an overreach. No action was taken; staff will return in November with corrected figures.

### Sole Source Approval: Magnesium Hydroxide for Wastewater Division
Ben presented the sole source justification for magnesium hydroxide, citing proven performance, the risk of chemistry instability when switching chemical vendors, and DNR compliance requirements. He noted a 5-cent per pound price increase but recommended continuing with the current supplier. Headley moved and Gellhar seconded. The motion passed on a voice vote with no opposition.
