## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Committee of the Whole met on September 2, 2026 to consider a proposal by Alders Heni and True Blood to commission a comprehensive, independent systemic audit of city government. After an extensive presentation and discussion, the committee unanimously approved a motion directing the executive committee to develop a scope of work, procurement process, and timeline for the audit, with the full proposal to return to the common council for approval before any contract is awarded. The meeting also addressed growing public concern about city financial reporting accuracy, including a reported $14 million discrepancy in motorpool fund history tables, a $36 million sewer debt understatement, and a material weakness flagged by external auditors tied to a $1 million prior period adjustment.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment on Agenda Items
Three speakers addressed the council. Joanne Leonard (923 Maple Hill Road), a former Marathon County Board member and retired Wisconsin Public Service manager, called for a comprehensive financial audit by outside experts, criticizing what she described as inadequate financial controls and expressed opposition to the mayor's alternative plan. Liz Hower (1106 North Third Street) called for an independent audit conducted by a firm with no prior ties to the city, citing the need for transparency and accountability. Deborah Ryan (702 Elm Street), a former alder (2020–2022) and retired accountant, raised a series of specific historical financial concerns including an allegedly unauthorized $5,000 legal opinion obtained by a former finance director, an unrecorded housing authority loan, vendor contracts signed without required authorization, and incomplete records requests; she called for the finance director to be relieved of daily duties.

### Approval of Minutes of Preceding Committee of the Whole Meeting (July 13, 2026)
Alder Larson moved to approve the minutes as presented; Alder Lukans seconded. The motion passed on a voice vote with no opposition.

### Presentation: Comprehensive Independent Systemic Audit
Alders Heni and True Blood jointly presented a detailed proposal for a citywide systemic audit, explicitly framing it as not a forensic audit and not intended to assign blame or uncover wrongdoing. Heni cited specific financial reporting concerns including a $14 million discrepancy in motorpool fund budget history tables over 10 years, a $36 million understatement of sewer debt in published schedules, and a material weakness identified by the city's own independent auditors linked to a $1 million prior period adjustment reported the same day. True Blood emphasized structural inefficiencies, including equipment and staff whose purposes are unclear, outdated organizational practices unchanged since the 1960s, a lack of meaningful exit interview processes, and the city hiring outside contractors at higher rates than employees for work existing departments are supposed to perform.

### Discussion of Motion as Amended
Multiple council members expressed support while raising concerns about implementation. Alders Slonski and Drew cautioned that audit findings might reveal the need for additional spending — including new staff, software, or equipment — rather than yielding tax savings, and urged clear public communication about realistic timelines for implementing changes. Alder Martins noted the prior Mercury motorpool study took over six to eight months and its recommendations were largely unimplemented due to levy limits and expenditure restraint, warning the same constraints could hinder this audit's outcomes. Alder Larson asked whether the audit would cover the potential purchase of the old WMC/NTC campus for police and fire, and was told the audit was intended to work in tandem with normal city functions rather than halt them. Mayor Doug Diny stated the city would continue normal operations and cited ongoing improvements already underway, including new budget presentation formats with bar charts for the 2027 budget. Absent Alder Tierney's written statement was read aloud, expressing strong support for the audit and stating her first preference would actually be a forensic audit; Alder Watson's written statement was noted as in the packet but not read at her request.

### Motion to Direct Alder Heni to Prepare All-Staff Memo
Following passage of the main motion, Alder Lukans moved to direct Alder Heni to prepare a concise memo to all city staff — including fire and police — summarizing the evening's discussion and inviting them to the future council meeting where the audit proposal will be considered. Alder Slonski seconded. A brief procedural question was raised about whether the motion was germane to the agenda; the presiding officer indicated she had confirmed with the city attorney it would be, and Alder Heni volunteered to write the memo regardless of the vote. The motion passed on a voice vote with no opposition.
