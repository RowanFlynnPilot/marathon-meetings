# Joint Board of Trustees and Finance and HR Committee Budget Workshop

**Organization:** Village of Weston

## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston held a joint budget workshop between the Board of Trustees and Finance and HR Committee to review the proposed 2027 budget, covering utility funds (water, sewer, stormwater) and the general fund. Key discussions centered on Mountain Bay Metro PD budget percentage calculations, employee wage structure and market positioning, and the utility rate outlook, with trustees and staff debating how to better compensate lower-paid employees. No final budget votes were taken; decisions on wages, the pay matrix, and borrowing were deferred to the October 5th meeting.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Two members of the public spoke. The first speaker, a woman identified as Grace, urged the board to think outside the box on wages, suggesting bonuses instead of across-the-board percentage raises to slow compensation growth for higher earners while accelerating pay for lower-paid employees. The second speaker, identified as living on 5000 Street Weston, raised multiple specific budget concerns: the Mountain Bay Metro PD budget showing a 3.09% increase versus the charter cap of 3% and the oversight board's approved 2.8%; the Everest Metro Municipal Court label on a $60,000 line item after that court's dissolution; a $0 allocation for building maintenance personnel despite $5,521 spent year-to-date; and criticism of the employee survey process, arguing full results were never presented to the board and that the board should set budget parameters and let department heads manage within them.

### August 2026 Budget Status Report
The report was acknowledged by both the Finance and HR Committee and the Board of Trustees with no significant concerns raised. Finance Director Jessica noted that aquatic expenses would not be finalized until December due to pending water utility bills. A concern was raised about refuse/recycling fuel costs trending higher, with Jessica indicating the rate may need to increase to approximately $25 and that a formula and projection would be brought back to the board in October.

### 2027 Budget - Water Utility Fund
Public Works Director Michael presented the water utility fund, noting Weston's water rates are on the lower end compared to neighboring communities. He reported the fund has positive net income currently, with a potential rate increase possible in the 2028-2029 timeframe if a major capital project is undertaken. Chemical costs have decreased by approximately $50,000 due to removing wells one and five from service. A spreadsheet typo was identified in the 2026 budget net assets beginning balance figure, which Jessica acknowledged needed correction. Ongoing PFAS settlement payments from 3M were confirmed as the source of miscellaneous revenue, with approximately five or six payments remaining.

### 2027 Budget - Sanitary Sewer Utility Fund
Michael reported that Rim Mountain Metro Sanitary District is projecting approximately a $100,000 cost increase to Weston for 2027 due to a major wastewater treatment plant upgrade to meet phosphorus limits. He noted the sewer fund has been running at a deficit for several years, and the sale of the old sewervac truck helped close the gap in the current year, but no similar large asset exists for 2027. A sewer rate increase was identified as likely necessary in 2027. Weston's rates remain among the lower ones compared to other district partners including Kronenwetter, Rothschild, and Mosinee.

### 2027 Budget - Stormwater Utility Fund
Michael described the stormwater utility, noting rates have been stable since a 2023 increase and the fund continues to show positive cash flow. A major upcoming project is the Business 51 storm pond, intended to improve compliance with DNR phosphorus requirements, with modeling showing 70-80% compliance achievement. Michael clarified that stormwater funds benefit rural areas through culvert projects, not just those with curb and gutter. Grant funding from DOT for the project and a pedestrian crossing has not yet been confirmed.

### 2027 Budget - General Fund Revenues and Expenditures
Jessica presented the general fund, noting the state confirmed an allowable levy increase of $732,852 from TIF closure plus approximately $95,000 in additional operational levy capacity, for a total allowable operational levy of $6,601,041. A $54,822 decrease in the Expenditure Restraint Program aid was flagged because the village's tax rate approaching the $5 threshold resulted in a penalty-like reduction, while shared revenue increased by $52,494. The budget assumes a 3% across-the-board COLA wage increase and a 10% health insurance increase, with final health insurance figures expected September 30th. The mislabeling of the Everest Metro Municipal Court versus Rothschild Area Municipal Court was acknowledged and will be corrected.

### Mountain Bay Metro PD Budget Percentage Debate
Trustees and staff engaged in extended discussion over why the Mountain Bay Metro PD budget line appeared to reflect a 3.09% increase when the charter caps increases at 3% and the oversight board approved 2.8%. Jessica explained that approximately $100,000 was moved to capital for police equipment, which skewed the percentage calculation on the remaining operating line, but the total dollar amount matched Mountain Bay's request. Trustees including Brent Olson and Barbara Ermeling expressed confusion and concern. The board directed staff to move all Mountain Bay Metro costs into the general fund operating line and shift equivalent borrowing to streets, and to reduce the capital allocation from $100,000 to $60,000 to match Mountain Bay's actual budgeted capital request, making the accounting cleaner and the percentage calculation accurate.

### Employee Wages, Pay Matrix, and Market Positioning
Extensive discussion was held on employee compensation. Michael noted that 11 employees are between the midpoint and maximum of the pay matrix, and that no employee exceeds 104% of market rate. Staff and trustees discussed how annual COLA increases combined with annual matrix adjustments effectively move the finish line, making it difficult for lower-paid employees to reach the midpoint. Trustee Olson calculated that total personnel cost increases including 3% COLA, WRS, health insurance, $56,000 in public works market increases, and $15,000 in merit/market contingency totaled approximately $390,000, or roughly $9,027 per employee, prompting concern about sustainability. A trustee proposed applying a flat dollar amount rather than a flat percentage to each employee's midpoint so that lower-paid employees would receive a proportionally larger increase. Jessica and Michael agreed this concept would be brought back October 5th along with three formal motions: matrix percentage increase, salary COLA percentage, and merit/market contingency amount. Staff confirmed the $56,000 in public works market increases would be tied to GTA funds.

### Employee Survey and Benefits Discussion
The board discussed results of an employee survey with a 62% participation rate (23 of 37 employees). Public comment had already raised concerns that full survey results were not presented to the board. Michael confirmed streets crews use 4-10 schedules in summer but utility staff found it logistically unworkable due to seven-day operational demands. The board reached informal consensus, with multiple trustees agreeing to discontinue considering summer hours or additional holidays and focus exclusively on financial compensation. A suggestion was made to revisit non-pay benefits on a periodic basis, perhaps every 18 months.

## Public Comment
Two members of the public spoke. The first, identified as Grace, addressed wage structure, urging the board to consider bonuses rather than across-the-board percentage raises to accelerate pay for lower-compensated employees while slowing growth for higher earners, and noting that money alone does not retain long-tenured employees. The second speaker, who identified an address on 5000 Street in Weston, raised specific budget line-item concerns including the Mountain Bay Metro PD increase exceeding the charter cap, a mislabeled Everest Metro Municipal Court line item, a $0 personnel allocation for building maintenance despite year-to-date spending, and questions about whether dissolved agency budgets were still being carried. The second speaker also criticized the employee survey process as incomplete and argued the board should set dollar parameters for department heads and let them manage operations within those guidelines.
