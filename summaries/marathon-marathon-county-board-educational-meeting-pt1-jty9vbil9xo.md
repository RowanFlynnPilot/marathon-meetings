## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Board of Supervisors held a wide-ranging educational meeting on August 20, 2026, centered largely on the Hub City Wind project and the county's legal options in response to Alliant Energy's proposed large-scale wind turbine development. Attorneys Bill Omin (UW-Madison Local Government Education Center) and Rebecca Roker (Atollus Law Firm) addressed supervisors' questions about the county's limited regulatory authority under state preemption, the risks of a moratorium, and the potential benefits and drawbacks of a Joint Development Agreement (JDA). The board also received presentations on the NTC-UWSP partnership, the Allison Dairland program, and heard extensive public comment overwhelmingly opposing a JDA.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment — Hub City Wind / JDA
Fourteen of the seventeen public commenters addressed the Hub City Wind project, uniformly urging the board to reject pursuing a JDA with Alliant Energy and instead pursue a moratorium and/or PSC intervention. Speakers cited the alternative energy committee's recommendation against a JDA, the county survey results opposing it, concerns about turbine size (up to 680 feet), health and safety unknowns, property value impacts, wildlife habitat near McMillan Marsh, lack of Alliant transparency, and NDAs in other JDAs. One speaker noted other counties such as Clark and Kenosha had adopted moratoriums. One speaker offered a counterpoint, arguing a well-negotiated JDA could bring revenue to offset projected county population and tax base decline.

### Public Comment — Cedar Creek Incfest Body Art Convention Licensing Fees (Resolution R53-26)
Three speakers — an event organizer, the Village of Rothschild tourism marketing director, and a tattoo studio owner — asked the board to vote no on Resolution R53-26 as presented, arguing the proposed per-practitioner licensing fee structure was inequitable, inconsistent with neighboring counties, and could cost the event up to $14,000, threatening its viability. They noted Brown County charges $1,250 for an event of 200-plus artists, while Sauk County charged $130 for the same event in 2024, and the convention is projected to bring roughly $500,000 in economic impact to the region.

### Committee Chair Report — Supervisor Dickinson on County Highway XX Developer Agreement
Supervisor Dickinson (appearing remotely, initially unable to be heard) briefed the board on three related items: an ordinance amendment for a four-way stop on County Highway XX, a resolution urging the state DOT to increase truck length allowances on Highway 51, and an upcoming developer agreement. He explained a new facility on County Highway XX and Trellwood Lane needs legal truck routes; state code restricts trucks over 65 feet from Business 51 but the state will not act until at least January 2027 due to elections. The developer agreement would have the developer fund 75% of an intersection improvement and 50% of resurfacing 2.3 miles of County Highway X, with a cancellation provision protecting the county from cost if the developer opts out.

### NTC and UWSP 'Purple and Blue Advantage' Partnership Presentation
Darren Ackley (NTC COO) and Gretle Stock (UWSP Dean of University College) presented the colocation partnership launching fall 2026, in which UWSP at Wausau will be embedded on NTC's Wausau campus. They highlighted cost savings (estimated $45,000–$70,000 less than attending a traditional four-year campus), seamless advising, shared transfer agreements, and local talent retention. NTC associate degree graduates average $54,000 annually, above the $44,000 living wage for Marathon County; the partnership aims to increase the region's bachelor's degree attainment rate from 28% toward the national 38%. Board Chair Gibbs thanked presenters; no questions were asked by supervisors.

### Hub City Wind — Legal Briefing and Board Q&A (Item 9C7A)
Attorneys Bill Omin and Rebecca Roker provided an extensive legal briefing on the county's options regarding the Hub City Wind project. They explained that because the project exceeds 100 megawatts, state statutes (Chapters 196 and 66) largely preempt local regulation, the PSC is only required to 'consider' (not follow) local comprehensive plans, and no local municipality has successfully stopped a project through PSC intervention. They characterized moratoriums as legally risky, disfavored under Wisconsin law, and potentially subject to legal challenge, while describing a JDA as the best available mechanism for the county to protect its interests contractually on issues such as setbacks, road repair, decommissioning, and environmental mitigation. Supervisors raised questions about health impacts, farmland preservation, environmental justice, airspace, wildlife, the timing of JDA negotiations relative to CPCN filing, and whether signing a JDA signals support for the project (attorneys said it need not). Omin confirmed he found no case in PSC dockets where a local government's intervention resulted in a project being denied.

### Board Deliberation: JDA Timing and Next Steps
Supervisors debated extensively whether to begin JDA negotiations immediately or delay. Supervisor Robinson and Supervisor Mast argued that waiting reduces negotiating leverage since Alliant has already secured land agreements, and that JDA negotiations typically take 8 months to over a year. Supervisors Gal, Sundulski, Jensen, and Straw argued for a delay of three to six months, citing the upcoming November election (which could change state-level policy), the township representatives' and residents' clear opposition to a JDA, and the desire to gather more information. Supervisor Nice suggested a middle path of examining JDA options without committing. No vote was taken at this meeting; Corp Counsel noted the executive committee made no formal recommendation, creating a parliamentary challenge, and suggested the board could consider two separate resolutions at the next meeting. Chair Gibbs indicated a recess before the remainder of the agenda.

## Public Comment
Seventeen people signed up; the following spoke: Joe Bergen (Town of Rib) opposed the JDA, citing the alternative energy committee's recommendation against it and questioning why a moratorium and intervention were not being pursued. Tren Spindler (Town of Rib, chair of an intergovernmental committee of five affected townships) opposed the JDA and urged a moratorium and PSC intervention, noting several ordinance options exist. Brandy Candenberg (Village of Maine, event organizer) asked the board to vote no on Resolution R53-26, arguing the proposed per-practitioner licensing fee structure could cost the Cedar Creek Incfest up to $14,000 and was inconsistent with neighboring counties. Dave Brua (Town of Wausau, Village of Rothschild tourism marketing director) also asked for further work on the licensing fee, noting the event's projected $500,000 economic impact. Mark Hannenburg (Village of Maine, tattoo studio owner and event co-organizer) opposed R53-26, describing the fee structure as a 'cliff' that disproportionately penalizes groups of seven practitioners versus six. Cindy Nelson (Town of Eau Pleine) opposed the JDA, pressing the board to explain why a limited moratorium had not been proposed and warning that she would track supervisors' votes through election time. Jackie Dillinger (Town of Rib) opposed the JDA, citing the Saratoga Township chairman's experience, warning about NDA clauses in JDAs, and noting PSC 128 guidelines date to 2011 when turbines were much smaller. Denise Kahn (Town of Rib) opposed the JDA, calling for independent research on health, groundwater, and soil impacts before approval, and warning against using Marathon County farmland as a testing ground. A resident from the Town of Ringle described concerns about shadow flicker, an airstrip on neighboring land requiring FAA-mandated 2,000-foot horizontal clearance, proximity to flight paths, and endangered brown bats in the area. Nikki Catrell (Brighton Township) opposed the JDA, stating she had moved from the East Coast for rural life and feared she would have to leave; she urged supervisors to research and advocate for constituents, arguing Alliant would not agree to meaningful setbacks in a JDA. Diane Freedman (Town of Eau Pleine) opposed the JDA, comparing the project to a science experiment with no guide book. Glenn Regolski (Town of Green Valley) opposed the JDA, questioning why Alliant entered Marathon County covertly through NDAs and good-neighbor agreements without approaching the county first. Becky Tyro (Town of Wiston, farm wedding venue owner) opposed the JDA and demanded to hear from supervisors at a public meeting why a limited moratorium could not be enacted. Trish Goldbach (Stratford) opposed rushing into a JDA, emphasizing that PSC 128 guidelines were written in 2011 and turbines have doubled in height. Harry Turkington (City of Madison) spoke in favor of a well-negotiated JDA, citing wind energy's improved reliability (52% capacity factor), Marathon County's projected 5.5% population decline through 2050, and examples of municipalities gaining community benefits through JDAs. Heidi Pesky (Town of McMillan) opposed the JDA, noting a Columbia County meeting the prior evening where no JDA existed, citing Wisconsinites for Local Control's PSC intervention work, noting the proposed turbines would be the tallest ever built for commercial power generation in the U.S., and raising concerns about medical helicopter flight paths to Children's Hospital. Christine Seafer (Town of McMillan) opposed the JDA, quoting an article arguing Wisconsin's electricity usage has been flat since 2005, alleging Alliant profits from building infrastructure regardless of local need, and expressing distrust of Alliant based on information provided at prior public forums.
