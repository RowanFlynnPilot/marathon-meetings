## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Human Resources, Finance & Property Committee met on Monday, November 9, 2026, to consider amendments to the 2027 budget and property tax levy resolution ahead of the full county board meeting. The committee approved three of four proposed budget amendments — including redirecting $6,300 budgeted for automated license plate readers into a committee contingency fund, adding $250,000 in projected sales tax revenue earmarked for legal costs related to wind energy intervention and broadband litigation, and updating the medical examiner fee schedule — while voting not to recommend a $3.5 million reduction in capital improvement funding. The levy resolution was forwarded to the full board for adoption.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Three members of the public spoke. The first, a former supervisor, urged the committee to use the opioid settlement fund — currently holding approximately $2.5 million with another $3.7 million expected — to offset opioid-related county expenses rather than relying on the tax levy. The second speaker, identifying himself as an engineer and finance professional, presented a spreadsheet he had emailed to committee members and administrator Leonard, arguing that phasing in the North Central Healthcare transfer to debt levy over two years would result in $3 million less in taxes while still maintaining adequate reserves; he recommended taking $2 million of the $3.5 million and applying it to the debt levy. A third speaker urged the committee to pause Flock license plate reader funding, citing concerns about mass surveillance, broad data sharing with over a thousand entities, ICE searches, and inadequate data security, and invited committee members to visit unflockwausau.org/marathon.

### Amendment 1: Automated License Plate Reader (Flock) Funding
Chair Robinson introduced Amendment 1, which would transfer the $6,300 budgeted for Flock automated license plate readers from the sheriff's department budget into the HR/Finance/Property Committee contingency fund, to be released only by a vote of the committee. Supervisor Nath stated he preferred stripping the funds entirely from the budget and returning them to the levy rather than placing them in a contingency fund. Vice Chair Pool raised concerns about the amendment limiting the sheriff's flexibility on data sharing, particularly for public safety matters involving ICE and human trafficking, and said he would not support it without changes. Chair Robinson clarified that a separate resolution would need to be adopted addressing data retention, access, and audit policies, and noted a productive morning meeting had occurred with the administrator, sheriff, and others. Kurt Gibbs moved to transfer the $6,300 to committee contingency; the motion passed but was not unanimous, with Supervisor Nath voting no.

### Amendment 2: Sales Tax Revenue Increase for Legal Services
Administrator Leonard explained that Amendment 2, authored by Supervisor Jensen who was absent, would increase projected sales tax revenues by $250,000 and place a corresponding appropriation in the administrator's special projects cost center for legal services, primarily intended for potential county intervention in a Public Service Commission proceeding related to the Hub City wind project. Kurt Gibbs expressed support but pushed to broaden the purpose to include litigation related to the county's broadband bond guarantee with Bug Tussle. Finance Director Fensky confirmed that if sales tax did not meet the increased projection, the designated legal funds would be allocated first before the surplus rolled to the non-EP departmental budget, and that historically the county budgets sales tax approximately 11.68% below Forward Analytics projections. Vice Chair Pool moved to approve the amendment; Gibbs seconded and successfully added a friendly amendment broadening the funds' use to cover intervention and other potential litigation. The motion passed but was not unanimous, with one member (identified as Cody) voting no.

### Amendment 3: Medical Examiner Pathologist Travel Fee Schedule
Administrator Leonard presented a staff-initiated fee schedule amendment adding travel time fees for the pathologist testifying in civil and criminal cases, noting the existing testimony rate was already in the fee schedule but a travel rate had been overlooked during budget development. No significant revenue impact was anticipated. Kurt Gibbs agreed to sponsor the amendment; he moved to incorporate the new fees, seconded by Supervisor Nath, and the motion passed unanimously.

### Amendment 4: $3.5 Million Reduction in CIP/NCHC Transfer
Administrator Leonard and Finance Director Fensky explained that Amendment 4, submitted by Supervisor Sundelski who was not present, would reduce the North Central Healthcare revenue transfer to capital improvements by $3.5 million, redirecting it toward debt service. Kurt Gibbs and Vice Chair Pool voiced strong opposition, with Gibbs citing the county's $25 million commitment for the new highway shop, the approximately $37 million remaining liability from the Bug Tussle broadband bond guarantee, and the risk that a $1.5 million debt service obligation to Bug Tussle cannot legally be placed on the levy without additional borrowing. Administrator Leonard warned that reducing the CIP now would likely trade a tax cut today for a larger tax increase with interest later, and cited additional uncertainties including potential Act 10 changes, federal 'One Big Beautiful Bill' Medicaid impacts, ARPA expiration, and reduced investment income as $55 million leaves the investment portfolio. Gibbs moved that the committee not recommend Amendment 4; the motion passed but was not unanimous, with Vice Chair Pool voting no, stating he wished to remain neutral.

### 2027 Budget and Property Tax Levy Resolution
Finance Director Fensky walked the committee through Resolution 78-26, which sets the 2027 budget and property tax levy. She explained that the total budget was $238,631,841 and highlighted the breakdown of operational levy, special purpose levies for bridge/culvert aid and library (which are levy limit exceptions), debt levy, prior year unused levy adjustment, and state special charges. She noted the resolution already incorporated $50,000 in committee contingency funds, subject to change based on board amendments the following evening. Kurt Gibbs moved to approve and forward the resolution to the full board; the motion was seconded by Supervisor Sean and passed unanimously.

### 28th Avenue Properties — Disposition
Administrator Leonard explained that properties acquired along 28th Avenue for a potential highway corridor relocation are no longer needed for that purpose after consultation with the City of Wausau and Village of Maine confirmed neither municipality wished to pursue the corridor realignment. He proposed listing developable parcels on Wisconsin Surplus, with any proceeds going back to the highway reserve and potentially applied to the highway shop project. Kurt Gibbs supported listing at minimum prices equal to fair market value. The committee reached consensus directing the administrator to return at a future meeting with a parcel-by-parcel overview including evaluation prices, developability assessments, and proposed courses of action, rather than taking a formal vote at this meeting.

### Year-to-Date Budget Reports (through August 2026)
Finance Director Fensky reviewed August 2026 year-to-date reports, noting no major concerns. She flagged that the homeless shelter budget had been fully expended and would shift to contingency, that Clerk of Courts psychology evaluation fees were running high but expected to stay within budget, and that the social services consortia accounting and a working capital transfer from the social improvement fund would require budget amendments before year-end. Chair Robinson asked staff to investigate whether permit revenue in CPZ/zoning was trending down given lower-than-expected figures, and Fensky agreed to bring clarification to the next meeting. A loss in the investment allocation in April was noted as a temporary paper loss that had been recouped in subsequent months.

### Other Business — Cherokee County Park Land Acquisition
Vice Chair Pool raised the previously approved acquisition of land adjacent to Cherokee County Park and suggested pausing the purchase to see whether the new state legislature would renew Knowles-Nelson Stewardship Program funding, which he said he now assessed at near-certainty. He proposed waiting approximately eight to ten months to potentially use Knowles-Nelson funds instead of county taxpayer funds, noting the land was not going anywhere. Chair Robinson acknowledged the suggestion and indicated the administration would take it under advisement, with no formal vote taken.

## Public Comment
Three members of the public spoke. A former supervisor spoke remotely, urging the committee to use the opioid settlement fund — holding approximately $2.5 million with $3.7 million more expected through 2038 — to offset opioid-related county expenses rather than taxing residents when substantial funds exist in reserve, suggesting up to $490,000 annually could be applied after retaining funds for jail treatment programs. A second speaker, who identified himself as an engineer and finance professional, presented a spreadsheet he emailed to committee members showing that phasing in the NCHC transfer over two years would reduce taxes by $3 million while maintaining reserves above policy minimums, and argued the county consistently over-budgets highway expenditures, making sound decision-making difficult; he recommended applying $2 million of the $3.5 million transfer to the debt levy. A third speaker urged the committee to pause Flock license plate reader funding, arguing the platform as implemented constitutes mass surveillance with 30-day data retention accessible to over a thousand entities across multiple states, that ICE has conducted searches through the system, and that officers have misused it; he outlined specific policy reforms and directed committee members to unflockwausau.org/marathon.
