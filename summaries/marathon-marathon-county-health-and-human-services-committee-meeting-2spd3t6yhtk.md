## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Health and Human Services Committee held its regular meeting, receiving budget presentations from North Central Community Action Program, United Way of Marathon County (211 services), and the ADRC of Central Wisconsin, all of which highlighted growing financial pressures amid flat or reduced funding streams. The committee also received a quarterly update on the county-city homelessness response foundational framework and a preview of the strategic plan survey process. No significant funding changes were approved; the ADRC's requested levy increase was confirmed as already built into the 2027 budget.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of August 5th, 2026 Meeting Minutes
Minutes were moved and seconded without discussion. The chair called for a voice vote and the motion carried.

### North Central Community Action Program - 2027 Budget Presentation
Executive Director Diane Senholtz presented CAP's 2025 impact in Marathon County: $2.6 million in goods and services including weatherization of 130 homes, emergency furnace repairs for 100 households, crisis rental payments of $289,000 for 681 households, and $300,000 in transitional housing for 10 households. Administrator Leonard noted the county's annual funding of $33,757 is used as match to leverage additional federal and state dollars, and stated his plan is to fund North Central CAP at the same level as prior years absent committee direction otherwise. Supervisor Rosenberg asked about gaps in service; Senholtz indicated rental assistance typically runs out by end of October. The committee raised the loss of AmeriCorps funding and Senholtz confirmed it is gone entirely.

### United Way of Marathon County / 211 Services - 2027 Budget Presentation
Ben Lee presented 211 operations, noting the service covers nine counties and is on pace to exceed 2025 call volumes due to increased income support and assistance calls and the Ringle tornado response. He described how 211 data directly informs United Way's $2.1 million in annual community investments, citing child care and food pantry access as examples where data-driven investments shifted community outcomes. Administrator Leonard noted the county's annual 211 contract is $40,000, also planned to remain flat. Lee highlighted new rural outreach efforts including the Athens Caring Circle food pantry, which arose from 211 zip code data identifying unmet need.

### ADRC of Central Wisconsin - Levy Request and Budget Update
Executive Director Mike Ray reported that nutrition program costs have risen nearly 50% over five years while funding has remained flat, forcing service reductions including cutting home-delivered meals from five to four per week for highest-need clients and three per week for moderate-need clients. The ADRC requested a $41,000 levy increase, which Administrator Leonard confirmed is already built into the 2027 budget draft; all four member counties must agree to the increase. Ray noted the Older Americans Act has not been reauthorized in nearly four years and Wisconsin provides only $650,000 statewide for nutrition programs compared to Minnesota's roughly $11 million. Supervisor Rosenberg suggested the committee consider passing resolutions advocating for increased state and federal funding. Ray also noted the ADRC's food vendor contract for Marathon, Lincoln, and Langlade counties is going out for RFP imminently and he hopes competitive bidding will help control costs.

### 5B: Discussion and Possible Action to Forward to County Board - PFAS Well Sampling and Mobile Crisis
Supervisor Robinson (identified as John in transcript) raised two items: the need to expand mobile crisis services beyond business hours, and an emerging PFAS-related well sampling grant opportunity through the DNR's PAS trust fund ($131 million pool). He noted the village of Maine area near the Greystone 3M facility has some of the state's highest PFAS readings and no current coordinated sampling. Robinson indicated grant proposals may be released in late September or October with an initial six-month award window and a 20% match that could be met with in-kind services. Chair Boots committed to discussing with Administrator Leonard and potentially scheduling a presentation at the next meeting, and asked Robinson to keep the committee apprised of DNR developments. The chair noted the topic needed to be formally agendized before further discussion.

### 6A: Homelessness Response Foundational Framework - Quarterly Update
Public Health Coordinator Megan Barnett delivered her quarter-three update, reporting progress across four foundational framework focus areas: a steering committee has been formed with city and county leadership, a standardized intake form collecting uniform population data launched August 10th, a 5-year strategic planning session with 50-plus partners is scheduled for October, and 16 key informant interviews with people experiencing homelessness have been completed to inform the plan. She reported 25% of WMC intake data from last year showed out-of-county residents (primarily Milwaukee, Madison, and Chicago), and confirmed at least two individuals were recently dropped off at Bridge Street Mission by Lincoln County law enforcement. Supervisor Rosenberg raised the question of communities petitioning the state attorney general regarding unlawful transport of unhoused individuals; Administrator Leonard explained a legal opinion has been requested, likely by Eau Claire, and outlined the Chapter 51 transfer process. Supervisor Conway praised the framework as exactly what the county board had asked for. Barnett also highlighted recent community wins: 15 Bridge Street Mission residents secured jobs, the Mong American Center and Open Door landed a grant for two 15-passenger vans for second and third shift transportation, and the first 'Stability Starts Here' multi-agency service expo was held at the community partners campus.

### 6B: Strategic Plan Survey Update
Chair Boots summarized results of 35 survey responses (roughly half from county board members, half from department heads) identifying top strategic plan priorities. 'Healthiest' and 'Safest' categories ranked highest overall. He noted 19 strategic objectives will go to the community for input, with a 20th to be added regarding preservation of agricultural land, added in part due to community interest around wind and solar development. He committed to emailing the full list to supervisors and presenting to other committees in October.

### Future Agenda Items and Tour of Bridge Street Mission
The committee tentatively agreed to add a tour of Bridge Street Mission following the September 30th meeting. Barnett agreed to coordinate with Bridge Street Mission to confirm the date. Ben Lee also highlighted that 47% of Marathon County residents 55 and older do not have enough to get by per the ALICE report, and urged a coordinated county and nonprofit advocacy effort for aging-related funding, noting Representative Schneider is authoring a bill for increased 211 funding.
