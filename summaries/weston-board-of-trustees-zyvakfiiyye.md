## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees held its regular November meeting, approving the 2027 operating budget and related fund budgets on split 4-3 votes, with the three newest trustees voting against citing excessive borrowing and debt concerns. The board also approved a $4 million promissory note to One Community Bank for the Mountain Bay Metro Police Department headquarters project, passing 6-1. Additional action included approving special assessments for road reconstruction projects and a condominium plat, while a lengthy public hearing drew sharp criticism from residents over the village's debt levels.

## Key Discussions
### Public Hearing: 2027 Budget
Finance staff presented a balanced budget of $10,794,907, with a total levy of $9,925,976, representing an increase of $1,389,554. The tax rate is estimated at $5.54, a 0.99% decrease, partly due to the closure of TIF District No. 1. Two residents spoke in opposition: Jim Street delivered pointed criticism, arguing the board 'maxed out the credit card' and spent every dollar from the TIF closure rather than paying down debt or saving, specifically thanking Trustees Clark, Olson, and a 'Trustee Patton' for voting against the budget. Morgan Woodruff questioned whether the village's financial ambitions were exceeding its budget, citing the new police headquarters and proposed park development while existing projects remain unfinished.

### Resolution 2026-028: Adopt 2027 Operating Budget
Board discussion prior to the vote surfaced ongoing disagreements about borrowing. Trustees Beck, Clark, and Olson all stated their concerns had not changed from prior budget meetings, characterizing the village as having maxed out its borrowing capacity. Trustee Ermeling raised the issue of zero-based budgeting and said the village missed an opportunity to use one-time TIF revenues to offset recurring costs or pay down debt. Village President Maloney acknowledged the concern about small capital items being financed through borrowing rather than included in the operating budget. The budget passed 4-3 with Beck, Clark, and Olson voting nay.

### Resolution 2026-029: Adopt Debt Service, Special Revenue, Capital Projects, and Proprietary Fund Budgets
This companion budget resolution was voted on immediately after the operating budget. Discussion was brief. The resolution passed 4-3, with Beck, Clark, and Olson again voting nay.

### Resolution 2026-027: Authorize $4 Million Borrowing — Mountain Bay Metro Police Department
Chris, identified as regional president of One Community Bank, appeared in person to answer questions about the loan terms. A key point of discussion was prepayment language in the note; Chris clarified that extra principal payments are allowed with no penalty and would reduce overall interest cost, but the village must still make regular monthly payments. Trustee Clark raised concerns about the timeliness of receiving the attorney review memo (dated October, delivered the day of the meeting) and asked whether the loan remained the lowest bid received — finance confirmed it was, at a locked rate. The resolution passed 6-1.

### Police Department Report
Chief reported 2,223 calls and 471 traffic stops with 307 citations for the month. On the police headquarters construction, the chief said frost walls are being poured, walls are scheduled for delivery December 18, and the project appears slightly ahead of schedule for a mid-to-late 2027 completion. A trustee pressed the chief on the lack of public updates about the TC Energy pipeline easement conflict, noting no oversight video had been posted since August 27 and that meeting minutes contained only limited references. The chief acknowledged Elizabeth handles video uploads and said he would follow up. Trustees also discussed the department's decision to stop assisting residents locked out of their homes due to community feedback and liability concerns.

### Fire Department Report
Chief announced that Captain Cody Ewan is being promoted from engineer following a captain's resignation three months ago, and Steven Hos will be promoted to engineer, leaving an open firefighter position to be filled after the first of the year. Recruitment closes Wednesday with interviews expected in mid-December.

### Mak Miller Park Master Plan and Cost Estimate
Sean presented a broad master plan from Rettler Corporation, estimated at approximately $4 million, emphasizing that approval authorizes no spending and is needed to qualify for grants and to document park impact fee usage from nearby development such as Green Tree. Trustees and President Maloney discussed public confusion over the cost estimate — particularly a widely circulated claim about a $2 million concession stand, which staff clarified was an off-hand comment at a parks meeting, not a board commitment. Michael noted a comparable concession/restroom line in the Kennedy Park estimate was $800,000. The board discussed whether the plan should be approved with or without dollar figures attached. No vote was taken at this meeting; Trustee Beck asked that Kennedy Park's concession stand question be returned to the Parks Committee.

## Public Comment
Four speakers addressed the board. Jim Street (address on file, Weston) spoke during the budget public hearing, sharply criticizing the board majority for spending all TIF closure funds rather than paying down debt, stating the village is 'bumped right up against their debt limit' for nearly 20 years, and naming Maloney, Zeyghami, Ermeling, and Lopes-Serrao as the four who 'sent us down this path.' Morgan Woodruff (6607 [inaudible] Lane) spoke during the public hearing questioning whether ambitions exceed the budget, raised concerns about the police headquarters and new park spending amid unfinished projects, and criticized the village's continued use of Flock cameras after other communities removed them. Jim Pissel (500-block street, Weston) spoke during general public comment, flagging procedural concerns: a missing organizational chart for agenda item 50, a request to move closed session items to open session, inadequate debt collection follow-up on agenda item 53, and vague language in the intergovernmental agreement with the Town of Weston regarding storm water cost-sharing. John Alman (1506 [inaudible] Avenue) spoke during public comment questioning the need for pickle ball courts at Mak Miller Park given location inconvenience, the $2 million concession stand figure, and the lack of a referendum on the police headquarters borrowing.
