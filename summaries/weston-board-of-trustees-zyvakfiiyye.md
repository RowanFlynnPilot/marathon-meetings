## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees held its regular meeting on Monday, November 16, 2026, highlighted by a contentious 4-3 vote to adopt the 2027 operating budget and an equally divided 4-3 vote on the debt service and capital funds budgets, with the three newer trustees (Beck, Clark, and Olson) voting against both over concerns about excessive borrowing and spending of TIF district closure funds. The board also approved a $4 million loan for the Mountain Bay Metro Police Department headquarters, approved a Mack Mueller Park master plan (without the cost estimate), and authorized an aquatic center building and pool evaluation funded from unassigned general fund balance rather than borrowing.

## Key Discussions
### Public Hearing: 2027 Budget
Finance staff presented the balanced 2027 budget totaling $10,794,907 in revenues and expenditures, with a total levy of $9,925,976 — an increase of $1,389,554 — driven partly by the closure of TIF District No. 1. Staff noted the village tax rate would decrease slightly to 5.54, though Marathon County's budget would add approximately 25 cents to the overall tax bill. Two members of the public spoke in opposition during the public hearing, with one arguing the board squandered a generational opportunity from TIF closure funds by maximizing borrowing rather than paying down debt, and another raising concerns about taking on new multi-million dollar projects while existing ones remain unfinished.

### Resolution 2026-028 – Adopt 2027 Operating Budget
The motion to adopt the 2027 operating budget was moved by Trustee Ermeling and seconded by Trustee Zeyghami. Trustees Beck, Clark, and Olson all stated they remained opposed to the budget due to excessive borrowing and missed opportunities to use TIF funds for debt reduction or savings rather than recurring costs. Trustees supporting the budget argued that the debt service reflects projects the full board had previously voted to pursue and that new firefighter funding was a key community priority. The resolution passed 4-3, with Beck, Clark, and Olson voting nay.

### Resolution 2026-029 – Adopt Debt Service, Special Revenue, Capital Projects, and Proprietary Funds 2027 Budgets
Moved by Trustee Lopes-Serrao and seconded by Trustee Zeyghami, this companion budget resolution covering non-general funds also passed 4-3. Trustees Beck, Clark, and Olson again voted nay, citing the same concerns about over-borrowing and lack of prioritization among capital projects. No additional extended debate occurred on this resolution beyond what had already been discussed on the operating budget.

### Resolution 2026-027 – Authorize $4 Million Borrowing for Mountain Bay Metro Police Department Headquarters
The regional president of One Community Bank attended to answer questions about the promissory note. Key discussion centered on the prepayment language in the loan documents, which the bank clarified allows additional principal payments with no penalty but does not eliminate the obligation to continue monthly payments. Trustee Zeyghami moved approval; Village President Maloney seconded. The resolution passed 6-1, with one trustee voting nay.

### Mack Mueller Park Master Plan and Cost Estimate
Sean from Parks presented the updated master plan for Mack Mueller Park, which includes pickle ball courts, a basketball court, a tennis court, a band shell, and other amenities, with a broad cost estimate of approximately $4 million. Trustee Lopes-Serrao moved to approve only the master plan — not the cost estimate — explaining that approving a cost estimate was unnecessary since no spending is authorized and a more precise figure would be needed for grants. Trustee Olson seconded. Several trustees noted the master plan is required to be eligible for state and federal grant funding and that no dollars are currently allocated. The motion passed 6-1, with one trustee voting nay.

### Aquatic Center Evaluation
Sean presented two proposals for an updated aquatic center evaluation — one from Raymaker and Associates for $16,000 (covering both pool and building, with $14,000 for pool and $2,000 for building) and one from WaterTech for $16,800 (pool only). Trustee Beck moved to approve the Raymaker proposal for both the building and pool evaluation. After Trustee Olson raised objections to funding the evaluation through capital borrowing, the motion was amended to specify funding from the unassigned general fund balance rather than borrowing. Trustee Clark seconded the amended motion, which passed. Staff noted the unassigned general fund balance is currently approximately $440,000 above the village's 33% policy threshold.

### Business 51 Storm Pond Construction Intergovernmental Agreement
Public Works Director Michael presented the intergovernmental agreement with Rothschild and Schofield for constructing a storm water retention pond within the Highway 29/Business 51 cloverleaf right-of-way. Discussion focused on the village's share growing from an estimated 125 pounds of phosphorus credit to 225 pounds, increasing the village's cost share to approximately 90% of the roughly $2.8 million construction cost. The board amended the agreement to cap the village's exposure at 5% above the bid cost estimate before requiring further board approval, then approved the amended agreement unanimously.

### Ross Avenue Riverbend to Paul's Intergovernmental Agreement
Trustee Lopes-Serrao moved approval and Trustee Zeyghami seconded. A resident speaker during public comment had raised concerns that the agreement language was vague and amounted to subsidizing the Town of Weston. Trustee Zeyghami noted that while the town benefits from storm water improvements, any annexation of land along the corridor would ultimately benefit the village's tax base. The agreement was approved without amendment; the board did not add specific dollar amounts or percentages to the agreement language despite the public comment request.

### Police Department Update and Communications
The police chief reported 2,223 calls for the month, 471 traffic stops, and 307 citations. He provided an update on the new police headquarters construction, noting footings are in, frost walls are being poured, walls are scheduled for delivery December 18, and the sewer lateral was connected the day of the meeting; a mid-to-late next year completion estimate was offered. Trustee Clark raised concerns that video updates on the police department's website had not been posted since August 27 and that the public had no easily accessible source for information about the TC Energy pipeline easement issue. The chief committed to improving communications. The chief also announced the department would no longer assist with residential lockouts, citing negative community feedback and liability concerns.

### Closed Session – Return to Open Session
The board returned from closed session at 9:29 p.m. A motion passed to move forward with a right-of-way purchase offer (the agreed-upon amount for a strip of land related to the Ross Avenue/Metro to Alderson project). The administrator's annual evaluation was deferred to the December meeting by unanimous vote to allow more time.

## Public Comment
Two speakers addressed the board during the public hearing on the 2027 budget. The first speaker spoke at length in opposition, arguing the board wasted a generational opportunity from the TIF district closure by spending every available dollar rather than paying down debt, praised the three trustees who voted against the capital borrowing plan, and criticized the four trustees who supported the budget for leaving a long-term debt burden. The second speaker asked the board to complete existing projects before taking on new ones such as a new police headquarters and a new park development, raised privacy concerns about flock cameras that other communities have removed, and urged the board to prioritize current residents over development-driven spending. During general public comment, three additional residents spoke. One questioned the lack of an organizational chart for committee appointments on the agenda, raised concerns about the intergovernmental agreement language for the Ross Avenue project, and urged the board to have the village attorney review it. A second speaker criticized the pickle ball court plans for Mack Mueller Park, questioned the reported $2 million concession stand figure, and argued large capital projects like the police headquarters should have gone to a referendum. A third speaker, who also spoke during the public hearing, expressed broader frustration with the budget and village spending, referenced concerns about flock cameras, criticized incomplete park projects, and drew the board's attention to a letter from a named community member distributed at the meeting regarding Mack Mueller Park.
