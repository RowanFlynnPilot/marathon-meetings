## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Parks and Recreation Committee met on Monday, August 31, 2026, and took action on several items including accepting a youth baseball scoreboard donation, recommending historic landmark designation for Yaki Park, approving updates to Chapter 2 of the city's comprehensive plan, directing staff to paint a center line on the River Edge Trail, and providing budget direction to the parks director for the 2027 budget cycle. The meeting was notable for substantive debate over pool staffing reductions, an urban forestry utility fee proposal, and multiple budget reduction strategies as the city faces fiscal constraints.

## Key Discussions
### Consideration of Minutes from July 6, 2026 Regular Meeting
The chair asked for any corrections to the minutes from the July 6, 2026 meeting. Alderperson Slonski moved to approve and Alderperson Lukens seconded. The minutes were approved unanimously with no corrections or debate.

### 3A: Accepting Donation of Scoreboard at Memorial Park and Approving Sponsorship Recognition
Parks staff (Jamie) explained that Wausau Youth Baseball is fully funding the scoreboard replacement at Memorial Park, as the previous Pepsi-branded scoreboard was removed when Pepsi changed its business arrangements. No sponsor has been secured yet; if one is found, it must be approved through the parks office per sponsorship policy. The item came before the committee due to the scoreboard's expected lifespan of 15-20 years exceeding the standard threshold, even though the cost is under $5,000. Alderperson Lukens asked whether the sign company would receive recognition in exchange for a discount; staff indicated that was not happening. Alderperson Slonski moved to accept the donation and Alderperson Neal seconded; the motion passed unanimously.

### 3B: Yaki Park Historical Landmark Designation
Staff and city planner Brad (last name not clearly stated) explained that designating Yaki Park as a historic landmark would require historic preservation commission approval at a public hearing on September 23, followed by plan commission and ultimately city council approval. Discussion clarified that the designation applies to the park grounds including the colonnade and fountain, not an adjacent historic home, and that landmark status carries more regulatory weight than a Class 2 historic district, requiring commission approval for any substantial changes. Staff noted the deed contains a reversion clause to the Yaki family if the city ever ceases public use, and the relationship between that clause and landmark status was flagged as an open question. Alderperson Trueblood moved to adopt the designation recommendation, Alderperson Slonski seconded, and the motion passed unanimously.

### 3C: Review of Natural Resources Section (Chapter 2) of City of Wausau Comprehensive Plan
City planner Carrie Edmonson presented updates to Chapter 2, noting that the parks department's input had been incorporated and that the chapter now reflects a more urban lens including green infrastructure and urban forestry goals. Committee members offered extensive additional feedback: Alderperson Lukens raised concerns about pesticide drift, native plantings, and groundwater contamination including PFAS and crumb rubber infill from artificial turf; Alderperson Slonski suggested strengthening wetland restoration language from 'consider developing' to actually developing such a program; Alderperson Trueblood raised lawn alternatives, native grasses, and reducing fertilizer use; Alderperson Lukens also suggested native grass education and light pollution as additions; and Alderperson Slonski suggested the city commit to making native planting transitions easier for homeowners. Alderperson Neal moved to approve Chapter 2 as presented with the new feedback incorporated, Alderperson Slonski seconded, and the motion passed unanimously.

### 3D: Request to Paint a Center Lane on the River Edge Trail
Alderperson Neal proposed painting a broken center line on the River Edge Trail from Winton to Oak Island, citing a similar project in Marquette, Michigan as a model, framing it as a psychological safety tool rather than an enforceable rule. Alderperson Trueblood supported the idea but raised concerns about the need for accompanying signage, noting that painted markings alone are often ignored and referencing the city's experience at the John Muir crosswalk; he also expressed interest in eventually creating separated bike and pedestrian lanes. Alderperson Slonski expressed mild concern about creating the appearance of an enforceable ordinance without actual enforcement. Staff confirmed there is currently no ordinance governing use of the trail. Alderperson Trueblood moved to proceed with painting the line using city staff on staff's own timeline to avoid additional contracting costs, with a cost report back to the committee before budgeting it as a maintenance item; Alderperson Neal seconded, and the motion passed unanimously.

### 3E: Department Budget Overview and Proposed Changes
Parks Director Jamie presented a 2027 budget proposal aiming for a roughly 1% increase over last year (driven by staffing costs) and outlined potential expense reductions and revenue increases requested by the mayor at approximately 2% savings. Key proposed cuts included rotating pool closures to reduce staffing costs (pools cost $449,000 annually with only 19% cost recovery), reducing wharf docks from seven to three (saving approximately $3,000), eliminating flower baskets from the general fund, potentially skipping the goats for one season and mowing invasive species instead, switching from tablet to liquid chlorine, and reducing the small projects budget by $5,000. A proposed revenue increase involved a new $2 per household per month urban forestry utility fee. Extensive debate occurred: Alderperson Trueblood opposed the urban forestry fee as a redundant charge on top of existing property taxes, while Alderperson Slonski supported it as a collective investment in a collective benefit, particularly if applied via utility bill to include tax-exempt properties. Alderperson Lukens raised concerns about pool closures impacting lower-income families and asked about non-resident fee differentiation. The committee ultimately passed three separate motions: (1) unanimously directing staff to include all operational expense savings except pool staffing and urban forestry fee in the mayor's budget proposal, with flower baskets eliminated from the general fund; (2) 3-1 recommending the urban forestry fee proposal to the mayor for potential ordinance action; and (3) 3-1 directing staff to include the pool staffing option of two pools open five days a week and three pools open on Saturday and Sunday (estimated savings of $19,854).

### 4A: Park Updates
Staff highlighted that work at the Oak Island skate park is nearing completion, with donated ramps expected to be finished by mid-September along with installation of a memorial plaque honoring two cousins whose families made donations. Alderperson Lukens requested consideration of an additional trash can on the northern section of the Riverlife Trail, primarily for dog waste; staff explained that trash cans are not placed along trails because there is no staff assigned to empty them regularly. Staff also confirmed that the previously broken water line to Yaki Park's fountain has been repaired and is ready to operate, but will not be turned on this season due to upcoming winterization; it will be operational next spring.

## Public Comment
One member of the public spoke. A representative who identified herself as a former director of the Leigh Yawkey Woodson Art Museum appeared on behalf of the Yawkey heirs and read a signed statement from five great-grandchildren of Cyrus Yawkey expressing full support for designating Yaki Park as a historic landmark, citing the importance of protecting the 106-year-old park deeded for public use in perpetuity. She left the signed statement with the committee.
