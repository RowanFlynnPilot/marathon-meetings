Dismissal of citizen complaint reconsideration (JV)

The meeting opened with a motion to dismiss the reconsideration of a citizen complaint identified only as 'JV.' The motion was made and seconded without debate, and was approved by voice vote. No discussion of the complaint's substance was recorded.

Wausau Police Department 2027 Budget Overview

In Chief Barnes's absence, a deputy chief-level official presented the proposed 2027 police budget. Key changes include ending the All Cities Management contract for adult crossing guards (saving ~$40,000), cutting the CSO budget by 50% (~$45,000 savings), maintaining the humane society non-dog stray contract at $18,500, restructuring the SRO contract to seek 100% school-year coverage by the district (~$50,000 savings), and reducing authorized sworn staff from 81 to 80 as foundation funding for the downtown officer program phases out. The city council also approved a $50,000 feasibility study into the UW-Stevens Point campus as a potential future police and fire facility.

Police Recruitment Update

Captain Pauls reported that a recruitment process launched in August drew just over 40 applicants, down sharply from approximately 400 applicants 25 years ago, though the quality of candidates was described as strong. The department is hiring 8 to 10 officers to address current vacancies and four anticipated retirements in early 2025. Twelve background investigations are currently underway, with at least three candidates expected to be brought to the commission at a special November 5th PFC meeting, as some candidates are also considering other departments.

Flock Safety ALPR System Update

Captain Graham provided an update on the department's Flock Safety automated license plate reader program, which has been before committee or council approximately seven or eight times since a contract was signed in 2023. He illustrated the system's reactive and proactive capabilities with a retail theft case in which ALPR identified a suspect vehicle, linked it to over $5,000 in additional thefts, and enabled a traffic stop while the suspect was returning to commit another theft. A commission member raised privacy concerns about potential misuse by officers or third parties, particularly in domestic violence situations; Captain Graham explained that public access to the system is not possible, that officer access requires multi-factor authentication, and that the system is CJIS compliant with monthly and annual audits. A transparency portal on the department's website was noted. The chair reminded the commission that its jurisdiction is largely limited to hiring, discipline, and investigations, making the discussion informational in nature.

Fire Department 2027 Budget and Hiring Update

Fire Chief [name not stated in transcript] reported that proposed 2027 budget cuts are achieved primarily by leaving the Division Chief of Training position—vacant since January 2025—open until July 1, 2027, and leaving a second position in the inspection bureau open for six months following an upcoming retirement. On hiring, the department interviewed two candidates in closed session earlier that day and had three more interviews scheduled that afternoon, with an additional application received the prior Friday. Nine new firefighter apprentices began three weeks prior and are now on 48-hour shifts while attending NTC two to three days per week.

Fire Department Succession Planning

The fire chief described plans to present a revised organizational chart to HR and the finance department before year-end, aimed at building redundancy and succession pathways. A key element would formally integrate the community care paramedic position under the EMS Division Chief, given that roughly 80% of city calls are EMS-related and the division currently has limited leadership redundancy. The chief noted a Lieutenant promotion interview will also need to be scheduled, potentially requiring a special December PFC meeting.
