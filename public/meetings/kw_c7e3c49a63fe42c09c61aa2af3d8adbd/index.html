The Village of Kronenwetter Parks Committee held its September 8, 2026 meeting, approving a fundraising brick program for Bushka Park and voting to add a zipline to the 2029 Friendship Park capital improvement plan. The committee also reviewed the full 10-year capital improvement plan and discussed logistics for the upcoming September 12 Bushka Park 5K Run/Walk fundraiser. Approval of August 17, 2026 Park Meeting Minutes

A committee member noted a spelling error in the minutes — 'bike rakes' should read 'bike racks.' A motion to approve with that correction was made by Patty and seconded by Tim. The motion carried on a voice vote with no opposition.

Bushka Park Fundraising Brick Components

Greg (Director of Public Works) presented a fundraising brick program through a company called Gift Bricks, noting that Wastatile agreed to donate all bricks needed and that the etching would be handled by Gift Bricks in southern Wisconsin. The committee agreed to offer three brick sizes — 4x8 at $50, 8x8 at $120, and 8x16 at $250 — representing roughly a 150% markup over cost, with black epoxy fill only for simplicity. The committee discussed advertising at the upcoming 5K, the farmers market, and via QR codes, and agreed to post ordering information on the village website. A motion was made by Patty and seconded by Elizabeth to direct the director of public works to move forward with the brick fundraiser at the agreed sizes and pricing; the motion carried unanimously.

September 12 Bushka Park 5K Run/Walk Fundraiser Event

Mishka reported that all nine police-recommended intersection volunteer positions had been filled with high-visibility vests, and that water bottles and a digital timer clock had been purchased. The start and finish line will be near the shelter at Bushka Park at 10 a.m. Committee members discussed volunteer assignments at intersections, the absence of a rain backup plan ('all weather' event), and the need for a first aid station. A staff member raised the concern that a quorum of trustees attending could constitute a walking quorum, and members were advised to be mindful of that. A donation box with a QR code, created by Kim, will be stationed at the main table.

Capital Improvement Project Costs

Greg presented a 10-year CIP list including: Bushka Park playground ($200,000), Norm Plaza Dog Park fencing ($50,000), Friendship Park rubberized surfacing ($80,000, 2029), a bandstand and bathrooms ($150,000), and Municipal Park bathrooms ($100,000). Significant debate arose over the Municipal Park bathroom, with Tim arguing the remote location, high maintenance costs, vandalism risk, and distance from water and sewer made it a poor investment, while other members countered that a disc golf course at that site would increase usage and justify the facility. Greg noted staff prefer porter potties due to vandalism and maintenance issues. The committee agreed to keep the bathroom line item but defer the decision, and also agreed to add the disc golf course to the quick fix/future list. A motion by Dan to recommend the capital improvement plan as presented to the village board was seconded and carried unanimously.

Quick Fix Item List

Greg reviewed completed and in-progress quick fix items, including power washing playground equipment, ordering a replacement slide for North Plaza, ordering inclusive swings, and updating park signage to reflect current ordinances. A committee member asked that park benches along walking trails be added to the list. Greg confirmed the broken globe piece at Bushka Park is on order and that the volleyball net at Smith Park has not yet been installed. Greg also noted he would investigate a boundary encroachment concern at Towering Pines park involving bike path users the following day using GIS.

Friendship Park Zipline

Mishka proposed using approximately $20,000 in savings from the reduced wellhead trail project to install a zipline at Friendship Park, citing a price of roughly $20,000 and noting it could boost community excitement and generate positive press for park fundraising. Greg confirmed there is adequate space at Friendship Park. Members debated whether the expenditure would undermine Bushka Park fundraising momentum, with some arguing it would signal village investment in parks while others felt Bushka Park should remain the clear priority. The committee ultimately declined to fund a zipline immediately due to uncertainty about trail project final costs and the desire to focus on Bushka Park, but Dan moved to add the zipline to the 2029 Friendship Park CIP line, increasing that budget from $80,000 to $100,000. The motion was seconded by Tim and carried unanimously. The committee also discussed the ADA accessibility advantage of using the bucket seat option on the zipline, which costs approximately $1,000 more.

Consideration of Items for Future Agenda

Members discussed the uncertain future of the 'Friends of the Parks' volunteer fundraising group, noting that the staff member who had been organizing it (Jennifer) is no longer with the village and little progress appears to have been made. Members discussed whether the CLIP committee would absorb parks oversight in the interim, with Dan and Patty both serving on CLIP. Members expressed interest in reviving the Friends of the Parks effort, possibly tied to Saturday's 5K event as a recruitment opportunity. A brief discussion occurred about committee compensation being deposited into the general parks fund rather than specifically to Bushka Park donations, with Greg clarifying it goes to the overall parks fund and could be redirected by committee vote with board approval.
