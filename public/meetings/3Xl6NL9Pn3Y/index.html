Discussion

Public Comment — Edgar Tax-Deeded Parcels

Dwayne Warner of Edgar appeared to express interest in two back-tax parcels in the Village of Edgar, explaining he owns surrounding property and that the land — consisting of creek bed and swamp — is not developable. He noted the Village of Edgar administrator indicated the village wanted to return the parcels to him to consolidate ownership. His public comment directly preceded and informed the committee's subsequent action on agenda item 5B3.

Approval of Minutes — October 7th and 12th Meetings

Chair Gibbs moved to approve the minutes of the October 7th and 12th meetings. A supervisor identified as 'Nis' (likely Nisl or similar) seconded. No discussion was held and the motion carried unanimously.

5B3 — County-Owned Parcels in Village of Edgar

Administrator Leonard outlined four options for two narrow, undevelopable tax-deeded parcels: sell to the Village of Edgar for its $100 offer, negotiate directly with adjacent owner Dwayne Warner, entertain a third interested party on one parcel, or re-list on Wisconsin Surplus at a lower starting price. The presiding officer favored re-listing on Surplus to avoid favoring one party. A motion was made to re-list both parcels on Wisconsin Surplus for 30 days at a $500 opening bid per parcel, with the matter returning to the board if unsold. It was noted that the county's back-tax costs ($568 and $440 respectively per parcel) would likely be a wash at the $500 price. The motion carried unanimously.

5B1 — Highway Facility Construction Resolution (Resolution 68-26)

The committee considered Resolution 68-26 authorizing staff to proceed with Phase 4 construction of the proposed new county highway facility. Highway Commissioner Kevin [last name inaudible] confirmed that bids had been received and validated, and that the guaranteed maximum price figure in the resolution represents a ceiling — actual costs may come in lower. Chair Gibbs moved to approve the resolution and forward it to the full county board for consideration that evening. The motion carried unanimously with no notable opposition.

5B2 — Budget Amendment: Additional Child Care Support Funding

Staff member Kristen [last name inaudible] presented a straightforward budget amendment to accept $12,582 in additional state child support funding earned through strong 2024 performance by the county's child support unit. Chair Gibbs moved to approve the amendment and forward it to the full board; Vice Chair Pool seconded. The motion carried unanimously with no discussion.

5B4 — Offer to Purchase 108 Myron Street, Wausau ($1 Sale to City)

Administrator Leonard explained that the county-owned property at 108 Myron Street has an appraised value of $0 due to interior mold and hazardous conditions, with an estimated demolition cost of up to $25,000. The City of Wausau offered $1 to acquire the property, demolish the building, and return the land to housing use through its Community Development Authority. Corporation counsel noted that under Wisconsin Chapter 292, the county as a tax-delinquency acquirer is exempt from environmental liability upon transfer. Chair Gibbs raised concern about ongoing environmental responsibility; counsel and the administrator clarified that liability transfers with the sale on an as-is basis. The motion to accept the $1 offer passed with one abstention.

5B5 — Resolution on 2030 Census Residency Rules

The presiding officer introduced a resolution opposing proposed federal changes to census residency rules (Chapters 60 and 70 of the Code of Federal Regulations) that would count Wisconsin snowbirds as residents of southern states where they winter, potentially costing Wisconsin a congressional seat and reducing its share of $2.8 trillion in federally allocated funding. Chair Gibbs noted NACO is also engaged on the issue. Supervisor [name inaudible] expressed support but wanted to confirm the resolution was narrow in scope — focused only on residency counting — before agreeing. The public comment period on the federal rule closes November 2nd. The motion to approve the resolution and forward it to the full board carried unanimously.

5B6 — Resolution: Operation Greenlight

Chair Gibbs described Operation Greenlight as a five-year-old program originating in New York that encourages municipalities to illuminate buildings in green during Veterans Week (November 4–11) to honor military veterans and raise awareness of services provided through the county's CVSO and the VA. A supervisor identified as 'Nisth' moved to support Resolution R71-26; Vice Chair Pool seconded. The motion carried unanimously with no opposition.

5B7 — Transfer of Property Adjacent to Highway Q

Highway Commissioner Kevin explained that the parcel appears to be old road right-of-way from a previous alignment of Highway 29, resulting in confused joint ownership with the town. The town agreed to accept a quitclaim deed, and county staff ensured Highway Q right-of-way was preserved in the CSM. The item had already been approved by the Infrastructure Committee. A supervisor moved to transfer the property; Chair Gibbs seconded. The motion carried unanimously.

6A — Year-to-Date Budget Update

Finance staff member Sam presented the year-to-date budget through July, noting the county is at 58.3% (7/12ths) of the year with payroll at 54.41% benchmark. Areas flagged for monitoring include Clerk of Courts professional services (likely to exceed budget due to court-ordered evaluations), Corporation Counsel legal fees (expected to be covered by the North Central Healthcare intergovernmental agreement), DA revenue shortfalls from discovery fee changes, and the Medical Examiner's first year of operations. Chair Gibbs praised the finance department's improved reporting and raised questions about the administrative burden of federal changes; staff noted the state has largely protected the county from shortfalls through legislative fixes, though impacts on North Central Healthcare charity care remain a longer-term concern.

6B — 2027 Budget / Airport Minimum Revenue Guarantee

Vice Chair Pool raised questions about the $150,000 annual levy for the airport minimum revenue guarantee program, noting the 2026 funds had not yet been deployed and asking whether they would roll over. Administrator Leonard explained that Synergy has secured community matching pledges and a revised fund management agreement is being finalized for transfer of the 2026 funds to Synergy; a $950,000 Small Community Air Service Development Grant was recently awarded for Detroit route expansion. The administrator indicated he does not expect a rollover of the 2026 $150,000 and suggested the board evaluate 2027 funding after the budget is adopted. The 2027 proposed budget was confirmed to have been published in the Wausau Daily Herald as required.
