The Village of Weston Community Life & Public Safety Committee met and addressed code enforcement updates, fire/EMS/police department reports, a new liquor license recommendation, and a key vote on a proposed pedestrian crosswalk at Newport Avenue and Business 51. The committee approved recommending a Class B beer and liquor license for Trailside Sports Bar and Grill, and voted to instruct the DOT to include the Newport Avenue crosswalk in design plans for the Business 51 reconstruction project, with one dissenting vote.

Discussion

Public Comment

Jim Po of 512 Girl Street, Weston, offered public comment. He humorously presented a wooden number five to recognize 6202 Ross Avenue for being on the code enforcement list for five years, calling it 'the pinnacle of commitment to municipal nuisance.' He also raised substantive questions about the proposed Business 51 crosswalk, questioning whether the $60,000 expenditure was a need or a want, how many residents would actually use it, and whether corridor businesses had been consulted or invited to the meeting.

Code Enforcement Update

Staff presented a code enforcement report showing over 100 cases opened annually, with 23 active cases older than one year including one dating to 2021 at a property with abandoned vehicles and solid waste. Code enforcement staff explained the 2021 case involves family deaths and medical issues that slowed compliance, but that the property owner's daughter has been cooperating and making progress this summer. Committee members questioned whether leaving resolved cases open was accurate or inflating case counts, and debated at what point long-standing cases should be escalated to legal action. Staff maintained that as long as progress is being made, they continue working cooperatively rather than pursuing court action, which can be costly and may not address the root cause.

Fire, EMS, and SAFER Report

Fire Chief presented a written report. A committee member asked about mold at Station 2, and Chief confirmed there had been water leaks which are believed to be fixed, with abatement and repair contractors lined up. Omni Glass had conducted exterior assessments and tracked the water intrusion to a second-floor window that was subsequently sealed.

Police Department / Metro Report

Police Chief reported 2,290 calls for service, 54 crashes, and 596 traffic stops. Committee members noted a spike in crashes particularly in the Brill area attributable to highway construction causing frequent lane changes. The Village of Weston recorded 24 crashes compared to 12 in Schofield and 14 in Rothschild.

Humane Officer Training Presentation

Officer Kum Mua, a night-shift officer with approximately 14 years of experience, presented on her 40-hour humane officer certification training through the Wisconsin Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection. She described how animal cases differ from typical law enforcement calls in that they require extensive follow-up monitoring and are often subjective, requiring on-site assessment of animals including physical inspection. Chief noted the department has three certified humane officers who handle major cases while other officers manage routine animal calls. Discussion also covered animal registration compliance, with staff noting it is somewhat easier now that registration is handled in-house rather than through a third-party online system.

Class B Beer and Liquor License – Doug's LLC / Trailside Sports Bar and Grill

Village Clerk Pam Bream confirmed online that all required paperwork, background checks, and publication fees had been submitted and a provisional license issued. The committee moved to recommend approval of the Class B beer and Class B liquor license for Doug's LLC doing business as Trailside Sports Bar and Grill. The recommendation will go to the full Village Board at its November 16th meeting for final approval.

Business 51 Reconstruction – Newport Avenue Pedestrian Crossing

Village engineer Mike presented the DOT's planned reconstruction of Business 51, currently scheduled for 2030-2031, and asked whether the committee wanted to include a pedestrian refuge island crossing at Newport Avenue in the design. The village's cost share would be roughly one-third, estimated at $40,000–$60,000 at today's prices. He noted the road median at Newport is currently only four feet wide and people are observed crossing diagonally without a safe refuge. Public works endorsed including it in design. Committee members generally supported it for future-proofing given nearby businesses, apartments, and the junior high school, with one member noting it would set up the corridor for potential future development. One member voted in opposition. The committee voted to direct the DOT to move forward with designing the crosswalk into the project plans.
