The Kronenwetter Plan Commission held a special September 10, 2026 meeting focused primarily on a conditional use permit for a Marathon County Highway Facility proposed at 2384-2388 Old Highway 51. The meeting drew significant public comment raising concerns about coal dust health hazards, road maintenance responsibility, tax revenue loss, and cemetery compatibility. The commission did not take a final vote, instead discussing conditions and leaning toward postponing a decision to its September 21 meeting to allow time for a written agreement between the county and St. Therese Gate of Heaven Cemetery and to resolve outstanding issues around road cost-sharing and zoning ordinance completion.

Discussion

Conditional Use Permit - Marathon County Highway Facility (Public Hearing)

Multiple public speakers addressed the commission. Joel Straub, Marathon County Supervisor for District 15, raised health and safety concerns about coal dust drift from the adjacent WPS power plant, citing state statute 709 on property condition disclosures, and urged the commission to require air quality data before approving. Speaker (identified as Mr. Baker) requested two conditions: a jurisdictional transfer of Business 51 from the village to the county for road maintenance, and widening Business 51 to four lanes from south of Village Way to the facility driveway. Kent Sharnosky argued the application failed all five conditional use criteria, challenged whether the required M2 zoning ordinance amendment had been properly completed, cited Wisconsin court cases placing the burden of proof on the applicant, and called the deal one-sided with no benefit to Kronenwetter residents. Bernie Kramer criticized the absence of village administrative leadership and echoed road upgrade concerns. Attorney Matt Edie spoke on behalf of St. Therese Gate of Heaven Cemetery, noting potential notice issues with the prior zoning change and requesting conditions to protect cemetery operations regarding noise, hours, and buffers.

Marathon County Response to Public Hearing Concerns

Marathon County Deputy Administrator Chris Holman described a hastily drafted two-page memo outlining a voluntary condition proposal to address St. Therese Cemetery concerns: a solid 8-foot fence along approximately 1,200 feet of the property line, supplemented by evergreen trees and shrubs, to be triggered when the active cemetery operations approach within 100 feet of the property line within a 12-month notice window. Highway Commissioner Kevin Lane addressed traffic concerns, stating JT Engineering analysis found no turn lanes would be required at current 5,000 vehicles/day volume on Old 51, and that the new location would have far fewer conflict points than the existing facility on 17th Avenue. Holman acknowledged coal dust concerns but stated WPS is federally and state regulated, noted a 2010 lawsuit resolved particulate emissions issues, and said they planned a higher MERV-rated HVAC filter as a proactive measure. He could not commit to jurisdictional road transfer or cost-sharing, stating those decisions require county committee and board approval.

Commission Discussion on Conditions and Path Forward

Commission members discussed the open-ended nature of unresolved conditions including the cemetery buffer agreement (not yet finalized), road maintenance cost-sharing on Old Business 51, and whether the M2 zoning ordinance change had been properly completed as required by village ordinance. Commissioner Bruce noted he wanted to see the fence/buffer agreement in writing before approving it as a condition. Trustee Lesniak summarized three key issues requiring resolution: finalization of the cemetery buffer agreement, a road cost-sharing or maintenance arrangement for Old Business 51, and confirmation the zoning change was properly codified by ordinance. The commission did not reach a vote, with members leaning toward postponing to the September 21 meeting to allow conditions to be properly documented, while county representatives requested specific direction on outstanding issues to address before that meeting.

Zoning Change - William Sywinsky, 1900/1904 Lea Road

William Sywinsky appeared and briefly explained the request to rezone 26.7 acres from Agricultural-Residential to RR-5 Rural Residential, stating the property has been in the family since 1972, his parents have passed, the family is trying to sell the home, and the rezoning is needed because the parcel is under 10 acres and they wish to adjust property lines. No other public speakers addressed this item. No vote was taken at this stage as it was the public hearing portion.

Text Amendment - Chapter 520 Zero Lot Line Homes

Bernie Kramer was the only public speaker, asking for more information on how zero lot line homes would be integrated with existing large lots in Kronenwetter, whether all homes in a new development could be zero lot line, and what the strategy would be for mixing lot sizes. No commission discussion or vote was recorded on this item beyond receiving public input.

Approval of Meeting Minutes (Items F and G)

Commissioner Bruce moved to approve two sets of minutes as presented; Dick seconded. No discussion. Motion carried 6-0.
