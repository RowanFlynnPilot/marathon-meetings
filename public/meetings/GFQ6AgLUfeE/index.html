The Wausau Economic Development Committee met on September 15, 2025, covering several significant development matters including survey results for the contaminated 1300 Cleveland Avenue site, a proposed mixed-use development agreement with Wangard for the Riverlife South project, and an early-stage public housing expansion proposal from the CDA. The Wangard development agreement failed to pass after a 2-1 vote, while the committee directed staff to schedule a green team meeting with the DNR and prepare a formal deed restriction formalizing non-industrial use of the Cleveland Avenue property.

Discussion

Public Comment

Four members of the public spoke. Colleen Armstrong, owner of property adjacent to the proposed Eminent Development Corporation site at Second Street and 2011 East Bridge Street, said she received no formal notification about the multi-story mixed-use project and demanded the city explain the oversight and pause the agreement until a neighborhood meeting is held. Tom Kian, identified as the neighborhood representative for the Cleveland Avenue project, urged the committee to ensure remediation is done concurrently with redevelopment rather than in advance, and asked that the new comprehensive plan formally reflect a non-industrial future land use for the property. Drew Wowski expressed support for accelerating Cleveland Avenue development, noting survey respondents favored green space and community amenities paired with housing. Becky Hda, a Cleveland Avenue-area resident, read directly from the survey's open-ended responses to emphasize that neighbors overwhelmingly want contamination cleanup before any other decisions are made.

Approval of Minutes from July 7th Economic Development Committee Meeting

A motion was made by Alder Kilian and seconded by Alder Lukans to approve the minutes. The vote passed unanimously with no opposition noted.

Survey Results for 1300 Cleveland Avenue

Assistant City Planner Carrie Edmonson presented results from 39 completed surveys following a May 28 neighborhood meeting. The top preferred non-industrial land use was a community center or senior center, followed by active outdoor recreation; a majority supported integrating housing if it enabled funding of preferred uses. Alder Kilian directed staff to schedule a green team meeting with the DNR before the next ED meeting to discuss remediation funding and align cleanup plans with development, and called for a formal deed restriction or resolution cementing non-industrial status for the property. The chair agreed and directed staff to bring a deed restriction proposal to the next meeting. Alder Neil emphasized that remediation depth is project-specific and that the city has previously committed to remediating to residential standards. Alder Lukans requested a cost breakdown for remediation under different development scenarios. No vote was taken on this item as it was informational with direction given to staff.

East Thomas Street and McClary Street RFI Update

Economic Development Manager Patrick reported that no formal proposals were received after the RFI was released in April, though informal discussions continue with entities including Habitat for Humanity. Staff recommended keeping the RFI open to allow continued negotiations and marketing. Alder Kilian noted approximately $600,000 in ARPA funds had been transferred to the CDA for infill housing. The committee voted unanimously to keep the RFI open and continue marketing the sites.

Development Agreement with Wangard for Riverlife South Project

Director Fifick presented a development agreement for 920 North First Street and 15 Fulton Street, translating the previously approved term sheet into a binding contract. Key terms include the developer purchasing city land for $100,000, a total anticipated development cost of $36.1 million, and the developer receiving 80% of new property tax generated via pay-as-you-go TIF reimbursement up to $4.84 million over a maximum of 17 years, with a 5.75% interest rate and a maximum total reimbursement of $8.35 million plus a look-back provision. Isaac from Quarles confirmed the material terms matched the approved term sheet. Alder Kilian stated she had never supported the project and would vote no. Alder Neil moved to approve, stating the numbers make sense and he could find no negatives. The motion was seconded by Alder Lukans. The vote resulted in two in favor and one opposed (Alder Kilian), with the chair also voting no, causing the motion to fail as it did not achieve a majority.

Proposed Meeting Time Change

The committee discussed moving the meeting start time from 5:15 to 5:30 p.m. The chair relayed that absent committee member Alder Tierney had communicated she was fine with 5:15 but not earlier. The motion to change the meeting time passed unanimously.

Accepting Donation of 313 North 1st Avenue Property

Director Fifick explained the 0.06-acre parcel, located behind Bridge Street Mission, was being donated by former owners MBX after the city's attorney's office worked through the chain of title. Alder Kilian raised concerns about potential contamination given the property's prior use as an ice and fuel company, and questioned whether a phase one or phase two environmental assessment had been done. The chair noted the property has no road access and is essentially a lawn strip between two residential properties, suggesting possible sale to an adjacent owner. Despite contamination concerns, the committee voted to accept the donation.

Wausau CDA Proposed New Public Housing Development

CDA Assistant Director Julie Burkinmeer presented an early-stage proposal to develop 18 to 64 new public housing units by repositioning the CDA's existing 46 scattered-site units to a Section 18 project-based voucher funding model, freeing up HUD Faircloth units for new construction. She emphasized the extreme demand—the wait list for the CDA's single one-bedroom unit is over five years—and that rents would remain income-based at 30% of household income. The proposed development would be in one consolidated location rather than scattered sites, requiring at least one acre. No specific site has been identified yet. The CDA board is expected to consider the repositioning plan in October. No vote was taken; this was an informational presentation.
