The Kronenwetter Administrative Policy Committee met on September 15, 2026, focusing primarily on a lengthy finance director update covering the 2027 budget process, debt restructuring, staffing concerns, garbage fee increases driven by diesel surcharges, and the 10-year Capital Improvement Plan. No formal votes were taken; the meeting was largely a working session to prepare trustees for October budget workshops. Key issues included a $54,000 unplanned fuel surcharge cost from Harter's garbage contract, a proposed restructuring of $863,200 in unspent TID 2 borrowing proceeds, and concerns about understaffing across village departments.

Discussion

Public Comment - Traffic on Old Highway 51 and Long-Range Planning

Bernie Kramer of 2150 East State Highway 153 addressed the committee, raising concerns about traffic management on Old Highway 51, arguing it is Kronenwetter's responsibility to coordinate with the county on traffic improvements rather than remaining passive. He also expressed skepticism about 10-year planning given how frequently elected boards change. An unidentified trustee noted she had spoken with Kramer earlier and agreed his suggestion about a potential turning lane for heavy equipment was worth considering.

Finance Director Report - Staffing and Operational Concerns

Finance Director John Jacobs provided an extensive update covering the financial audit (targeting mid-October), TID 3 closure audit (joint review board planned for November), property reassessment by Associated Appraisal (fieldwork underway on 3,000 parcels with buildings and 4,400 land parcels), and a potential future RFP for auditing services. Village Administrator Jim [last name inaudible] and Jacobs both emphasized the village is severely understaffed, with Jacobs noting there were days he was the only person in the office. Discussion centered on the need for a deputy finance director and the strain on interim clerk Kim, who is also managing election duties.

Garbage Fee - Diesel Fuel Surcharge

Jacobs presented a handout analyzing the impact of diesel fuel prices exceeding $4 per gallon — the threshold triggering a fuel surcharge clause in the Harter's garbage contract. Since March 2026, diesel has exceeded $4 (reaching over $6 per gallon), creating approximately $54,000 in unplanned costs for 2026, which the village plans to absorb using the $60,000 contingency reserve and $43,000 from TID 3 closure revenue. For the 2027 budget, trustees discussed three diesel price scenarios ($5.75, $6.00, $6.25 per gallon) that would add $28–$30 per household to garbage charges on top of the contract's standard 4% increase. Trustees agreed to plan for the worst-case $6.25 scenario, adding approximately $30 to the rate.

2027 Operating Budget Preview

Jacobs and the administrator outlined anticipated 2027 budget additions: a deputy clerk position (funded 100% by general fund), a possible fourth utility crew member (funded by water/sewer rates), and retention of funding for the planning tech position. Police officers union contract negotiations are ongoing for a proposed three-year contract; a first bargaining session with the teamsters is scheduled for September 24. The state-mandated levy limit increase for 2027 is only $16,306, well short of anticipated cost increases, meaning the village will again need to use the general obligation debt service exception — similar to the $874,000 exception used in 2026.

2027-2036 CIP Budget - Debt Restructuring

Jacobs detailed a proposed restructuring of $863,200 in unspent TID 2 borrowing proceeds that cannot be used for TID 2 projects (the Flannery Jamrose project was scrapped after bids came in over $1.2 million). The funds must be spent by late 2027 and will be transferred to the village's general capital project fund, with the associated debt absorbed by the tax levy debt service fund. This eliminates one of two previously anticipated debt service 'drops' — the 2030 drop — while preserving the 2029 drop of approximately $226,000. Jacobs noted the restructuring may allow TID 2 to close a year or two earlier than scheduled. Budget workshops are scheduled for October 13 (full budget review at 5:30 p.m.), October 19 (3:00 p.m. start to accommodate Plan Commission at 6:00 p.m.), and October 20 (6:00 p.m.).

Future Agenda Items - Committee Ordinance and Public Comment Policy

The administrator noted that Village President Dan Joling had referenced a potential ordinance change at the prior village board meeting to allow a member of the Police and Fire Commission to simultaneously serve on another committee — currently prohibited by ordinance. The administrator said the matter will go to the village attorney before coming to APC. Trustee Mary Solheim raised the topic of revising public comment procedures at APC, and the administrator indicated a recommendation will be brought forward, noting that allowing emailed public comment on non-agenda items is unusual statewide and has created problems.
