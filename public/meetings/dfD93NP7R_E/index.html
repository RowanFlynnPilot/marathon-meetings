Valor Award Presentation to Deputy Edward Alt-Bneck

The Sheriff presented the Sheriff's Office Valor Award to Deputy Edward Alt-Bneck (known informally as 'Sheriff Eddie') for his actions on August 5th during a 24-minute pursuit of an attempted homicide suspect. The Sheriff described how Deputy Alt-Bneck used his patrol vehicle to execute a ram maneuver — classified as a deadly force decision — to stop the fleeing suspect on Meer Street, putting himself in significant personal danger. The Sheriff noted Alt-Bneck was hospitalized afterward and that his first words upon being called were 'Sorry about your car.' Lieutenant Matt Bell was credited with authorizing the use of the ram. The committee applauded the deputy.

Recreation Deputy Program Overview

Parks, Recreation & Forestry Director Jamie Py presented the county's Recreation Deputy program, which has been operating since 2020 and was highlighted at a national parks conference in Phoenix. The program embeds Sheriff's Office deputies — currently Brian Majeski and Ethan Burmeister — into the parks department to patrol 38 city parks, 17 county parks, and 10 county forest units, funded through the parks budget at one FTE. Py noted the program has increased compliance, improved camper safety perceptions, driven ordinance updates, and has been popular enough that eight strong internal candidates applied for the most recent opening. Committee members expressed support, and County Administrator Leonard praised it as a model for other intergovernmental partnerships.

Central Wisconsin Community Mediation – 2027 Budget Presentation

Randy Westgate, director of Community Mediation of Wisconsin (which absorbed the program from Judicare/Legal Action), presented results of the $15,000 annual county contract. He reported that in the past year, 865 of 1,029 contested small claims cases (84%) were resolved without trial, including a 97% resolution rate for eviction cases. Westgate emphasized the program prevents homelessness and saves an estimated $60,000 in court time annually. Administrator Leonard indicated funding would remain at $15,000 for 2027, but Westgate signaled the 2028 request would likely be $20,000. No action was taken; the item was informational.

Radio Paging Quality Challenges – Edgar, Hamburg, Texas

Sheriff Bilip and Captain Berdall presented two options to address paging dead zones in the Village of Edgar, Town of Hamburg, and Town of Texas: a $500,000–$520,000 infrastructure fix (new tower at Bug Tussle site) versus a pilot of the G2 app at $15 per user per year (approximately $720 annually for Edgar). The Sheriff and Administrator Leonard strongly recommended the G2 pilot, which simultaneously pushes call information via pager, the G2 app, and the 'I Am Responding' app. Captain Berdall confirmed the Edgar fire chief has provided a list of firefighters to begin the pilot. Radio tech Kellen Stouse addressed a question from Supervisor [inaudible] about satellite communications, explaining that public-safety satellite systems are not yet viable but may be in 10–20 years with Starlink-type technology. No formal vote was taken; this was an informational update.

Year-to-Date Budget Reports

Finance staff member Sam reported all public safety departments are within their payroll benchmark of 46.74% and operating budgets are on track. The one area being monitored is a projected $130,000 revenue shortfall in the District Attorney's office due to a state-level change in discovery fee rules — the state public defender's office dramatically reduced what it will pay for discovery. Administrator Leonard noted DA Mayo is working with the DA association and the Wisconsin Counties Association lobbyist on a potential state-level fix. Supervisor [inaudible] asked about remaining ARPA funds; Leonard and Sam confirmed approximately $480,824 remains unspent of the original $26,356,580 award, with roughly $200,000 expected to be reallocated to a previously approved project before year-end.

Rib Mountain Law Enforcement Services Contract Review

Administrator Leonard and Sheriff Bilip presented data showing that Rib Mountain-assigned deputies handle only about 29.2% of calls for service in the village, with non-assigned deputies covering the remaining ~71%. The county has provided law enforcement to Rib Mountain since 2020; since 2023, Rib Mountain (now a village) is legally required to have law enforcement. Three deputies are currently assigned to Rib Mountain, with a fourth planned. Sam reported those three deputies cost just under $400,000 from January 2024 to January 2025. The committee discussed the service-level implications of pulling deputies from other sectors to cover Rib Mountain calls, with Chair Gibbs asking about the volume of case numbers generated (estimated ~1,600 per year). Administrator Leonard noted the levy to support the sheriff's department grows faster than net new construction, making cost-recovery agreements like this one financially important. The committee prepared to move into closed session to continue contract negotiations discussion.
