The Marathon County Board held a special meeting on October 1, 2026 to consider Resolution 55-26, directing Alliant Energy to submit a draft proposal addressing community concerns about the Hub City Wind Project by November 1st. Discussion

Staff presentation: Options for county engagement with Alliant Energy

County Administrator Lori McSkimmons presented a table of four options for the board: (1) negotiating a Joint Developer Agreement (JDA), estimated cost up to $20,000; (2) intervening in the PSC permitting process, estimated $75,000–$200,000+; (3) adopting a moratorium, cost unknown and noted as likely unenforceable on a 100-megawatt project; and (4) appealing the PSC's decision after issuance, estimated $20,000–$50,000. She noted PSC staff confirmed no intervention has ever stopped a project and that Columbia County's similar moratorium effort was sent back to committee. She emphasized options are not mutually exclusive and that the county could not file for intervention until after Alliant submits its formal PSC application, which could come as early as mid-December 2026.

Resolution 55-26 — Hub City Wind Project engagement with Alliant Energy

Supervisor Robinson, who authored the resolution and chaired the alternative energy advisory committee, explained it does not commit the county to a JDA but rather asks Alliant Energy to submit a draft proposal addressing community concerns by November 1st, after which the board would evaluate next steps. Supervisor Dickinson expressed strong opposition, stating he would not vote for anything that moved toward a JDA and questioned whether Alliant would engage in good faith. Supervisor Leur raised concerns that the resolution puts the county in a reactive position and called for an energy summit to address broader regional energy policy. Supervisor Ritter, Supervisor Nath, Supervisor Rosenberg, and Supervisor Robinson defended the resolution as a proactive first step to gauge Alliant's willingness to address constituents' concerns on issues including decommissioning, setbacks, emergency response, property values, and health impacts. The resolution passed but was noted by Chair Gibbs as not unanimous.

Announcements: Budget process and strategic plan survey

Supervisor Robinson announced that the county administrator's proposed budget was accepted by the Human Resources Finance Property Committee and is now the county board's budget. Key dates include: amendment submissions by October 9th, committee authorization for publication on October 12th, a public hearing on November 2nd at 3:00 p.m., and full board budget adoption on November 10th. Chair Gibbs noted the public hearing was moved to 3:00 p.m. on November 2nd to avoid conflicts with Election Day on November 3rd. McSkimmons announced that a public survey on the county's strategic plan — narrowing 20 objectives down to 10 priorities — would open October 2nd and run through October 23rd.
