November 4, 2026 · 1h 12m

The Wausau Economic Development Committee met on November 4th and took action on three substantive items: authorizing staff to prepare a TID 8 project plan amendment for infrastructure projects, approving the $1 purchase of a tax-delinquent property at 108 Myron Street for future affordable housing, and debating a deed restriction and resolution for city-owned property at 1300 Cleveland Avenue. The Cleveland Avenue item resulted in a procedural deadlock, with a 2-2 tie on the amended resolution, sending the matter to the full city council on November 24th with section 4 removed from the deed restriction.

Discussion

Approval of October 6th Meeting Minutes

A motion to approve was made by Alder Neil and seconded by Alder Killian (noted in transcript as 'Kellian'). No discussion occurred and the minutes were approved by voice vote.

TID 8 Project Plan Amendment

Director Fifick explained the amendment would authorize staff to engage a consultant to draft a project plan amendment for Tax Increment District 8, adding infrastructure project costs including lead service line replacement, asphalt overlay, 17th Avenue reconstruction, water and sewer upgrades, and traffic signal replacement. Alder Killian expressed she was not prepared to vote without knowing whether lead line replacement funding covered private or entire replacement, but the motion carried 3-1. Staff noted the item had already been approved 5-0 at the Finance Committee and was placed on the November city council agenda.

Purchase of County-Owned Property at 108 Myron Street

Staff member Tammy presented details on the tax-delinquent property, which has been vacant for years due to a broken pipe causing black mold, rendering it too dangerous to enter. The county approved the city's $1 offer unanimously on October 20th, with full county board consideration set for November 10th. Funding for purchase and demolition would come from Community Development Block Grant funds, not tax levy, with DPW performing the demolition. The end goal is disposition for affordable housing for income-qualifying households. Alder Killian expressed safety concerns about demolition given the black mold but said she would support the motion. Alder Lukans noted for viewers that CDBG/HUD funds, not taxpayer levy money, would be used. The motion carried unanimously by voice vote.

Deed Restriction and Resolution for 1300 Cleveland Avenue

Director Fifick and the committee debated a draft deed restriction prohibiting industrial uses on the city-owned property at 1300 Cleveland Avenue, while preserving existing non-conforming uses including Marathon County/Wausau Parks log and wood chip storage and DPW training approved by a 2024 resolution. Alder Tierney's written comments, read into the record by Director Fifick, argued that non-conforming uses should end and the property cleaned up as promised to neighbors. Parks representative Jamie testified that relocating log storage requires council direction on an alternative site of approximately one acre, noting the ongoing Emerald Ash Borer program creates continuous need. Mayor Diny reported that fire training had already been relocated, and miscellaneous DPW equipment and a trailer had been cleaned up. Alder Killian moved an amendment to remove section 4A from the deed restriction, discontinue DPW training by February 1st, and give Parks 6 months to relocate; that amendment failed 2-2. Chair Heniki then moved a narrower amendment to remove only section 4 from the deed restriction, which passed 3-1. The main motion on the resolution as amended then failed 2-2, sending the matter to the November 24th city council meeting with section 4 removed from the deed restriction and a redrafted resolution to be prepared by staff.
