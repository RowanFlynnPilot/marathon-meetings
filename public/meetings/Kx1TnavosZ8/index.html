The Village of Weston held a joint Comprehensive Plan Steering Committee meeting of the Village Board of Trustees and Planning Commission to review progress on a 10-year comprehensive plan update. Consultant Mark Roers of MD Roers Consulting presented survey results, stakeholder interview findings, draft vision statements, and preliminary chapter goals and initiatives covering land use, economic development, housing, transportation, and intergovernmental cooperation. The committee reached consensus on using draft vision statement 'C' for public presentation and set a public workshop for November 12, 2024, from 5 to 7 p.m.

Public Comment

No speakers appeared in person or online. President Maloney noted it would be entered into the record and included in the packet.

Meetings and Milestones Update

Consultant Mark Roers outlined the comprehensive plan update process, noting that a community survey drew nearly 700 respondents, stakeholder interviews reached 24 identified contacts with 10 completing interviews, and a young adult questionnaire targeting ages 20–35 yielded 48 responses via social media. Roers explained the plan is divided into three volumes, with Volume 2 (Vision and Directions) as the current focus, targeting a public hearing by March. The board noted that 48 young adult responses exceeded the typical weekly newsletter poll response of about 27.

Stakeholder Interview and Young Adult Questionnaire Results

Roers summarized cross-cutting themes from all input channels: safety ranked as the top community value across all age groups; residents want more retail, restaurants, and groceries; interviewees emphasized need for workforce housing, particularly entry-level single-family homes and duplexes over large apartment complexes; and intergovernmental respondents favored expanding collaborative services while keeping Weston's suburban character distinct from larger neighbors. Young adults specifically cited school quality, parks, family orientation, housing affordability, and indoor recreation as priorities.

Draft Vision Statement Review

Roers presented three vision statement options (A from 2016, B from the recent strategic plan, and C newly drafted by Roers with AI assistance). Pre-meeting homework showed C received seven votes, A received one, and B received none. Discussion focused on tweaking C: Trustee Brent Olson suggested removing the word 'thoughtfully' from 'Weston will grow thoughtfully,' with others suggesting 'intentionally' as a substitute; one reviewer had suggested removing 'and welcoming' from the opening line, which drew pushback; another suggested adding 'can' language in multiple places, which was debated. The committee reached consensus to bring a refined version of C to the November 12 public workshop.

Community Growth Map and Geographic Vision

Roers presented a generalized growth pattern map highlighting three mixed-use corridors (Scoffield Avenue, County Road X/Camp Phillips Road, and Weston Avenue) and identified a potential third interchange at Ryan Street as a priority transportation and land use initiative. Trustee or commissioner Gary Garn noted that Marathon County has a no-trucking restriction on Highway J south of Weston Avenue, which would need to be addressed for future southern industrial expansion. Extended discussion covered a potential bridge crossing north from the Caleb Drive area of Sandy Lane subdivision to improve connectivity, with staff noting the Oakclair River floodplain narrows at that point and that the northeast annexation area is known to the town chair.

Hyperscale Data Center Policy

Roers recommended a draft initiative to 'support more industrial development while restricting hyperscale projects,' defining hyperscale as typically requiring 100 or more acres. He cited the DeForest example of a 1,500-acre proposal that was rejected and noted concerns about power consumption, long construction periods, land obsolescence, and community division. Board members expressed agreement with establishing a policy and raised questions about when to address related issues such as solar fields, wind turbines, and EV chargers. Roers noted solar field incentives are diminishing and cited a 2,500-acre proposal near Cambridge. The committee appeared to support the proposed policy direction without a formal vote.

Housing Chapter and State Law Initiatives

Roers outlined new state-level tools including a pay-as-you-go TIF district specifically for residential development on lots no larger than 7,500 square feet with house size caps (1,500 sq ft single-story, 2,000 sq ft two-story), aimed at starter homes. He also noted infrastructure reimbursement mechanisms for developers and referenced the Truth in Planning law requiring communities to plan for sufficient housing land and be open to rezoning consistent with their plans. The board asked whether developer-fronted infrastructure costs could deter development; Roers said hybrid approaches are possible and that lenders are generally familiar with these structures.

Tax Incremental Financing — New Generation

Roers explained that TID No. 1, the larger of Weston's two active districts, will close at year-end, creating room to establish new, smaller, project-specific TIDs. He cited DeForest's model of seven active TIDs and described the shift toward pay-as-you-go (PAYGO) structures where developers front infrastructure costs and are reimbursed through incremental tax revenue, closing districts faster. No vote was taken.

Pre-Meeting Initiative Prioritization Results

Staff reported results of a pre-meeting homework exercise in which board and commission members ranked draft initiatives. 'Continue existing and pursue more opportunities for collaborative services' ranked first with six votes. Tied for second with five votes each were 'maintain the function and condition of Weston's roads' and 'help expand and attract retailers, restaurants, grocers, and industries in targeted sectors.' Tied for third with four votes each were 'phase growth according to utility, road, stormwater, and service capacity,' 'reinvest in older neighborhoods and housing,' and 'update Weston's five-year strategic plan.'

Shaping Weston's Tomorrow Public Workshop

The committee confirmed a public workshop titled 'Shaping Weston's Tomorrow' for November 12, 2024, from 5 to 7 p.m. Roers outlined plans for interactive small-group discussions rather than a lecture format, organized around topics such as transportation and natural resources. A board member requested a poster be created within 22 hours to publicize the event at a community event the following evening; staff confirmed they could produce one using Canva. Roers encouraged board members who attend to listen rather than actively participate in small groups.
