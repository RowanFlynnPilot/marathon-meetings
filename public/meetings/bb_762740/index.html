The DC Everest School Board held its September 2026 regular meeting, approving a 19.1% health insurance premium increase with Aspirus Health Plan for the 2027 plan year and receiving updates on the planned relocation of the IDEA and Odyssey alternative schools as part of an ongoing district facility consolidation effort driven by declining enrollment. The board also heard reports on teacher hiring trends, summer professional development, and community economic impact from the GTCC facility before adjourning to closed session to discuss seclusion and restraint matters.

Discussion

WASB Legislative Update and Statewide Referendum Landscape

Grabko reported that the WASB September update noted 71 Wisconsin school districts will have 78 referendum questions on the November ballot, bringing the 2026 statewide total to 154 questions across 145 districts — down from a record 241 in 2024. She noted this context was relevant given DC Everest's ongoing facility study and community survey. WASB's four legislative priorities for the 2027-29 budget cycle were also outlined, including increasing special education categorical aid and providing inflationary increases to spendable resources.

Student Representative Report

New student council president Bin Campman, a senior at DC Everest Senior High, reported on the school year's start including a sophomore orientation assembly, homecoming plans with a board games theme, and strong athletic results. Notable highlights included the girls tennis team's 14-3 record and a 5-0 sweep, boys soccer wins including a 'Derby' rivalry victory over Wausau West, and the first-ever inclusion of freshmen in homecoming activities such as powderpuff and hallway decorating.

Superintendent Report

Superintendent Dr. Nye discussed several topics: implementation of Wisconsin Act 89 tightening adult-student communication expectations; DC Everest's participation in the national Brightspots cohort with a site visit to Rothschild Elementary highlighted; launch of the community-wide facility survey running through October 9th; GTCC being fully booked every weekend for the coming year with the World Horseshoe Tournament generating an estimated $5.1 million total economic impact this summer; and a potential social media class action lawsuit that the district has been invited to join, which Nye said would be discussed in closed session next month. He also announced Rachel Rentmester as the incoming executive assistant, succeeding the retiring Ellen.

IDEA and Odyssey Relocation Update

Dr. Lindell presented plans to relocate both alternative schools from their current shared building, citing declining enrollment creating feasibility for decommissioning the facility. IDEA, which transitioned from a charter school to a district program in April, is planned to move to the high school campus footprint for the next school year, enabling students greater access to senior high courses. Odyssey is planned to relocate to the 'blue wing' of Evergreen Elementary, with a separate entrance maintained. Board members asked about parent concerns; Lindell and Dr. Nye acknowledged difficult community conversations, noting parents are particularly concerned about the loss of dedicated square footage, and that fundraising suggestions were raised but cannot resolve the underlying staffing cost issues from declining enrollment. Current enrollment caps are 72 students per school; IDEA is in the upper 60s and Odyssey is also in the upper 60s.

Mission Moment: Rothschild Raptor Mural

Principal Mr. Alexen presented the completed Rothschild Raptor mural installed in August on the school's gymnasium stage. The project was funded through community partnerships including People's State Bank, the DC Everest Foundation, Clover Belt Credit Union, Brokaw Credit Union, and others. The mural features a raptor alongside imagery of Rib Mountain, Lake Wausau, and the local landscape, and was designed by local muralist Jessica Copeki. Alexen noted the school's theme this year is 'Raptor Nation.'

Health Insurance Renewal - Aspirus Health Plan

Dr. Nye presented the recommended renewal with Aspirus Health Plan for the 2027 plan year, noting an initial proposed increase of 44% was negotiated down to 31% and ultimately to 19.1% with plan design changes including increased deductibles and co-insurance. The district will maintain a 0% premium option for full-time employees, add employer HSA contributions of $1,000 single/$2,000 family, and cap part-time employee premium contributions at 30% for family coverage. Nye estimated approximately 75% of staff would not see an increase in out-of-pocket costs based on historical claims. The board approved the renewal without discussion.

General Annual Hiring Report

HR Director Sarah presented the hiring report, noting 28 teacher hires this year consistent with 29 last year, with 71% having five or fewer years of experience and 40% previously connected to DC Everest. The teacher candidate pool has rebounded to pre-COVID levels. Support staff openings dropped from 17 to 11 since the report was compiled. Board members including Yee Leng Xiong, Larry Schaefer, and Lindsey Lewitzke asked about geographic origins of hires, retention of early-career teachers, and what board policies contribute to successful hiring. Schaefer raised concerns about the well-documented pattern of losing teachers with under five years of experience. Special education was identified as the greatest area of ongoing hiring need.

Summer Professional Development Update

Dr. Lindell reported over 2,100 hours of professional development completed over the summer, with a focus on PLC work, academic clarity, and responsive classroom training. Over 50 staff attended responsive classroom training funded by grants, with Rothschild Elementary serving as the pilot school. Lindell noted teachers are reporting this as among the best starts to a school year they can remember, with staff physically tired but not emotionally depleted — a meaningful shift in teacher experience.

Adjournment to Closed Session

The board voted unanimously to adjourn to closed session pursuant to Wisconsin State Statute 19.85(1)(f) to discuss seclusion and restraint matters involving specific persons whose personal or disciplinary data, if discussed publicly, could adversely affect their reputation.
