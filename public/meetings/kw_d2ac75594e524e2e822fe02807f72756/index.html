Discussion

Public Comment - Resident Water Quality Complaint

Resident Lori Foundry of Rainbow Drive/County Road XX described severe black and orange sludge in her water supply, corroded fixtures, clogged drains, and water she considers unsafe to drink or use for washing food. Greg (Public Works Director) explained the problem stems from manganese buildup at a dead-end line serving only two customers, and that flushing every six to eight weeks has not resolved the issue. The committee discussed options including pipe scrubbing, installing an RO filtration system, exploring a pipe-looping project estimated at $300,000–$400,000, and potentially obtaining an easement from a neighboring property. Staff committed to having lead water utility worker Mark Mackey visit the property to take water samples, and the committee expressed willingness to provide some form of bill compensation. The looping project was identified as a candidate for the Capital Improvement Plan.

Approval of Minutes

Jim Buck moved and Jessica Stowell seconded to approve the June committee meeting minutes. No discussion was noted. The motion carried unanimously 4-0 on a voice vote.

Director of Public Works and Utilities Report - Lift Station No. 3

Greg reported that wipes are clogging the vacuum-style Lift Station No. 3 nearly every day, requiring staff to spend roughly 1.5 hours daily clearing pumps at significant cost. Letters were mailed to customers warning against flushing wipes. The committee discussed using sewer cameras to identify offending addresses once a new utility employee starts the following Monday, and confirmed the village ordinance allows billing customers for damages caused by illegal dumping. Greg noted plans to televise sewer lines and work with Green Bay Pipe and TV to pinpoint problem sources.

Director of Public Works and Utilities Report - Village Garage Project

Greg reported that the public works crew completed site excavation and gravel base work for the village garage project, and that ST Ellenbecker is expected to begin pouring concrete imminently, before cold weather requires heat blankets.

Finance and Treasurer Report - August Financial Statements

John (Finance/Treasurer) reported that the water utility showed revenues of approximately $513,000 through eight months against a positive fund balance of $183,000 after expenses, slightly below the prior year's $217,000 surplus. The sewer utility showed revenues of $786,000 against a surplus of $198,000, lower than the prior year's $312,000 due to elevated Rib Mountain Metro Sewage District charges that surged from a budgeted $460,000 to an estimated $572,000 for 2026. Total water utility debt stands at approximately $2.949 million, scheduled for payoff in 2043; the sewer utility currently carries no debt. No rate increases are anticipated for 2027.

Septic Tank Ordinance Discussion - Northland Lutheran High School Holding Tank Request

A representative from Northland Lutheran High School presented the school's request to install a holding tank for a small concession stand with two single-stall bathrooms used seasonally for soccer and baseball games, located approximately 407 feet from the nearest utility lateral. The committee developed amended ordinance language including: non-residential use only, seasonal use up to seven months, minimum 400 feet from nearest lateral, minimum lot size of one acre, tank size meeting Marathon County minimum requirements, and requiring approval from both the Public Works Director and the Utility Committee. Jim Buck moved and Dan Raskowski seconded to recommend the amended ordinance change to the Plan Commission and Village Board; the motion carried 4-0. Staff will draft language and consult the village attorney on whether a public hearing is required.

2027 Budget Discussion - Water and Sewer Utilities

John and Greg presented the proposed 2027 utility budgets. No water or sewer rate increases are proposed for 2027. On the sewer side, projected revenues of approximately $1.371 million and inclusion of $548,100 in Rib Mountain Metro costs still yield a projected net profit of roughly $7,800, making a rate increase unnecessary. On the water side, revenues are projected at $947,000 driven in part by an estimated $90,000 in connection fees from the Marathon County garage project; after accounting for expenses including one additional half-time crew employee shared between water and sewer, the water utility is near break-even. The committee also discussed the Marathon County garage's plans to use wells for brine-making and car wash operations rather than municipal water, with committee member Alex Fedbeck pushing for a cost-benefit analysis on requiring municipal water connection to capture long-term revenue. Borrowing for the 2027–2029 CIP is proposed to be delayed until after July 1, 2027, to defer principal and interest costs to the 2028 budget.
