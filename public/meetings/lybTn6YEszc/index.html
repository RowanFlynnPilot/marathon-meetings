Approval of August 6th, 2026 joint committee meeting minutes

Chair Morash called for a motion and second, which were obtained without named movers or seconders identified in the transcript. There was brief clarifying exchange before a voice vote was taken. The minutes were approved without opposition.

McDevco Annual Update — 2027 Budget Development

Kim Weber, executive director of McDevco, presented on McDevco's three pillars: educate, incubate, and finance. She reported the Wausau Business Incubator is now 93% full (up from 61% post-COVID), with manufacturing at 100% capacity. She noted McDevco has deployed the full $1 million in Marathon County revolving loan funds, with lending approaching $1 million in 2024. McDevco receives $40,000 annually from Marathon County for educational programming. A supervisor asked about agricultural loan demand; Weber said most farm-related activity involves septic system loans rather than business expansion loans. Another supervisor asked about the microloan default rate; Weber reported approximately 0.12%. The administrator indicated the plan absent contrary direction is to continue funding McDevco in 2027 at prior levels.

LifeWorks Program — Advance Wisconsin Employment (taken out of order)

Brian Kish, executive director of Advance Wisconsin Employment, and Amy Hopa, program operations manager, presented the LifeWorks pilot program targeting justice-involved individuals for manufacturing workforce placement. The pilot cohort of six referrals saw three participants successfully complete three weeks of training including move-to-manufacturing skills at NTC, with employer tours and guaranteed interviews scheduled at Wausau Tile, Green Heck, Crystal Finishing, and Schooty Metals. Kish requested $25,000 annually from the county to fund quarterly cohorts at approximately $12,000 each. Committee members raised questions about legal authority to fund such a program, KPI reporting requirements, whether funding could be structured as a match with participating manufacturers, and whether the program was regional or Marathon County-specific (Kish confirmed funds would be designated for Marathon County). Corporation Counsel Desmond cautioned that statutory restrictions may limit the county's legal authority to fund the program. Administrator Leonard referenced a prior committee allocation process and suggested re-establishing a competitive application process. The committee directed staff to evaluate legal authority, feasibility, and potential KPI and match structures and report back.

Greater Wausau Prosperity Partnership — WEDC Talent Recruitment Grant Update

Renee Kings, senior director of GWPP, reported that GWPP was selected as one of 17 recipients of a WEDC talent recruitment grant, receiving $500,000 — the largest award tied with one other community — out of a statewide $5 million investment. The program aims to recruit 30 out-of-state households to the greater Wausau region by providing a $10,000 relocation grant, personalized relocation support, and community integration services. Eligibility requires residing outside Wisconsin at application, combined household income of at least $55,000, and relocation within six months of acceptance. A supervisor asked about recapture provisions if the 24-month residency commitment is not met; Kings confirmed there is no financial recapture provision but that quarterly check-ins and employer reporting are planned. The program website was previewed and is set to launch the following week.

UW Extension Monthly Report

Jen (last name not stated) provided monthly reports for June, July, and August. Highlights included a successful county fair, a youth summer theater camp with over 40 enrollees, and the conclusion of three AmeriCorps staff terms with one returning. She noted UW Extension expert Bill McKen has been providing information on joint development agreements to a local work group and that a community resilience workshop on green infrastructure was held that day with staff from Marathon, Portage, Waupaca, and Wood counties. UWSP Wausau Report

Miranda (last name not stated) reported that UWSP Wausau held its first day of classes at the new NTC location, with students successfully finding the new site. UWSP signage has been removed from the Marathon County campus to avoid student confusion, except at the Center for Civic Engagement which continues programming. Enrollment is slightly down as expected during the transition, with official 10th-day figures to be presented at the October meeting. Miranda noted ongoing coordination with the county on items to be left behind for future occupants.

Year-to-Date Budget Reports

Sam (last name not stated) reported no concerns in either the library or UW Extension budgets. The library is at 93.1% of non-levy revenue budget and under its payroll benchmark, with overall expenditures at 43.2% of budget. UW Extension expenditures are anticipated to come in below budget due to a vacancy, with a corresponding reduction in donations revenue. A coding discrepancy in the professional services line was noted as a budget transfer issue with no fiscal concern.

RFP Proposals for UWSP Marathon County Campus — City of Wausau Proposal

Randy Fifick, representing the City of Wausau, presented the city's proposal requesting a six-month due diligence and planning option before committing to acquisition. He noted that a budget modification to fund architectural, engineering, and environmental studies was delayed due to a lack of quorum at the August council meeting and would be introduced at the next meeting. Supervisor Robinson asked whether the proposal was divisible (specifically regarding the dormitory demolition), whether the city could provide an early exit clause if feasibility is determined sooner than six months, and whether the city would cover holding costs during the study period. Fifick indicated all items are negotiable but noted that industry practice would have the existing property owner (the county) bear holding costs, drawing a pointed response about the county's fiscal constraints. Committee members also raised questions about parking allocation and the central heating plant operation relative to multiple potential tenants.

RFP Proposals — Wisconsin Ice Volleyball Club and Other Proposals

Zod Davis, representing Wisconsin Ice Volleyball Club, joined by phone and proposed either purchasing three buildings for $5 each ($15 total) or leasing all three buildings and parking lots for $540 annually for a minimum of five years, with the club covering utilities and snow removal. Davis emphasized the fieldhouse meets all their needs for a nine-month-per-year program drawing players from a wide regional area. Chris (staff, last name not stated) summarized the Lone Wolf Works proposal for the central heating plant, which proposed a $75,000 purchase for conversion to a small automotive restoration and custom fabrication business, with no external financing required. Staff noted CVA could not attend but would present at the HR Finance and Property Committee meeting the following week. The proposals were noted as not scored in a traditional RFP sense per county board direction, but evaluated against criteria established in the RFP document.
