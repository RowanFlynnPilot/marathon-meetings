Administrative Policy Committee and Village Board - Budget Workshop 2

October 20, 2026

The Village of Kronenwetter held its second 2027 Budget Workshop covering public safety budgets (police and fire/EMS), TIF district updates, community development staffing, and a future debt service preview. The board's most significant action was recommending and approving keeping the contingency fund at $60,000 rather than the proposed $70,000, saving approximately $12 on a $200,000 home's village tax share. Village Administrator John Jacobs also previewed that the 2028 budget will likely require approximately a 20-cent tax rate increase to cover new debt service if the capital improvement program borrowing proceeds.

Discussion

Historical Tax Rate and Equalized Valuation Overview

Village Administrator John Jacobs presented a historical chart showing the village's assessed and equalized tax rates since 2020, noting that at full equalized valuation the rate would drop from $4.26 to approximately $2.71-$2.80. He contextualized the village's rate against Marathon County, two school districts, and NCTC, noting the village represents only about 20-25% of a resident's total tax bill. He noted Marathon County's rate of $3.48 is higher than the village's proposed rate.

Port-a-Potty Budget and Vandalism

Staff presented a cost chart for port-a-potty options ranging from approximately $6,700 for six standard units to over $21,000 with ADA-accessible units. Staff recommended a combination of three regular and three ADA units as a reasonable middle ground. Officials identified Bushka Park and Friendship Park as the primary vandalism sites, noting most damage occurs during daytime hours and that the contracted vendor has a policy of pulling units if vandalism occurs. No formal vote was taken; the matter was flagged for parks committee review.

TIF District Administration Costs - Legal Guidance

Jacobs reported receiving guidance from financial advisor Ariana Schmidt of Ehlers clarifying that administrative costs, professional services, and DOR fees can continue through the maximum life of a TID even after the expenditure period ends, but no new capital costs can be incurred after that period. Jacobs also disclosed a correction: TID 1 and TID 4 expenditure periods ended in 2019, not 2024 as previously documented. TID 2 had special legislation allowing expenditures through 2024. All three TIDs can continue accruing administrative costs until closure.

Police Department Budget Presentation

Police Chief Terry [last name inaudible] presented a largely status-quo budget, noting the primary cost increases stem from funding two officers for a full 12 months versus a partial year. A $21,700 line for new officer startup costs was removed. Capital equipment was cut by $9,000. The chief noted a planned hire is expected mid-to-late December at earliest, which could yield some payroll savings. Crossing guard pay was raised to $20/hour to aid retention. A $1,500 full-body restraint wrap system was requested as new equipment. The municipal court clerk overlap budget for Diane's retirement transition was also discussed, along with a proposed municipal judge salary increase from $5,500 to $6,000 effective April 2027.

Fire and EMS Department Budget Presentation

Fire Chief Teresa O'Brien presented a budget with modest increases, primarily for radio replacement ($20,000 from fire budget, $5,000 from EMS) and $800 in additional training. EMS call volume is projected at 492-500 for 2026-2027, up from 420 budgeted, a 25% increase driven largely by mental health-related calls. The EMS wage budget of $68,546 is expected to run approximately $9,000-$10,000 over, requiring a budget amendment in November. O'Brien highlighted three supplemental grant funds — 2% fire dues, FAP EMS grant (~$35,000/year), and a fire donations fund — totaling approximately $70,000 in non-general-fund expenditures annually.

Community Development Staffing and Planning Tech Position

Jacobs noted savings in 2026 due to the gap between Pete's departure and Nathan's hiring on August 3rd. The planning technician position, funded from April 2026, remains unfilled, creating roughly $30,000 in 2026 savings. Two interviews have been conducted with candidates who have GIS experience. Planning commission meetings are proposed to increase from 12 to 16 annually, and zoning board of appeals from 2 to 4 meetings, resulting in modest cost increases. A $10,000 placeholder for a potential TID 5 feasibility study was recommended to remain in the budget.

TIF District Tax Increment and Distribution Center Impact

Jacobs presented estimated tax increment figures: TID 1 at $212,000-$213,000 (down from $255,000 due to decreased equalized valuation), TID 2 over $1,034,000 (up from $954,000), and TID 4 at $147,000. He noted debt restructuring freed up $863,000 in principal for TID 2, accelerating its potential closure. Once the distribution center in TID 1 is fully built out, it is projected to generate an additional $444,000 in annual tax increment, potentially paying back the $2.8 million general fund advance well before the 2044 maximum life. The board discussed adding interest charges to the inter-fund advance, with Jacobs noting the LGIP rate (approximately 3.25-3.5%) could be used.

Capital Improvement Program Borrowing and 2028 Debt Service Preview

Jacobs walked the board through a preliminary debt service analysis for a proposed $1,950,000 borrowing (including closing costs) at an estimated 4.75% over 20 years, mirroring a September 2024 Rothschild borrowing. The analysis showed existing debt service could increase from approximately $735,000 levied in 2027 to approximately $873,000 in 2028, representing roughly a 20-cent tax rate increase. Jacobs stressed this does not affect the 2027 budget but will need to be planned for in 2028. He noted the village has no existing debt beyond 2033. Trustee [inaudible] suggested exploring bank financing similar to prior practice rather than bond issuance.

2028 Budget Pros and Cons

Jacobs verbally presented a list of anticipated 2028 budget factors. Pros included: increased building permit revenue from new subdivisions, potential diesel price decrease eliminating the ~$30/household garbage fuel surcharge, higher highway aid formula funding from infrastructure investment, significantly higher net new construction valuation post-revaluation, no board salary overlap increases, reduced attorney fees as a goal, no three-month court clerk overlap cost, and no revaluation cost ($190,000/year savings). Cons included: projected sewer utility rate increase, deputy clerk moving to 12-month funding, four elections in a presidential year, higher building inspection costs, 20-cent debt service increase, and need to replenish the capital replacement fund.

Contingency Fund Vote

Guy Fidel moved to reduce the contingency fund from the proposed $70,000 to $30,000, arguing residents face hardship and a reduction would build board credibility. The motion failed for lack of a second. APC member Chris Clutes then moved to recommend keeping the contingency at $60,000, seconded by Mary Solheim; the APC recommendation passed. Erin Mishka then moved at the village board level to keep the contingency at $60,000, seconded by Solheim. The motion passed; Jacobs noted the reduction from $70,000 to $60,000 lowers the tax rate from $4.26 to $4.20 and saves approximately $12 on a $200,000 home's village portion.
