The Wausau Plan Commission held its November 17, 2026 meeting, addressing a contested rezoning request for 317 East Kent Street and reviewing the housing chapter of the city's updated comprehensive plan. The rezoning request from urban mixed-use to heavy industrial failed on a 2-2 tie vote and will advance to the full City Council for a decision at its December 8 meeting. Approval of October 20, 2026 Regular Meeting Minutes

A motion to approve the minutes was made by a member identified as True Blood and seconded by a member identified as Bugamman. No corrections were offered and the minutes were approved unanimously by voice vote.

Public Hearing: Rezoning of 317 East Kent Street from Urban Mixed-Use to Heavy Industrial

Three members of the public spoke during the hearing. A resident from Mount View Boulevard opposed the rezoning, citing proximity to the Eau Claire River Conservancy — approximately 200 feet away — and risk of stormwater runoff contaminating the conservancy's 49.3 acres of pine, hardwoods, and wetlands. A second resident from Sturgeon Road expressed conditional support, noting the nearby Holtze-Krauss landfill history and arguing that if the operator — which he believed to be a local contracting company — complies with city and DNR rules, it would be unlikely to harm the conservancy. A representative of the Friends of the Lower Eau Claire River Advocacy Group opposed the rezoning, arguing that stormwater from the 5.4-acre site drains directly through an HDPE culvert to the river wetlands with no filtration, and that heavy industrial zoning runs with the land and could allow more onerous future uses even if the current operator is responsible.

Discussion and Possible Action: Rezoning of 317 East Kent Street from Urban Mixed-Use to Heavy Industrial

City staff explained the applicant sought the zoning change to construct a salt shed storage building and attached office, and that a 2016 conditional use permit for soil sifting and compost storage was voided in the comprehensive zoning rewrite, leaving the site as a legal non-conforming use that cannot be expanded without rezoning. Staff recommended denial, stating the proposal does not advance the purposes of the zoning title, is inconsistent with the comprehensive plan's future land use designation of suburban residential, and constitutes spot zoning. A member identified as Bugamman moved to approve the rezoning in the affirmative; the motion was seconded by a member identified as True Blood. The vote failed 2-2 and will be forwarded to the City Council for its December 8 meeting.

Wausau Comprehensive Plan Update – Chapter 3 Housing

Sam Wessel of the North Central Wisconsin Regional Planning Commission walked the commission through the draft housing chapter, covering housing cost trends, supply and demand dynamics, cost burden rates, unhoused population data, housing type variety, and state legislative changes. Commissioners asked pointed questions about what is driving higher rental prices relative to local incomes, potential ordinance barriers to housing development, incentivizing multigenerational housing, discouraging land hoarding, mobile home and manufactured housing options, condominium financing barriers, and corporate ownership of rental properties. Staff noted Wausau's recently rewritten zoning code — which won a statewide planning award for addressing missing middle housing — puts the city ahead of most Wisconsin communities on the zoning side. Discussion of Act 173, which requires densities to be written into future land use map categories and provides automatic approval for projects meeting those densities, was flagged as requiring several future meetings to address properly.
