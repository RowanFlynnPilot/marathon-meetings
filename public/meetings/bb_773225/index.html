Discussion

Approval of Minutes

Board President James Bouché sought a motion to approve minutes from September 28, 2026. The motion carried with two members voting in opposition; exact names of those opposed were not stated.

Red Granite Charter School Principal Presentation

Principal Ma Mangan presented on the school's third year of operation as a public Waldorf school, reporting approximately 65 students including 24 new 4K students, 100% teacher retention, and expansion to a second early childhood classroom. Board members, including one identified as Pat McKee, praised word-of-mouth marketing and noted the school's no-screen and outdoor-focused approach as enrollment drivers. The board asked questions about space and what drove the enrollment increase but took no formal action.

Audit of the Bills

Bouché sought a motion to approve the audit of bills as presented in board books. The motion carried with opposition noted but not counted by name.

Facilities Usage Fee Schedule - Phase One Modifications

Assistant Superintendent Dr. Elizabeth Channel and Buildings and Grounds Director Ryan Ormansky presented a first phase of modifications allowing 501(c)(3) organizations, Title 36 youth groups (such as scouts), and governmental agencies to request fee waivers for facility use during normal operating hours (6:30 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.). A board member noted the importance of stewardship of taxpayer-funded buildings while supporting community partnerships. The motion to recommend the first phase to the full board carried.

Legal Counsel Expenses Q1 2026-27 (Informational)

Dr. Channel confirmed no additional information beyond what was in board books; no action was taken on this written informational report.

Hmong Language Offering

Director of Secondary Education Dr. John Tossky and Dr. Cwell presented findings on potentially offering Hmong as a language course, noting highlights such as community engagement, college credit potential, and innovative positioning, as well as challenges including declining enrollment in existing global languages, difficulty finding and retaining a licensed Hmong teacher (no licensure programs exist in Wisconsin for the DPI 1389 license), and curriculum development costs. Board members Charles Burger and others asked about Wave virtual delivery, summer program pilots, and demand measurement. Lance Trollop and Pat McKee cautioned that adding a language amid declining enrollment and budget constraints could come at the expense of existing language offerings. Board President Bouché noted Act 31 obligations regarding Southeast Asian heritage language instruction. The board directed staff to gauge student interest before the next presentation on this topic; no vote was taken.

Tech Ed Course Approvals - Digital Design and Manufacturing

CTE Coordinator John Wy presented a restructuring of the graphics and Fab Lab pathways, combining Graphics 1 and Fab Lab into a new 'Digital Design and Manufacturing' course, renaming Graphics 2 to 'Graphic Design and Software,' and reducing Graphics 3 (Warrior/Lumberjack Graphics) from a double block to a single block while retaining NTC dual credit. Teachers and administration were reported to be fully supportive. The motion to recommend approval to the full board carried.

Academic Career Plan (ACP) 2026-27

CTE Coordinator John Wy presented the district's ACP in alignment with PI26, highlighting student success stories, innovative pathways including aviation, robotics, auto collision, and math and construction, and two goals: expanding work-based learning participation by 10% and reducing undecided seniors from 11-15% to under 8%. Board members praised the presentation and noted the importance of ACP for community awareness. The motion to recommend approval to the full board carried.

Annual Student Fees 2027-28

Dr. Kempky reported no increases to student fees for 2027-28, noting ongoing monthly collaboration with Dr. Channel and athletic directors on athletics funding. The motion to recommend approval to the full board carried with opposition noted.

Special Meeting: Final 2026-27 Budget Adoption and Tax Levy

Dr. Channel presented the finalized 2026-27 budget, reporting a mill rate of $6.26 per thousand—28 cents less than projected at the annual meeting—driven by increased equalization aid and increased property values. Enrollment declined by 215 students from fall 2025 to fall 2026, with a three-year rolling average down 133 students. The district is approximately $13 million behind inflationary pace, representing a cumulative $71 million deficit since 2011. The private school voucher aid deduction totals $4,629,595, adding about $65 per $100,000 home. The board approved the final budget and set the tax levy at $44,385,271—the same as the prior year—while defeasing approximately $11 million in debt. A board member requested additional data on equalized value trends, transportation reimbursements, and special education enrollment for use in legislative discussions.
