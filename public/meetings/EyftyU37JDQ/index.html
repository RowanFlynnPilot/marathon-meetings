The Marathon County Board held a special meeting focused on the Hub City Wind Project's proposed Joint Development Agreement (JDA) from Alliant Energy and a separate issue involving Bug Tussle's failure to make required bond payments. Seven members of the public spoke overwhelmingly against the JDA, and board supervisors expressed strong disappointment with Alliant's response to the county's earlier resolution, with the board ultimately voting to go into closed session to discuss strategy on both matters.

Discussion

Public comment time limit appeal

Board Chair Gibbs initially ruled that seven public speakers would divide 15 minutes (approximately two minutes each). Supervisor Dickinson moved to appeal the ruling and allow three minutes per speaker, seconded by another supervisor. Supervisors Gal, Straw, Sundowski, and Dickinson all spoke in favor of three minutes, arguing the meeting was specifically called for this issue and constituents deserved a full hearing. The appeal passed and three minutes per speaker was granted.

Public comment — Hub City Wind Project and JDA

Seven speakers addressed the board, all opposing the proposed JDA with Alliant Energy. Speakers raised concerns about aviation safety near the proposed turbines, inadequate health and safety protections in the JDA, wildlife migration impacts, road damage liability, shadow flickering, Alliant's honesty based on its track record at other projects, vague contract language, and lack of community benefit provisions. Debbie Stone, president and CEO of Wisconsin for Local Control, offered professional regulatory and legal assistance to the county, noting her organization is an intervenor in Alliant's Columbia Wind PSC case. Tren Spindler, chair of the towns' intergovernmental committee, urged the county not to sign the JDA and instead update its comprehensive plan, zoning, and ordinances, warning that weak county zoning was why the project was sited in Marathon County.

Overview of Alliant Energy response to Resolution 55-26 and potential next steps

County Administrator summarized that in October the board passed Resolution 55-26 requesting a contractual agreement from Alliant addressing the advisory committee's concerns; Alliant's draft response was included in the board packet and shared with impacted township representatives. He outlined options including engaging in negotiations, refusing to negotiate, and preparing for PSC intervention. Supervisor Robinson, chair of the local impact advisory committee, said he was 'terribly disappointed' in Alliant's response, calling it a 'canned JDA' that ignored community input and did not go beyond statutory requirements, and said he was torn between negotiating and preparing for intervention. Supervisor Dickinson argued the county has no leverage with Alliant and called on the board to take a firm stand. Supervisor Sundowski called the JDA 'garbage' and cited a constituent survey of 1,395 respondents showing a vast majority opposed, with only three of 150 forum attendees in favor. Supervisors Cavelli, Ritter, and Morash raised concerns about whether the county's current legal counsel (Atol's law firm) has a conflict of interest given that Alliant Energy is listed as a corporate partner of the Wisconsin Counties Association. Multiple supervisors called for hiring outside legal counsel or working with Wisconsin for Local Control.

Bug Tussle bonding and loan agreement update

The county administrator reported that Bug Tussle failed to make a sufficient deposit into its debt service fund to cover required bond payments. Marathon County's share of the 2021 $25 million conduit bond issuance is $347,491, due by April 8 of next year. The county also has a separate direct $8 million loan with Bug Tussle entered in 2024. Supervisor Dickinson publicly apologized for his role in supporting the Bug Tussle contract despite community warnings, saying he was 'embarrassed.' The administrator recommended closed session to discuss legal and negotiation strategy related to the non-payment notices.

Motion to go into closed session

Supervisor Cyver moved that the Alliant Energy discussion remain in open session to keep the public informed, but county counsel advised the cleaner procedural path was to go into closed session only for Bug Tussle while leaving Alliant in open session. Board Chair Gibbs argued he wanted to preserve negotiating strategy on the Alliant matter as well, stating he was not in favor of the JDA but wanted closed-session discussion on intervention strategy. Supervisor Luer ultimately moved to go into closed session covering both Bug Tussle bond non-payment strategy and Alliant Energy JDA next steps; Supervisor Peak seconded. The motion passed on a roll call vote and the board recessed into closed session.
