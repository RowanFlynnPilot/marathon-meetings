Rules Review Committee Meeting

November 9, 2026 · 1h 15m

The Wausau Rules Review Committee held an organizational 'table-setting' session on November 9th to identify potential revisions to the City of Wausau's standing rules (Chapter 2.16 of municipal code). No formal votes or rule changes were made; the committee identified several areas for future revision including public comment procedures, standing committee appointments, absence notification, subcommittee chair elections, and the reporting chain from this committee to council. The committee agreed to reconvene in mid-January 2027 after members submit redlined proposed changes to the city attorney.

Discussion

Reporting chain: committee of the whole vs. direct to council

Committee members, including Alder Tierney and Terry Kilian, questioned why recommendations must go to the committee of the whole before the full council, calling it redundant since the committee of the whole is essentially the same body. City Attorney Vinnie confirmed the reporting structure was set by the resolution creating this subcommittee. Mayor Doug Diny suggested the committee of the whole step allows all alders to weigh in on rule changes before council action. No formal change was made; it was noted the resolution could be amended via suspension of the rules at a future council meeting.

Rule 16 - public comment engagement policy

The chair raised whether the existing practice of not engaging with public commenters should be codified in the rules, prompted by recent media coverage. Alder Hanei argued the current practice was proper and warned against a 'knee-jerk reaction to a mischaracterization by a media outlet.' Terry Kilian said a brief response to a direct question from a commenter could be respectful and appropriate. Alder Tierney raised concerns about managing time and consistency in contentious situations. Mayor Diny noted responses have historically been limited to procedural direction from the chair, and that the practice only became controversial after media reported on it. No rule change was finalized; members were asked to submit proposed language.

Standing committee appointments - proposed rule addition

Alderperson Lou Larson raised during public comment that he and Alder Rasmussen were removed from long-held committee assignments, which he attributed to retribution. The chair noted two alders had requested a rule providing continuity for experienced alders in committee appointments. Alder Hanei and Terry Kilian both stated they were not in favor of changing the rule giving the mayor appointment authority, with Hanei noting it could undermine the mayor's prerogative and citing Marshfield's annual reappointment model as a possible alternative. No change was recommended.

Rule 13 - subcommittee chairs and executive committee chairperson

Alder Tierney argued subcommittees such as bike and pedestrian should elect their own chair and vice chair, consistent with how standing committees operate. Terry Kilian additionally questioned why the executive committee — a standing committee — is required by Rule 13H to be chaired by the council president rather than electing its own chair. The city attorney noted some boards and commissions are governed by statute and cannot be changed, but council standing committees and subcommittees are within the rules' scope. No formal change was made; proposed language would be drafted for the next meeting.

Suspension of rules and consent agenda efficiency

Alder Tierney expressed strong opposition to routine suspension of the rules, noting a previous rules committee under Gary Gelman had addressed the same recurring problem. Alder Hanei raised concerns about consent agendas containing items from committees some alders did not serve on, and suggested items that failed in committee should never appear on the consent agenda. Mayor Diny noted the old agenda software listed committee vote tallies alongside resolutions and suggested that practice be restored. The city attorney cited Rule 14 as already allowing committee chairs to give brief reports to council on their items. No rule changes were made; members encouraged to submit redlined proposals.

Number and structure of standing committees

Alder Hanei raised the question of whether the city has too many committees, noting items sometimes travel through multiple committees before reaching council. He suggested exploring whether fewer committees with more members could mean more efficient city business and broader aldermanic awareness of issues before council votes. The chair acknowledged this was raised by the mayor at the organizational meeting and said the door remains open for the committee to consider recommending structural changes. No action taken.
