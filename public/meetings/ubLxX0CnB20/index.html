Discussion

Winter Street Maintenance Policy — Counterflow Plowing on Center Median Boulevard Streets

After extensive public comment from roughly ten speakers over several months of meetings, Alder Slonsky moved to amend the Department of Public Works winter maintenance policy to permit snowplow counterflow to traffic whenever otherwise safe to do so; the motion passed unanimously. City Attorney Ann Jacobson confirmed Wisconsin Statute 346.05(2) allows vehicles engaged in highway maintenance to operate on the left-hand side of the road, and that a written internal policy would protect plow drivers under city liability. DPW Director Dustin [last name inaudible] acknowledged the counterflow practice had been discontinued based on a misinterpretation — originally prompted by a state patrol comment about one-way streets that was incorrectly applied to divided two-way boulevard streets. Alder Slonsky criticized DPW for not providing requested cost/time comparisons between plowing methods as asked at the prior meeting, while Alder Martins and others praised community advocates for their thorough, data-driven advocacy over three years. District 1 Alder Carol Lukans appeared as a public commenter urging the committee to act that night so residents would not face another winter under the old policy.

Assessment Rates for 2027 Construction Projects

City engineering staff presented proposed 2027 assessment rates, noting that at $55 per accessible foot the city recovers roughly 22% of roadway reconstruction costs — down from a historical target of 60%. Staff recommended raising the sewer lateral replacement assessment from $500 (unchanged for approximately 25 years) to $1,000, noting actual city costs range from $1,500 to $2,500 per lateral. Alder Martins moved to approve the rates with street reconstruction raised to $60 per accessible foot and the sewer lateral assessment raised to $1,000; the motion passed unanimously. Alders Heni and Slonsky raised questions about whether percentage-based assessments would be fairer than flat dollar rates, but staff explained that flat rates are required for statutory notice purposes and provide more equity across projects of varying complexity.

Vacating Alley Bounded by Lake View Drive, Bertha Street, Elmwood Boulevard, and Midway Boulevard

A motion was made and seconded to vacate the alley; city engineering noted a sanitary sewer runs through the alley and the city must retain an easement. A friendly amendment was adopted to include easement retention language, and the motion passed unanimously. Public commenter Michael Moran (2513 Elmwood Boulevard) spoke in support of the vacation during public comment.

Vacating 134-Foot Southerly Extension of Liberty Ridge Way

A speaker at the public hearing representing property owners Chris and Margaret [Gdori, phonetic] testified in support, explaining the owners want to secure the end of the road as a private drive. City engineering confirmed both sewer and water infrastructure are present and easements must be retained. Alder Heni moved to vacate with easement language; Alder Slonsky seconded after confirming engineering had no objection. A committee member asked for clarification that the remainder of Liberty Ridge Way remains a public road, which staff confirmed. The motion passed unanimously.

Painted Curb Extension Project — Fourth Street and Scott Street (Wausau River District)

TJ [last name not stated] from city staff presented a River District proposal to install plastic bollards and road paint at the east side of the Fourth and Scott intersection downtown to create pedestrian refuge areas and shorten crossing distances. The project had already been approved by the Bike and Pedestrian Committee. The River District committed in writing to cover bollard replacement costs through 2032; the Center for Visual Arts provided verbal assurance of low- or no-cost repainting assistance. Alder Slonsky moved to approve; Alder Martins seconded and expressed strong support, noting it was a proven traffic-calming method at a difficult intersection. The motion passed unanimously. Alder Wizkowski suggested exploring additional painted intersection treatments downtown, with staff noting that could be brought to a joint Bike Ped and Arts Commission meeting.

2027-2028 Capital Infrastructure Projects Review

Alder Heni raised concerns about the sustainability of current borrowing levels, noting the levy is rising again, and said he is working with Director Linman to identify capital projects that could be deferred to reduce borrowing by $4–5 million. Alder Martins noted that the drinking water treatment facility solar array (projected at $1.98 million) effectively failed after a U.S. EPA grant was not received, and that a downtown street traffic flow redesign at $150,000 is still under discussion tied to TID 12. Staff emphasized that deferring road reconstruction has ripple effects pushing all subsequent projects back and frustrating residents expecting scheduled work.

2026 Construction Projects Update

Staff reported that the downtown utility project (Second Street and Division Street), Grant Street utility upgrades, and Stark Street reconstruction (5th to 12th) are all 100% complete. Eighth Avenue reconstruction (Bridge to Spruce) is approximately 90% complete with final paving expected the week of October 19th. Asphalt overlays and sidewalk projects are complete city-wide. The riverfront parking lot near Athletic Park is under construction with paving expected the following week. Staff noted this is one of the earliest project completion years in recent memory. Discussion touched on coordination challenges with the lead service line replacement contractor, and committee members advocated for better public-facing communication tools — including a simpler website flowchart and use of the existing pothole hotline (715-261-6960) and Wausau Streets email — to reduce resident confusion about road work timelines.
