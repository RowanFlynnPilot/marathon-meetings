Town Hall Meeting: Overview of Professional City Management Models

September 15, 2026
City AdministrationGovernment StructureBudgetStaffingEconomic Development

The City of Wausau held a public town hall meeting to educate residents and council members about professional city management models, specifically the city administrator and city manager forms of government. Dr. Michael Ford of UW-Oshkosh and Jay Shambo, a current interim city administrator, presented information on governance structures, fiscal data, and practical experience. No votes were taken; the meeting was informational only, though Mayor Doug Diny indicated the city is actively exploring hiring a city administrator and looking at including funding in the budget, potentially phasing in as early as mid-2027.

Discussion

Logic of Professional Local Management

Dr. Ford explained that professional management refers to hiring an administrator or manager by the elected council to oversee day-to-day operations, insulating government from political volatility. He emphasized that the best form of government is the one that works for the community and is understood and accepted by residents. He stressed that role clarity is essential and that role confusion is a primary way this model fails.

City Manager vs. City Administrator Models

Dr. Ford distinguished between the Chapter 64 city manager model, used by 12 Wisconsin cities, and the more common ordinance-based city administrator model used by 117 cities. Key differences include that under the city manager model police and fire report to the manager, while under the administrator model the mayor retains that authority. Mayor Diny noted Wausau has a robust police and fire commission and a long tradition of separation, suggesting the administrator model is more appropriate.

Wisconsin Data and Fiscal Comparisons

Dr. Ford reported that 129 of 190 Wisconsin cities have an administrator or manager, making it the dominant form. Of Wausau's peer second-class cities, 10 of 15 have an administrator or manager. Fiscal data from the Wisconsin Department of Revenue showed professionally managed cities have lower general government operational spending per capita but slightly higher overall tax burden per capita. Jay Shambo argued a single $2 million grant could offset 10 years of administrator salary costs.

Roles, Accountability, and Democratic Concerns

Dr. Ford addressed whether the model is anti-democratic, acknowledging that residents cannot directly vote out an administrator but retain accountability through the council. Survey data was presented showing council members under administrator forms report better group dynamics, stronger strategic planning, and clearer performance measures. Jay Shambo and a former fire chief who worked in Wausau both spoke strongly in favor of the model, with the former fire chief stating the strong mayor form is 'really inefficient' from his experience working in Wausau starting in 2000.

Process for Changing Government Form

Dr. Ford outlined the statutory options: a two-thirds council vote to modify the charter ordinance, with a 60-day public petition window to force a referendum; a direct council vote to go to referendum; a community petition drive; or a simple ordinance change not requiring two-thirds. He cautioned against the simple ordinance route for reasons of public legitimacy and hiring attractiveness. He noted a non-binding referendum is no longer permitted under current state law.

Mayor's Intent and Budget Timeline

Mayor Diny stated he has attempted to advance this idea previously, including trying to insert it into the budget, but it did not gain council traction. He indicated the city is looking at including administrator funding in an upcoming budget, possibly phasing in starting July of a future year to put half the cost in the 2027 budget. He acknowledged only one of 11 council members, Bruce Trueblood, was present at the town hall.

Public Comment on Council Dysfunction and Control

Multiple residents expressed support for the administrator model. A former council member who served 1998-2006 described budget meetings as inefficient and said an administrator would have greatly helped. Alderperson Bruce Trueblood asked attendees not to assign negative motives to absent council members, noting several were working or dealing with family emergencies. One resident raised concerns about retaining democratic control and keeping taxes manageable. A resident asked about the audit process and noted a 2021 motorpool audit whose recommendations were not fully followed.
