The Marathon County Board held an educational meeting focused primarily on a detailed presentation for the proposed phase four construction of a new county highway facility. No votes were taken at this educational session; the resolution authorizing phase four construction (estimated at $54,980,537) was scheduled for consideration at the Human Resources Finance and Property Committee meeting on October 20, followed by the full County Board the same evening. Supervisors also reviewed upcoming voting items including rezones, solid waste policy, and a potential Cherokee Park land purchase.

Discussion

Standing committee announcements

Supervisor Robinson announced the HR Finance & Property Committee would meet October 20 before the County Board to discuss funding for the county highway facility resolution, a resolution declaring November as Project Greenlight Month for veterans, and a resolution opposing proposed US Census rule changes that could shift Wisconsin 'snowbird' residents to states like Florida and Arizona, potentially costing Wisconsin a congressional seat and federal funding.

Town of Reedbrock wind/solar siting ordinances and Town of Wausau data center ordinance (Resolutions 59-26, 60-26, 61-26)

Supervisor Lemmer raised concerns about the county's shift from substantively reviewing town ordinances to a purely procedural compliance check, worrying about 'rubber stamping.' Corporation Counsel explained the shift is intentional and designed to limit county liability, noting towns that opt out of county zoning are responsible for enforcing their own ordinances. He acknowledged the odd statutory requirement for county approval even when towns have opted out, and confirmed the Reedbrock ordinance had previously been returned to the town for a required new public hearing before being resubmitted. Supervisor Sandowski asked whether the Wausau data center ordinance addressed hyperscale data centers; Chair Gibbs reiterated the county's role is solely procedural compliance.

Cherokee Park land purchase — Resolution 66-26 (up to 15 acres, not to exceed $X)

Supervisor Jensen asked about the Knowles-Nelson Stewardship Fund referenced in the resolution; Administrator Landon and Supervisor Robinson explained the fund's legal framework exists but currently has no appropriation, and was included in case it is reauthorized so the county could capture outside funding. Supervisor Hagen asked three questions about need, tax impact, and maintenance costs; Parks Director Jamie Paully (participating remotely) explained the parcel is forested riverfront land that would make Cherokee Park 'whole' by eliminating a gap along the river, that taxable value lost would be minimal given current forest classification, and that no additional maintenance costs are anticipated unless trails or improvements are added in the future.

Phase four highway facility construction — educational presentation

Deputy Administrator and Facilities Director Chris Holman led an extensive presentation alongside project architect Ned Dirksen, Miron project executive Eric Brandt, and Excel Engineering's geothermal specialist Corey Powers. The guaranteed maximum price for direct construction was presented as $53.3 million, with a total project cost of $54,980,537 including contingencies, design fees, and owner FF&E. Key topics included value engineering steps already taken (consolidating hazardous storage, switching to energy wheel, reducing card-access doors), geothermal HVAC justification (bore field cost $1.9 million with 40% IRA tax credit reducing county cost to approximately $1.14 million; Dunn County comparison showing 33% less electricity and 84% fewer natural gas therms), coal dust mitigation via MERV 8 and MERV 14 dual filtration, and a construction timeline targeting spring 2027 site work start and August 2028 substantial completion. Supervisors asked numerous questions about geothermal reliability in extreme cold (backup gas boilers confirmed), filter maintenance costs (Dirksen pledged to provide costs by Tuesday), LEED certification (architect stated equivalent energy efficiency measures are incorporated without pursuing formal certification), truck traffic flow, building expansion capacity, and whether competitive bidding was followed (Holman confirmed sealed public bid process with lowest qualified bidder selection and validation calls completed by Miron).
