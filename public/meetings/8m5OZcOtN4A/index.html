Discussion

Flock Camera Contract — Cease Use and Strike Future Appropriations

Alder Solanski moved a two-part motion: refer to the Finance Committee to strike 2027 and 2028 Flock contract appropriations, and refer to the Common Council to direct the police department to immediately cease using Flock cameras and bag them so Flock cannot access them. Alder Trueblood expressed concern about breach of contract implications, noting the city's commitments should mean something. Alder Lukens voted in favor citing reports that Flock had reinstalled cameras in other municipalities after contracts were terminated. Alder Solanski explained the two-part structure addressed both financial liability and continued surveillance separately, noting the city had already spent $150,000 on the contract. A question arose about whether the Marathon County Sheriff's Department could still place Flock cameras on county roads within the city; city attorney indicated the county could place cameras on county property without city authorization. The motion passed unanimously.

Community Care Paramedic Program Update

Community Paramedic Matt Kozlowski (also referred to as Kosloski in self-introduction) presented an 8-minute condensed report on the program's first year and a half. The program has served approximately 50-70 patients and achieved a 63% reduction in 911 calls — 273 fewer calls — translating to an estimated $300,000 in avoided medical bills for vulnerable residents. Kozlowski shared case studies including an elderly veteran nearly evicted due to suspected dementia, a patient triple-dosing diabetes medication out of confusion, and an elderly woman repeatedly falling due to a broken sofa, all of whom received direct hands-on assistance. Alders Solanski and Lukens praised the program; Kozlowski noted he is currently the only community paramedic for Wausau and is working with Aspirus to expand to Stevens Point, Merrill, and Wisconsin Rapids.

Deer and Feral Cats Within City Limits

Alder Solanski raised the issue as her most frequently heard constituent complaint, noting uncertainty about what the city can actually do. Patrol Captain Melinda Pauls outlined existing options: archery hunting is permitted on private property and some city property with police department registration; the DNR has an online nuisance deer reporting portal; and the city could request additional deer tags from the DNR as Rib Mountain has done. On feral cats, Pauls noted three CSOs are still dedicated to animal control. The chair and Alder Solanski suggested publicizing existing ordinances against feeding wild animals through the city newspaper and improving the city website's usability for residents seeking quick animal-related guidance.

Police Department Third Quarter Report

Captain Pauls noted it was another busy quarter with a focus on mental health calls and high-risk calls. No specific figures were discussed in the meeting beyond what was included in the written packet. The committee had no questions and placed the report on file.

Tavern Activities Report — August and September / 100-Point Demerit Incident

Captain Pauls described an incident in which a gunshot victim arrived at Aspirus hospital and investigation traced the shooting to a Wausau bar. Surveillance video and witness statements — including one indicating a bartender announced shots fired to help patrons flee before police arrived — led to the establishment receiving 100 demerit points for failing to report the shooting. Alder Watson asked what demerit points mean; it was explained that at 100 points, the city sends a letter of concern and the establishment's owners are summoned to appear before the committee at the next meeting for the committee to determine penalties such as fines or license suspension. The committee confirmed the matter would appear on the next agenda. A 50-point violation at a venue called Intermission was noted but no action was taken, as the threshold for committee action is 100 points.
