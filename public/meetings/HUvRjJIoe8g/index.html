Parks and Recreation Committee Meeting

August 31, 2026
ParksBudgetUrban ForestryHistoric PreservationYaki ParkRiver Edge TrailAquatic Centers

The Wausau Parks and Recreation Committee met on August 31, 2026 and took action on several items including unanimously approving acceptance of a donated scoreboard for Memorial Park, unanimously recommending historic landmark designation for Yaki Park, approving amendments to Chapter 2 of the city's comprehensive plan addressing natural resources, and directing staff to paint a centerline on the River Edge Trail. The committee also received and debated a detailed 2027 department budget overview that included proposals for pool staffing reductions, dock consolidation, and a potential urban forestry fee, with no final budget votes taken but significant discussion about the financial sustainability of city pools and other park amenities.

Discussion

Public Comment – Yaki Park Historic Designation

Kathy Foley, former director of the Leigh Yawkey Woodson Art Museum, spoke on behalf of five Yawkey heirs — great-grandchildren of Cyrus Yawkey — reading a signed statement expressing full support for designating Yaki Park as a historic landmark. Foley noted the park is 106 years old and deeded for public use in perpetuity. She left the signed statement for the committee record.

Minutes Approval – July 6, 2026 Meeting

The chair asked for any corrections to the minutes of the July 6, 2026 regular meeting. With none offered, Alder Slonsky moved to approve and Alder Lukans seconded. The minutes were approved unanimously.

3A: Scoreboard Donation at Memorial Park

Parks staff (identified as Jamie) explained that Wausau Youth Baseball is funding the entire scoreboard donation, which replaces an old Pepsi-branded board. No sponsor has been secured yet; if one is found, it would require approval through the parks office per the sponsorship policy. The item came before the committee due to the scoreboard's 15-to-20-year lifespan exceeding the threshold requiring committee review even though the cost is under $5,000. Alder Slonsky moved to accept the donation, Alder Neil seconded, and it passed unanimously.

3B: Yaki Park Historical Landmark Designation

Staff clarified that the designation applies only to the park grounds — including the colonnade and fountain — not to the Yawkey family home located a block away. City staff member Brad explained that as a landmark, the historic preservation commission would have binding authority (not merely advisory) over any substantial changes to the park. Staff noted that if the city ever ceased to use the property as public space, it reverts to the Yawkey family per the deed. A public hearing at the Historic Preservation Commission is set for September 23, followed by plan commission and ultimately city council approval. Alder Troub moved to adopt the designation, Alder Slonsky seconded, and it passed unanimously.

3C: Natural Resources Chapter 2 – Comprehensive Plan Review

City planner Carrie Edmonson and parks staff presented proposed updates to Chapter 2, informed heavily by the City of Madison's comprehensive plan and work with city forester John Kahhan. Committee members proposed several additions: Alder Lukans raised concerns about pesticide drift, native plantings, and the environmental impacts of artificial turf with crumb rubber infill; Alder Slonsky requested strengthening language around restoring damaged wetlands from 'consider developing' to a firmer commitment; Alder Troub raised the need to address native grasses and lawn alternatives in parks and residential areas; Alder Lukans also raised light pollution as it relates to bird habitat. Alder Neil moved to approve Chapter 2 as presented with the new feedback incorporated, Alder Slonsky seconded, and the motion passed unanimously. The chapter next goes to plan commission and then city council.

3D: Painting a Centerline Lane on the River Edge Trail

Alder Neil proposed painting a broken centerline stripe on the River Edge Trail from Winton to Oak Island, citing a successful example in Marquette, Michigan and safety benefits for mixed-use path users. Alder Troub supported the idea but emphasized the need for accompanying signage such as 'keep right except to pass,' noting that lines alone may be ignored without reinforcement. Alder Slonsky expressed mild hesitation about creating the appearance of an enforceable rule that would not actually be enforced. Staff noted a prior example of painting 'slow, children present' markings near a playground that appeared to reduce complaints. Alder Troub moved to proceed with painting the line using city staff on staff's own timeline to avoid additional costs, with a cost report back to the committee for future budget planning; Alder Neil seconded. The motion passed unanimously.

3E: 2027 Department Budget Overview and Proposed Changes

Parks Director Jamie presented a budget built to the mayor's directive of a flat 'cost of doing business' increase, coming in at approximately 1% over last year. The largest proposed cost-reduction measure was rotating pool closures across the three aquatic centers to reduce staffing costs; the department spends $449,000 on pools annually with only a 19% cost recovery rate. Two pool rotation options were presented — one keeping two pools open five days a week with all three open on weekends, and one closing all three pools one day per week for higher savings of approximately $29,781. Committee members generally favored keeping all three pools open Saturday and Sunday. Other proposals included reducing warf docks from seven to three (consolidating into a wider central platform), pausing goat use on Barker Stewart Island for one year with mowing as an alternative, switching from tablet to liquid chlorine in bulk, reducing the small projects budget by $5,000, and adding a $2-per-household-per-month urban forestry fee. Alder Troub strongly opposed the urban forestry fee as an additional burden on taxpayers already funding forestry through the property tax levy; Alder Slonsky expressed interest in exploring it but acknowledged its regressive nature for single-family homeowners. No formal votes were taken on budget items; the committee was asked to provide direction to staff on which operational changes to include in the budget submission to the mayor.
