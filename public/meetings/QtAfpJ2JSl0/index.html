The Wausau City Council Economic Development Committee met on October 6th and took action on several items including directing staff to draft a deed restriction for 1300 Cleveland Avenue to prevent industrial use, approving a planning and due diligence agreement for the UWSP at Wausau campus, and approving a Habitat for Humanity development proposal at 424 Burns Street. The meeting concluded with the committee voting to adjourn into closed session to negotiate a potential city purchase of 108 Myron Street.

Discussion

Approval of Minutes

Alder Tierney moved and Alder Kilian seconded approval of the minutes. Alder Kilian raised a correction, noting that the minutes referred to her with male pronouns and requested the error be fixed. The chair acknowledged the error under item C and confirmed it would be corrected. Minutes were approved as corrected.

November Meeting Reschedule

Staff recommended moving the November 3rd meeting (Election Day) to Wednesday, November 4th, as Monday and Thursday were booked with other committees. The chair noted it would not work for Alder Tierney and that absent member Alder Lucans needed to be consulted. No formal motion was taken; staff was directed to confirm availability with Alder Lucans before finalizing the date.

4A: 1300 Cleveland Avenue - EPA Technical Assistance and Deed Restriction

Staff reported the city has been confirmed eligible for EPA technical assistance, which would provide a consultant to conduct a financial feasibility analysis of reuse options identified through public engagement. Alder Kilian expressed concern that an email sent to the EPA by city staff characterized the public-identified reuse options as 'too narrow,' saying the committee and residents had never been told that. Kilian also raised that the property continues to be used for industrial purposes—including storage of trailers, old lampposts, and vehicles by DPW and the fire department—which she said undermined resident trust. City Attorney Jacobson confirmed it is legally permissible to place a deed restriction on city-owned property but noted the zoning has already been changed to residential and that any future council could remove a deed restriction. The committee debated how specific the restriction should be, with Alder Neil cautioning against language so narrow it might deter beneficial development. The committee ultimately directed staff and the city attorney, with input from Alder Kilian, to draft a deed restriction prohibiting industrial use consistent with public input meeting outcomes, to be brought back for formal approval at the next meeting. Staff also noted a 2024 council resolution allows limited DPW training use of the site, which may need to be revisited.

4B: UWSP at Wausau Campus - Planning and Due Diligence Agreement

Director Fick explained the planning and due diligence agreement would give the city exclusive access to the county-owned UWSP campus property for six months to conduct architectural, engineering, and environmental review, contingent on a budget modification for the fire and police departments also passing at the next council meeting. Alder Neil noted a prior council vote on feasibility funding had failed but that the item was reintroduced after the required 30-day waiting period and would come before council the following Tuesday. The chair asked about Wausau School District interest in joint use; staff confirmed Chief Barnes has been meeting with the school district and they have expressed interest in partnering to share space and defray costs. Alder Killian moved to approve and the motion carried.

4C: 424 Burns Street - Habitat for Humanity Proposal

Staff presented a Habitat for Humanity offer to purchase city-owned property at 424 Burns Street for approximately $12,000, with the plan to subdivide the parcel, sell a portion to an adjoining property owner, and build a Habitat home on the remainder. Alder Neil noted a strong track record with Habitat as a development partner and saw no need for closed session. He moved to approve the application, which was seconded by Alder Tierney and passed unanimously.

108 Myron Street - Closed Session for Purchase Negotiations

Staff recommended entering closed session to discuss the potential city purchase of 108 Myron Street, citing that open discussion would compromise the city's negotiating position. Alder Neil made a formal motion citing Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(e) for deliberating on the purchase of public property for competitive or bargaining reasons. The motion passed on a voice roll call vote and the committee adjourned to closed session.
