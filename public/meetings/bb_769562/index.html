Discussion

Consent Agenda Approval

The consent agenda was approved unanimously without discussion. It included staff appointments, replacements, contract changes, separations, leaves, retirements, minutes from four prior sessions (September 14, September 21 closed sessions, and September 28 special session), payment of bills, budget status, board member salaries, and donations from numerous community donors including Kulie Cards and Gaming, St. Andrew Lutheran Church, Walmart, and others.

Career and Technical Education and Work-Based Learning Update

CTE Coordinator John Winter presented an overview of CTE programs, recent community partnership events including a career day at Green Heck (60 students from East and West), the Build My Future event at Kobe Window with 400 students and 60 businesses, Food for America for fourth graders, and an Army Humvee visit to the auto shop. Winter detailed three work-based learning programs—Advanced Marketing Internship (48 students), Business Opportunities at West, and Youth Apprenticeship (72 students)—explaining the DWD grant structure, 450-hour requirement, and dual grant funding. Board members Charles Burger, Sarah Brock, and Cory Sillars asked questions about AI's impact on career clusters, the transition from 16 to 14 state career clusters, potential state budget funding for high school trades programs, and the possibility of county-wide or regional collaboration on CTE programs. Sillars raised the idea of inter-district collaboration to save costs, and Winter noted the aviation 101 WAVE program as an existing model.

Class Action Lawsuit Against Social Media Companies

Assistant Superintendent of Operations Dr. Elizabeth Channel presented details of a nationwide class action lawsuit against Meta, TikTok, Snap, and Google, alleging the companies designed platforms to addict youth, causing mental health harms including anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation. Channel noted recent litigation victories including a $375 million New Mexico judgment against Meta and a $27 million settlement in a Kentucky school district case, and explained there is no upfront cost to the district—only a 25% contingency fee if damages are recovered. The board voted unanimously to approve a resolution authorizing retention of legal counsel and initiation of litigation. Board President Bouché read the full resolution aloud before the vote.

2027-2028 School Calendar Approval

The board was informed that the DPI waiver for an early school start date—previously presented at the September Committee of the Whole by Communications Coordinator Diana White and Assistant Superintendent of Academics Dr. Katie Caswell—had been approved by DPI as of the prior week. The board voted unanimously to approve the 2027-2028 school calendar pending that waiver, which had already been granted to allow an early start due to anticipated construction.

Policy 2370 - Educational Opportunities (Wisconsin Seal of Biliteracy)

The board voted unanimously to approve proposed changes to Policy 2370, Educational Opportunities, to formally recognize the Wisconsin Seal of Biliteracy as an educational opportunity offered in the district. Dr. [Johnsky/Tossky - name unclear in transcript] had presented the background information at the September Committee of the Whole meeting. No additional discussion was noted at this meeting.

Board Member Professional Growth, Recognitions, and Closed Session Postponement

Board President Bouché recognized Sarah Brock for achieving WASB Level 2 status and acknowledged his own WASB Level 5 recognition, both awarded at the Region 5 WASB meeting. Sillars noted he would attend DPI boundary appeal board hearings on October 20-21 in Hortonville regarding a potential school district dissolution, which could reduce Wisconsin's 421 districts. Bouché announced the closed session for superintendent evaluation was postponed to October 28 because three board members were absent and he wanted all nine members present for the evaluation.
