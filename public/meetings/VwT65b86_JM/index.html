Village of Weston · Board of Trustees

Joint Budget Workshop Meeting

October 19, 2026 · 1h 53m
BudgetRefuse and Recycling FeesDebt ManagementPublic Safety BuildingMountain Bay Metro Police Department

The Village of Weston held a joint budget workshop with the Finance and Human Resources Committee and Board of Trustees to review the proposed 2027 budget across all funds and make key decisions on refuse fees and the public hearing notice. The board approved a $30 increase in annual garbage fees (raising the rate to $235.50) for 2027, with plans to re-evaluate annually, and approved the public hearing notice for the 2027 budget with a 5-2 vote. A projected 8-cent decrease in the tax rate was noted, though a future reassessment of all village properties is anticipated as the assessment ratio has fallen below required thresholds.

Discussion

Public Comment

Jim Johnson of 5000 Street, Weston, addressed the board on several concerns. He argued that the refuse reserve fund would be depleted within months and criticized the village for not billing the Town of Weston its approximately $100,000 share of fuel surcharges, calling it the result of 'Maloney's handshake deals from the 90s.' He also challenged a budget slide showing a tax rate decrease, saying it was misleading because it reflected a property revaluation rather than reduced spending, and questioned why the Mountain Bay Metro Police Department budget showed a 4.63% increase when their oversight board approved less than 3%.

Approval of October 5, 2026 Joint Budget Workshop Meeting Minutes

Both the Finance and HR Committee and the Board of Trustees voted to approve the minutes with one correction. Trustee Beck noted that the minutes incorrectly recorded her vote as 'aye' on a 6-1 motion on page 5, when she had actually voted 'nay.' The correction was acknowledged and the minutes were approved as amended.

2027 Budget — Tax Rate and General Fund Review

Finance Director Jessica presented the proposed 2027 budget, noting the projected tax rate decrease had shifted from 10 cents to approximately 8 cents after updated assessment ratio data arrived on Friday. She explained the village's assessment ratio has dropped to 0.86, triggering a future mandatory reassessment, likely within a few years. Extended discussion covered the Mountain Bay PD budget showing a 4.63% increase, which staff explained was an artifact of the way $100,000 was moved from the capital fund to the general fund in 2026, not an actual contract overage. Trustee Ermeling raised numerous line-item questions including overtime in committee budgets, ice rink wages, pet licensing pass-throughs, DPW salary transfers back from TIFF, and the street operations bonus line of $66,000, which staff confirmed was the transportation aid increase discussed at the prior meeting and part of the approved compensation plan.

2027 Budget — Debt and Borrowing Discussion

Trustees raised significant concerns about the village's total debt load. Trustee Beck pressed staff to confirm whether the $4 million bank note for the Mountain Bay police building was included in Ellers' projected combined principal of $49,380,132, expressing concern the real total could be $53-54 million. Staff confirmed the $4 million was included in Ellers' figures after a trustee found the notation in the orange header of Table 9. Jessica clarified total village outstanding debt is approximately $55 million at end of 2026 when revenue bonds are included. Trustees requested a review of the village's debt policy, last updated in May 2008, be placed on a future agenda. A trustee also recommended the board formally approve loan covenants for the $4 million police building bank note, which staff agreed to bring back for board review.

2027 Budget — Special Revenue, Capital, and Utilities

Discussion of special revenue funds included TIFF 1 closure with approximately $1.5 million anticipated to be allocated to taxing districts pending audit completion, and TIFF 2 remaining open as the deficit is not yet paid off. Room tax fund discussion prompted a trustee to suggest a dedicated future meeting to discuss allowable uses for tourism revenue beyond the current split between the CVB and transfers to the aquatic center and capital equipment fund. On utilities, staff noted a sanitary sewer rate increase will be needed in 2027, with Finance noting the remote metro sewage district costs have risen nearly $1 million over the past decade and are difficult to predict. Pool leak detection was reported ongoing since October 8th with results still pending.

Refuse and Recycling Fund — Garbage Fee Increase (Item 9)

Staff presented options for annual garbage fee increases, with a $30 increase (to $235.50) included in the proposed budget. The estimated year-end fund balance is $62,400 before any rate change. Public commenter Jim Johnson's earlier argument that the Town of Weston should be billed its share of fuel surcharges (approximately 4% of total costs, estimated at $100,000 for 2026) was addressed; staff clarified the town pays the same per-unit rate as village residents and is billed accordingly. After discussion, Finance Committee member Rickert moved to increase the fee to $235.50 for 2027 only, with re-evaluation in future years. The motion passed unanimously in committee and was adopted by the board, with Trustee Olson voting nay.

2027 Budget Public Hearing Notice (Item 10)

Staff presented the draft public hearing notice and noted the estimated assessed tax rate would be updated to approximately $5.52 based on new assessment data. After brief discussion about the net impact on a $300,000 home (approximately $30 reduction in property taxes roughly offsetting the $30 garbage fee increase), the Finance Committee approved the notice with the amended tax rate. The full Board voted 5-2 to approve, with Trustees Clark and Beck voting nay. The public hearing is scheduled for the November 16th board meeting at 6 PM.
