The Marathon County Board of Supervisors held its annual budget hearing on November 2, 2026 for the proposed 2027 budget. HR Finance Committee Chair Robinson and County Administrator Leonard presented a status quo budget that drops the property tax rate by one cent to $3.48, funds a new highway facility without new debt, and maintains current service levels. Three members of the public testified, two raising concerns about the county's Flock license plate reader camera system and one questioning the highway facility funding strategy.

Discussion

2027 Budget Overview

HR Finance Committee Chair Robinson outlined the year-long budget development process including a 'bucket challenge' prioritization exercise by board members, review of fees, and direction to avoid new debt. Administrator Leonard presented key budget metrics: the property tax rate drops one cent to $3.48, equalized value grew 6.25%, net new construction was 1.545%, and the county ranks 13th lowest in per capita spending statewide. Leonard emphasized the highway facility funding plan — moving the $3.5 million North Central Healthcare payment onto the debt levy to avoid new borrowing for the nearly $55 million project — as the cornerstone of the budget.

Social Services Department Staffing Increases

Supervisor Sundowski questioned a proposed increase of 12 positions and roughly $4 million in the Social Services budget. Administrator Leonard and Social Services Director Christa explained that the Children's Long-Term Support (CLTS) social worker positions are fully federally funded through Medicaid billing and driven by state wait-list requirements, while the economic support specialist positions are funded through a state contract related to federal quality assurance requirements tied to the 'big beautiful bill.' Leonard clarified that positions are added incrementally as caseloads require, and that if funding streams disappear, the positions are eliminated without board action.

Highway Facility Funding Strategy

Supervisor Cavelli and Supervisor Nisth questioned the phased implementation of the highway facility debt levy strategy, citing concerns raised by former supervisor David Baker in public testimony. Baker had argued that placing the full $3.5 million NCHC payment on the debt levy in year one contradicted the phased approach described in Resolution 4926, and asked whether proceeds from property sales or reserves could reduce the $25 million funding gap. Administrator Leonard defended the approach, explaining that delay increases risk of having to borrow for other capital projects in 2029-2031, and that proceeds from the sale of West Street shop, 28th Avenue parcels, and the Thomas Street property (400 East Thomas) could shorten the inter-fund loan period but are best evaluated after the annual audit.

Flock License Plate Reader System — Budget Implications

Following public testimony from Courtney Olsen and Felix Gallow calling for defunding the Flock camera system, Supervisor Morash asked what budget options exist. Administrator Leonard explained that grant funds for the original five cameras are fully expended and $6,300 is in the sheriff's budget to continue that contract through the remainder of 2027; a second grant covering three cameras does not expire until March 2029 with no budget funds required. Sheriff Bip addressed the board, defending the technology as a legitimate law enforcement tool, stating that searches require a case number and are limited to specific personnel, that data is not shared with private entities, that audits occur twice monthly, and that the system is not used for immigration enforcement. Supervisor Robinson announced he had arranged a meeting between the sheriff and Gallow to work toward resolution. The board agreed to refer detailed discussion to the November 10 Public Safety Committee meeting.

User Fees and Equity Concerns

Supervisor Leur raised concerns that increased user fees disproportionately burden lower-income residents and asked whether the county tracks the impact of fee increases on service utilization. Administrator Leonard acknowledged the concern, noted that the marriage license fee was deliberately not increased at the county clerk's recommendation, and emphasized that fee-setting is ultimately a board policy decision. He also noted two additional fee changes — a professional services fee in the medical examiner's office and minor solid waste adjustments — would be brought to the board before final budget adoption.
