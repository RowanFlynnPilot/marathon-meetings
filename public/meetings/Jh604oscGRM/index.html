Discussion

Public Comment – General

One speaker (identified as Jim) delivered pointed criticism of the planning and zoning department's reactive rather than proactive approach to conditional use permit compliance, specifically questioning how a property owner was allowed to occupy a non-ADA-compliant, unsafe building for months without enforcement action. Jim questioned why there was no ongoing dialogue with applicants during the two-year permit window and stated, 'Does it take for someone to get killed in that building and then oh my goodness, now we'll do something.' A second online commenter, Jacob Beldock, confirmed his written email regarding US AM31/47 Grand Avenue CUP 2024-0072 had been included in the packet.

Item 5: US Venture Inc. – Conditional Use Permit for Freight Terminal at 6002 Industrial Drive

Planner Jennifer presented the request for a CUP for a cross-dock tire distribution operation with two inbound 53-foot semis per day, seven delivery box trucks, and no outdoor storage. Applicant representative James Fland (Director of Real Estate, US Venture/US Auto Force) participated online, explaining the operation relocates from Rib Mountain and employs seven people. A neighbor, Brian (305 [street name]), raised concerns about traffic safety on the recently rebuilt path along the street given e-bikes and scooters, as well as noise and hours of operation. Jim P. spoke in support, noting he had a 20-year relationship with the company and stating the commission was acting in a quasi-judicial role under Wisconsin's 'shall issue' standard for conditional use permits. A motion to approve including a stormwater/tracking condition initially failed for lack of a second; a revised motion was approved removing condition D (limiting on-site trailer staging) and adding a condition requiring the applicant to work with village staff on preventing gravel/dirt tracking onto the road. An amendment adding the tracking/stormwater condition was voted on separately and approved before the main motion passed.

Item 6: Bug Properties LLC – Amendment to CUP 2023-0129 at 6406 Scoffield Avenue

Staff planner Jennifer and code official Roman presented the noncompliance status of Bug Properties LLC, with approximately 30% of required concrete installed and multiple accessibility, signage, and permit issues outstanding nearly three years after original CUP approval. The applicant, Bruce Gus, was not present at the start of the hearing and joined Zoom late, after the public hearing had already been closed. Jim P. (public commenter) expressed frustration that the applicant was not present and questioned the department's lack of proactive communication. Commissioners debated whether to grant the requested one-year extension to September 30, 2027, with several members objecting to that timeline. The commission approved a staggered amendment: all items except concrete must be completed by November 30, 2026, with concrete work extended to September 30, 2027.

Item 7: Special Exception Sign Permit – Turf MD at 5605 Meer Street

Staff presented a request to replace the cabinet on an existing 20-foot freestanding pylon sign that has a 5-foot setback where current code requires a 20-foot setback equal to sign height. Staff noted the pylon was originally installed in 1996 under old standards. Commissioners agreed the sign could not practically be relocated and approved the special exception sign permit unanimously with minimal debate.

Item 8: Mountain Bay Metro Police Department Site Plan Modification – 3600 Community Center Drive

Police Chief and project representatives explained that a TC Energy gas pipeline was found to be located outside its recorded 1960s easement, forcing the building footprint to shift approximately 12 feet west and the parking lot to be reconfigured, including adding a pedestrian walkway to the front entrance. The commission acknowledged the site plan changes and voted to recommend the Village Board authorize staff to issue an early start permit for footings and foundation only, contingent on verification that setback requirements are met, with a full amended site plan to return for formal approval in November 2026. Commissioner Zeyghami raised concern about whether TC Energy could excavate the parking lot in the future, and a commissioner suggested consulting the village attorney to explore whether TC Energy bears any cost obligations given the mislocated pipeline.

Item 11: Monthly Project Update – Tommy's Express and US AM31

Jennifer reported that Tommy's Express car wash had allowed its certificate of environmental insurance to lapse, with a new certificate in progress through corporate. Commissioners expressed serious concern about operating in a wellhead protection area without insurance coverage. One commissioner recommended that future conditional use permits include an automatic termination provision if the certificate of insurance expires, with a 30-day advance notification requirement, arguing staff should not have to bring this to the commission — it should be an automatic enforcement mechanism.
