The Wausau City Council held its first regular meeting in five weeks on October 13th, taking action on several significant items including approving full city funding for the private-side lead service line replacement program (estimated $4.5 million gap), passing a rezoning ordinance for Honey Locust Court over objections from the district alder, approving a transit fare collection system upgrade for Metroride, and defeating a drinking water treatment solar array project after EPA grant funding fell through. The meeting also featured extensive public comment on the Flock license plate reader camera system and boulevard snow plowing policy.

Discussion

Proclamations

Mayor Diny presented three proclamations: one designating 2026 Fire Prevention Week with a focus on lithium-ion battery safety; one commending the Wausau Fire Department for rescuing two occupants — including a member of the U.S. House of Representatives — from a downed aircraft in Lake Wausau on September 12th; and one proclaiming October as Pedestrian Safety Month. Fire Chief Jeremy Cop accepted the rescue commendation on behalf of department personnel. Chris Filtz from the Bike and Pedestrian Advisory Committee accepted the pedestrian safety proclamation and highlighted committee accomplishments including the e-bike ordinance and faster sidewalk clearing requirements.

Public Comment on Flock License Plate Reader Cameras

Multiple speakers urged the council to terminate the city's contract with Flock Safety, a license plate reader camera vendor. Courtney Olsen and Felix Gallow, both of 1206 Highland Park Boulevard, asked the council to omit the $50,000 Flock payment from the 2027 budget, deliver written notice of termination under contract section 11.16, and pass a surveillance technology ordinance requiring a council vote before future acquisitions. Gallow cited a website at unflockwausau.org and alleged Flock improperly shares and monetizes resident data. Danielle Fischer echoed those concerns, stating studies showing crime reduction were funded by Flock or similar companies. No council action was taken on this issue at this meeting.

Public Comment on Boulevard Snow Plowing Policy

Five residents from Highland Park Boulevard and surrounding streets — Karen Briggs, Danielle Fischer, Carrie Mor, Alice Kether, and Mary Broadhead — thanked the Infrastructure Committee for passing a resolution (Resolution 5526) to permit counterflow plowing so that snow is directed to the median strip rather than to residents' driveways. Speakers asked the council to tighten the language of the resolution so it cannot be changed at departmental discretion, with Broadhead proposing specific amended wording directing that plowing on boulevard streets push snow to the median strip.

File 26-10003: Rezoning Honey Locust Court from SR2 to DR8

Alder Tierney (District 9) pulled this item and asked for postponement so residents could meet with the Planning Commission before a vote, arguing that placing eight twin-home structures per acre on the cul-de-sac represented a significant change for the Great Tree subdivision area. Director Feifer and city staff clarified that the adjacent 40 lots are already zoned DR8, the land is privately owned by the developer, and a public hearing had already been held. Alder Killian supported postponement, citing the importance of resident feedback. Alder Martins opposed postponement, arguing zoning would be consistent with the rest of Honey Locust Court. A motion to postpone to the next meeting (moved by Alder Neil, seconded by Killian) failed 3-8. The main motion to approve the rezoning then passed 8-3.

Youth Engagement Committee of the Whole

Alders Wiscowski and Terry Kilian brought forward a request to schedule a committee of the whole focused on youth engagement and retention in Wausau. Wiscowski outlined four objectives: directly engaging young people, gathering firsthand perspectives on leaving and staying in Wausau, workshopping ideas collaboratively, and identifying actionable endeavors for 2027. The format would involve student councils from Wausau East and Wausau West high schools in a relaxed workshop environment, with a follow-up session planned for April. Alder Lukans asked that the scope include youth who stay locally rather than only those who leave for college. The motion passed unanimously 11-0, with a tentative date in the first two weeks of December.

File 26-10005: Drinking Water Treatment Solar Array Project

Alder Martins explained that the Finance Committee had voted against the solar array project after the city failed to receive a U.S. EPA grant that would have covered up to 50% of costs. Without the grant, city borrowing would extend the payback period to nearly 20 years — close to the panels' expected 25-30 year lifespan. The Finance Committee voted to deny the project. The full council voted 0-11 against approving the project, effectively killing it.

File 26-10006: Funding Option for Private-Side Lead Service Line Replacement

Consultants from CIP presented data showing Wausau leads the nation in per-capita lead service line replacements, with over 3,500 lines replaced and approximately 2,850-2,900 remaining. Construction costs in Wisconsin have risen 56% over two years, and the current low-interest BIL (Bipartisan Infrastructure Law) funding at 0.25% is set to sunset. The Finance Committee recommended Option C — the city covers the full estimated $4.5 million private-side gap for 2027 rather than charging homeowners — on a 3-0 vote. Alder Martins explained the rationale was equity across all residents. The full council approved Option C unanimously 11-0.

File 26-0921: Lead and Galvanized Service Line Replacement Ordinance (Reconsidered)

The council first voted 9-2 to reconsider this ordinance, which had previously failed. Alder Hanniki proposed an amendment adding incremental repayment schedules (3-6 month or 1-2 year periods) for property owners responsible for remaining balances, but withdrew the amendment after the council passed full city funding under File 26-10006, making the repayment language largely moot. City Attorney Jacobson clarified that Section 9 of the ordinance regarding property owner responsibility is not inconsistent with the funding resolution but serves as a contingency for any stragglers after grant funding expires. Alder Wiscowski raised concerns about enforcement language (shall/may/will) and the penalty timeline. Staff indicated enforcement would focus on landlords who repeatedly refuse access. The ordinance was passed, though the final vote tally was not clearly stated in the transcript.

File 260905: WISGO/Cubic Fare Collection System for Metroride

Deputy Director Melinda presented on behalf of Metroride, explaining the new fare collection system allows payment via app, card, or traditional cash vault. Alder Killian confirmed cash payment remains an option for those without digital access. Alder Martins noted the system is largely grant-funded and also includes automated vehicle location (AVL) technology so riders can track bus arrival times. Alder True Blood raised access concerns, suggesting a vending machine at the transit depot be considered for future planning. The motion passed 11-0.
