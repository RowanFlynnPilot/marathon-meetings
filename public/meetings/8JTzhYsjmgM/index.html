The Wausau Finance Committee held its first of four budget workshop sessions for the 2027 city budget on October 5, 2026. The proposed budget includes a total city levy of $40.63 million, up $2.5 million (6.76%) from the prior year, with $1.45 million of the increase driven by a voter-approved referendum. The session covered high-level budget impacts, public works, motorpool, water/wastewater, and parks department budgets, with significant discussion around motorpool funding shortfalls, health insurance cost pressures, and the need for revenue diversification.

Discussion

Mayor's High-Level Budget Overview

Mayor Doug Diny presented the top-line 2027 budget: $40.63 million total city levy, up $2.5 million from 2026, with an assessed tax rate of $9.24 per thousand. He noted that $1.45 million of the increase (3.8%) is attributable to the voter-approved referendum, and the remaining 1.9% stems from operational factors. Directors were directed to seek a 2% decrease in cost-to-continue expenses; four positions are being held vacant (~$410,000 in savings). A new city administrator position at $150,000 is included, envisioned to review and implement recommendations from a pending citywide audit. No new fees are proposed, consistent with the council's stated opposition to regressive fees.

Finance Director Budget Presentation — Revenues, Levy, and Assessed Value

Finance Director Maryanne detailed that computer aid came in at $557,000 versus a budgeted $513,000, yielding approximately $44,000 in additional revenue — a modification not yet reflected in the budget book. She noted assessed value is estimated at 1% growth pending mid-October confirmation, meaning the tax rate could shift. Impact on a median home is estimated at $99.73. She also distributed a Forward Analytics report showing Marathon County's net new construction is covering only about half of CPI growth since 2010, illustrating structural revenue constraints. The garbage contract increase of approximately $291,000 — absorbed into the levy rather than passed to ratepayers — was cited as a key cost pressure.

Position Vacancies and HR Position Authorization

An alderperson (identified as Vicki in context) raised a concern that an additional HR position budgeted at $100,000 had not been fully authorized by the council, noting she voted against it previously and was unaware of it returning for a vote. Maryanne and other officials explained the position is being brought back at the HR committee meeting the following week, and that it had previously been approved with the understanding it would be filled after a new director was hired. The vacancies held open include: one downtown police officer FTE (previously foundation-funded), one assistant city attorney FTE, a half-year fire division chief/inspection lieutenant FTE, a DPW surveyor half-FTE, a parks administrative coordinator half-FTE, and a CCITC project manager FTE.

Public Works Department Budget

DPW Director Eric presented the public works budget, which decreased roughly 4% overall. Discussion focused on leaf pickup operations — clarifying the city never conducted two full collections but eliminated the two-phase map that caused public confusion. Alderperson Watson raised a concern about significant reductions in contractual services (~$80,000) relative to the DPW request, with Eric noting the cuts were made line-by-line with the mayor and finance director and that the city surveyor position remains unfilled due to lack of qualified applicants. Fee structure was discussed, with committee members noting inspection and land-use permit fees remained flat while animal licensing fees were proposed to increase — a disparity questioned by at least one alderperson.

Motorpool Budget and Mercury Audit

The motorpool discussion revealed a recurring structural shortfall: departments requiring motorpool services cannot afford full cost recovery through their levy-funded budgets. In 2025, actual motorpool charges to the general fund totaled $5.6 million against budgeted amounts of $4.1 million, leaving a $1.4 million gap partially covered by investment income ($370,000) and departmental savings. Alderperson Hanie questioned how many Mercury audit recommendations had been implemented; fleet staff confirmed four were addressed (hiring a fleet analyst, reassessing rental rates, increasing staffing, reducing fleet age) but acknowledged the core problem is that departments cannot afford the equipment they need. Eric argued the city must diversify revenue — specifically citing a wheel tax, stormwater utility, and converting garbage to a special charge — to sustainably fund motorpool. The aerial truck replacement was flagged as a deferred need: lead time is 24 months for a standard model or up to 51 months for a custom unit; a vendor offered a ~$200,000 discount for prepayment, which the city historically does not do.

Water and Wastewater Utility Budgets

Eric and Maryanne presented both utility budgets, which remained relatively flat year-over-year. A notable item is $1.667 million in congressionally directed spending originally earmarked for granular activated carbon replacements; the EPA ruled that use ineligible (classifying it as operations rather than capital), so the city intends to redirect the funds toward an ortho phosphate corrosion control system required by mandate to be operational by 2028. Staffing increases in both water and wastewater utilities were confirmed as previously approved. Other financing sources in both funds represent borrowing for capital projects, including $6 million for public-side lead service line replacement.

Parks Department Budget

Parks Director presented a 2027 proposed budget only $18,652 over the 2026 adopted budget, achieved through operational reductions approved by the parks committee: rotating pool schedule (two pools open weekdays, all three on weekends), elimination of the downtown flower basket program from the operating budget (potentially to be funded from room tax at committee's discretion), and reduction of WARF docks from seven to five. Fee increases and adding the Brockmere Park main building to the rental schedule contributed to revenue offsets. No significant capital improvement projects are planned for 2027; focus will shift to deferred maintenance. An Oak Island tennis court renovation will be pursued through a Community Development Block Grant. The skate park refurbishment was cited as complete. Discussion included creative ideas for flower basket sponsorships modeled on programs in Merrill and Napa Valley.

Closing Discussion — Health Insurance, Asphalt Overlay, Animal Control, Block Cameras

Mayor Diny noted asphalt overlay was increased from $600,000 to $800,000, citing that inflation-adjusted road spending is less than half of levels from 10-12 years ago. He flagged health insurance as a critical long-term concern: a 9.6% increase this year (~$450,000), with a family plan costing the city approximately $35,000 annually (88% city share), on a trajectory to double in 6-7 years. An alderperson raised the 3% COLA plus 1.5% step increase compounding as a further concern. An alderperson indicated intent to propose an amendment related to animal control fund levy independence and requested data ahead of Tuesday's police budget session. Another committee member flagged the $50,000 block camera contract as controversial and subject to scrutiny on Tuesday.
