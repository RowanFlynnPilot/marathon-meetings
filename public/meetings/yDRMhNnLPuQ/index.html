The Marathon County Human Resources, Finance, and Property Committee met and addressed several significant items including a credit union lease situation at 400 Thomas Street, conduit financing consent for a local hotel project, an ARPA funding update, a detailed presentation on the sheriff's office contract with the Village of Rib Mountain, and a Wisconsin Public Service easement matter. Discussion

Approve August 25th and August 8th joint meeting minutes

Chair Gibbs called for approval of the August 25th minutes and the August 8th joint meeting minutes with the EEC. Vice Chair Pool moved and Supervisor Hart seconded. The motion carried with no opposition noted.

Credit union lease at 400 Thomas Street

County administrator reported that the credit union, which leases space at the former social services building at 400 Thomas Street, has not provided a firm move-out date despite multiple requests. Chair Gibbs asked whether the county could invoke the 180-day notice provision in the lease to force the issue. Administrator confirmed the authority exists under the lease. The administrator advised against formally directing termination that day, noting a closed session update was planned, and said he would convey to the credit union that the committee had discussed the possibility of issuing notice.

August claims and question costs

A motion was made to approve claims and question costs for August 2026. The motion carried with no opposition. Chair Gibbs noted to staff that weekend work to prepare materials was appreciated but not expected.

Conduit financing consent via Public Finance Authority for Wasa Hotel Group

County administrator Leonard explained that Wasa Hotel Group requested consent to access up to $10 million in bonding through the Public Finance Authority (PFA) for hotel renovations and debt refinancing, with no financial obligation or impact on the county's general obligation bonding capacity. Managing member John Kofi described the hotel's turnaround from being the second-worst performing Holiday Inn Express out of 2,600 to the top 2%, with $3.5 million invested since 2017 and approximately $285,000 in annual room tax contributions. Bond counsel Frank Gare (Corals and Brady) confirmed there is no statewide cap on PFA issuance. Vice Chair Pool moved to consent; Chair Gibbs seconded. The motion carried unanimously.

ARPA funding update and reallocation plan

Finance staff reported that of the original $26,356,580 in ARPA funds, approximately $480,824.39 remains unspent. The most active remaining projects are the 9M forest septic system and Shelly renovation, and potentially the fiber optics Bug Tussle project. Staff indicated a resolution to reallocate unspent funds to a previously approved project will be brought to the committee next month to minimize clawback risk ahead of the December 31st expenditure deadline.

Sheriff's office contract with the Village of Rib Mountain

Sheriff Chad Phillip and County Administrator Leonard presented detailed data analysis by Data Officer Michael Schultz showing that dedicated Rib Mountain deputies handle only about 29% of calls in the village, with 70-71% handled by non-dedicated county deputies. The data showed peak call volume occurring from noon through early evening, prompting a shift in deputy scheduling toward afternoon hours. Administrator Leonard noted the current contract undercharges Rib Mountain by approximately $30,000 per deputy annually when indirect costs are properly calculated. Vice Chair Pool asked whether the three Rib Mountain deputies also serve other areas; the sheriff confirmed they do, including SWAT, dive team, and crash reconstruction duties, with roughly 60 documented instances of responding outside Rib Mountain. The committee was advised that as a village, Rib Mountain is now legally mandated to provide law enforcement and that any contract requires county board ratification. Further discussion was deferred to closed session.

Wisconsin Public Service easement at Sunny Bell Park

Administrator Leonard briefly described this as a carryover item from a previous meeting, noting that WPS had proposed a valuation for a natural gas regulator station easement at Sunny Bell Park that the committee had previously indicated was insufficient. Director Pauly was referenced as involved in the valuation discussions. No substantive open-session debate occurred; the matter was referred to closed session for direction to administration.

Motion to enter closed session

Chair Gibbs read the motion to enter closed session under Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(e) for bargaining reasons, covering two topics: the sheriff's office contract with the Village of Rib Mountain and the WPS easement at Sunny Bell Park (Sunny Bell Park natural gas regulator station). Vice Chair Pool seconded. A roll call vote was taken and the motion carried.
