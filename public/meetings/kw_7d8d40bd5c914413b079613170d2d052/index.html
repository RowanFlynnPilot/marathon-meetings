Community Life, Infrastructure and Public Property (CLIPP) Committee Meeting

October 5, 2026

The Kronenwetter CLIPP Committee held its second review of the proposed 2027-2036 Capital Improvement Program (CIP), a 10-year plan totaling approximately $4.3 million in 2027 expenditures across roads, parks, utilities, public safety equipment, and facilities. No formal vote was taken on the CIP itself, as adoption is scheduled for the November 9 budget hearing; discussion focused on project priorities including Maple Ridge Road reconstruction, fire truck preordering timelines, lift station upgrades, a future water tower and well, and parks improvements at Bushga Park. The committee also briefly addressed a $1.2 million borrowing deadline of October 2027 and the fiscal implications of shifting $863,000 in TID 2 proceeds back to the village's general debt service.

Discussion

2027-2036 Capital Improvements Program - Overview

Village Administrator John presented the second draft of the 10-year CIP, noting the primary additions since the first draft were park committee items with cost estimates placed into specific years. He emphasized the 2027 year is the only year being formally adopted in the upcoming budget cycle, with 2028-2036 serving as a planning tool. The total proposed 2027 CIP expenditure is approximately $4.3 million spread across the general fund, capital projects fund, capital equipment fund, utility funds, and the park improvement fund. The administrator stressed the village has historically failed to invest in infrastructure — one employee noted it had been 26 years since the village last paved a road.

Fire Department Rescue Truck Preorder (2029)

The administrator noted the rescue/fire truck slotted for 2029 requires a formal commitment to preorder three to four years in advance, meaning an order placed now would likely not be received until 2030 or later. No down payment is required at order time, but the village board would need to sign a preorder agreement in a future budget year. The committee acknowledged this commitment does not need to be finalized in the next few months but cannot be deferred indefinitely without pushing delivery further out.

Maple Ridge Road Reconstruction

Trustee Myszka and President Joling identified Maple Ridge Road as the highest priority road project, noting it is the only village road with freeway access and the county recently improved adjacent county roads. Public Works Director Greg confirmed that core samples have been taken but a full engineering design is still needed; realistically, construction would begin in 2028 at the earliest. The administrator proposed potentially splitting the project across 2027 (engineering/design) and 2028 (construction), and noted that $1.2 million in leftover 2024 borrowing proceeds — which must be spent by October 2027 — could be committed to Maple Ridge. The committee discussed that approved bids from the village board may be sufficient to legally 'commit' those funds by the deadline. A question was raised about whether the steep hill section east of Spring could be graded down; Greg cautioned this would involve significant excavation costs, slope design, and potential DNR involvement.

Municipal Building Maintenance

Trustee [Patty Tukowski] raised concerns that only modest capital funds were set aside in 2027 for the municipal building despite known maintenance issues including roof leaks. The administrator acknowledged the building has suffered from years of inconsistent maintenance and explained that some repairs — such as heated roof tape and handicap entrance fixes — would be absorbed into the operational budget rather than the capital plan. He framed the CIP as a working document that will evolve as priorities shift.

Parks CIP Items and Fundraising Update

The parks committee additions, including playground equipment for Bushga Park and fencing for other parks, were incorporated into the CIP with 2027 borrowing projected at approximately $175,000, partially offset by $25,000 from the park improvement fund. Trustee [Patty Tukowski] noted the parks committee had unanimously recommended adding a zip line and rubber surface to Friendship Park for the 2029 improvement cycle; the administrator acknowledged this was not yet in the document and committed to adding it before the October 19-20 budget workshops. On fundraising, Trustee Myszka reported approximately $20,000 raised to date, including $1,348 from the 5K event and a $236 donation from the 50/50 raffle winner.

Sewer and Water Utility CIP Items

Public Works Director Greg detailed planned lift station upgrades (stations 5, 7, 9 panel replacements and generator additions), the removal of lift station 10 serving approximately 60 homes, and the in-progress removal of lift station 3 which uses a vacuum system prone to daily clogging. He highlighted the need for a vac/jet back truck (approximately $500,000) and a waste truck, both of which are dependent on the new garage being completed around February. The water utility section flagged a projected $11 million need for a new well and water tower, with the administrator cautioning this is a rough high estimate spread over multiple years and subject to a five-to-six-year Public Service Commission approval process. A potential TIF district was discussed as a way to offset water infrastructure costs, though TID 1 in the target area is severely distressed and cannot be overlaid with a new TID boundary.

Debt Service and Revenue Structure

The administrator explained that shifting $863,000 in TID 2 bond proceeds back to the village's general debt service — because the Flanner Jamerals project came in over budget and did not use those proceeds — will modestly increase the village's tax levy debt service obligation but allow TID 2 to close earlier, potentially releasing approximately $80 million in valuation and generating an estimated $250,000 annually in additional property tax revenue. Trustee [Paul Maja] recalled the administrator's earlier explanation that a significant drop in existing debt service around 2029 would help absorb new borrowing, which the administrator confirmed but noted the restructuring has reduced two prior projected drops to one drop of approximately $200,000 in 2029. For 2027, general obligation borrowing is proposed for only two projects: Bushga Park playground equipment and Maple Ridge Road, totaling approximately $1.8 million.

Highway Aid Formula Impact

The administrator noted that completing Kronenwetter Drive in 2025 ($2.2 million project) has already increased the village's annual state highway aid by over $50,000 for the 2027 budget year due to the state's three-year and six-year spending averages. He projected that continuing capital investments in roads and police staffing would further increase highway aid in 2028 and beyond, framing infrastructure investment as self-reinforcing from a revenue standpoint.

Next Meeting and Future Agenda Items

The committee noted the November 2nd meeting falls the day before the general election. Trustee [Patty Tukowski] indicated she would be working the election and would need to start at 6:30 a.m.; the committee briefly discussed moving the meeting start time to 5:30 p.m. with general agreement but no formal action. The administrator stated the November 2nd meeting would primarily provide a CIP update and possibly address the fire truck commitment timeline. Trustee Myszka requested a follow-up on the park bricks project for a future agenda.
