EEED & HRFC Committee Meeting

September 9, 2026
Economic DevelopmentCampus RedevelopmentPublic SafetyArts and CultureUW-Stevens Point Wausau Campus

The Marathon County EEED and HRFC committees held a joint meeting to review four RFP responses for redevelopment of the UW-Stevens Point Wausau campus, plus hear an update on negotiations with the Marathon Center for Arts and Culture. Committees heard presentations from Wisconsin Ice Volleyball Club, the Center for the Visual Arts, Wausau Police Chief Barnes on the City of Wausau's public safety campus proposal, and Parks & Recreation staff before voting unanimously to enter closed session to discuss negotiation strategy on the RFP responses.

Discussion

RFP Overview — Four Proposals Summarized

Administrator Leonard and Chris Holman presented four RFP responses at a high level, noting this was not a traditional scored RFP but a committee-driven criteria process allowing for negotiation. Holman clarified that the Lone Wolf Works proposal (central heating plant for automotive/fabrication) was essentially a contingency proposal dependent on the heating plant being decommissioned, and was not immediately actionable. A question arose about whether the county could counter-offer; Leonard confirmed further negotiations were anticipated and that any property transfer requires full county board ratification.

Wisconsin Ice Volleyball Club Proposal

Board member Jody Davis explained the club's 25-year history and urgent need for dedicated gym space, citing displacement from Wisconsin Valley Lutheran, scheduling conflicts at Northland Lutheran, and costs of $25–$44/hour at facilities like CTI. Davis clarified that while the proposal included the East and West Arts buildings, the fieldhouse is the club's exclusive priority. The club indicated openness to subletting the pool and other spaces to organizations like the YMCA and youth hockey. In response to a question, Davis confirmed the arts buildings were included to help the county, not because the club needed them.

City of Wausau Proposal — Police Department Campus Relocation

Police Chief Barnes explained the city's motivation: relocating the fire department into the existing police station to avoid an estimated $30–50 million new fire station construction cost, then moving the police department to the UW campus. He described a vision including the Wausau School District potentially occupying the south hall of North-South Hall to address $3 million in deferred maintenance on district offices, and creating a shared regional law enforcement training hub. Barnes emphasized the 6-month due diligence period is to hire third-party experts to assess renovation costs, not a commitment, and stressed the proposal could save taxpayers tens of millions of dollars long-term.

Center for the Visual Arts Proposal

Executive Director Mara Mullen described CVA's 40-year history, 40,000 annual gallery visitors, 90-plus classes per year, and severe space constraints at their downtown location at 427 North Fourth Street — including a two-plus-year camp waitlist and inability to offer oil painting due to ventilation issues. She said the East and West Arts buildings would allow CVA to add a wood shop, print studio, expanded ceramics with a gas kiln, and more youth programming. She noted that building from scratch would cost millions, making the UW campus a highly attractive option, and proposed assuming full operational costs starting January.

Parks Department Community Recreation Proposal

Parks Director Jamie presented a high-level concept for operating the fieldhouse as a county or city community recreation center, with programming including open gyms, leagues, pool use (noting YMCA interest in 5-days-per-week pool rental), county employee fitness center access, senior programming, and potential youth hockey dryland training. She was candid that the facility in its current form would likely require a tax subsidy rather than generate revenue, comparing it to the city's pools. She also flagged that Parks staff currently park in the UW lot and would have no alternative if that lot changed hands.

Marathon Center for Arts and Culture Update

Administrator Leonard reported that negotiations with Marathon Center for Arts and Culture — directed by the committee after the first RFP round for the Center for Civic Engagement building — are ongoing but delayed because the group has not yet received its 501(c)3 nonprofit status, which is critical for a tax-exempt property transfer by the January 1, 2027 goal. He said they are discussing interim occupancy mechanisms and that the group may want to accelerate the move-in timeline. He also disclosed that the group informally requested the county act as employer for a new executive director position, fully reimbursed, but noted this raised serious liability concerns and no action was requested at this meeting.

Campus Map Orientation

Supervisor Kremes requested a satellite map view of the campus. Holman oriented committee members to the layout, identifying the theater/Center for Civic Engagement as a standalone green parcel not connected to the central heating plant, with all other red parcels tied to the heating plant. He identified the fieldhouse, pool, fitness center, racquetball courts, art buildings, dorms (Marathon Hall), and central heating plant locations. Chief Barnes and Parks Director Jamie discussed parking constraints, noting the county has historically maintained Marathon Park lots for the university and that parking solutions would need to be negotiated among all parties.

Motion to Enter Closed Session — EEED Committee

A supervisor identified as Leur moved to enter closed session under Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(e) for competitive bargaining reasons, specifically to discuss RFP responses for the UWSP Wausau campus and the Marathon Center for Arts and Culture executive director position request. Corporation Counsel Ryan confirmed the statutory requirements for closed session had been met. The motion passed 7-0 on a roll call vote.

Motion to Enter Closed Session — HRFC Committee

Vice Chair moved to enter closed session under the same statutory authority with identical scope. The motion was seconded by Supervisor Hart and passed 4-0 on a roll call vote. Staff present were confirmed as authorized to remain for the closed session.
