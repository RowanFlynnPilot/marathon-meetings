Executive Committee Meeting

September 17, 2026
Wind EnergyHub City Wind ProjectBroadband InfrastructureBudgetPSC Intervention

The Marathon County Executive Committee held a lengthy public meeting dominated by debate over the proposed Hub City Wind project by Alliant Energy. After 10 public commenters urged the committee to reject a Joint Development Agreement (JDA) with Alliant and instead pursue PSC intervention, the committee voted 8-2 to forward a resolution to the full County Board directing Alliant Energy to submit a proposed agreement addressing concerns raised by the alternative energy work group, with the agreement to be shared with affected townships upon receipt. The committee also received an update on the troubled Bug Tussle broadband project before moving into closed session.

Discussion

Public Comment – Hub City Wind Project

Ten speakers signed up, with nearly all opposing a JDA with Alliant Energy and urging the county to instead pursue intervenor status at the PSC. Speakers including Tom Wilcox of Farmland First argued a county-level JDA risks preempting township zoning authority and asked the committee to join the townships' intergovernmental committee at the PSC. Joe Kiwok of the Town of Cleveland cited Columbia County's intervention as a model to follow. Mary Clues, speaking on behalf of the Marathon County Farmers Union, urged the county to negotiate hard rather than cede its power. Dean Beck warned against signing any NDA and urged baseline road assessments before construction. Glenn Regowski cited a Wausau Pilot poll showing the Hub City Wind Farm generated the strongest reader opposition of any poll, and noted he had seen no supporters from the five affected townships at any meeting.

Approval of August 13, 2026 Executive Committee Meeting Minutes

Chair Gibbs called for approval of the minutes as presented in the packet. Supervisor Robinson moved and Supervisor Morash seconded. No discussion was held. The motion passed with an abstention granted to Supervisor Gao.

2027 Annual Budget Development and Policy Recommendations

Administrator Leonard briefly noted that budget work is proceeding toward a September 29th presentation deadline, at which point the budget becomes the board's budget. Supervisor Robinson reminded all standing committees to weigh in at their September meetings, noting the Human Resource Finance Property Committee meets at 1:00 p.m. and the ERC at 3:00 p.m. on the 29th. No action was taken.

Discussion Regarding Request from Supervisor Endrris

Supervisor Mask questioned how Endrris contacted all his constituents given reports that his phone was out of service. Supervisor Boots disclosed that two AI detection tools rated the document 82–85% likely AI-generated, and he demonstrated by generating a counter-resolution using AI himself. Supervisor Robinson said regardless of authorship, the request raised real concerns but was 'overly broad' and potentially very expensive—estimating intervention costs at $50,000 to $200,000 or more depending on scope. Supervisor Gao recommended placing the request on file for now. No formal motion or vote was taken; the item was effectively deferred pending further scoping and cost analysis.

Consideration of Potential Engagement with Alliant Energy – Hub City Wind Project

CPZ staff presented a pros/cons matrix of four options: JDA negotiation (estimated up to $20,000), PSC intervention ($50,000–$200,000), moratorium (viewed as legally unenforceable for a 100+ MW project under Wis. Stat. 66.0401), and post-CPCN appeal. Staff noted the PSC could not recall any case where intervention stopped a wind project. Supervisor Robinson moved the 'Robinson resolution,' which directs Alliant Energy to submit a proposed agreement addressing work group concerns by November 1, 2025, with a further amendment added requiring the agreement be shared with governing bodies of affected townships upon receipt. Supervisor Gao proposed an alternative amendment drawing on the second resolution (putting negotiations on hold) but it was ruled out of order as it did not amend the Robinson resolution. Corporation Counsel clarified the committee's task was simply to forward a resolution to the full County Board, not to finalize county policy. The amendment to share the agreement with townships passed with one or more opposed; the full amended resolution passed 8-2 on a roll call vote.

Bug Tussle Broadband Update

Administrator Leonard summarized the history: a 2021 $25 million conduit financing for Bug Tussle Wireless to build a fiber ring around Marathon County, followed by a county-approved $8 million loan ($5M to complete the ring, $3M as match for a PSC broadband grant). The ring was never completed—only half was built due to cost overruns—and Bug Tussle has now requested an additional approximately $45 million from Marathon County as part of a $500 million ask across all participating counties for BEAD projects. Infrastructure committee received the Bug Tussle presentation September 3rd. The item was moved to closed session due to bargaining considerations and potential litigation.
