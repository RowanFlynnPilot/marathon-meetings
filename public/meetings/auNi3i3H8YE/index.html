Discussion

Award Presentations to Marathon County Medical Examiner's Office

Greg Kimble of the American Tissue Services Foundation presented a certificate recognizing the Medical Examiner's Office for its partnership in tissue donation, noting that in 2025 over 600 people will have improved quality of life from recovered tissues, with Marathon County's donor registry rate of 57.8% exceeding the state average of 55.7%. Katie Shara of the Lion's Eye Bank of Wisconsin presented the 2026 Crystal Vision Award to the Medical Examiner's Office for outstanding commitment to eye donation. The ME office's autopsy supervisor Nick Hanky and medical legal investigator Leah Shite were present to accept the awards, with the ME office representative expressing honor at receiving both awards simultaneously.

Consent Agenda (Items C9 through C13E)

Supervisor Drebeck moved and Supervisor Conway seconded approval of the consent agenda covering items C9 through C13E. There was no discussion. The motion carried but was noted as not unanimous.

Infrastructure Committee Chair Appointment - Aaron Griner

The clerk read the appointment of Supervisor Aaron Griner to serve as infrastructure committee chair, replacing Chris Dickinson, with a term expiring at the April 2028 organizational meeting. Griner delivered a prepared statement emphasizing the importance of infrastructure to Marathon County, acknowledging novel challenges like large-scale transportation, energy production, data processing, and AI development projects, and pledging fiscal responsibility and data-driven deliberation. He thanked Chair Gibbs, Vice Chair Boots, and Vice Chair Ga for their support. Motion was made by Supervisor Watson and seconded by Supervisor Gao; the motion carried but was not unanimous.

Amendment to Resolution 68-26: Property Divestment Strategy

Supervisor Sandalski introduced an amendment requiring the board to develop a property divestment strategy for the existing West Street highway facility before construction is complete, with sale proceeds applied to the general fund, and with an explicit goal to return the property to the property tax rolls. Administrator Leonard clarified that recent practice has been to issue requests for proposals evaluating redevelopment plans, with any sale requiring board approval. Supervisor Robinson noted that any sale would likely fall to a future board. Supervisor Sybert expressed frustration that property assets are frequently cited to offset project costs but rarely actually sold. The amendment was seconded by Supervisor Ga and carried, though not unanimously.

Resolution 68-26 as Amended: Phase 4 Construction of New Highway Facility ($55 Million)

An extended debate followed on the $54,981,000 guaranteed maximum price highway facility in Kronenwetter. Highway Commissioner Lang and project manager Chris Hullman detailed operational benefits including indoor equipment storage to extend vehicle life, improved wash bay capacity, geothermal HVAC with an estimated $760,000 IRA tax credit on the $1.9 million borefield, and consolidation of West Street and Mosinee operations. Opponents including Supervisors Straw, Ha, and Drop raised concerns about cost ($55 million versus an estimated $30 million alternative), the facility's proximity to WPS's Weston power plant and coal dust drift, and traffic access concerns on Old 51. Supporters including Supervisors Robinson, Shaneborn, Jensen, Nath, Boots, Mask, and Ga emphasized the nearly 100-year-old existing facility's structural failures, safety hazards, equipment deterioration from outdoor storage, and the risk of higher costs if construction is delayed. Administrator Leonard stated this would be built without bonding, calling it an unusual achievement for a $50+ million project, and referenced Sauk County's 2022 highway shop costing $50 million. Supervisor Mask called the question; debate was ended by a two-thirds majority vote, with queued speakers still allowed to finish. The resolution as amended was then brought to a vote and carried, though not unanimously.
