The Weston Parks and Recreation Committee met to discuss aquatic center repairs and financials, approve the Machm Miller Park master plan, and review the Kennedy Park master plan and quarterly update. The committee voted unanimously to recommend the Machm Miller Park master plan to the Village Board, and held substantive discussion about the future of Kennedy Park development, including the farmers market location, a potential hockey facility in the northwest corner, and fundraising priorities for a future concession stand.

Approval of Minutes

The minutes from the September 28, 2026 meeting were moved and seconded without discussion. The committee approved them unanimously on a voice vote.

Public Comments

Two public comments were received. Tony Case, a farmers market vendor from Ringle, made three suggestions to improve traffic flow and parking at the Kennedy Park farmers market lot, including widening the entrance to three lanes, adding a permanent directional sign to overflow parking, and exploring use of a strip of land near the power boxes for additional parking. An online commenter identified as Jim criticized the committee sharply, calling out the lack of a Friends of Kennedy Park financial update in the meeting packet, the absence of cost estimates or recommendations in the aquatic center repair staff report, and the failure to include a timeline or realistic financial impact for the Machm Miller Park plan, stating the estimated total cost exceeds $4.137 million and calling the omissions 'unacceptable.'

Update on Aquatic Center Repairs

Staff member Sean reported that contracts are in place with New Pools for leak detection ($12,000) and for motors, pumps, and valves ($123,000-$128,000), both previously approved. Leak detection identified a suspected area near the play structure, close to a previous leak location, and deck removal began to verify it; no cost estimate for repairs was available when the packet was sent Wednesday. Staff explained the pressure-check sonar method used for leak detection and noted the pool has been winterized with RV antifreeze, with water still needing to be returned to the deep well.

Update on Aquatic Center Financials

Sean presented the current operational budget showing revenues of $246,000 against a budget of $226,000, and expenditures of $230,000 against a budget of $256,000, for a positive variance of approximately $16,000 revenue over expenditures compared to budget. The pool special revenue fund balance stands at approximately $29,000, which would grow to $45,000 with the $16,000 surplus, pending remaining utility and winterization costs through December. Staff noted the operational budget does not include capital expenses such as the outside contractor contracts, which run through the CIP.

Machm Miller Park Master Plan and Cost Estimates

Staff presented the most recent version of the Machm Miller Park master plan and cost estimates, noting the plan is not yet approved and there is therefore no current fiscal impact. The total estimated cost is high and the plan is envisioned as a 10-to-20-year roadmap for consistency as committee membership changes. Notable additions from public input included tennis and basketball courts; staff flagged that the cost estimate omits redoing existing walking paths, only pricing new trail sections at approximately $90,000. A committee member moved to recommend the plan to the Village Board, which was seconded by Roger, and the motion passed unanimously on a voice vote.

Kennedy Park Master Plan and Quarterly Update

Staff provided a quarterly update noting Friends of Kennedy Park received $17,500 from youth baseball and nearly $4,000 from concession stand proceeds from a softball tournament, bringing the estimated community foundation balance to approximately $124,939.55, with $98,000 dedicated to lights installed at Ray Jack field on October 19-20. The second annual Glow Games indoor event is scheduled for February 20, 2027. Jimmy Olson of the Friends of Kennedy Park addressed the committee, confirming fundraising is ongoing with no brakes applied, and that internally the group sees a concession stand as the next major goal, while remaining flexible given uncertainty about the farmers market location and a potential hockey facility in the northwest corner. Committee members discussed moving the farmers market to the northwest corner near the proposed hockey facility off Altaverie and Newport, which would allow back-to-back ball diamonds to remain and provide a flatter, potentially covered space for market vendors. The committee reached informal consensus that the farmers market will likely remain at field 2 for the next year, the next development step will take more than a year, and fundraising focus should shift toward a concession stand building, with village consideration of phase 2 parking improvements.
