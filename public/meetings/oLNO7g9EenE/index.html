City Council Infrastructure and Facilities Meeting

November 12, 2026 · 55m
RoadsUtilitiesStormwaterSouth 11th Avenue ReconstructionLeaf CollectionKolbe and Kolbe Millwork

The Wausau City Council Infrastructure and Facilities Committee met and approved several items related to the South 11th Avenue reconstruction project affecting Kolbe and Kolbe Millwork, including handicap parking additions and multiple driveway variances. The committee also discussed the formation of a Boulevard Beautification Special Committee, initiated a conversation about selling or offloading city-owned vacant parcels, and held an extended discussion on fall leaf collection operations, costs, and future contract considerations. No major items were rejected; most business passed unanimously with minimal debate.

Discussion

Handicap Parking on North Second Street, McCloud Street, and Grant Street

Staff explained the request came from Church of the Resurrection to add two more handicap stalls, and that two stalls added during the Second Street reconstruction had not been codified in the ordinance. The committee moved to approve the item to both add new stalls and clean up the ordinance. The motion passed unanimously with no discussion.

Preliminary Resolution for Special Assessment for 2027 Street Construction Projects

Staff described the item as self-explanatory, noting approval would allow the city to set public hearings on special assessments, typically in December, in order to begin bidding projects. The motion was seconded by Wisowsky and passed unanimously with no discussion.

Kolbe and Kolbe Millwork Driveway and Approach Variances (Items 4C–4F)

Staff explained that the South 11th Avenue reconstruction project required coordinating with Kolbe and Kolbe Millwork, whose campus straddles the road, over multiple meetings. The committee approved lay-down curb instead of full-head curb to preserve perpendicular parking at 1323 South 11th Avenue, waived the concrete approach requirement (staff noted asphalt costs roughly one-quarter the price of concrete at approximately $2 versus $8.50 per square foot), and approved two additional oversized driveways at 1330 and 1320 South 11th Avenue. A question about stormwater impact of the larger driveway was answered by staff, who called it minimal. All four items passed unanimously.

Recommendation to Extend Sidewalk on South 11th Avenue from Chellis Street to Fleith Street

Staff proposed adding sidewalk on the west side of 11th Avenue primarily to provide safe passage for Kolbe workers crossing to the manufacturing facility, with future connectivity potential toward 3M Park. Questions were raised about cost (staff said it would be a 50/50 cost share but did not have figures available) and whether bike lanes were planned (confirmed they were not). The motion was initially overlooked before being made and seconded by Alderman Martin; the item passed unanimously.

Boulevard Beautification Special Committee Formation

Kilian announced the formation of a special committee (not a subcommittee, as it includes public members) authorized by the October 27 council resolution. Appointed members include Alder Wiskowski as chair, city engineer Alan Wislowski PE, and citizen members Tim Hau, Dave Nutting, and Trevor Frank. All appointments are pending city council approval at the next meeting. Discussion briefly touched on a possible pre-Christmas organizational meeting, though some members noted travel conflicts in December.

City-Owned Vacant Parcels

Alder Wiskowski raised the topic, expressing interest in identifying city-owned vacant parcels with no development plans in order to offload them — particularly small slivers — back onto the tax rolls, potentially by selling to adjacent property owners. Other members raised questions about contamination (noting 1300 Cleveland is known to be contaminated), how the city acquired the parcels, and whether some listed as 'vacant' are actually serving purposes such as water retention. The committee agreed to bring the item back at the next meeting with a refined, narrowed list removing parcels already slated for development or serving active purposes, and directed staff to consult with Eric and Randy on contamination status.

Fall Leaf Collection Operations and Future Contracts

Alder Slansky raised constituent confusion about multiple leaf pickup rounds; Public Works staff member Dustin appeared and explained the city guarantees one full pass through the city with a baler, with follow-up vac trucks and sweepers that residents misinterpret as additional scheduled collections. Dustin noted complaints were actually down this year after consolidating to a single-phase map. The committee learned that all relevant contracts — tractor/baler, bale hauling, and yard waste — are expiring, and the current farmer-partner does not want to renew; the city may need to lease-to-own a baler. Alder Wiskowski raised the possibility of reducing or modifying leaf collection given the roughly $400,000–$500,000 annual cost, while Alder Martin cautioned that leaf collection helps reduce phosphorus and total dissolved solids discharging into the Wisconsin River under the TMDL requirements. Staff also explained that to earn DNR leaf credits, the city would need three to four collection rounds per neighborhood and an ordinance prohibiting leaves in the roadway — requirements currently unmet. The committee agreed to bring leaf collection back as a full agenda item in January, including contract options, communication improvements, service level expectations, and sustainability considerations.
