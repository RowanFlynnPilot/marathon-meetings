Discussion

Proclamations

Mayor Diny read proclamations for Domestic Violence Awareness Month (October 2026) and Crash Responder Safety Week (November 2–6). Representatives from the Women's Community, identified as Shannon and Tess, were present for the domestic violence proclamation but declined a formal presentation. The crash responder safety proclamation was noted as being presented to the fire department later in the week.

Stormwater Utility Study and Stormwater Quality Management Plan Presentation

Justin Gataski of Strand Associates presented a two-part report: an update to the city's stormwater quality management plan and a stormwater utility feasibility study, both funded 50% by a Wisconsin DNR grant ($170,000 total project cost). Gataski explained the city currently meets total suspended solids reduction requirements (26.5% vs. 20% required) but falls significantly short on total phosphorus reduction (20.3% vs. 68.6% required under the Wisconsin River TMDL). The team recommended Alternative 2—constructing five structural best management practices (wet ponds) combined with water quality trading with neighboring MS4 communities—as the best path to compliance. Gataski also outlined a stormwater utility framework, estimating a cost of $100–$175 per year per equivalent runoff unit (ERU) for residential properties, with 36,265 total ERUs in the city. Alderperson Lukans asked about artificial turf and non-paying entities (schools, nonprofits) under a utility model; Gataski confirmed those entities would pay under a utility system. Alderperson Hanicki asked about likely resident concerns, with Gataski emphasizing the need for public education about the equity benefits of shifting from tax-based to fee-based funding.

Minutes Approval: October 13, 2026

A motion was made and seconded (second from True Blood) to approve the minutes. No questions or debate were raised and the minutes were approved by voice vote.

Public Comment on Flock Cameras and Boulevard Planting

Six members of the public spoke. Barbara [last name unclear] and others supported the proposed terrace beautification subcommittee and boulevard planting. Courtney Olsen, executive director of a domestic violence agency and chair of the Wisconsin Domestic Violence Fatality Review Initiative, urged the council to vote yes on Item 9 to end Flock camera use, citing stalking risks, documented officer misuse in Milwaukee and Menasha, and noting that public health and safety voted 5-0 and finance removed the $50,000 Flock line from the 2027 budget. Felix Gallow (1206 Highland Park Blvd.) and Carrie Morola (1025 Everest Blvd.) also urged the council to cover and end the Flock system. Danielle Fischer (1212 Highland Park Blvd.) asked the council to discontinue Flock immediately and not fund any like technology going forward.

Consent Agenda (less two pulled items)

Items 26-0707 (full-time HR coordinator) and 26-1013 (terrace beautification special committee) were pulled for separate consideration. The remaining consent agenda was approved on a motion from Watson, seconded by Neil, passing 10-0.

File 26-0707: Full-Time Human Resources Coordinator Position

Alderperson Watson pulled this item, expressing concern that adding a position before an upcoming comprehensive organizational audit could lead to a hire that later must be let go, and asked whether contracted services could serve as a bridge. Alder Tyranny defended the position, stating the HR department is 'barely treading water' and would go underwater if they lost one more person without help. Police Chief [unnamed] clarified that a $10,000 professional development allocation in the fiscal note represents shared savings from bringing crossing guard services in-house, saving the city roughly $40,000–$50,000 annually. Alder Martins noted the city's annual financial auditor had just urged better capture of institutional knowledge. Alder Heni confirmed the HR committee approved the position unanimously. The motion passed 9-1.

File 26-1013: Terrace Beautification Special Committee

Alderperson Larson pulled this item, expressing concern about adding more government and raising questions about public health, tree maintenance interference, and tall grass. Alderperson Trublood (chair of infrastructure and facilities) clarified the current boulevard planting ordinance dates to the 1970s and is vague, and that this temporary subcommittee—modeled after Madison's ordinance—would clarify it rather than create new bureaucracy. Alder Lukans confirmed the sustainability committee had been involved in developing the concept. The council approved the terrace beautification special committee by unanimous voice vote.

File 26-0706: Data Centers Zoning Ordinance (Reconsideration)

The council first voted 8-2 to reconsider the previously passed data centers zoning ordinance. On the main motion, Alder Lukans asked whether utility capacity verification was built into the ordinance; Planning Director Edmonson confirmed the ordinance requires applicants to submit energy, water, wastewater, and sound reports to assess utility capacity before a conditional use permit is granted and that the city could deny or require infrastructure upgrades. Alder Heni raised concerns about applicants self-sourcing favorable studies, and Edmonson said the city can contract for independent studies at the applicant's expense. Alder Martins asked about NDAs, and Edmonson said she is generally not in favor of NDAs as they complicate transparency, and the city attorney would need to review any NDA before signing. The ordinance was approved 9-1.

File 26-1014: Safe Drinking Water Revolving Loan Program Reimbursement Resolution

A resolution declaring official intent to reimburse expenditures from the Safe Drinking Water Revolving Loan Program for the FY 2027–28 lead service line replacement program was moved by Watson and seconded by Slantsky. No discussion was raised and it passed 10-0.

Item 9: Resolution to Cease Use of Flock Data and Cover Flock Cameras

Following an 8-2 vote to suspend the rules, the council debated at length a resolution directing the Wausau Police Department to cease using Flock data and cover all cameras. Police Chief [unnamed] raised concerns about the specific wording directing the police department to 'climb poles,' clarified Flock owns the cameras and contract language prohibits tampering, and asked for a legal opinion on authority. City Attorney Ann [last name inaudible] and a colleague confirmed the council has authority to direct the mayor (as chief executive) to order coverage of the cameras but not to directly order the police chief to do so physically. Alder Watson moved an amendment directing the appropriate city department—under instruction of the chief executive officer—to physically cover the cameras within two business weeks; this passed 9-1. Alder Trublood expressed concern about breaching the contract, while Alders Lukans, Tierney, and Martins argued the original vote was made without knowledge of Flock's data misuse and AI expansion and that public trust demanded action. The chief confirmed that access to Flock data can be restricted via a software switch, but uncovered cameras continue to capture and store images for 30 days even without city access. The main motion as amended passed [tally not explicitly stated in transcript before cut-off].
