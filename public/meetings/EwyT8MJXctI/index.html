The Marathon County Public Safety Committee held its November 2026 meeting focused heavily on the Sheriff's Office's Flock automatic license plate reader (ALPR) program, with significant public input and committee debate over data privacy, oversight, and whether to defund the cameras. The committee also received presentations on the Sheriff's Office drone program and a 2027 budget overview. No formal votes were taken at the committee level, though a budget amendment moving $6,300 in Flock funding to a contingency fund was noted as pending a full County Board vote that evening.

Approval of September 8th and October 6th, 2026 Meeting Minutes

One supervisor objected to approving the minutes, alleging that his request to place Flock cameras on the October agenda was deflected off-camera by Administrator Lance Leonard, and that this was not captured in the record. The clerk explained that minutes only capture motions and actions, with video timestamps serving as the official record. The chair noted that Lance Leonard helps set agendas and that is why members are referred to him. The objecting supervisor refused to approve the minutes; the motion carried over his objection with the majority voting aye, though no specific tally was stated.

2027 Annual Budget Overview - Flock ALPR Funding

Supervisor Robinson (referred to by others) explained that the Human Resources Finance and Property Committee had reviewed an amendment to move $6,300 budgeted for Flock cameras into a contingency fund, contingent on resolving concerns about access, oversight, and data retention. County Administrator Lance Leonard and Corporation Counsel Brian [inaudible] clarified that the county board cannot directly control sheriff's operational contracts due to the sheriff's status as a constitutional officer, creating a legal gray area. Committee members questioned whether pulling funding would actually force cameras down and whether future contracts would require board approval; both questions were left largely unresolved pending further legal analysis.

Sheriff's Office Drone Program

Sheriff Chad Bileb presented the drone program, noting the office operates three types of drones used for crash investigations, search and rescue, building clearing, and community events. The team includes approximately seven members, with three or four Part 107 FAA-certified pilots. Captain Seibert was present to answer technical questions. Committee members asked about recreational drone pilots potentially assisting or interfering with law enforcement operations; the sheriff indicated legal liability and HR concerns would preclude using volunteer pilots, and that recreational operators are not required to submit footage unless legally compelled.

Flock ALPR Contract Update and Committee Discussion

Sheriff Bileb addressed the committee's previously submitted questions: the office contracts for eight Falcon ALPR cameras funded by Wisconsin DOJ grants, plus two operated for the Village of Rib Mountain; cameras are located on I-39 and State Highway 29; early termination costs are $500 per camera. The sheriff acknowledged that up to 1,580 agencies have sharing access and that, following feedback from Felix Gallo, Captain Stefanik is auditing and significantly reducing that list. The sheriff maintained that Marathon County does not subscribe to Flock's traffic analytics product and that raw data is deleted after 30 days, but acknowledged disagreement with Gallo on what the contract's perpetual license covers. Multiple supervisors and non-committee county board members spoke against the program, citing mass surveillance concerns, Fourth Amendment issues, and lack of independent oversight, while supervisors Hoppa and one other expressed support or ambivalence, noting real public safety benefits. Sheriff Bileb stated he would not voluntarily remove the cameras, and indicated he expects state legislation on ALPR to emerge in the upcoming session.
