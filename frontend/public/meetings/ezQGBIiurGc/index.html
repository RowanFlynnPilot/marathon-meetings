The Wausau Parks & Recreation Committee met on Monday, July 6, 2026 and took action on two items: approving a recommendation to accept cash in lieu of parkland dedication for the West Hill subdivision near 72nd Street and Stewart Avenue, and approving moving forward with a formal maintenance agreement with the Central Wisconsin Off-Road Cycling Coalition (CEWOC) for Silvin Hill bike trails. Discussion

Consideration of Minutes from June 1, 2026 Meeting

The committee considered the minutes from the June 1, 2026 meeting with no edits proposed. Tom Neal moved to approve and Kristin Slonski seconded. The minutes were approved unanimously.

West Hill Subdivision Preliminary Plat Park Dedication

Parks staff Jamie presented the West Hill subdivision near 72nd Street and Stewart Avenue, noting the applicant proposed satisfying a park dedication requirement through cash payment rather than land. Staff noted the area falls within a gap zone in the city's outdoor recreation plan where no city park is within a 10-minute walk, and that the required three-acre land dedication would likely shrink below the minimum neighborhood park standard once lots were removed. Tom Neal moved to accept cash in lieu of land dedication, with Bruce Trueblood seconding; Kristin Slonski asked clarifying questions about playground proximity and noted the lack of amenities for older youth. The motion passed unanimously, and staff indicated comments would be entered into the Evolve software for the full plan commission and city council review process.

RiverLife Wharf (Warf) Funding Discussion

Parks staff Jamie explained that the cost to install and remove the seven RiverLife docks — $8,750 last year — had been removed from the operating budget by city council and shifted to the small projects fund, which was not its intended purpose. Rex Terrace and his co-owner Hunter, of HRT Dock and Waterfront Services, attended and explained the difficulty of driving 24-foot poles into the riverbed and confirmed pricing is per section and pipe. Committee members generally agreed the docks should remain in the operating budget and that the full complement of seven was likely excessive; Kristin Slonski stated a preference for one boat slip plus the two kayak launches, while Tom Neal suggested exploring lease or ownership arrangements with the RiverLife Apartments and Wanguard development. No formal vote was taken; staff was directed by consensus to place dock costs back into the operating budget and return with itemized cost options for reduced configurations.

Urban Forestry Update: Emerald Ash Borer, IRA Grant, Tree Plantings, Invasive Species

City Forester John provided a comprehensive update: roughly half of the city's ash tree inventory has been removed since 2019, with approximately 350 removals targeted per year; the switch to the product Mectite has improved treatment outcomes; and 135 private ash trees have been removed under the grant program with about $29,000 of the $100,000 private removal allocation remaining. The city received the largest IRA grant in the state through the DNR — nearly half a million dollars — which allowed tree plantings to increase from roughly 500 per year to 970, with Legacy Tree contracted at $300 per tree with a two-year warranty. John reported a successful school tree-planting event at GD Jones with third and fifth graders. On invasive species, he described control efforts for Japanese knotweed, garlic mustard, and buckthorn, noting the goats returned to Barker Stewart Island today; 56 goats including one recovering from nerve damage caused by an insect are now on the island. Alders asked about partnering with neighboring municipalities on knotweed control and about organizing volunteer removal events, which John supported with the condition of a brief training and set dates.

Maintenance Agreement with Central Wisconsin Off-Road Cycling Coalition (CEWOC) for Silvin Hill Bike Trails

Staff Jamie explained that the longstanding handshake agreement with CEWOC needed to be formalized as a five-year written contract, modeled on a similar county agreement, following CEWOC's direct application for room tax dollars without first going through the city department. The city attorney reviewed the draft and supported it; staff noted the language would be tweaked to include annual reviews tied to CEWOC's presidential turnover cycle. Tom Neal moved to approve moving forward with finalizing the contract, Bruce Trueblood seconded, and the motion passed unanimously. The agreement will appear on the next city council agenda for final approval.

Park Updates and General Discussion

Tom Neal raised several items: a tick report at Brock Meyer Park was monitored by staff and was short-lived with no action taken; Adirondack chairs for the riverfront have been delivered and are awaiting assembly; and Neal suggested strategically opening up vegetation along the northern RiverLife trail corridor to create river vistas as a marketing tool for future development, which staff and John the forester supported, noting ash removals and buckthorn cleanup in that stretch are already planned. Discussion also touched on ballard access control at the Lincoln Park tot lot (staff to verify if already installed), the memorial bench program, the possibility of more permanent dock infrastructure, and debris accumulation at the Barker Stewart Island bridge, which is addressed in coordination with DPW equipment.
