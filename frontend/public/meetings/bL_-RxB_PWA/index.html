The Village of Weston held a joint Board of Trustees and Finance/HR budget workshop on Monday, August 24, 2026, focused on the 2027 budget process. The board approved a water revenue bond resolution accepting approximately $1.3 million in state financing for a water treatment facility project (with over $3 million in principal forgiveness), voted unanimously to hold trustee compensation steady at $7,200 and $8,400 for the 2027–2029 term, and received an extensive educational presentation on governmental fund accounting, levy limits, equalized values, and capital improvement planning. Discussions centered on budget priorities, staff retention, the compressed levy environment, and how to align village spending with community-identified needs like public safety and road maintenance.

Discussion

Public Comment

One member of the public, identified as Jim Pensel, addressed the board. He expressed concern about the 6 p.m. start time limiting public participation and asked future meetings be moved to 6 p.m. He also raised concern that there was no agenda item addressing village goals, that staff wish-list costs were not included in the packet, and that the first substantive item appeared to be pay increases rather than citizen-identified priorities such as public safety and roads.

Resolution 2026-023: Waterworks System Revenue Bonds

Finance Director Jessica presented the resolution authorizing up to $1,291,283 in waterworks system revenue bonds for the water treatment facility project. She explained the total project cost is approximately $4.3 million after accounting for $272,460 removed due to PFAS class action settlement proceeds, and that the state's Safe Drinking Water Loan program provides over $3 million in principal forgiveness, meaning the village repays only the $1.291 million at an interest rate of 0.365%. A trustee confirmed TIF funds could not be used for water works. The resolution was approved unanimously by the board.

Budget Process, Fund Accounting, and Levy Limits Presentation

Finance Director Jessica delivered an extensive educational presentation covering governmental fund accounting (general, special revenue, debt service, capital, and proprietary/enterprise funds), modified accrual basis accounting, state reporting requirements, the levy limit worksheet, and equalized value. She noted the village's net new construction allowance for the upcoming budget year is 1.624%, based on $31.3 million in new construction against a total equalized value of approximately $1.9 billion, and that the CPI is currently at 3.4%. She also explained how the pending closure of TIF 2 may allow an additional approximately $400,000 in levy capacity but could jeopardize the village's $138,000 Expenditure Restraint Program payment if not managed carefully through fund allocation strategies.

2027 Budget Priorities Discussion

Board members discussed whether stated budget priorities aligned with citizen-identified needs from the comprehensive plan, where road maintenance and public safety ranked highest. Trustee Clark questioned whether items like a robotic lawnmower represented true village priorities versus staff wish-list items, and suggested the language be reworded to reflect the underlying goal of staff reallocation and retention. Trustee Lopes-Serrao explained the Eau Claire River Trail priority in the context of kayak trail passability and tourism funding rather than levy dollars. Discussion also covered the need for longer-term departmental goal-setting (one, three, and five years), the potential need to update the village's strategic plan (last completed through 2025, estimated $8,000–$10,000 to redo), and the approximately $200,000 in SAFER grant-funded firefighter salaries that will return to the general fund upon TIF closure.

Pay Plan Adjustments, Wages, Health Benefits, and Direct Primary Care

Trustee Clark argued the board should review mandated costs, revenues, and operational needs before discussing pay increases, saying it gives a 'bad look' to lead budget workshops with compensation discussions. A finance committee member requested detailed data before making recommendations, including the number of employees at each pay range, supervisor-to-employee compression, examples of merit pay usage, and total all-in cost per 1% wage change. The Village Administrator noted the health insurance consortium (Spectrum) has preliminarily estimated a 15% premium increase for next year, though last year's initial estimate of 20% came in at negative 5%; the village has a 10% placeholder in the developing budget. The village is self-insured for dental through Delta Dental. Staff discussed a three-part wage structure: pay matrix adjustment, COLA (3% placeholder), and merit/market adjustments. Discussion touched on alternative compensation strategies including HSA contributions, additional holidays, 4/10 work schedules, and wellness incentives.

Trustee Compensation for 2027–2029 Term

Village President Maloney stated he did not believe trustee compensation needed to change, noting it was last adjusted approximately eight years ago from $4,800 to $7,200 (trustee) and $6,000 to $8,400 (president) after 24 years without change. Trustee Ermeling agreed no increase was needed. The Finance/HR committee moved and approved holding compensation flat, and the board then made and approved the same motion unanimously. Trustee Beck offered to share a county clerk spreadsheet of 2025 compensation rates from municipalities across Marathon County.

Capital Improvement Plan Review

Finance Director Jessica walked through the 2026–2027 capital improvement fund sheets covering facilities, streets, and equipment. She clarified that all approved CIP projects still require final board approval before contracts are signed or funds are spent. A finance committee member noted what appeared to be a data entry error — $1 million listed under 'workers comp' for the Howland project — which staff confirmed was a coding error (should be under contracted services/street and curb). The director noted a $100,000 placeholder for pool leak detection is highly speculative at this stage. She also discussed upcoming general obligation and revenue bond borrowing to support CIP expenditures, with Ehlers as financial advisor and Quarles as bond counsel; the village currently holds a Moody's Aa3 bond rating.

Staff and Trustee Remarks

Village Administrator Michael read excerpts from a LinkedIn article about a nonprofit leader who purchased a $400 espresso machine, using it to argue that investing in employee well-being is responsible stewardship, not waste. Multiple trustees and staff members echoed the importance of staff retention, noting the high cost of turnover and training, and that staff efforts — such as nearly saving $3 million on the drinking water loan by working nights and weekends — often go unrecognized. A trustee acknowledged receiving a staff card and expressed gratitude for the gesture. Maloney noted concern about residents 'attacking' local government on minor matters and suggested the board do more to visibly support staff.
