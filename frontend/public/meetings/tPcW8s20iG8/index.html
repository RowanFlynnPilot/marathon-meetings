The Wausau City Council met on Tuesday, September 8, 2026, handling a full agenda including proclamations, committee presentations, and several significant votes. The council approved a development agreement with Wanguard LLC for the Riverlife South redevelopment project (7-4) and issued a $6 million tax increment revenue bond for the Foundry on Third Phase 1 (10-1), while narrowly failing to pass a lead service line replacement ordinance (5-6) — a vote that was immediately set for reconsideration on September 22nd. The council also unanimously rejected a proposed perpetual easement agreement with Lamar advertising and approved department restructuring for Public Works and an HR reclassification.

Discussion

Proclamations

Mayor Diny issued three proclamations: September as Ovarian Cancer Awareness Month, September as National Suicide Prevention and Action Month, and September 17-23 as Constitution Week. A representative from the Daughters of the American Revolution accepted the Constitution Week proclamation and encouraged residents to visit local bookstores and the Marathon County Public Library to learn more about the Constitution.

Sustainability, Energy, and Environment Committee Presentation

Committee representative Jesse Karns presented an update on the committee's work, including a five-year sustainability action plan expected to be presented to council in April 2027, a community and business sustainability survey running September 1 through October 31, Earthweek initiatives, a sustainability award, composting demonstration plans in partnership with a local composting service, and efforts toward the city's 2023 greenhouse resolution goal of 100% clean energy for city government by 2050. Alderperson Lukens encouraged committee members to seek input from alders and highlighted the direct effects sustainability initiatives have on city budgets and residents.

File 06-909: Non-Binding Letter of Intent with TLC Properties/Lamar for Perpetual Easement at 1514 North Third Street

The resolution came to the floor having been voted down unanimously in the Infrastructure and Facilities Committee. Alderpersons Slonski, Neal, Trueblood, and Martens all spoke against it, arguing that a permanent easement would limit future redevelopment options for a parcel currently under active negotiation, and that a lease arrangement would provide more flexibility and revenue over time. Martens noted the city has owned the property since 2006 and that outdoor advertising scarcity means the company would not willingly give up the location. The motion failed 0-11.

File 06-906: Organizational Structure Changes and Succession Planning for Department of Public Works and Utilities

Public Works Director Eric Lynman presented the reorganization, confirming to Council President Kilian that the proposed fee changes for water, utility, and garbage were not dependent on this restructuring and that changes are within the current budget. Watson noted the importance of succession planning. Hoenecke asked about winter plowing priorities with the restructured staffing and was reassured plowing remains the top priority. Lukens confirmed that direct resident services would not be affected; only a rarely-filled part-time facilities position is being eliminated. Larson stated he would withhold support pending a city audit but the motion passed 10-1.

File 26-0918: Reclassification of HR Specialist to HR Generalist

Multiple alderpersons — Wiskowski, Slonski, Tierney, Martens, and Larson — strongly supported the reclassification, noting the employee has been performing generalist-level duties for an extended period without appropriate compensation, and that the HR department is critically understaffed. Trueblood raised concerns about the long-term fiscal impact noting the savings covering this year's cost difference may not be sustained in future budgets. Hoenecke sought clarification on the exact salary figure and confirmed with Interim HR Director Keenan that the resolution amount controls. The motion passed 11-0.

File 26-0921: Ordinance Creating Chapter 13.66 Lead and Galvanized Water Service Line Replacement

This item prompted the most extensive debate of the evening. Supporters including Martens, Slonski, and Lukens argued the ordinance is necessary to qualify for future grants, enable enforcement against unresponsive landlords, and meet a federal 2037 deadline; Martens noted the city has replaced over 3,700 lines in three years at largely no cost to residents. Opponents including Kilian and Trueblood argued that early promises of no cost to residents are being walked back and that a financing program defining residents' obligations should be adopted before mandating replacement. Hoenecke moved an amendment requiring that penalties, enforcement, and water disconnection not occur until the council adopts a financing program defining maximum obligations, repayment terms, and hardship considerations; Tierney seconded. After extensive debate including Director Lynman clarifying that the PSC requires a mandatory ordinance and that partial replacements are not permitted under federal rules, Hoenecke withdrew the amendment without objection. The main ordinance then failed 5-6. Trueblood, as a member of the prevailing side, immediately moved for reconsideration at the September 22nd meeting.

File 26-0919: Development Agreement with Wanguard LLC for Riverlife South Redevelopment

Neal led support for the agreement, noting the previously approved term sheet passed 8-3 and that the development agreement faithfully reflects those terms without material change — including a $100,000 land sale price, a projected $28-36 million assessed value, 80% TIF increment returned to the developer and 20% retained within the TID, and no upfront city funds or loans. Watson asked about the assessed value methodology, the gap between projected and minimum required investment, and confirmed with the developer's representative (David Rele, present in the audience) that nothing material had changed from the term sheet. Martens noted the project's importance for net new construction and the city's tax base. The agreement passed 7-4.

File 26-0992: $6 Million Taxable Tax Increment Revenue Bond for Foundry on Third Phase 1 LLC

Martens explained that the revenue bond is simply the legal mechanism for fulfilling existing contractual TIF payment obligations from the Foundry on Third project approved in 2022, not new city debt exposure. Kilian, noting she has not been a strong supporter of this type of development, stated the city has a contractual obligation and would therefore support it. The resolution passed 10-1.

Adjournment to Closed Session

The council voted unanimously to adjourn to closed session on two separate litigation matters: a settlement offer in Marathon County Case No. 24-CV-40 (Green Acres at Greenwood Hills LLC vs. City of Wausau) and a settlement offer in Marathon County Case No. 25-CV-245 (WA M Younger vs. Curly Kale LLC et al.). Before adjourning, Trueblood announced a September 23rd Historic Commission public hearing on landmark designation for Yawkey Park, Wiskowski announced a public town hall on September 21st at the library, and Mayor Diny announced a September 15th town hall featuring a speaker from Oshkosh.
