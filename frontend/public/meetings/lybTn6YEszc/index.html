Approval of August 6, 2026 Joint Committee Meeting Minutes

The chair called for a motion and second to approve the minutes. With no substantive discussion, the committee voted in favor. The minutes were approved.

2027 Annual Budget Development and McDevco Annual Update

Kim Weber, executive director of McDevco, presented on the organization's three pillars—educate, incubate, and finance—noting the organization has been in partnership with Marathon County since approximately 1978 and receives $40,000 annually from the county for educational programming. Weber reported that the Wausau Business Incubator is now 93% full, up from 61% when McDevco took over management in 2021, and that all $1 million in county revolving loan funds have been deployed into the community. A supervisor asked about agricultural loan demand and Weber noted it is limited, as farmers typically work with specialized ag lenders. Another supervisor asked about microloan default rates and Weber reported a rate of approximately 0.12. Administrator Leonard indicated the plan absent other direction is to continue funding McDevco in the 2027 budget as previously done.

LifeWorks Program Presentation from Advance Wisconsin Employment

Brian Kish, executive director of Advance Wisconsin Employment, and Amy Hopa, program operations manager, presented the LifeWorks pilot program, a pre-employment training program for justice-involved individuals that completed its first cohort on the day of the meeting. The pilot served up to six participants referred from probation and parole, with three successfully completing the full three-week program including Move to Manufacturing training at NTC; participants were offered tours and guaranteed interviews with local manufacturers including Greenheck, Crystal Finishing, and Schuette Metals. Kish noted the organization's federal workforce funding has been cut 40% since 2021 and requested $25,000 annually from the county to run four cohorts per year at roughly $12,000 each. Committee members expressed interest but raised concerns about legal authority for the county to fund such a program, the need for deliverables and KPIs, and whether a competitive or matching grant process would be more appropriate. Corporation Counsel reminded the committee of statutory limitations on county funding of outside programs. Administrator Leonard agreed to bring back a report evaluating legal permissibility and options for a structured process.

Greater Wausau Prosperity Partnership WEDC Talent Recruitment Grant Update

Renee Kings, senior director of the Greater Wausau Prosperity Partnership, presented an update on the organization's WEDC talent recruitment grant, one of 17 awarded statewide from a $5 million pool, with GWPP receiving the maximum $500,000. The program aims to recruit 30 out-of-state households to the greater Wausau region by offering a $10,000 relocation grant plus concierge services, community connections, and employer partnerships, targeting households with incomes of at least $55,000. A supervisor asked whether there is a recapture provision if recipients leave before 24 months; Kings confirmed there is no recapture provision but that quarterly check-ins and employer reporting are planned. The program was set to begin accepting applications the following week.

UW Extension Monthly Report

UW Extension staff member Jen presented monthly reports for June, July, and August. Highlights included a successful county fair with strong youth participation, a youth summer theater camp with over 40 enrollees, and the conclusion of three AmeriCorps staff terms—one of whom will return for another year. Jen also noted that UW Extension's Bill McKen has been providing resources on joint development agreements to a local Marathon County work group, and that a community resilience workshop on green infrastructure was held the same day, attended by staff from Marathon, Portage, Waupaca, and Wood Counties.

UWSP Wausau Report

Miranda reported that UWSP Wausau held its first day of classes at its new NTC location the previous day, with all students successfully finding the new space. She noted that all UWSP signage has been removed from the Marathon County campus to avoid student confusion, except at the Center for Civic Engagement which continues to hold events. Miranda confirmed that university items are being assessed and moved to Stevens Point or Marshfield campuses, with a goal of completing the building handoff by end of November and a clean December transition.

Year-to-Date Budget Reports

Sam presented brief year-to-date budget reports for the library and UW Extension. The library is running at 93.1% of non-levy revenue budget and under its payroll benchmark. UW Extension expenditures are expected to come in under budget due to a staffing vacancy, with a corresponding reduction in donations revenue. A coding discrepancy in the professional services line was noted as a budgetary artifact, not an actual overage. No concerns were flagged in either budget.

UWSP Wausau Campus RFP Proposals Review

County staff member Chris summarized the four RFP responses: (1) the City of Wausau's comprehensive proposal for most of the campus including a six-month due diligence period for architectural, engineering, and environmental review, with the city representative Randy Fifick noting flexibility on most terms including the dormitory demolition timeline and holding costs during the study period; (2) Wisconsin Ice Volleyball Club seeking use of the fieldhouse, locker rooms, and conditioning spaces at a nominal lease or purchase price, with willingness to assume utilities and maintenance costs; (3) Lone Wolf Works proposing to repurpose the central heating plant for automotive restoration and fabrication; and (4) the Center for Visual Arts proposing to acquire or lease the east and west arts buildings for studio and youth programming. Chris noted utility holding costs for the first quarter could reach just under $100,000. Supervisor Robinson of HR Finance and Property asked whether the components of the city's proposal are divisible and whether the city would cover holding costs during due diligence; Fifick indicated both are negotiable. The committee voted to enter closed session to discuss negotiation strategy.

Motion to Enter Closed Session

A committee member moved to enter closed session pursuant to Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(e) for competitive or bargaining reasons, specifically to discuss strategy related to negotiations with proposers for the UWSP Wausau campus RFP and divestment strategy. Supervisor Hagen seconded the motion. A roll call vote was taken and the motion passed.
