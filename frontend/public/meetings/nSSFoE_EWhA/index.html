Discussion

Approval of September 1, 2026 Committee Minutes

Supervisor Shaneborn moved to approve the minutes and Supervisor Drew seconded. No discussion was noted and the motion carried unanimously.

5A1 - White Knight Acres LLC Rezone, Town of Wayne

Staff presented the petition to rezone from General Agriculture to Rural Residential near Chesk Road and Aspen Drive. No members of the public spoke for, against, or as interested parties. Supervisor Drebeck moved to approve based on findings of fact, conclusions of law, and the town's approval; Supervisor Sefelt seconded. The motion carried.

5A2 - Elizabeth Kurt and Doug and Barb Kurt Rezone, Town of Huitt

Staff presented a rezone of two parcels — one to General Agriculture and one to Rural Residential — near Mile Road and Sunrise Road. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Shaneborn moved to approve based on findings of fact, conclusions of law, and the town recommendation; Supervisor Brew seconded. The motion carried.

5A3 - Donna Klein Tippy Rezone, Town of Reed

Staff presented the petition to rezone from General Agriculture to Rural Residential near County Road J. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Sefelt moved to approve and forward to the county board based on findings of fact and conclusions of law; Supervisor Enson seconded. The motion carried.

5A4 - Dwayne Johnson Rezone, Town of Hull

Staff presented the petition to rezone approximately 8 acres from Low Density Residential and Farmland Preservation to Rural Estate near Elderberry Road. The property owner, Dwayne Johnson, testified that the parcel was low land formerly used as a cowyard that had little farming value and that he intended to sell it to an adjacent homeowner. No other testimony was offered. Supervisor Sefelt moved to approve and forward to the county board; Supervisor Shaneborn seconded. The motion carried.

5A5 - Thomas and Pamela Balls and Devon and Louisa Lammansky Rezone, Town of Rib Falls

Staff presented the petition to rezone from General Agriculture to Low Density Residential near Schoolhouse Lane and Countryside Drive. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Brew moved to approve based on findings of fact and conclusions of law and the town's approval; Supervisor Enson seconded. The motion carried.

5A6 - Austin Leel Rezone, Town of Holton

Staff presented the petition to rezone from General Agriculture to Rural Estate near County Line Road. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Shaneborn moved to approve based on findings of fact, conclusions of law, and the town recommendation; Supervisor Brew seconded. The motion carried.

5A7 - Marvin Martin Rezone, Town of Hull

Staff presented the petition to rezone from Farmland Preservation to Light Industrial near County Road N and Silver Maple Lane. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Drebeck moved to approve based on findings of fact, conclusions of law, and the town's approval; Supervisor Shaneborn seconded. The motion carried.

5A8 - Jordan Reynolds Rezone, Town of Hall

Staff noted the agenda incorrectly listed the destination zone as Light Industrial but that the public notice and petition correctly stated Farmland Preservation to Rural Estate near Dill Creek Lane. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Sefelt moved to approve the rezone to Rural Estate and forward to the county board; Supervisor Drebeck seconded. The motion carried.

5A9 - Spencer Joint Revocable Trust Rezone, Town of McMillan — Postponed

Staff reported that the Town of McMillan had not yet submitted a resolution for this rezone petition and recommended postponing until the town submits the required resolution. Supervisor Shaneborn moved to postpone; Supervisor Enson seconded. The motion to postpone carried.

5C1 - Acquisition of Land Adjacent to Cherokee Park

Parks and Recreation Director Jamie Paulie presented via video a proposal to purchase approximately 14 acres of wooded land adjacent to Cherokee Park — a 12-acre piece to the north and a 2-acre wooded piece to the east — at the assessed value of $4,450 per acre, funded from the parkland dedication account which holds approximately $344,000. Paulie noted the Nelson Stewardship Fund is no longer available and that the farmland portion would remain in private ownership. Supervisors Enson and Drebeck asked questions about acreage discrepancy in the appraisal (which cited 19 acres) and potential timber harvest. Paulie confirmed only the wooded parcel would be purchased and that timber options had not been fully assessed. The parks, forestry, and recreation committee had already recommended approval. Supervisor Sefelt moved to approve the acquisition and forward to the Human Resources and Finance and Property Committee; Supervisor Brew seconded. The motion carried.

5C2 - Acceptance of Donated Pontoon Boat

Director Paulie presented a proposal to accept a donated 1990 19-foot aluminum pontoon boat with a 50-horsepower motor from a school district that was divesting it after changes to their fishing program. The boat, valued at approximately $4,000, would expand the county's youth fishing program at no cost. Supervisor Brew moved to accept the donation; Supervisor Enson seconded. The motion carried. Supervisor Sefelt joked that the next forestry and parks meeting should be held on the pontoon boat.

5B1 - Town of Reabrook Wind Energy Ordinance

Land Resources Manager Shad Harvey explained a new procedural change: Marathon County now only verifies that independent towns followed proper process when adopting their own zoning ordinances, with the towns themselves — not the county — bearing legal responsibility for the substance of their ordinances. Corporation counsel confirmed the county is not vouching for the legality of the ordinance language. Chair Kurt Gibbs asked whether the county still reviews ordinances for compliance with state statute; corp counsel confirmed it does not review the language itself. Supervisor Leur expressed concern about the word 'hope' in describing the county's legal protection and sought clarification; corp counsel acknowledged the procedure is new and untested but said the probability of county liability is low. Supervisor Drebeck moved to accept the Town of Reabrook's wind energy system siting ordinance; Supervisor Sefelt seconded. The motion carried but was noted as not unanimous.

5B2 - Town of Reabrook Solar Energy Ordinance

Harvey presented the same procedural framework as applied to the wind energy ordinance. Chair Gibbs asked whether corp counsel reviews ordinance language; counsel confirmed it does not. Supervisor Sefelt moved to accept the Town of Reabrook's solar energy ordinance; Supervisor Brew seconded. The motion carried but was noted as not unanimous.

5B3 - Town of Wausau Data Center Ordinance

Harvey presented the Town of Wausau's data center ordinance under the same procedural review. Chair Gibbs noted only one supervisor and the town chair had signed the application and the clerk had not dated it, and asked whether that created complications. A town official present explained that one supervisor position is currently vacant after a resignation, and that the clerk's auto-signature was discussed with staff and deemed acceptable. The town official also questioned why town ordinances must come through county review if the county accepts no legal responsibility; corp counsel and Chair Ritter acknowledged the statutory requirement is frustrating but cannot be changed without legislative action. The town official noted they had been waiting over two months since their August approval for the county process to complete. Supervisor Shaneborn moved to approve the data center ordinance for the Town of Wausau; Supervisor Drebeck seconded. The motion carried but was not unanimous.

6A1 - Initial Conversation on Development of Data Center Ordinance

Staff planner Shad Harvey opened a broad brainstorming session on what issues a county data center ordinance should address, noting a 12-month moratorium is in place. Supervisor Brew recounted a conversation with a technical college instructor who argued that data centers use far less water than commonly believed and called for accurate, unbiased information. Supervisor Drebeck asked how data centers would be zoned — light industrial, heavy industrial, or otherwise — and Harvey noted that scale-based tiering (large-scale vs. small-scale) is being considered. Chair Gibbs asked whether county zoning authority over data centers remains intact unlike wind and solar; Harvey confirmed local authority has not been preempted. Supervisor Leur recommended bringing in independent experts to filter fact from fiction on water and energy use, referenced NACO's toolkit, and raised concerns about cumulative impacts including diesel backup generators and noise. Chair Gibbs noted a WCA call had discussed model ordinances from a law firm and also suggested a data center notification ordinance applicable to municipalities outside county zoning. Land use specialist Garrett Pogle confirmed one developer inquiry had been received roughly a year to a year and a half prior but no specific application was filed. The committee agreed more education is needed before drafting ordinance language, and staff will continue research and seek expert speakers.

6A2 - Strategic Plan Development Update

Staff briefly reported that a survey of 58 comprehensive plan objectives was narrowed to the top 20, and a public online survey will be released in October asking residents to rank their top 10 priorities. Results will be brought back to the county board to identify 10 objectives for the next strategic plan. No action was taken.

Year-to-Date Budget Report for CPZ, PRF, and SWD

Finance staff reported budget-to-actual figures through June, noting the payroll benchmark for the period is 46.74% and all three departments are below that. CPZ is at 59.5% of revenue budget and 35.27% of expenditure budget; Parks is at 53.79% revenue and 30.9% expenditure; Solid Waste is at 29.84% revenue and 26.8% expenditure, with the lower expenditure figure expected to increase as construction on the new landfill cell progresses, potentially into 2027. No concerns were identified.

Announcements

Staff announced ERC hearing dates for 2027 have been placed in the packet. CPZ staff highlighted an October 21 SaltWise smart winter maintenance event at Uncommon Pints in Rothschild. County Administrator announced the 2027 proposed budget was delivered to the county board that day, with a public hearing on November 2, HR Finance and Property Committee action on October 12, and county board adoption on November 10; amendment submission deadlines of October 9 (pre-publication) and November 6 (post-publication) were provided. Chair Robinson noted it is a tight budget and urged supervisors with amendment ideas to reach out to the administrator and finance director early.
