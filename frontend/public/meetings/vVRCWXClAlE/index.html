The Marathon County Board held its educational and adjourned organizational meeting on Thursday, September 17, 2026, taking action on numerous rezoning ordinances, approving amendments to the animal waste management ordinance after an extensive public outreach process, and voting to enter closed session to discuss the county's broadband partnership with Bug Tussle. The most significant action was passage of Ordinance 41-26 amending Chapter 11.02 to require nutrient management plans and establish seasonal limits on winter manure spreading, following nearly two years of collaborative work between county conservation staff and local farmers.

Discussion

Public Comment – Animal Waste Ordinance

Six speakers addressed the animal waste ordinance. Five dairy farmers and farm operators from across Marathon County spoke in support, emphasizing the year-and-a-half collaborative process, the value of nutrient management plans for farm profitability and water quality, and the unanimous consensus reached by the farmer work group. A representative from River Alliance of Wisconsin also spoke in support, praising Marathon County's collaborative watershed groups as among the best organized in the state. One farmer spoke in opposition, arguing the ordinance constituted government overreach, that the work group was stacked with supporters, and that experienced farmers should not be forced to adopt plans or pay fees.

Public Comment – Town of Green Valley Rezoning

Two speakers from the Town of Green Valley addressed the proposed rezoning of a parcel from its current split-zoned status to general agriculture. A speaker representing the town board argued the town's formal disapproval should carry statutory weight and that the half-acre parcel and commercial contractor business were incompatible with agricultural zoning, warning of precedent risks for other townships. A neighboring resident echoed precedent concerns, stating that if the rezoning was approved, other residents would seek similar half-acre agricultural rezonings to build storage structures.

Infrastructure Committee Item D14 – Appointment

Supervisor Sybert raised concerns about the previous month's vote in which 19 supervisors voted no on an appointment, stating he had called 15 of them and none could say the candidate was unqualified, attributing the no votes to 'sour grapes.' He moved to postpone the vote until the following month so supervisors could better evaluate the candidates. Supervisor Straw asked whether the infrastructure committee could function without a chair, and was told the vice chair serves as acting chair and the committee can appoint an acting chair under Robert's Rules. The motion to postpone carried.

Town of Green Valley Rezoning – Sawyer Daquet, Ordinance 33-26

County land resources manager Shad Harvey presented the background, clarifying the parcel is 27 acres (not a half-acre), already split-zoned, and that the future land use map designated the area as agricultural. He explained the town's disapproval resolution did not meet certification requirements and was not corrected in time. Corporation counsel confirmed the board is not automatically required to approve a rezoning even if criteria are met. ERC member Supervisor Ritter noted the ERC did not take the decision lightly and that both the petitioner and town representatives wanted a decision at the committee meeting rather than a referral back. Supervisor Morash noted that delay would leave the property owner's equipment outside all winter. Supervisor Straw said he favored letting the town take another swing, but the rezoning was ultimately approved on a non-unanimous vote.

Animal Waste Management Ordinance Amendment – Ordinance 41-26

County Conservationist Kirsty Hidenrike and conservation analyst Matt Repang presented the ordinance amendments, which require nutrient management plans for farms winter-spreading liquid manure on frozen soils and establish seasonal restrictions on winter spreading for farms above 300 animal units, particularly targeting February and March. Attorney Jane Landredy participated virtually and praised the county's collaborative approach. Multiple supervisors questioned the fee structure, monitoring plans, enforcement mechanisms, effects on small farms, and connections to algae blooms in Lake Dubay and the Big Eau Pleine reservoir. Staff clarified that fees would be set by ERC and the board in a future process, that a free nutrient management planning class is available through the county, and that implementation is delayed to 2028. The motion carried on a non-unanimous vote.

Bug Tussle Broadband Project Update and Closed Session

Administrator Leonard provided an extensive open-session summary of the county's history with Bug Tussle, including a 2021 conduit bond issuance of $25 million, a subsequent direct county loan of $8 million, and Bug Tussle's disclosure in June that the planned fiber ring would not be completed as originally designed due to cost overruns and insufficient return on investment. Leonard noted Bug Tussle presented a scope modification to the infrastructure committee on September 3rd and has proposed participation in a new approximately $45 million conduit issuance tied to federal BEAD program broadband grants. Because of competitive bargaining interests and potential litigation, the board voted unanimously to enter closed session for further deliberations.

DC Everest School District Facility Study Presentation

Superintendent Casey Nye and Buildings and Grounds Director Jason Jablonski presented a two-year facility study driven by declining enrollment and an aging junior high school building. The proposal would decommission the junior high, redistribute grades to two existing buildings, add a new parent drop-off loop at the middle school and Mountain Bay campus, and construct an addition at the senior high to accommodate ninth graders. A community survey on aquatics was noted as ongoing. Board Chair Gibbs asked how declining incoming class sizes would affect long-term facility needs; Nye explained the smaller classes are already reaching the elementary level. No vote was taken.
