The Village of Weston Public Works Committee met on Monday, September 14, 2026 and took action on a wide range of infrastructure items, including approving typical section designs for three 2027 road reconstruction projects, recommending vacation of old utility easements on a commercial property, approving a closeout change order for the Gelan Avenue/Alderson Street roundabout project (final cost $846,959.25, about $7,000 under bid), and recommending updated winter parking regulations. The committee also received detailed staff reports on active construction projects, street operations, utility maintenance, and a failed TAP grant application for a pedestrian crossing improvement.

Discussion

CIP Update (Staff Report)

Staff presented a Wisconsin Policy Forum benchmarking tool showing Weston ranks fourth lowest in street spending per street mile among regional communities, with one committee member suggesting the data be published in the village newsletter to inform residents. Staff reported the Gelan Avenue/Alderson Street roundabout is open and operating well, with at most two cars backed up on any leg during the first week of school. Staff also reported that a TAP grant application for pedestrian crossing improvements at Sternburg Avenue and County Road X was not awarded by the DOT, as only two projects in Weston's population group received funding out of over $4 million in requests with only about $2 million available; staff indicated the crossing can still be incorporated when County Road X is reconstructed without grant funding.

Street Operations Update

Staff reported that DPW crews completed lane wedging, milling of approximately 14,000 linear feet (2.66 miles) of roadway, adjustment of 36 manholes and 22 water valves in preparation for overlays, and that a new aerial/bucket truck was delivered (approved in August 2025) and is being outfitted in-house. Staff noted that Portage County can no longer perform striping work outside the county under a statutory change, so Farnner Asphalt will be contracted for long-line painting. A committee member noted that fuel costs were at 75% of the $90,000 annual fuel budget after approximately 7 of 12 months, which staff acknowledged is running slightly high but manageable within the broader $1.4 million street maintenance budget.

Business 51 / Newport Avenue Pedestrian Crossing Discussion

Staff described ongoing negotiations with the DOT project manager regarding a pedestrian crossing at Newport Avenue on Business 51, noting the DOT's cost estimate of approximately $20,000 for village-side impacts (primarily driveway apron adjustments) is not yet substantiated by actual design. Committee members agreed the crossing is not worthwhile without a flashing pedestrian beacon, estimated at $20,000–$30,000, and expressed frustration that the DOT PM had not responded to outstanding questions. The committee voted unanimously to direct staff to continue pursuing the crossing, acknowledging potential collateral costs to the village, with staff indicating they would escalate to the DOT regional traffic engineer if no response is received within a week.

Wasata Tile Property Easement Vacation

Staff presented a surveyed map confirming that a 1987 water and sanitary sewer easement on the Wasata Tile property runs perpendicular to Business 51 rather than parallel, meaning vacating it poses no future risk to the village's interceptor sewer corridor. The easement vacation was requested by the property owner as part of a real estate transaction, and the property owner paid for the survey work. The committee voted unanimously to recommend the village board vacate the existing water and sanitary sewer easements within the Wasata Tile property.

Fergie, Delhoney, and Post Avenue Typical Section Discussion (2027 CIP)

Staff presented recommended typical sections for three narrow 2027 reconstruction streets. The committee voted unanimously to proceed with: Alternative A (no sidewalk, concrete shoulder/swales) for Delhoney Street given its 38.2-foot corridor and dead-end nature; Alternative C (sidewalk on one side — the west side) for Fergie Street; and Alternative D (sidewalk on the Wendy's/north side only) for Post Avenue, with staff noting a 10-foot private utility setback behind the sidewalk will be incorporated based on lessons from prior projects.

Howland Avenue Typical Section Discussion (2027 CIP)

Staff presented typical section options for Howland Avenue, which has 70–80 feet of right-of-way west of Camp Phillips and 60–66 feet east of Camp Phillips. The committee approved two separate motions: first, to proceed with Alternative 2 (multi-use path on the west side of Camp Phillips Road) with the path width changed from 8 feet to 10 feet to match the existing Camp Phillips path; second, a separate motion — raised by a committee member — to add a 5-foot sidewalk on the east (north) side of Howland Avenue from the YMCA driveway east to Heritage Hills Drive, filling a gap in the existing pedestrian network. Both motions passed unanimously.

Gravel Road Maintenance Discussion

Staff identified approximately 23 homes served by the village's gravel roads (Von Kennel South, Harney, Kamichek, Jones, Wesnik, and East Nick), noting paving all of them would cost roughly $1 million. A resident spoke during the discussion, sharing that a similar five-house road (Highland) was paved by the village with utilities approximately 15–20 years ago at about $15,000 per 120-foot lot. The committee reached consensus — voting unanimously on a motion to do nothing further at this time — that paving is not cost-justified at current density and that staff should continue maintaining the roads as they have been, with paving reconsidered only if significant additional residential development occurs.

Jennick Avenue Final Assessment

Staff presented final vs. preliminary assessment calculations for the Jennick Avenue project, finding them extremely close: sanitary sewer final rate was just over $8,000 vs. a preliminary of just under $8,500; water and sewer lateral rates were within about $50; water main final was about $200 above preliminary; asphalt pavement was within 14 cents per square foot; and gravel road cost came in under estimate. The committee voted unanimously to recommend finalizing the assessments as presented, without requiring a full formatted report to come back next month.

Western Avenue Final Assessment

Staff asked the committee whether the Western Avenue project — which had two separate preliminary assessments (Ryan Street to County Road J, and County Road X to Ryan Street) — should be finalized as two separate reports or combined into one. Committee members agreed the outcome would be essentially the same either way. The committee voted unanimously to direct staff to produce one combined final assessment report for both phases.

Alderson Street / Gelan Avenue Change Order No. 2

Staff presented the closeout change order for the Gelan Avenue/Alderson Street roundabout project. Additional costs for geotextile fabric and breaker run to address soft subgrade areas and a modification to a sanitary sewer wye connection were offset by quantity reductions elsewhere, resulting in a final project cost of $846,959.25 — approximately $7,194.30 under the original bid. A committee member noted they had spoken with parents and teachers who were very happy with the roundabout. The committee voted unanimously to recommend the board of trustees approve Change Order No. 2.

Winter Street Parking Ordinance Update

Staff presented a proposed update to the winter parking ordinance to align Weston with Rothschild and Schofield under the Mountain Bay Metro jurisdiction: no parking on all village streets from 2:00 a.m. to 7:00 a.m., November 1 through March 31. Staff also requested six designated areas with alternating-day parking restrictions from 7:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. on the same seasonal schedule to allow plow trucks to access historically problematic streets while still providing street parking options for residents. The Community Life/Public Safety Committee had already concurred. The Public Works Committee voted unanimously to recommend the ordinance modification.
