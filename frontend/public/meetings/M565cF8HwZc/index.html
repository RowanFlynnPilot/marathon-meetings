Infrastructure and Facilities Committee Meeting

July 9, 2026 · 1h 52m

The Wausau Infrastructure and Facilities Committee met on Thursday, July 9, 2026, hearing extensive public comment from roughly a dozen residents and two alderpersons about snow plowing practices on boulevard streets. The committee voted to table the snow plowing policy question until August pending cost estimates for equipment upgrades, approved several infrastructure items including a transportation project plat and two public hearing resolutions, and approved a rectangular rapid flashing beacon at Thomas Street while rejecting a similar request for Stevens Drive and Golden Meadow Street for lack of a second. The meeting was notable for the volume and unanimity of public opposition to the current outward-plowing policy on the city's historic boulevard streets.

Discussion

Snow Plowing Policy on Boulevard Streets

Following extensive public comment, committee members debated whether to restore the historical practice of plowing snow toward center medians on the city's boulevard streets. Public works staff explained that current equipment has permanently right-mounted wing plows, that reversing direction to plow toward the median would require driving contraflow in violation of state law, and that adding left-side wings to two loaders could cost roughly $40,000–$50,000 per machine. Staff also noted efficiency losses of two to six additional hours per route per storm event under the old method. One alderperson moved to bring the issue back to the August meeting with hard cost estimates for equipping trucks with bilateral wings and options for keeping boulevards passable; the motion passed. Alderperson Slonski's written statement, read into the record by the chair, urged the committee to maintain the current outward-plowing policy, citing safety, uniformity, timely removal, and cost.

South 52nd/East Wausau Avenue and North 6th to North 18th Street Transportation Project Plat (Project ID 699-Z00-01)

Staff presented the transportation project plat for a WisDOT-led connecting highway project at approximately 70% design completion, requiring small property takings and easements before a real estate professional can approach property owners. Staff recommended approval as the first step to allow right-of-way acquisition to proceed. A motion to approve passed without dissent.

Preliminary Resolution to Set Public Hearing to Vacate Alley Bounded by Lake View Drive, Bertha Street, Elmwood Boulevard, and Midway Boulevard

Staff noted a valid petition had been received and that the resolution would only set a public hearing at which neighbors could testify for or against vacating the alley, with the committee then voting on the merits. No substantive opposition was raised. A motion to approve the preliminary resolution passed.

Approve Sculpture at Southeast Corner of Third Street and Washington Street

Staff explained the item came before the Infrastructure and Facilities Committee because the sculpture would be placed in the public right-of-way, requiring an encroachment permit with insurance through the city attorney's office. The Arts Commission had previously approved the placement on a 4-1 vote. The sculpture would remain privately owned and maintained at no cost to the city. One member asked about sight-line impacts and was advised engineering would make a final determination at placement. The motion to approve and forward to the city attorney for an encroachment agreement passed unanimously.

Renewal of Storm Water Memorandum of Agreement with Surrounding Communities

Staff briefly explained that Marathon County owns and operates storm sewer and storm water facilities in surrounding communities and that the memorandum ensures adherence to municipal ordinances and MS4 compliance. Staff recommended approval and a motion to renew passed without discussion.

Preliminary Resolution to Set Public Hearing to Vacate a Portion of Liberty Ridgeway

Staff explained a petition was received from the landowner at the end of Liberty Ridgeway and that development plans in the area had changed, with a large home going up to the west. The resolution would only set a public hearing. A member asked whether the developer intended further development past the stub and was told plans had changed. The motion to set the public hearing passed.

Developing Preliminary Engineering Plans to Implement the Wausau Center Urban Design and Transportation Master Plan

Staff presented the 2019 Wausau Center Urban Design and Transportation Master Plan, noting that the successful removal of Highway 51 and 52 designations from downtown now allows the city to pursue two-way street conversions and other changes. Staff sought committee support for including approximately $150,000 in the 2027 budget for 30% engineering plans. One alderperson expressed strong support and discussed specifics including converting Forest Street to two-way, a protected bike lane potentially on the north side, and concerns about the proposed roundabout at Grand Avenue and Forest Street. Another alderperson declined to support committing funds at this stage given competing budget priorities, suggesting it be revisited during budget discussions. When a motion to develop preliminary engineering plans was made, it failed for lack of a second.

Approve Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacon at East Thomas Street (West of McCleary Street)

Staff recommended approval of an RRFB at the River Edge Trail crossing on East Thomas Street, noting the crossing met all criteria except minimum pedestrian volume but that observers recorded extremely long wait times suggesting the lack of a beacon discourages trail use. The bicycle and pedestrian advisory committee supported the installation. One member raised whether cheaper alternatives such as additional signage or curb narrowing had been exhausted and noted $20,000 is still a significant expenditure; however, the motion passed, with one recorded opposed vote noted in the transcript.

Approve Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacon at Stevens Drive and Golden Meadow Street

Staff did not support this RRFB installation, noting traffic volumes are much lower than Thomas Street and that staff observed no conflicts or extended wait times. The pedestrian counts were taken June 4 when many students had already finished finals. One alderperson moved to postpone to the October meeting to obtain more accurate pedestrian counts during the school year; that motion died for lack of a second. No motion to approve was made, leaving the item without action.
