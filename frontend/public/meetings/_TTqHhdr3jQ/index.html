Finance Committee Budget Session #4 Meeting

October 20, 2026 · 2h 22m
BudgetDebt ServicePublic Safety TechnologyTID 8 DevelopmentRoads and Infrastructure

The Wausau Finance Committee held its fourth budget session on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, working through the 2027 budget with numerous amendments. The committee approved the 2027 budget as amended by a 4-1 vote, with key changes including removal of $50,000 in Flock ALPR camera funding (redirected to general fund street maintenance or public safety motorpool), removal of $150,000 for a city administrator position (redirected to council consulting services for a pending city audit), and removal of several deferrable capital items. The amended budget recommendation will advance to the full Common Council for a public hearing on November 10th.

Discussion

Public Comment on Flock ALPR Cameras

Five members of the public spoke during public comment, all urging the committee to remove Flock ALPR camera funding from the 2027 and 2028 budgets. Speakers cited privacy concerns, the risk of the system being used as a stalking tool, ongoing litigation in Wisconsin against municipalities using Flock data, and the argument that nonappropriation carries minimal legal risk. One speaker detailed the specific budget line changes needed and noted the Public Health and Safety Committee had voted 5-0 the prior evening to recommend removing the Flock funding.

New Property Record Maintenance Fees

City Assessor Rick Rubo presented new proposed fees for property record maintenance and parcel splits/combinations, noting about 12 Wisconsin municipalities have adopted similar fees. Rubo explained that the city currently receives no reimbursement for significant staff time involved in updating physical and electronic assessment records when permits are issued or parcels are split and combined, citing a 50-unit condominium project as an example of the administrative burden. The committee later approved these new fees by voice vote.

Updated Assessed Value and Tax Rate Impact

Finance Director Maryanne reported that the final assessed value came in at a 2.56% increase — significantly stronger than the estimated 1% — bringing the total to approximately $464,994,200. This revised the projected 2027 tax rate down from an estimated $9.24 to $9.10 per thousand, reducing the estimated tax impact on a $200,000 home from $9,973 to $7,152. The committee noted the city is still awaiting apportionment figures from the county and school district.

Motor Pool Budget Reconciliation

Finance Director Maryanne presented a motor pool reconciliation showing general fund department budgets totaling approximately $4,018,000 and entity-wide totals of $4.5 million, against projected motor pool revenues of $4.8 million and budgeted expenses plus capital purchases leaving a shortfall of $448,000. She noted a target of an additional $1.3 million would be needed to fully recover costs, but that strong 2026 general fund performance would likely allow a budget modification to true up the capital and support the full fee structure for both 2026 and 2027.

Capital Budget, Debt Service Scenarios, and TID 8 Discussion

Finance Director Maryanne presented three debt service scenarios (flat 10-year, structured 10-year, and 15-year) for proposed 2027 and 2028 infrastructure borrowing, noting that a structured 10-year amortization would best control levy growth. She also presented a proposal to amend TID 8's project plan to include 17th Avenue design ($250,000), asphalt overlay ($300,000), and up to $2.5 million for lead service line replacements, potentially allowing the district to close as early as 2035. Alderperson Hoenecke and others raised questions about balancing essential capital needs against deferrable projects, with Hoenecke advocating for stricter scrutiny of non-essential borrowing.

Alcohol and Licensing Fee Amendments

City Clerk staff presented proposed changes to the alcohol and licensing fee schedule, noting most fees are capped by state statute. Proposed changes included a new $100 late publication fee and a $150 late fee to encourage timely license renewals, a reduction of the group publication fee from $30 to $25, an increase in the lapsed bartender license fee from $125 to $130, a reduction of the alcohol premise amendment fee from $150 to $25 to improve enforceability, and an increase in the Class A beer license from $350 to $400. Alderperson Tierney moved to adopt the revised alcohol and cigarette license fees as advised by staff; seconded by Watson, approved unanimously by voice vote.

Flock Camera Funding Removal Amendment

Alderperson Tierney moved to remove $50,000 for Flock cameras from the 2027 budget and an equivalent amount from the 2028 budget, seconded by Watson. Discussion centered on where to redirect the $50,000, with the finance director suggesting it could be moved to the general fund to support motor pool line items in police, fire, or street maintenance. Mayor Diny recommended keeping the funds within public safety for items such as crowd control barricades. The motion as amended to redirect the funds within the general fund to street maintenance or public safety passed by voice vote.

City Administrator Funding Removal Amendment

Alderperson Tierney moved to remove $150,000 budgeted for a city administrator and redirect it to the council's consulting services line item to fund a previously approved city audit. Alderpersons Watson and Hoenecke expressed support for redirecting the funds toward the audit rather than eliminating them entirely, noting a council resolution authorizing the audit had passed the prior week. After discussion about whether to place funds in reserve (which would require a two-thirds vote to access), the committee agreed to move the $150,000 to council consulting services. The amendment passed by voice vote.

Alley Paving Removal Amendment

Alderperson Tierney moved to remove $45,000 for alley paving, seconded by Hoenecke. Public Works Director clarified that this funding is only used when residents petition for new alley paving — the city currently has no petitions for 2027 — and that removing it would only require any future petitioners to wait until the 2028 budget. Alderperson Neal withdrew a suggestion to retain $15,000 for the most critical needs after the director's clarification. The amendment passed by voice vote.

Tree Planting Removal Amendment

Alderperson Hoenecke moved to remove $25,000 for street tree planting, seconded by Watson for purposes of debate. Parks staff clarified that this budget replants trees on reconstructed streets — specifically 11th Avenue and Ethel Street for 2027 — and that improved planting techniques using root collars reduce sidewalk damage. Alderpersons Neal, Martens, and Watson spoke strongly against removal, citing community character, property values, environmental benefits, and a duty to residents whose trees were removed during prior reconstructions. Hoenecke clarified he was not opposed to trees but objected to incurring 10-year debt for $25,000; he said he would support funding it from cash. The motion failed 4-1, with Hoenecke casting the only yes vote.

TID 12 Tool/Design Engineering Removal Amendment

Alderperson Tierney moved to remove $150,000 for Forest Street two-way conversion design engineering from TID 12, seconded by Hoenecke. City Engineer Alan explained the study would design roundabout and signal changes needed to convert First Street and Forest Street to two-way traffic following removal of the State Highway 52 designation. The committee noted TID 12 is still in early increment years and fiscally fragile, having recently relied on a donor amendment from TID 7. The motion passed by voice vote.

TID 8 Capital Project Reallocation Amendment

Chair Martens moved to reallocate $250,000 for 17th Avenue design, $300,000 for asphalt overlay, and up to $2.5 million for lead service line replacements from the general capital projects fund into TID 8, seconded by Hoenecke. Finance Director Maryanne noted this would reduce the debt service levy but not total borrowing. The amendment passed by voice vote.

Final Vote on 2027 Budget as Amended

After all amendments were acted upon and a withdrawn motion to use reserves to offset borrowing, the committee voted on the 2027 budget as amended. The budget passed 4-1, with Hoenecke casting the lone no vote. The recommendation will proceed to a Common Council public hearing on November 10th. The committee was also informed that auditors will present 2025 financial results at the next Finance Committee meeting and that financial advisors from Ehlers may also attend.
