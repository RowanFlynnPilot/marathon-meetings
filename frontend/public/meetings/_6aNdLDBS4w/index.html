The Village of Weston Board of Trustees held its regular meeting on Monday, August 17, 2026, addressing a range of items including approval of a joint resolution for financing the new Mountain Bay Metropolitan Police Department headquarters, denial of a rezoning request for a storage facility on Weston Avenue, denial of an animal fancier license application, and a decision to continue the farmers market at Kennedy Park. The board also approved a 10-year bank loan of $4 million with Community One (Inner City) bank for the police headquarters construction, and allocated Everest Metro Police Department fund balance to offset borrowing costs.

Discussion

Public Comment

Six members of the public spoke during public comment. One speaker praised Weston's transparency efforts and urged civic engagement, recounting how resident turnout reversed a board vote on renaming Scoffield Avenue. Two speakers addressed the animal fancier license agenda item — the applicant explained her care for her four dogs and willingness to expand fencing, while a neighbor raised concerns about waste and odor affecting their property. Two vendors from the farmers market urged the board to keep the market at its current Kennedy Park location, with one noting a business drop of 40% due to the recent move and another reporting improved traffic following the Alderson roundabout opening. A sixth speaker urged the board to override the planning commission's denial of a rezoning request for a storage facility on Weston Avenue, arguing the denial was improperly influenced by the planner's personal preferences.

Joint Resolution for Mountain Bay Metropolitan Police Department Headquarters Financing

Trustee Beck moved and Trustee Olson seconded the resolution establishing a joint intergovernmental agreement between Weston, Rothschild, and Mountain Bay Metro PD for debt authorization related to construction of a new police headquarters. Trustee Ermeling raised concerns about the repayment structure, noting Mountain Bay Metro's $130,000 annual minimum payment could take 34-40 years to fully repay the village's loan, and that the village would need to increase its levy approximately $206,000 annually to cover the full loan payment. The village administrator and police chief clarified that $130,000 is a minimum and the oversight board can direct larger payments, including from fund balances, with a goal of 10-year payback. The resolution passed 4-2, with Ermeling and one other trustee voting no.

Farmers Market Location (Kennedy Park)

Trustee Maloney moved and Trustee Lopes-Serrao seconded a motion to approve the parks committee recommendation to continue the farmers market at Kennedy Park. Trustee Ermeling expressed concern that the park's green space was being consumed by the market, while Trustees Lopes-Serrao, Olson, Beck, and Maloney all supported remaining at Kennedy Park, citing costs already invested in the new location, absence of viable alternatives, and evidence of improving traffic following the Alderson roundabout opening. Olson outlined specific steps to address parking and safety concerns, including enforcement of parking rules and vegetation trimming near intersections. The motion passed 4-1, with Ermeling casting the lone no vote.

Rezoning Request at 4205/4307 Weston Avenue (Project 2026-263)

Trustee Lopes-Serrao moved to uphold the planning commission's recommended denial of the rezoning request from B2 to Limited Industrial for a proposed self-storage facility. Village Planner Jennifer Higgins explained that the rezoning was inconsistent with the comprehensive plan, which designates the Weston Avenue corridor as a retail development area, and noted that a potential developer had met with staff the prior week showing interest in the area. Because the planning commission had recommended denial, a 5-of-7 supermajority would have been needed to override; with only five trustees present, all five would need to vote in favor of approval, making it procedurally easier to affirm the denial. The motion to uphold the denial passed unanimously.

Animal Fancier License at 5512 North Birch Lane

Trustee Ermeling moved to uphold the planning commission's recommended denial of the animal fancier license. Trustee Lopes-Serrao, who had voted to deny at the planning commission, stated he now opposed the denial because there was no codified space requirement for large-breed dogs and no founded violations from police investigations of odor complaints. Discussion addressed neighbor concerns about waste runoff along the shared property line, the applicant's stated willingness to expand fencing, and the fact that the license must be reapplied for annually and can be revoked. The motion to uphold denial passed 3-2, with Lopes-Serrao and one other trustee voting no.

Bank Proposals for Police Headquarters Financing

Finance Director Jessica presented three bank RFP responses for a $4 million loan. Community One (Inner City) offered the lowest rate — 4.69% for 10 years or 5% for 20 years — with the 10-year option saving approximately $1.5 million in interest compared to 20 years. Jessica recommended the 10-year loan, noting that the closure of the TIF district would produce an estimated 90-cent tax rate decrease, partially offsetting the increased debt levy. Trustee Olson moved and a second was provided to approve the 10-year loan with Community One; the motion passed unanimously.

Allocation of Everest Metro Police Department and Municipal Court Fund Balance

Trustee Beck moved to apply $362,354.31 in one-time funds from the Everest Metro PD and municipal court fund balance toward the Mountain Bay Metro headquarters construction, reducing the village's borrowing amount from $4.355 million to $4 million. Trustee Olson seconded; the motion passed unanimously with no notable opposition.

Policy for Written Comments at Public Meetings

Discussion arose over inconsistent handling of written public comments across village committees. President Maloney and Trustee Ermeling expressed support for reading written comments into the record if submitted timely, while Trustees Lopes-Serrao, Olson, and Beck opposed having board members read others' words aloud, citing concerns about accountability and potential misuse. The board reached consensus that written comments should be received, distributed to all committee members, referenced by name and subject at the meeting, and placed on file — but not read aloud. A motion to that effect passed unanimously; a draft policy will be brought back for review.
