Finance Committee Budget Session #2

October 6, 2026 · 2h 29m
BudgetPublic SafetyStaffingFlock ALPR CamerasCity Administrator

The Wausau Finance Committee held its second budget session on Tuesday, October 6, 2026, covering proposed 2027 budgets for the police, fire, transit, and general government departments. Discussion was dominated by debate over Flock camera (ALPR) technology, the proposed $150,000 allocation for a potential city administrator position, and animal control fee adjustments, with no formal votes taken and amendments to be submitted in writing for a future session.

Discussion

Police Department 2027 Budget

Police Chief presented a 'cost to continue' budget with several adjustments totaling approximately $150,000 in savings or new revenue: ending the crossing guard contractor contract (saving ~$40,000-$45,000 and a portion from the school district), reducing the CSO budget by approximately half (~$45,000), cutting the animal control non-dog stray budget from $37,000 to $18,500, and anticipating a new negotiated school resource officer contract with the school district at 100% reimbursement for the nine school months (~$56,000 new revenue). The department also plans to drop from 81 to 80 sworn positions as Jed Alexander Foundation funding for a downtown beat officer expired, while repurposing a patrol officer to continue the two-officer downtown program. Alderperson Watson asked about VOCA and beat patrol grant funding; the chief confirmed beat patrol funding was maintained at the same level.

Flock ALPR Camera Technology

Alderperson Neal initiated a lengthy debate asking to remove the $50,000 Flock camera line from the budget, citing public distrust of the corporation's data handling. The police chief strongly defended the technology, stating the city's contract runs through December 31, 2028 with no exit-for-convenience clause, that all data belongs to the city, that photos are retained only 30 days, and that officers are required to document reasons for every license plate search. Alderperson Hoenecke expressed similar concerns about the potential for a 'dragnet' surveillance mosaic and said constituents oppose it, though he clarified he was not impugning the current department's use. Alderperson Tierney requested the department's Flock policy be shared with all council members, which the chief agreed to arrange. Mayor Diny noted the city is on the leading edge of ALPR policy compared to other municipalities. The chief noted a second public health and safety committee presentation is scheduled for October 19th, and no vote was taken on removing Flock from the budget.

Animal Control Fee Amendment

Alderperson Watson introduced a written budget amendment proposing to lower the pet license fee for spayed/neutered dogs or cats to $25 (from $30), with a $10 microchip discount, keeping non-spayed/neutered at an increased rate, and funding any resulting revenue shortfalls from the animal control fund balance (approximately $146,000). Finance Director Maryann explained the fund balance dynamics and the city's pass-through obligations to the county. Watson acknowledged the fund would continue to decline but argued animal control is a public service benefiting all residents, not just pet owners, and said she wants a deeper analysis next year. No vote was taken; the amendment was submitted in writing for consolidation at a future session.

Fire Department 2027 Budget

The fire department deputy chief described a budget focused on small cuts across multiple line items rather than raising fees again, including eliminating FDIC national conference attendance, cutting psychological services and miscellaneous administrative expenses, and proposing to purchase a robotic lawn mower (~$10,000 one-time cost) to eliminate ongoing mowing contracts for Station 2. The operations division chief position will remain vacant through July 1, 2027, and a known upcoming 40-hour employee retirement will also be left open for six months to meet budget targets. The chief noted the department is currently down six members from full staff and highlighted the community care paramedic program's success, stating Aspirus has verbally committed to continued partnership funding when the three-year trial ends in 2027. Alderperson Tierney raised whether fire mutual aid to surrounding townships should eventually involve a fee, which the chief acknowledged as a complex issue given fire service traditions.

Transit (Metro Ride) 2027 Budget

Transit Director Ron presented a budget reduced by $81,635 from prior years, achieved largely by aligning historical expenditures with actual spending patterns and anticipating lower maintenance and fuel costs with the arrival of 10 new buses replacing 2009-era vehicles. The director noted the CAD AVL system costs will be covered 100% by FTA ARPA funding through 2028, after which costs will increase. A potential UMO Wisco fare payment system, not yet approved by council, would bring a cost increase but is expected to increase ridership. Alderperson Neal asked about a miscellaneous revenue line showing only $66 in 2025 actuals; Finance Director Maryann explained transit accounting rules require certain revenues to be restated as contra expenses per FTA regulations.

$150,000 City Administrator Allocation in General Government

Mayor Diny defended including $150,000 in the general government budget as planning-ahead funding for a potential city administrator, noting a town hall was held September 15th and public engagement is ongoing, and that any structural change must be in place before the April 2028 mayoral election cycle. Alderperson Neal argued the allocation looks like a fait accompli to residents and said it should be struck from the budget, noting the amount and timing are premature since an audit has not yet been conducted. Alderperson Hoenecke agreed, saying the audit should precede any administrator hiring decision to avoid bias. Alderperson Tierney concurred, saying funds should be earmarked for the audit instead. Alderperson Watson suggested the $150,000 could be moved to the council's contractual services budget line as a possible amendment. Finance Director Maryann confirmed a budget modification could transfer those funds later in the year. Maryann also noted that $150,000 equates to approximately $7.50 on a $200,000 assessed-value home. No vote was taken.

General Government Departments (City Attorney, Clerk, HR, CCITC)

City Attorney Ann reported the office reduced its budget by keeping one assistant position vacant and by switching from Westlaw to Lexis Nexus at a significantly lower cost (~$50/month vs. ~$700/month). The city clerk noted a ~$31,000 reduction due to fewer elections in 2027 and savings from no longer absorbing credit card convenience fees, shifting that 3% fee to customers. Discussion arose around an HR coordinator position (approximately $99,000 including benefits) that had previously failed a council vote 7-1 and is set to be reintroduced at the HR committee; Alderperson Watson questioned why that position was prioritized over other vacancies. CCITC costs showed a notable year-over-year increase attributed primarily to software licensing, including Office 365, Workday, and public safety software, totaling approximately $564,000 in direct charges plus the city's 23% share of CCITC operations.

Budget Amendment Process and Next Steps

Committee members discussed how to handle proposed amendments, with the chair and finance director advising that written amendments be submitted to the clerk and compiled for a future session to avoid a walking quorum via email. Maryann offered to share a spreadsheet tool allowing members to calculate the levy impact of proposed changes. The chair noted the budget does not need to be finalized this week; Wednesday's session will cover special revenue funds, capital budget, TID districts, and debt service, while Thursday is reserved for other areas of interest and amendment consolidation. The public hearing is anticipated at the November 10th council meeting, with budget adoption no later than November 24th to meet the mid-December tax bill mailing deadline.
