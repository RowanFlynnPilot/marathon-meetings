The Marathon County Board of Supervisors met on Tuesday, October 20, 2026 and took several significant votes, most notably approving Resolution 68-26 as amended to authorize Phase 4 construction of the new highway facility in Kronenwetter at a guaranteed maximum price of approximately $55 million. The board also appointed Aaron Griner as Infrastructure Committee Chair, passed an Operation Greenlight resolution honoring veterans, approved a Child Support Agency budget amendment, and adopted a resolution calling for reconsideration of proposed federal census residency rules.

Discussion

Award Presentation to Regional Forensic Science Center

Greg Kimble of American Tissue Services Foundation presented a certificate and teardrop award to the Marathon County Medical Examiner's Office, noting that in the coming year over 600 people would benefit from tissues recovered with the office's assistance and that 57.8% of Marathon County residents are registered organ donors, above the state average of 55.7%. Katie Shara of the Lion's Eye Bank of Wisconsin presented the 2026 Crystal Vision Award to the Medical Examiner's Office for outstanding commitment to eye donation. Representatives of the Medical Examiner's Office, including autopsy supervisor Nick Hanky and medical legal investigator Leah Shite, accepted both awards.

Consent Agenda Items C9 through C13E

Supervisor Drebeck moved and Supervisor Conway seconded approval of consent agenda items C9 through C13E. The motion carried but was not unanimous, with no discussion recorded.

Infrastructure Committee Chair Appointment – Aaron Griner

The board considered appointing Aaron Griner as Infrastructure Committee Chair to complete a term expiring at the April 2028 organizational meeting, replacing Chris Dickinson. Griner delivered a lengthy statement emphasizing his commitment to data-driven deliberation, fiscal responsibility, and preparing the county for novel infrastructure challenges including transportation, energy, data processing, and AI development. The motion by Supervisor Watson, seconded by Supervisor Gao, carried but was not unanimous.

Resolution 68-26: Phase 4 Construction of New Highway Facility

The board engaged in extensive debate over the approximately $55 million guaranteed maximum price for a new highway facility in Kronenwetter. Supervisor Sandalski offered a successful amendment requiring the board to develop a property divestment strategy for the existing West Street site aimed at returning property to the tax rolls and applying proceeds to the general fund. Highway Commissioner Lang and project manager Chris Hullman detailed operational benefits including indoor equipment storage to extend vehicle lifespans, improved wash bay capacity, and geothermal HVAC with an estimated $760,000 federal tax credit on the $1.9 million borefield. County Administrator Leonard emphasized that the $54,981,000 figure is a guaranteed maximum price under construction management at risk, that no bonding is planned, and that construction costs have risen significantly over years of delay — citing a comparable Sauk County highway shop approved in 2022 for $50 million. Opponents including Supervisors Straw, Hapa, and Drop raised concerns about the total cost, coal dust drift from the nearby Weston power plant, and traffic access. Supervisor Mask called the question, which passed, ending broad debate. The resolution as amended was approved but not unanimously.

Resolution 71-26: Operation Greenlight

Chair Gibbs explained that Operation Greenlight, started approximately five years ago by NACo, calls for turning lights green during Veterans Week to recognize veterans' service. Moved by Supervisor Cavelli and seconded by Supervisor Morash, the resolution passed unanimously with no discussion.

Resolution 72-26: Child Support Agency Budget Amendment

The board considered a budget amendment to recognize additional FY2024 state performance incentive funds for the Child Support Agency. Because it is a budget amendment, a two-thirds majority was required. Moved by Supervisor Gao and seconded by Supervisor Robinson, the resolution carried but was not unanimous, with no discussion recorded.

Resolution 73-26: Reconsideration of Proposed Census Residency Rules

Supervisor Robinson explained that proposed federal census rule changes would use January through April 1 presence — rather than tax home — to determine residency, potentially reclassifying Wisconsin snowbirds as residents of southern states and reducing the state's share of $2.8 trillion in federally allocated funds. Supervisor Pool clarified that the resolution is narrowly focused on the residency definition and not broader census changes. Moved by Supervisor Griner and seconded by Supervisor Nisth, the resolution carried but was not unanimous.

Motion to Reconsider Consent Agenda Item 13C1 (ARPA Funds Reclassification to Forensic Science Center)

Supervisor Straw moved, seconded by Supervisor Hapa, to reconsider the consent agenda item reclassifying excess ARPA funds to the completed forensic science center project. Administrator Leonard explained that Treasury guidance requires ARPA funds to be allocated to previously identified projects by December 31, 2024, and that reclassifying the funds to the forensic science center — rather than returning them to the federal government — would free up already-spent county dollars to flow back into the capital improvement plan for future board use. Supervisor Robinson warned of potential federal clawback if funds were spent on non-identified projects. The motion to reconsider was defeated.
