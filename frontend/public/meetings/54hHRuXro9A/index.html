The Village of Weston Parks Committee held its July 2026 meeting focused primarily on two major issues: the future of the farmers market at Kennedy Park and maintenance funding for the Oaklair River water trail. The committee voted to continue the farmers market at Kennedy Park and directed staff to research funding opportunities for river trail maintenance, while also hearing extensive public comment praising and critiquing both the farmers market relocation and park conditions.

Approval of minutes from June 22, 2026

A motion was made by a member identified as 'Lewis' and seconded by a member identified as 'Easter' to approve the minutes. The motion passed unanimously on a voice vote. Public commenter Jim Pensel later raised a complaint that a letter he submitted after the June meeting was not included in the record; the committee explained it was excluded because it was not given during the public comment period.

Public comments on farmers market relocation to Kennedy Park

Five speakers addressed the committee. Ken Mus praised the move to Kennedy Park as safer and better for vendors, urged the committee to invest in and stabilize the market long-term rather than consider further moves. Antonet Casease, owner of Danny's Gyros, provided a before-and-after comparison, arguing accessibility concerns are largely temporary due to roundabout construction and that overflow parking is underutilized; she urged surface improvements for those with mobility issues and warned that moving to a remote location like Mock Miller Park would kill the market. Brian Tra, a resident across from Kennedy Park, expressed concern about park damage from vehicle traffic during baseball tournaments, lack of oversight, and a growing homeless presence at the dugouts since porta-potties were installed. Jim Pensel sharply criticized the Friends of Kennedy Park fundraising pace (only $105,950 raised excluding two large donations over three years), called for terminating the MOU and finding a new fundraising entity, criticized pool operating costs of over $80,000 annually in subsidies plus capital expenses, and condemned DC Everest Youth Baseball for leaving the park in poor condition after tournaments.

Update on Kennedy Park renovation and capital campaign

Parks staff noted the same quarterly update shared with the board was included in the packet, with no major new information. Jimmy Olivesson of DC Everest Youth Baseball (5503 Wing Creek Drive) thanked the board and parks department for supporting the recent baseball tournament, reporting a large positive turnout, heavy use of pool passes (156 of 288 passes distributed were used over the weekend), strong vendor activity at the farmers market, and positive economic spillover to local restaurants. Staff noted that the community foundation balance is $103,000 and a Musco lighting quote to finish lights on Ray Jack Field came in at $98,000, making the lighting project the likely next improvement. Staff also pushed back on the narrative that the entire Kennedy Park project was supposed to be 100% fundraised, noting the village has always had facilities there that require village investment regardless.

Update on Oaklair River Trail benches project

Staff reported that resident Caitlyn, who proposed the idea at the June meeting, does not want to oversee the project herself and would prefer a school or community organization to lead it with student and local artist involvement. The concept involves painting existing benches and possibly starting with a garbage can as a test project using acrylic paints. Staff indicated no action is expected until next year at the earliest, and noted minimal village cost would be involved beyond some paint supplies.

Farmers market at Kennedy Park master plan

Staff presented the master plan and survey results through June 30, reviewing why Kennedy Park was chosen over alternatives evaluated last fall. Committee members discussed whether the farmers market location on the Alderson Street side of the park could be incorporated into the master plan, potentially reconfiguring the northwest corner near the old wellhouse and warming house buildings. Staff noted the current market has more vendors than the previous location, vendors park on-site rather than using the parking lot, and simple traffic flow improvements like connecting the two parking lots with a small driveway could help. One committee member stated she was supportive of continuing at Kennedy Park, noting none of the alternatives were viable and that the old Camp Phillips location is not village-owned. A motion was made to continue the farmers market at Kennedy Park and direct staff to research funding opportunities and improvements within the master plan; the motion passed on a voice vote.

Oaklair River water trail maintenance and funding

Dan Higginbotham and Tim White presented on the Oaklair River water trail, noting approximately 5,000 paddlers used the river last year and that a group of 8-10 volunteers currently maintains it. They reported seven trees were cleared this spring with $600 from Marathon County Parks Foundation and $600 in room tax funds, and noted the City of Schofield spent $3,750 and previously $6,400 on similar work. They requested a small annual budget line item for emergency maintenance, flagged a drainage issue making the ADA launch unusable, and outlined plans to pursue national water trail designation for the Oaklair and Big Rib Rivers to complement the existing Great Pinery Heritage Waterway designation. Tim White noted economic data showing paddlers generate $2-4 million in economic benefit annually and that 58% of visitors do at least two forms of outdoor recreation. A motion was made to direct staff to look into available funding sources for river maintenance; the motion passed on a voice vote.
