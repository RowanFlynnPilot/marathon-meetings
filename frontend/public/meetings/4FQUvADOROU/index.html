Board Regular Meeting Pt.1

The Marathon County Board of Supervisors held its regular meeting on Tuesday, August 25, 2026, taking action on more than a dozen items including amendments to battery energy storage and large energy generating facility ordinances, a moratorium on data centers, authorization to proceed with phase three of the new county highway facility, and several budget and fee schedule matters. Among the most debated items were the highway facility resolution—where cost concerns prompted lengthy discussion before the board voted to authorize staff to seek bids—and the battery energy storage ordinance, which was amended to increase residential and structure setbacks from 300 to 500 feet before passing.

Discussion

C11A: Infrastructure Committee Chair Appointment

Supervisor Arstead moved to remove C11A for a separate vote, arguing Supervisor Gao was the superior choice for Infrastructure Committee chair due to his prior county board experience in Dane County, his handling of tornado recovery in Ringal, and his record as vice chair. Vice Chair Chris Dickinson supported Gao, noting he had stepped in effectively when Dickinson missed meetings and would be a stronger voice on the executive committee given anticipated JDA discussions. Board Chair Gibbs and Vice Chair Boots defended Supervisor Griner, citing practice of not placing first-term supervisors in committee chair roles. The motion to appoint Supervisor Griner as Infrastructure Committee chair failed; Gibbs announced the chair and vice chair would confer and bring a recommendation the following month.

Ordinance 27-26: Battery Energy Storage Systems

Extensive debate centered on setback distances, waterway protections, firefighting equipment requirements, and whether the ordinance was sufficiently robust. Supervisor Sundowski moved to refer back to the Environmental Resources Committee (ERC) for strengthening, seconded by Supervisor Hapa; that motion failed. Supervisor Dickinson then moved to amend the residential setback from 300 feet to 500 feet based on staff recommendation; Supervisor Sundowski successfully amended that motion further to extend the 500-foot setback to all structures—not just dwellings—and the amendment to the amendment passed with Supervisor Conway recorded as a no. The amended ordinance ultimately passed, though not unanimously.

Ordinance 28-26: Moratorium on Data Centers

The board considered a moratorium of up to 12 months on data centers under General Code Chapter 17 to allow staff time to develop an ordinance, as no current chapter 17 provisions address data centers. No significant debate was recorded. The motion passed, though not unanimously.

Ordinance 29-26: Large Energy Generating Facilities

Staff and outside counsel Rebecca Roker explained this novel ordinance—the first of its kind in Wisconsin—would require developers to submit engineering plans to the county at least 60 days before a PSC application, giving Marathon County an early engagement mechanism. Supervisor Leur raised concerns about vague, unmeasurable language in sections on public health and adverse impact. Supervisor Sundowski moved to refer back to ERC and consider a moratorium; that motion failed. Supervisor Dickinson successfully amended the ordinance to require that engineering plan information be transmitted to all municipalities in Marathon County; the amendment passed unanimously. The ordinance as amended then passed, though not unanimously.

Resolution 49-26: Phase 3 Construction of Proposed New Highway Facility

This was the meeting's longest debate. Administrator Leonard clarified the resolution only authorizes staff to release bid documents and approves a general financing framework using previously issued debt—it does not authorize construction or bonding. Supervisor Krebs spoke at length against proceeding at the current projected cost (potentially approaching $60 million), comparing it unfavorably to other local public facilities and calling for a taxpayer referendum. Supervisors Dickinson, Robinson, Griner, Gao, Ness, and Vice Chair Boots supported moving forward, emphasizing the deteriorated state of the West Street facility, the need to also accommodate the Mosinee garage, rising construction costs, and that bids would reveal actual costs with further board approval still required. Supervisor Sundowski urged selling the existing facility at fair market value and applying proceeds to the new project; Administrator Leonard confirmed staff would strongly recommend that approach. After debate was called by a two-thirds majority vote, the resolution passed, though not unanimously.

Resolution 50-26: LRIP Funding Budget Amendment

The board voted to amend the 2026 budget by $429,793 for Local Road Improvement Program funding. No discussion was recorded. The motion passed unanimously.

Resolution 54-26: Removing Seasonal Weight Limits on County Trunk Highway X

After a brief voting system correction requiring a revote, the resolution to remove seasonal weight limits on County Trunk Highway X passed unanimously with no substantive debate.

Resolution 51-26: Non-Lapsing Contingency Fund for Medical Examiner's Office

The board voted to amend the fund balance policy to create a non-lapsing contingency fund for the Medical Examiner's Office. No debate was recorded. The motion passed, though not unanimously.

Resolution 52-26: 2027 Capital Improvement Program Tentative Project List

The board approved the tentative 2027 CIP project list with no recorded discussion. The motion passed, though not unanimously.

Resolution 53-26: Health Department Fee Schedule for Tattoo and Piercing Licensure (as amended)

Administrator Leonard presented a staff-recommended amendment to the original packet proposal, reached after post-meeting conversations with event organizers. The revised fee schedule removes permanent tattoo and piercing establishments from the update, shifts temporary licensing from a tiered 1-to-6 to 1-to-8 practitioner structure, allows event organizers to serve as the global applicant, and sets a 60% fee rate for events occurring before November 1, 2026. Supervisor Jensen moved the substitute amendment, seconded by Supervisor Rosenberg; it passed unanimously. The resolution as amended then passed, though not unanimously.
