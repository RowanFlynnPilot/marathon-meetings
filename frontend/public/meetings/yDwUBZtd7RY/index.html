Environmental Resources Committee Meeting

August 4, 2026 · 2h 39m
Renewable EnergyData CentersBattery Energy StorageAnimal Waste ManagementZoning Amendments

The Marathon County Environmental Resources Committee met on Tuesday, August 4, 2026, taking significant action on three zoning code amendments: approving an updated battery energy storage systems ordinance (with an amendment increasing residential setbacks from 200 to 300 feet for commercial systems), approving a 12-month moratorium on data centers in county-zoned towns, and approving a new large electric generating facilities ordinance requiring developers to share project information with the county. The committee also approved a farmland preservation rezone in Brighton, sent a Gunther rezone back to the town for incomplete paperwork, and received an update on the animal waste management ordinance, directing staff to schedule a public hearing for the September meeting.

Discussion

Marshfield Rezone Withdrawal Announcement

Chair Ritter announced at the opening of the meeting that the City of Marshfield had voluntarily withdrawn its request to rezone from rural estate to general agriculture. The withdrawal was attributed to Marathon County's solar ordinance being more restrictive than state law, which does not permit counties to require a rezone for solar projects. Corporation counsel's determination was to be added to the packet for committee review, and the item was removed from the agenda.

5A1: Jason Flieger / J Good Rezone – Farmland Preservation to Rural Estate and General Agriculture, Town of Brighton

Staff Garrett Pagel presented the rezone request for approximately 37.68 acres at the intersection of State Highway 153 and County Road F, noting the Town of Brighton had recommended approval on June 12, 2026. Supervisor Griesbach asked about church uses in the rural estate district and was told churches are a permitted use requiring no conditional use permit. No public spoke for, against, or as interested. Supervisor Drewek moved to approve based on findings of fact, conclusions of law, and town approval; Supervisor Korth seconded; approved unanimously.

5A2: Tim Vreeland / Noah Weinkauf Rezone – General Agriculture to Rural Residential, Town of Gunther

Staff presented the rezone request in the Town of Gunther. Supervisor Drewek raised concern that the town's resolution did not include answers to required questions, noting it sets a bad precedent and could cause documentation problems. After confirming no public wished to speak, Supervisor Shameborn moved to send the rezone back to the town to complete the paperwork properly; Supervisor Korth seconded; motion carried unanimously.

5A4: Battery Energy Storage Systems Ordinance Update

Staff Chad Harvey presented updates to the existing battery energy storage ordinance, including clarifications on hazard mitigation, emergency response plans, first responder training, monitoring, and decommissioning. Supervisors raised questions about road access for emergency vehicles, setbacks for larger commercial systems, cumulative noise impacts, and tracking of battery disposal. The town of Easton's chair, speaking as an interested party, raised concerns about fire suppression capacity and groundwater contamination for large commercial systems. Following deliberation, Supervisor Drewek moved to amend the commercial setback from dwellings from 200 to 300 feet; Supervisor Korth seconded; amendment carried but not unanimously. Supervisor Lemmer then moved to forward the amended ordinance to the county board; Supervisor Korth seconded; motion carried.

5A5: Moratorium on Data Centers

Staff presented a proposed 12-month moratorium on data centers in county-zoned towns to allow time to develop a proper ordinance. Chair Gibbs clarified that the moratorium would only apply to the 18 county-zoned towns, not cities, villages, or self-zoned towns. Corporation counsel clarified the moratorium is temporary, not permanent. Staff proposed an amendment to prevent circumvention via multiple accessory data centers by applying a cumulative 5,000-square-foot threshold. Supervisor Lambert moved to approve the staff amendment; Supervisor Anson seconded; amendment passed. Supervisor Jerrybeck then moved to approve the moratorium as amended; Supervisor Merrill seconded; motion carried.

5A6: Large Electric Generating Facilities Ordinance

Staff and Corporation Counsel presented a novel ordinance — described as the first of its kind in Wisconsin — requesting that developers of large electric generating facilities submit their pre-engineering plans to the county within 30 days of filing with the DNR or PSC, to allow the county to make informed decisions about intervention or joint development agreements. Supervisors debated staffing capacity, the 30-day timeline, lack of enforcement penalties, and coordination with the existing local energy impact advisory committee. County Administrator Leonard clarified the ordinance is a request mechanism, not a binding requirement. Supervisor Robinson raised questions about docket notification and tighter timelines. After extensive discussion, Supervisor Drewek moved to approve the amendments; Supervisor Korth seconded; motion carried but not unanimously.

5B1: Town of Reedsville Rock Wind Siting Ordinance

Staff reported that the Town of Reedsville Rock's amended wind and solar ordinance, which had previously been sent back for being more restrictive than state law, still contained provisions staff believed were overly restrictive, and that the town had used a 2024 public hearing rather than holding a new one for the new amendments. Staff recommended sending it back to the town to hold a proper public hearing. A supervisor moved to send it back; Supervisor DeBroux seconded; approved unanimously.

5C3: Amendments to Schedule of Cash Deposits, Chapter 16 and Chapter 19

Staff Jamie presented a housekeeping item to add missing fee entries to the cash deposit schedules for the county forest (Chapter 16) and county park (Chapter 19) ordinances, following a reorganization approved last March that left some ordinance provisions without corresponding fees. Supervisor Jerrybeck asked about whether the smoking prohibition fee covers e-cigarettes; staff said they would verify and address it in future ordinance amendments. Supervisor Lemmer moved to approve; Supervisor Schonborn seconded; approved unanimously.

6A: Animal Waste Management Ordinance Update

County Conservationist Kristy Heidnerich provided an update on the two-year-long ordinance development process, noting state approval has been received for the prohibition on winter spreading of liquid manure in February and March for farms of 300 animal units or greater, while buffer requirements are still awaiting state review. The farmer work group and staff both recommend moving forward with the approved ordinance changes. Supervisors indicated readiness to proceed to a public hearing, and staff was directed to place the ordinance on the September 1, 2026 agenda as a voting item.
