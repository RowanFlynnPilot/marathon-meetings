Approval of July 7, 2026 Minutes

The committee approved the July 7, 2026 minutes with no discussion or noted changes. The vote was taken by voice and passed without opposition.

2027 Budget Development: Women's Community Nonprofit Funding

Jane Graham Jennings, executive director of the Women's Community, presented the organization's use of the county's $55,000 annual contract, explaining it covers about 16% of the roughly $348,000 cost to deliver contracted services including domestic violence victim support, lethality assessment protocol work with the sheriff's department, and child advocacy center follow-up. She noted the county funds also leverage an additional $350,000 in state and federal grants. County Administrator Lance Leonard indicated his approach is to hold nonprofit funding flat in the 2027 budget absent contrary board direction.

2027 Budget Development: Fee Review Process

Administrator Leonard explained that following the county board's direction to project a 3% fee increase, staff circulated spreadsheets to all departments to identify where statutory limits, cost changes, or market sensitivity might affect that approach. Vice Chair [name not clearly stated] asked about assessing fees for court no-shows; Leonard explained that while judges can forfeit bonds for failures to appear, the county board cannot direct judicial action due to separation of powers, so there is no direct county mechanism to impose such a fee.

Sheriff's Office Fleet/Rolling Stock Discussion

County Fleet Manager Chris [last name not stated] presented a memo outlining a plan to address the gap in rolling stock funding after 2026 saw no vehicles purchased. The plan involves rolling existing leases, extending others with mileage room, and moving 17 additional vehicles into the lease fleet program. A proposed CIP allocation of $456,548 was noted for 2027, though a funding gap of $138,876 remains unidentified. Discussion included the high cost of vehicle upfitting at Belco in Columbus, an emerging proposal to use a state innovation grant to build a regional shared upfitting operation with municipal partners including the City of Wausau, Mountain Bay, and Kronenwetter police departments, and outreach to NTC, McDevco, and Watta for workforce pipeline support. No resolution was brought forward as fleet funding is typically decided in November.

Jail Stay Fees

Captain Chad [last name not stated] presented a memo showing that total 2025 jail fee revenue was approximately $303,660, encompassing all jail fees—not just pay-for-stay and first-day fees. He explained that the daily fee of $20 and first-day fee of $30 apply primarily to sentenced inmates, who make up only about 10% of the jail population, and that collection is very difficult given the indigent and high-need nature of the population. Administrator Leonard noted a planned ordinance cleanup to replace specific fee amounts in the ordinance with references to the county fee schedule, to avoid future mismatches, and indicated this would be brought as a future agenda item.

5A2 – New Position Requests and Reclassifications for 2027 Budget

Administrator Leonard walked through several position changes: the DA's office would abolish a 1.0 restitution specialist position and create a 1.0 victim witness specialist and 0.5 administrative specialist, leveraging state Chapter 950 reimbursement; the sheriff's office would abolish a senior admin position and create a corrections officer position; the clerk of courts would reclass an administrative assistant to administrative specialist; and the medical examiner's office would abolish a deputy chief (physician) position and create a senior administrative assistant, yielding substantial cost savings. The committee approved a motion to support inclusion of all new positions and reclassifications in the proposed budget and forward them to HR, Finance and Property Committee.

5B1 – Update to Marathon County Ordinance 5.09, Chief Deputy Appointment

Captain Chad presented changes required by 2025 Wisconsin Act 196, which merged the roles of chief deputy and under sheriff so the chief deputy now assumes the sheriff's duties if the sheriff is incapacitated, and removed the chief deputy from civil service selection. Staff also recommended removing a provision barring the chief deputy from seeking the office of sheriff, citing both practical concerns about limiting the candidate pool and potential legal issues under state statute. The committee moved to forward the amended ordinance to the county board, which passed by voice vote.

5B2 – Creation of Non-Lapsing Contingency Account for the Medical Examiner

Administrator Leonard and Medical Examiner Director Jess [last name not stated] proposed creating a non-lapsing contingency account capped at $250,000 for the medical examiner's office, to be filled from year-end operating surpluses rather than levy. Jess explained the rationale including the high cost of specialized equipment such as a $300,000 full-body X-ray machine. Supervisor John [last name not stated] expressed support but cautioned that the board should periodically review all such accounts and noted the cap was higher than those for most other departments. The committee approved a motion to forward the proposal to the county board; it passed by voice vote.

6A – Flock Safety ALPR Technology Educational Presentation

Captain Jeff Stefanic presented an overview of the sheriff's office use of Flock Safety automated license plate reader technology, in use since 2023 with 10 stationary cameras at a cost of $26,500 per year, fully grant-funded. He described data retention (30 days for images, indefinite audit logs), CJIS compliance, and a personnel policy requiring legitimate law enforcement purpose for every query. Committee members raised concerns about privacy, civil liberties, wrongful stops, and transparency: one supervisor cited a case in Milwaukee/Brookfield where an innocent woman was stopped at gunpoint following a Flock alert and called the cameras an infringement on constitutional rights; another suggested the sheriff's office develop a public-facing transparency dashboard similar to what Wausau PD has implemented; and questions were raised about whether the contracts came through the county board (staff noted the grant process involved county board chair signatures) and whether audit language should be changed from 'may' to 'shall.' Captain Stefanic noted audits occur at least twice monthly and that a public dashboard is being worked on.
