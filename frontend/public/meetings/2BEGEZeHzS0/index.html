Board of Trustees and Finance/HR Committee (Budget Workshop)

The Village of Weston held a joint Board of Trustees and Finance/HR Committee budget workshop on Tuesday, September 8, 2026, focused on preliminary 2027 budget review including wage and benefit estimates, general fund structure, special revenue funds, and a discussion on potential modified summer hours for administrative staff. The board also approved reclassifying an engineering technician position from exempt (salaried) to non-exempt (hourly) status retroactive to his July 6 hire date, and convened briefly in closed session regarding litigation strategy related to a Weston-Everest Metro Police Department payout, ultimately taking no action.

Discussion

Public Comment

Three residents addressed the board. The first raised concerns about the proposed Friday afternoon closures and urged the village to ensure hourly employees are paid only for hours actually worked if reclassified, and confirmed with staff that overtime is calculated over 40 hours per week. The second resident criticized the lack of a clear answer to what a 1% wage increase costs in total, argued against closing village hall on Fridays as inequitable to frontline workers, and suggested extended evening hours as an alternative; he also noted that budget presentation materials were added to the packet the day of the meeting. The third resident questioned why he was required to hire a licensed plumber to replace anti-siphon faucet fixtures following a village water inspection, arguing the work was simple and his house should be grandfathered in.

Approval of August 24, 2026 Joint Budget Workshop Minutes

Minutes were approved separately by both the Finance/HR Committee and the Board of Trustees. The Finance/HR Committee motion was made by a member identified as Daniels and seconded by a member identified as Pavlo; the board motion was made by Beck and seconded by Ermeling. Both passed on voice votes with no opposition.

2027 Budget — Wage and Benefit Estimates and General Fund Review

Finance staff presented wage and benefit cost estimates at 0%, 1%, 2%, 2.5%, and 3% wage increase scenarios, noting a preliminary 10% health insurance increase was incorporated. A trustee pressed staff on why the math for a 1% raise did not produce a simple 1% increase in the wage line, and staff explained that election workers, board members, and others are excluded from the scheduled increase. Trustees debated whether budget materials should be provided in advance of meetings or presented at the meeting with explanation; Administrator Gabri and finance staff acknowledged the spreadsheet lacked sufficient context. Staff also presented a Wisconsin Policy Forum municipal comparison tool showing Weston ranks among the lowest in general government spending and shared revenue per capita among comparable municipalities, and explained structural budget constraints including the levy cap tied to net new construction, declining transportation aids, and the pending closure of TIF District 1 which will return approximately $400,000 to the general fund.

Special Revenue Funds — Refuse/Recycling, Aquatic Center, TIF Districts, Room Tax, Civic/Social/Park Trust

Staff presented preliminary 2027 budgets for each special revenue fund. The refuse/recycling fund faces a projected $62,000 fund balance drawdown largely due to diesel fuel surcharges under the Harder's contract; staff indicated the per-unit fee may need to increase from $20.50 and said a final recommendation would be brought back after reviewing August data. The aquatic center is projected to nearly break even, with attendance down slightly from the prior year but revenues higher due to fee increases; a facility assessment ($15,000 estimate) was discussed as a potential future expense. TIF District 1 is closing and will distribute remaining funds to taxing districts after a final audit. TIF District 2 (Schofield Avenue) carries a roughly $470,000 projected deficit through end of 2027 due to a state reassessment that slashed the district's value. The room tax Weston-only account balance is growing (approximately $230,000 through Q2) and trustees noted funds should be deployed for tourism purposes. The civic and social fund will resume a $500 scholarship through DC Everest after a lapse of several years.

FLSA Reclassification of Engineering Technician Position

The village administrator explained that the engineering technician hired in summer to serve as on-site inspector for the Concord project was incorrectly classified as exempt (salaried) when the position was created, and requested reclassification to non-exempt (hourly) with back pay. The Finance/HR Committee initially approved retroactive reclassification to August 17, then amended the motion to extend the effective date back to the employee's actual hire date of July 6, reasoning that if the classification was incorrect from the start, the correction should apply from day one even though no overtime was incurred in that earlier period. The Board of Trustees approved the same motion. Staff estimated the overtime owed for time already worked is less than $1,000 in wages, and noted the position saves the village significantly compared to contracting a consulting engineer at approximately $120 per hour.

Modified Summer Hours for 2027 (Potential Friday Afternoon Closures)

Trustees discussed whether to implement a modified summer schedule in which administrative staff would work longer hours Monday through Thursday and close Friday afternoons, similar to schedules in Rothschild, Schofield, and Wausau. Staff noted public works already operates a four-day 10-hour schedule in summer. Concerns raised included inequity between office staff and frontline workers (utilities, parks, police, fire) who cannot participate, potential confusion for residents, prior unsuccessful attempts at similar schedules, and the small number of employees who would actually benefit (estimated fewer than 10). One board member asked staff to survey employees on their preferences among all retention tools — wages, benefits, modified hours, and additional holidays — rather than evaluating each in isolation. No action was taken; the item was deferred for further discussion at a future budget workshop.

Closed Session — Litigation Strategy (EMPD Payout)

The Board of Trustees voted to convene in closed session pursuant to Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(g) to confer with legal counsel regarding strategy in litigation or likely litigation concerning a Weston-Everest Metro Police Department payout. Trustee Clark, participating by Zoom, was excluded from the closed session per consistent board practice. Upon returning to open session, Trustee Olson moved to take no action based on the closed session discussion; the motion was seconded by Ermeling and passed unanimously.
