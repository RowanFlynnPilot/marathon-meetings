The Wausau City Council met on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, and took action on a range of significant issues including a rezoning request for Honey Locust Court (approved 8-3 over objections from the district alder), a lead service line replacement ordinance and funding plan (city to cover full private-side costs, approved 10-1), a transit fare collection system upgrade for Metroride (approved unanimously), and a boulevard snow plowing policy amendment restoring counterflow plowing to push snow toward center medians (approved unanimously). The council also approved a $50,000 feasibility study and a due diligence option with Marathon County for the UW-Stevens Point at Wausau campus as a potential public safety building, then adjourned into closed session regarding litigation with Georgia-Pacific.

Discussion

Proclamations

Mayor Diny presented three proclamations: designating the week as Fire Prevention Week with a focus on lithium-ion battery safety; commending the Wausau Fire Department for the September 12, 2026 aircraft rescue on Lake Wausau in which two occupants, including a U.S. House member, were rescued; and proclaiming October as Pedestrian Safety Month and October 15 as White Cane Safety Day. Fire Chief Jeremy Cop accepted the rescue commendation and praised inter-agency coordination. Chris Filtz of the Bike and Pedestrian Advisory Committee accepted the pedestrian safety proclamation and highlighted the committee's work on e-bike ordinances, faster sidewalk clearing, and flashing beacon installations.

Public Comment – Flock License Plate Camera System

Four residents spoke opposing the Flock automated license plate reader (ALPR) contract. Speakers argued the city should decline to appropriate the $50,000 December payment, omit Flock funding from the 2027 budget, deliver written termination notice under section 11.16 of the agreement by November 15, and pass a surveillance technology ordinance requiring a public council vote before future acquisitions. Concerns cited included Fourth Amendment implications (referencing a Wisconsin federal lawsuit and a WILL lawsuit against Waukesha County), domestic violence victim safety, improper data anonymization, and broader data-sharing practices by Flock.

Public Comment – Boulevard Snow Plowing Policy

Four residents from Highland Park Boulevard and Everest Boulevard spoke in support of reverting to the historical practice of plowing snow toward the center median boulevard rather than to the curb side. Speakers praised the Infrastructure and Facilities Committee for passing Resolution 5526 and urged the full council to adopt clear, binding language so the policy cannot be changed unilaterally by staff again. One resident proposed specific directive language: 'to amend the winter maintenance policy to direct that plowing pattern on the boulevard streets will push the snow to the median strip, authorizing counterflow plowing as required to achieve this outcome.'

File 26-10003: Rezoning Honey Locust Court (SR2 to DR8)

Alder Tierney pulled this item, arguing that rezoning nine lots on a cul-de-sac to DR8 (twin homes, up to 8 units per acre) in the Green Tree subdivision warranted a neighborhood meeting before council action. Director Feifer clarified the lots are privately owned, that the adjacent 40 lots are already DR8, and that twin homes are individually deeded units. Alders Killian and Neal supported postponing for a neighborhood meeting; Alder Martens opposed delay, noting consistency with the existing DR8 zoning on the street. A motion to postpone to the next meeting failed 3-8. The main rezoning motion then passed 8-3 over Tierney's objection.

Youth Engagement Committee of the Whole

Alders Wiskowski and Killian requested a committee of the whole to engage student councils from Wausau East and Wausau West high schools on youth retention, city planning, and what would draw young people back to Wausau. Alder Lukens advocated including youth who stay locally and do not attend four-year colleges. Mayor Diny noted the mayor's office is planning an annual mock council event with 40-50 youth leaders. The council approved scheduling the committee of the whole (tentatively early December) unanimously 11-0.

File 26-0921: Lead Service Line Replacement Ordinance (Reconsidered)

The council first voted 9-2 to reconsider the ordinance, which had previously failed. Alder Hoenecke proposed an amendment adding specific incremental repayment schedules for property owners, but withdrew the amendment after the council subsequently adopted Option C funding (city covers the full private-side gap). Alder Martens explained the ordinance provides enforcement leverage against non-participating landlords and strengthens the city's standing for grant funding. City Attorney and waterworks staff confirmed the ordinance's financing language is not inconsistent with the full-funding resolution but serves as a contingency for potential stragglers after primary federal funding ends. The ordinance passed 10-1.

File 26-0905: WISGO/Cubic Fare Collection System for Metroride

Metroide Deputy Director Melinda presented the new app- and card-based fare collection system, confirming cash payment would remain an option. Alder Killian confirmed accessibility for riders without smartphones. Alder Martens noted the system is largely grant-funded, boosts ridership by reducing friction, and also adds automated vehicle location (AVL) so riders can track buses. Alder Trueblood asked about ongoing costs; staff from CCITC noted approximately $24,000 annual maintenance plus a small per-transaction fee after initial grant coverage. The contract was approved unanimously 11-0.

File 25-427: 700 Grand Avenue Development Agreement Tax Increment Grant

Staff confirmed the developer reached total development costs and received occupancy on September 24, triggering the city's contractual obligation to pay 90% of the tax increment grant within 30 days. The resolution approving the multi-year budget modification passed unanimously 11-0 with little debate.

File 26-10005: Drinking Water Treatment Solar Array Project (Failed)

Alder Martens explained the city had hoped for a U.S. EPA grant to cover up to 50% of the solar array cost at the water treatment plant, but did not receive it. Without the grant, city borrowing would stretch the payback period to nearly 20 years on a system with a 25-30 year lifespan. The Finance Committee voted to not proceed; since motions are made in the affirmative, a vote to approve the project failed 0-11.

File 26-10006: Funding Option for Private-Side Lead Service Line Replacement (Option C)

CIP consultants Sean Agard and a colleague presented data showing Wausau leads the nation in per-capita lead service line replacements, with over 3,500 lines replaced and roughly 2,800-2,900 remaining. They outlined three funding options for the private-side gap; the Finance Committee unanimously recommended Option C, in which the city covers the entire estimated $4.5 million private-side gap for 2027, arguing it is most equitable, captures remaining BIL funding at 0.25% interest, and avoids significant construction cost escalation (construction prices up 56% over two years statewide). The council approved Option C unanimously 11-0.

Winter Street Maintenance Policy Amendment (Boulevard Snow Plowing)

The council debated language in Resolution 5526 permitting snowplow drivers to travel counterflow to traffic on boulevard streets 'when it would otherwise be safe to do so.' Alders Larson and Neal expressed concern the discretionary language could allow DPW to revert to the old practice; Alder Slonski and DPW Director Lynman defended it as necessary to protect driver safety and CDL licenses, with Lynman committing that counterflow plowing to the center median would occur in the vast majority of full plow events. Alder Lukens noted a city attorney opinion confirmed counterflow plowing is legal under state statute for municipal operations. The policy amendment passed unanimously 11-0. Following passage, Alder Larson withdrew his prior request for a committee of the whole on the snow policy.

File 26-0806: UW-Stevens Point at Wausau Campus Feasibility Study ($50,000)

The council debated a $50,000 budget amendment to fund a feasibility study for converting the former UW-Stevens Point at Wausau campus (518 S. 7th Ave.) into a police and fire public safety facility. Alder Larson opposed it, questioning why the county is eager to offload the building and calling it a low priority. Alder Martens expressed doubts about the ultimate project but supported the study to gather more information. The vote required a two-thirds supermajority and passed 9-2.

File 26-0806: Planning and Due Diligence Option with Marathon County for WSP at Wausau Campus

The council took up a companion resolution approving a planning and due diligence option agreement with Marathon County for the same campus property. Alder Watson announced she would abstain, citing her dual role and uncertainty about her district's position. The motion passed 9-1 with one abstaining, requiring only a simple majority after the rules were suspended.

Adjournment to Closed Session

The council unanimously adjourned to closed session pursuant to Wis. Stat. §19.85(1)(g) to confer with legal counsel regarding litigation strategy and a potential settlement offer in City of Wausau v. Georgia-Pacific LLC et al., Case No. 35-CV-4, U.S. District Court for the Western District of Wisconsin. Mayor Diny indicated the council would likely return to open session afterward.
