Human Resources, Finance, & Property Committee Meeting

July 8, 2026 · 1h 39m
BudgetStaffingSNAP Quality AssuranceFacilities DevelopmentUser Fees

The Marathon County Human Resources, Finance & Property Committee met to address several budget and operational matters, including approving a SNAP quality assurance staffing plan funded entirely by state dollars, approving a District Attorney's office comfort room project using non-lapsing funds, and adopting a new county donation acceptance policy. The committee also discussed 2027 budget development strategies, a user fee guidance framework, and the status of two county-owned surplus properties on River Drive and Thomas Street, with the Thomas Street property generating new interest after the state of Wisconsin released an RFP for a Wausau-area crime lab location.

Discussion

Approval of June Claims and Questioned Costs

Chair Robinson raised a question about a $46,590 payment to American Transmission Company appearing in the claims report at line 1528. Finance Director Sam explained that the payment was actually a customer payment — environmental impact funds received by the county — that appeared in the report due to ACH/ETF sequencing. The committee accepted the explanation and approved the claims unanimously.

2027 Annual Budget Development — Mandatory/Discretionary Programs and Rates and Fees

County Administrator Leonard and Chair Robinson presented the budget development framework, explaining that committees are being asked to review mandated versus discretionary programs and weigh in on service levels and fee structures before the administrator submits a budget in late September. Administrator Leonard described a new 'bucket challenge' approach where board members will physically allocate chips among funding priorities to signal preferences. Chair Gibbs asked about specific dollar values behind percentage-based revenue assumptions; Leonard estimated roughly $2 million in new revenue. One supervisor asked about T-hangar fees at the airport; Leonard and Robinson noted that airport fees fall under the Central Wisconsin Airport Board, a joint Marathon-Portage County entity, and offered to refer the concern there.

Budget Amendment: 2025 Wisconsin Act 116 SNAP Quality Assurance Mandate

Krista from the Department of Social Services explained that federal changes under HR1 and Wisconsin Act 116 require significantly more SNAP quality assurance reviews — at least 25% of applications, or roughly 425 reviews per month across the IM Central consortium. The state is providing 100% funding for new positions, with no tax levy impact. Vice Chair Pool noted Wisconsin's current 5.72% error rate is below the 6% federal threshold and that penalties above that threshold would be costly, saying he supported the measure. The motion was approved unanimously.

Resolution Approving Use of District Attorney Non-Lapsing Contingency Fund for Comfort Room Project

District Attorney Mayo explained the project would create a separate entrance to the DA's victim comfort room so that victims can access the space without entering the secured office area, addressing both privacy and cybersecurity compliance concerns. Administrator Leonard noted the project is estimated at $10,000 or less, praised the DA's office for building up the non-lapsing fund, and confirmed it had also passed through the Public Safety Committee the previous day. Chair Gibbs moved to approve; the motion carried unanimously.

2027 Budget Assumptions and Priorities

Administrator Leonard outlined key revenue assumptions: 1.65% net new construction growth (yielding approximately $850,000–$900,000 in new revenue), $17 million in sales tax (conservatively projected), slightly higher shared revenue, and flat utility aid, totaling roughly $2 million in new money. Supervisor Watson raised concern about the 8% health insurance cost assumption, noting some counties are projecting 15–20% increases; HR staff said the estimate is based on current claims modeling but is being closely monitored. Leonard noted that a 20% rate would require plan design changes. The committee did not take formal action, with direction to revisit assumptions and formally adopt them at the next meeting.

Resolution to Approve Marathon County Donation Policy

Policy author Candace presented a new framework delegating authority to the county administrator to accept donations, gifts, grants, or sponsorships of $10,000 or less without full board approval, subject to strict conditions including no conflicts of interest, no pay-to-play arrangements, and no ongoing operational obligations beyond existing budgets. The policy was prompted by the administrative burden of routing a $50 donation through three committees and the full board. Supervisor Hart raised a question about whether reporting should be quarterly to this committee rather than annually to the full board; it was clarified that reporting will be annual and will accompany a budget amendment, coming to this committee first and then to the full board. The committee directed that the draft be corrected to reflect annual rather than quarterly reporting before advancing. The motion carried unanimously. Chair Robinson also acknowledged Candace's upcoming departure from county employment.

Budget Amendment to Accept Wisconsin Emergency Management Grants

The finance director explained that the Sheriff's Department applied for two Wisconsin Emergency Management Grants totaling $64,568 to purchase night vision devices and mounts for the SWAT team and robotic equipment for the bomb squad. The grants were not included in the 2026 budget, requiring an amendment. The committee approved the amendment unanimously with no substantive debate.

User Fee Guidance Document Discussion

Vice Chair Pool initiated the discussion, arguing that the county needs a consistent, formal approach to setting user fees rather than the current ad hoc department-by-department method, and that fees should reflect actual cost of service delivery. Administrator Leonard provided background on the complexity of program-based budgeting versus line-item budgeting, noting Marathon County previously attempted priority-based budgeting around 2016 but abandoned it partly due to inadequate accounting systems. The committee agreed to develop a user fee guidance policy over time with ongoing input from Vice Chair Pool, with no formal action taken today.

River Drive and Thomas Street RFP Updates

Facilities Director Chris reported that River Drive RFPs are due the following week and at least one or two submissions are expected based on walk-through attendance, while no prospective buyers attended either Thomas Street walk-through. The committee discussed engaging a commercial real estate agent for Thomas Street. Administrator Leonard then disclosed that the Wisconsin Department of Justice had released a 156-page RFP that same morning seeking approximately 28,600 square feet in the greater Wausau area for a crime lab on a 10- or 20-year lease — a square footage that roughly matches Thomas Street. The committee directed administration to review the RFP, reach out informally to DOJ to gauge interest, and also contact Wausau Fire and Police Departments about potential interest. The matter will return at the July 28th meeting, with a goal of disposing of both properties by end of calendar year.
