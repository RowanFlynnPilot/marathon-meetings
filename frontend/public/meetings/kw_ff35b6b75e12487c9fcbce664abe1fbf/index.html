Revised Administrative Policy Committee Meeting

November 17, 2026

The Village of Kronenwetter's Administrative Policy Committee held its November 17, 2026 meeting, approving several budget-related recommendations including a $5,000 park grant match, a multi-item 2026 budget amendment, and health insurance renewals for two employee groups. Finance Director John Jacobs led extensive discussions on tax increment district closure timelines, the 2027 budget, and upcoming property revaluation — all of which carry significant long-term financial implications for village taxpayers. The committee also agreed by consensus to skip its December meeting and reconvene January 19, 2027.

Discussion

Approval of Minutes — October 13, October 19, and October 20, 2026

The committee moved to approve all three sets of minutes together. During discussion, committee member Guy Fridl noted he had previously raised a question about whether areas along Maple Ridge Road have developable sewer and water capacity; the presiding officer directed that the question be noted in the minutes and referred to Public Works Director Greg for a response at a future meeting. The motion was approved 4-0.

Item F: Update on Estimated Closure Dates for All Tax Increment Districts

Finance Director John Jacobs provided a detailed overview of projected closure timelines for all four TIF districts. TID 3 was already closed in February 2026, eight years early, returning approximately $3 million in valuation to the tax roll and adding $1,139 to the levy in 2027 with a full effect of roughly $8,300 thereafter. TID 2 is projected to close by 2029 — five years early — which could add up to $219,000 to the tax levy by 2031; Jacobs noted the $863,200 borrowed in late 2024 must be spent or committed by October 2027 and may remain in TID 2 to fund Maple Ridge Road work. TID 4 is expected to close around 2031–2032 after going temporarily cash-flow negative. TID 1, boosted by a large distribution center project, could close by 2039 — five years early — adding approximately $140,000 annually to the levy by 2041. Jacobs declined to publicly state the distribution center's estimated market value, saying the developer had not yet disclosed it publicly, but confirmed $15 million as a first-year rough estimate. When asked about charging interest on the village's advance to TID 1, Jacobs said he would seek guidance from the village's financial advisor before making a recommendation.

Item G: 2027 Budget Presentation Highlights

Jacobs highlighted key elements of the adopted 2027 budget, noting the assessed tax rate rose 22 cents to $4.25 but that after the pending property revaluation — required by state law to be completed by end of 2027 — the rate will fall significantly, comparable to what occurred after the 2021 revaluation. He explicitly addressed social media claims that the rate would reach $5.00, calling them false and explaining that revaluation mechanics bring the assessed rate in line with the equalized rate. Budget additions include fully funding two new patrol officers (the second position still unfilled), a new utility operator starting January 1, a deputy clerk starting April 1, and a 3.5% cost-of-living wage increase across the board. Health insurance for non-police staff came in at 4% rather than the feared 8% increase.

Item H: December 2026 Assessed Tax Rates

Jacobs presented estimated combined tax rates for both Mosinee and D.C. Everest school district residents, noting the final numbers were expected before Thanksgiving. For Mosinee district taxpayers, the total rate is projected to rise roughly $1.04, or about 6%; the village's share of that increase (22 cents) is the third-lowest among taxing jurisdictions, with Marathon County's increase (42 cents, 8.4%) being the largest. D.C. Everest district residents face an estimated 85-cent total increase, or about 4.9%. Jacobs reminded residents that the village comprises about 23% of the total tax bill and cannot answer questions about other jurisdictions' charges.

Item I: 2026 Budget Amendment #5 — Park Grant Matching Funds

Jacobs presented a $5,000 transfer from the general fund to the park fund to match a grant from the Wausau and Marathon County Park and Recreation Foundation, consistent with a prior parks committee recommendation to the village board. The committee moved to recommend board approval. The motion carried 4-0 on a roll call vote.

Item J: 2026 Budget Amendment #6 — General Fund and TID 3 Fund

Jacobs outlined a series of revenue and expenditure adjustments to bring the 2026 general fund budget in line with actual activity. New revenues include a $54,370 oil pipeline termination state aid payment (previously unknown) and a $42,855 transfer from the closing TID 3 fund. Expenditure increases cover ambulance run overruns (projected at 492 runs vs. 420 budgeted), village attorney costs roughly doubling to ~$60,000 due to a personnel investigation and a surge in open records requests, $25,000 in additional municipal building repairs including a $14,500 hot water heater replacement, fuel surcharge fees for refuse and recycling totaling ~$57,000, and miscellaneous solid waste and cleanup week overruns. The contingency reserve draw was reduced from $60,000 to $38,815 to offset these changes. The TID 3 fund closure transfers were also included. Guy Fridl moved the recommendation; Mary Solheim seconded; approved 4-0 on roll call.

Item K: 2027–2029 Health Insurance — Team Care

Jacobs reported that Team Care unexpectedly provided a three-year proposal, locking in a 4% premium increase for 2027 ($469.50/month), a maximum 4% increase for 2028 ($488.30), and a maximum 6% increase for 2029 ($517.60). Approximately 15–20 non-police-union employees are covered. The committee expressed positive surprise at the multi-year offer. Misty Swanson moved to recommend board approval; Guy Fridl seconded; approved 4-0 on roll call. Jacobs noted the proposal must be signed by December 6.

Item L: 2027 Health Insurance — United Health Care (Police Union Group)

Jacobs presented a 14.2% premium increase from United Health Care for the seven-member police union group, raising the monthly premium from approximately $14,000 to $16,417. He noted staff is exploring whether this group could be moved to Team Care, but complications include two employees currently waiving coverage (Team Care only allows one waiver), individual rate variability by age and family size, and a submission deadline of December 12. Guy Fridl moved to recommend the quote to the board for consideration in light of additional future information; Mary Solheim seconded; approved 4-0 on roll call.

Future Agenda Items and December Meeting Cancellation

Committee member Guy Fridl suggested a future agenda discussion on building reserve funds modeled after Marathon County's approach, crediting Jacobs for stabilizing village finances but expressing a desire to go further. Jacobs and the presiding officer discussed the impracticality of a December 15 APC meeting given that the December 28 village board meeting was likely canceled, meaning any recommendations couldn't be acted on until January 11. By consensus the committee agreed to cancel the December 15 meeting and reconvene January 19, 2027, when financial audit materials or internal year-end statements may be available.
