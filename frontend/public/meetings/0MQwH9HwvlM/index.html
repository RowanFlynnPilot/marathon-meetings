Approval of Minutes - June 2, 2026

Minutes were moved by Alder Kilian and seconded by Alder Neil. The motion carried with no opposition noted.

3A: Business Campus Available Properties

Staff member Randy presented an overview of how city-owned properties in the Wausau Business Campus are advertised on the city website, including interactive maps with wetland delineation, utility information, and property flyers. He also demonstrated use of Crexi and LoopNet to track privately listed properties, noting three currently for sale in the campus — one vacant strip between Stewart and Highway 29 (listed 917-plus days), a warehousing facility (also listed over 900 days), and the former DOJ crime lab site. Alder Neil raised concerns about passive marketing, suggesting a proactive electronic newsletter outreach to regional developers and business leaders. Staff acknowledged marketing budget cuts and noted that committee and council direction on prioritizing staff time is needed.

3B: Employment Demographics and Housing Data

Randy presented data showing Marathon County's labor force participation rate of 65% exceeds state and national averages, and that unemployment has remained in the 3-4% range since 2015. He presented housing cost data showing a 43.7% increase in single-family construction costs and a 62.3% increase in Marathon County median home sale prices since 2019. Alder Kilian noted that despite high prices, homes receive multiple above-asking offers quickly and the real constraint is limited inventory, partly because existing homeowners with low mortgage rates are reluctant to sell. Alder Neil asked whether investors rather than owner-occupants were driving demand; Kilian said Director Feifick does not perceive significant corporate home buying in the area. Staff noted an estimated underproduction of approximately 1,000 housing units from 2006 to 2024.

3C: Community Development Block Grant Program Overview

Community Development Manager Tammy Stratz presented an overview of the city's CDBG, HOME, lead safe homes, commercial rehab loan, and property disposition programs. The city's current CDBG allocation is $582,162, with 15% ($87,000) available for public service activities. She noted the homeowner rehabilitation loan program currently has no waiting list despite outreach, a reversal from prior years when waits were two to three years. She also noted that first-time home buyers assisted through the down payment program are being outbid in the current market and cannot compete without inspection contingencies. Stratz highlighted active projects including a modular home at 1019 West Bridge Street and a six-unit apartment complex at 405 South 8th Avenue, and noted the city is on its seventh lead safe homes grant cycle. Alder Neil asked whether tax rebate incentives could help sellers accept income-qualified buyers; Stratz confirmed that tax rebates are not permissible in Wisconsin.

4A: Planning Option Agreement - Eminent Development Corporation

Staff clarified this was a planning option — not a development agreement — allowing developer Julian Walters of Eminent Development Corporation to conduct due diligence on a city-owned commercial parcel of approximately 62,612 square feet. Alder Kilian opposed the motion, arguing the city does not need additional housing given existing and pipeline projects (Foundry on Third, Scott Street development, Commonwealth apartments, Wayneguard 143-unit project, Gorman project, and Gdorsy senior project), and objecting to selling a parcel valued at approximately $563,500 for $1 plus city subsidy. The committee chair countered that vacant city properties generate no revenue and noted some have sat unsold for over six years. Alder Neil supported moving forward but requested future presentations include data-driven housing needs analysis and noted the project currently lacks an affordable/low-income component. Julian Walters, joining remotely, said the planning option period would allow a market study and a more solution-based approach. The motion was approved with at least three votes in favor and Alder Kilian voting no; exact tally not stated.

4B: Planning Option Agreement - CD Smith Construction (DOJ Crime Lab RFP)

Todd Wonderlick of CD Smith Construction requested a planning option on a business campus property to allow submission of the site in response to a Wisconsin state RFP for a new DOJ crime lab facility, due July 23. CD Smith recently completed the Wausau-area state crime lab (closed May 2026, over 200,000 square feet). Wonderlick explained the RFP process typically takes two to three months after submission before a selection is made. Alder Kilian confirmed with Director Feifick that the project is a relocation of the existing DOJ crime lab, would add minimal new jobs, and critically, the building would be privately owned with DOJ leasing — making it a taxable property. The chair noted staff proximity advantages for keeping the lab in the area. The motion was approved unanimously.

5A: 1300 Cleveland Avenue Public Engagement Process Update

Staff member Carrie presented an update on the second community engagement session held in late May, which drew over 30 participants including neighborhood residents, alders, the mayor, and city staff. The session covered survey results from a January engagement session, a feasibility analysis of the top five non-industrial land uses identified by residents, and an interactive questionnaire. A total of 43 surveys were collected (29 paper, 14 online), with the survey closing July 1. Staff is compiling data and will present a comprehensive analysis at the August meeting. An email list of 102 stakeholders is being maintained for ongoing updates. Alder Kilian thanked staff for their work and noted neighborhood appreciation.

5B: Economic Development Committee Website Description Update

Randy reported that following the committee's direction at the previous meeting, city attorney Anne Jacobson is leading a broader review of descriptions and duties for all standing committees — not just the Economic Development Committee — which will include website language updates. The committee was informed this process is underway and they will have an opportunity to review all standing committee descriptions holistically.
