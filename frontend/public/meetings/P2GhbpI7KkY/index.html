The Marathon County Board held its annual budget hearing on November 2, 2026, receiving a presentation of the proposed 2027 budget from County Administrator Leonard and HR Finance Committee Chair Robinson. The budget features a property tax rate drop of 1 cent to $3.48, maintains current service levels, and is anchored by a no-new-debt funding strategy for the approximately $55 million highway facility relocation from West Street to the Highway 51/Cronin Wetter site. No budget amendments were adopted at this meeting; the board will consider amendments and adopt the final budget at a November 10, 2026 meeting.

Discussion

Overview of proposed 2027 budget

HR Finance Committee Chair Robinson outlined the year-long budget development process, noting no amendments had been made since the administrator's September 29 presentation. County Administrator Leonard presented key budget metrics: the property tax rate drops 1 cent to $3.48, Marathon County has the lowest per capita spending and levy rate in the region, and net new construction growth was approximately 1.545%. Leonard emphasized the cornerstone of the budget is shifting the $3.5 million North Central Healthcare payment onto the debt levy to fund the new highway facility without incurring new debt, a strategy he called unprecedented in scale among Wisconsin local governments.

Public hearing on proposed 2027 budget — Flock license plate reader cameras

Two residents testified urging the board to defund the county's Flock Safety license plate reader system, citing mass surveillance concerns, lack of board oversight over the grant contracts, data-sharing with approximately 1,397 agencies across roughly 40 states, and a 2022 contract granting Flock a perpetual license to county citizen data. They asked the board to amend the budget to prohibit county funds — including grant funds — from going to Flock without a board vote, and to require public hearings before accepting future surveillance technology grants. Sheriff Bib subsequently addressed the board, defending the technology as a law enforcement tool with ongoing audits conducted twice monthly by a captain, stating the data belongs solely to Marathon County, that deputies must have an active case number to access the system, and that the department does not use it for immigration enforcement. Supervisor Krebs supported the sheriff's position, comparing it to other law enforcement tools. Supervisor Robinson announced he had arranged a meeting between the sheriff and one of the public commenters to explore resolution. The board agreed to take up the issue in depth at the next Public Safety Committee meeting on November 10.

Public hearing on proposed 2027 budget — Highway facility funding strategy

A resident, a former county board member, testified raising four concerns: the long-term taxpayer implications of Scenario 2A projecting $62.9 million in additional debt levy by 2042, the full $3.5 million shift to debt levy in year one versus the phased approach described in Resolution 4926, and whether proceeds from the Thomas Street, River Drive, and 28th Avenue properties or excess reserves could reduce the $25 million funding gap. Administrator Leonard responded that placing the full amount on the debt levy promptly is necessary to limit the period during which the general fund is being drawn down, reducing the risk of needing to borrow for other capital projects; he confirmed property sale proceeds and post-audit excess reserves would be applied to shorten the interfund loan term. Supervisors Cavelli and Nisth pressed for further detail on phasing options and use of highway reserves, with Finance Director Sam Fensky clarifying the working capital calculation methodology.

Social services staffing — 12 new positions

Supervisor Sundowski questioned the rationale and cost for 12 new positions in the Social Services department. Administrator Leonard and Social Services Director Christa explained that the Children's Long-Term Support (CLTS) social worker positions are fully federally funded through Medicaid billing based on caseload metrics required by the state, that the county is under a corrective action plan obligation to eliminate its waitlist, and that positions will be filled incrementally as caseloads grow. The quality assurance or PCR positions are funded through the state contract stemming from federal requirements and carry no direct tax levy impact; if federal or state funding ends, the positions would be eliminated.

User fees and equity concerns

Supervisor Leur raised concerns that increasing user fees disproportionately burdens lower-income residents and asked staff to track the impact of fee changes on service utilization. Administrator Leonard acknowledged the tension between cost recovery and equity, noted that some fees — such as the marriage license fee — were not increased at the county clerk's recommendation, and stressed that fee and service-level decisions are ultimately board policy choices. He flagged solid waste tipping fees as highly consequential and noted two additional fee items — a professional services fee in the medical examiner's office and minor solid waste changes — would be brought before the board before final budget adoption.

Tax-deed property sale — Resolution 77-26

Supervisor Jensen questioned why the county would sell a tax-delinquent property to the city for $1 when over $75,000 in back taxes and penalties were owed. Administrator Leonard explained that an appraiser valued the property at $0 due to environmental hazards requiring demolition, making cost recovery impossible, and that the city's offer — which does not meet the board's policy requiring the county to be made whole on past-due taxes — was brought to the full board precisely because it falls outside standard policy. The city intends to remediate the property and return it to the tax rolls. Supervisor Robinson noted the county has become more aggressive in earlier title-taking to prevent such situations from accumulating. Supervisor Griner asked whether a part-time position focused on delinquent property recovery would be cost-effective; Leonard suggested the county's existing process has largely caught up and that by 2028 the work would be mostly maintenance.

Educational discussion of November 10 action items

The board previewed several items scheduled for action on November 10, including an appointment of Jennifer Marzu to the Local Emergency Planning Committee, four rezoning ordinances (Town of Green Valley, Town of Marathon, and two in the Town of McMillan), an ATV/UTV ordinance for Highway 34, and two zoning amendment resolutions. Supervisor G flagged concerns about the ATV/UTV ordinance that she planned to raise at the Infrastructure Committee. Supervisors Sandulski and Morash expressed support for the ATV/UTV ordinance based on constituent calls. Supervisor Drew noted that a letter from an attorney regarding the Town of Marathon rezone (MK7 Enterprises) would be discussed at committee the following day. Regarding UW-Stevens Point property, Leonard reported due diligence is underway with the City of Wausau on most buildings through end of Q1 of next year, while the theater building is being pursued for a one-year lease with the Marathon County Center for Arts and Culture ahead of a nonprofit transfer.
